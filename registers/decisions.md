# Decision register

What was decided, why it matters, the choice, and how easily it can change, in the form LH F05 gives. The kind says which sort of decision it is: a direction, an operating choice, an accounting convention, or a refinement to the method.

D-0005, 26 September 2026. Direction: Lighthouse publishes by its process.
Why it matters: it is the difference between a watch that publishes and one that waits. A piece goes out when a review on the strongest available model finds nothing that must be fixed; its release note names the reviews; a person can pull anything. Three decisions stay with a person: a claim that harm is happening now, a claim that a named system is compromised, and any act that is irreversible outside the repository.
How easily it changes: not easily; it is founding scope, and the charter says it.
The first drive showed the process working: an editor and then a reviewer found five factual errors in the first drafts against the primary sources, and the corrections are in the records. A person's reading is the scarce resource, and it is spent on direction.

D-0003, 26 September 2026. Direction: outward research does not wait for local calibration.
Why it matters: it decides what Lighthouse spends its first months on. LH001, the standard candle, calibrates the instruments Lighthouse runs itself; an outward study validates its own sources within its own boundary and says whose instruments supplied the evidence.
How easily it changes: not easily; it is founding scope, revisited only if LH001's calibration turns out to change how an outward series should be read.
The first drive showed it: two defensible records came from others' observations before LH001 existed. It also settles that LH002 runs on a historical window, and that editing an article is its own ticket, distinct from the study it draws on.

D-0004, 26 September 2026. Refinement to the method: LH F02's activity surfaces gain a Correctness surface for test, sanitiser and build outcomes.
Why it matters: the earliest xz signal was a memory checker's output in a build pipeline, weeks before the timing anomaly that led to detection, and no surface in the design would have recorded it.
How easily it changes: as easily as any surface; a study that reads it and finds its unit or caveats wrong revises it.
Two caveats travel with it: a passing check does not establish correctness, and an error does not establish malice.

D-0002, 26 September 2026. Operating choice, temporary: the strongest models sit where claims are made and checked. Opus 5.5 takes research, essays and study write-ups; Fable 5.1 takes the release review; Sonnet 5 keeps collection, source checks and tabulation; Haiku only a chore the driver cannot do with hb.
Why it matters: the errors found in the first drafts came from a research pass on Sonnet, and the review that caught them ran on Opus. The review is where money buys the most.
How easily it changes: at any time; one task kind measured on two models would settle it, as Q-0002 proposes.
The price table supports it: Opus 5.5 reads cache at Sonnet's rate and doubles only writes and output; Fable 5.1 reads at close to Opus 5.5's rate and costs more on writes and output, so a review on it costs about twice an Opus review. Every subagent writes about 45,000 tokens of harness context before it starts.

D-0001, 26 September 2026. Accounting convention, and an operating bound: Claude spend is recorded at list rates as a measure, not a bill, because it comes from a subscription; Harbour's own calls through OpenRouter are the only money that leaves per call. A drive is bounded by its count of dispatches, three by default and never more than five, and by twenty dollars of measured subagent spend.
Why it matters: it keeps the cost figures honest about what they are, and it stops a runaway task.
How easily it changes: the bound at any time; the convention when the work moves to hosted Harbour or OpenCode, where Claude spend would be pay as you go.
The driver's own spend is measured and, so far, unbounded, which the ledger should show before anyone bounds it.
