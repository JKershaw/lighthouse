# Decision register

What was decided, why it matters, the choice, and how easily it can change, in the form LH F05 gives. The kind says which sort of decision it is: a direction, an operating choice, an accounting convention, or a refinement to the method.

D-0003, 26 September 2026. Direction: outward research does not wait for local calibration.
Why it matters: it decides what Lighthouse spends its first months on. LH001, the standard candle, calibrates the instruments Lighthouse runs itself; an outward study validates its own sources within its own boundary and says whose instruments supplied the evidence.
How easily it changes: not easily; it is founding scope, revisited only if LH001's calibration turns out to change how an outward series should be read.
An observing agent's reading, passed on by the keeper, argued for it, and the first drive showed it: two defensible records came from others' observations before LH001 existed. It also settles that LH002 runs on a historical window, and that editing an article is its own ticket, distinct from the study it draws on.

D-0004, 26 September 2026. Refinement to the method: LH F02's activity surfaces gain a Correctness surface for test, sanitiser and build outcomes.
Why it matters: the earliest xz signal was a memory checker's output in a build pipeline, weeks before the timing anomaly that led to detection, and no surface in the design would have recorded it.
How easily it changes: as easily as any surface; a study that reads it and finds its unit or caveats wrong revises it.
Two caveats travel with it: a passing check does not establish correctness, and an error does not establish malice.

D-0002, 26 September 2026. Operating choice, temporary: Opus 5.5 takes tasks that need reasoning; Sonnet 5 keeps collection, source checks and tabulation; Haiku only a chore the driver cannot do with hb.
Why it matters: it sets what a drive costs and, untested so far, what its research is worth.
How easily it changes: at any time; the keeper suggested it once a Sonnet task was known to cost one to two dollars, and one task kind measured on both models would settle it, as Q-0002 proposes.
The price table agrees with the choice: Opus 5.5 reads cache at Sonnet's rate and doubles only writes and output. The launcher's opus alias resolved to Opus 5.5 that day, and a probe showed that every subagent writes about 45,000 tokens of harness context before it starts.

D-0001, 26 September 2026. Accounting convention, and an operating bound: Claude spend is recorded at list rates as a measure, not a bill, because it comes from the keeper's subscription; Harbour's own calls through OpenRouter are the only money that leaves per call. A drive is bounded by its count of dispatches, three by default and never more than five, and by twenty dollars of measured subagent spend.
Why it matters: it keeps the cost figures honest about what they are, and it stops a runaway task.
How easily it changes: the bound at any time by the keeper; the convention when the work moves to hosted Harbour or OpenCode, where Claude spend would be pay as you go.
The driver's own spend is measured and, so far, unbounded, which the ledger should show before anyone bounds it.
