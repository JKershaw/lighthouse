# When was CVE-2026-98432 reported, fixed, numbered, shipped and described, and how do those dates sit beside the disclosure policies each party states?

**In short.** We asked when each step in the public life of one Linux kernel flaw happened, the one Anthropic's model wrote an exploit for, and how those dates sit beside the disclosure rules the parties publish. No public source we could read says when the flaw was reported to anyone, so the report date stays unknown. The fix's author date is 1 October (when it was first public isn't dated); it was in stable kernels released on 11 and 15 October, and its CVE was published on 15 October, 12 days before Anthropic's post of 27 October. SUSE dates the earliest of its advisories we read that name the flaw two ways, a release date of 24 October on the advisory pages and publication on 29 October on its CVE page; a read of SUSE's public announcement list on 21 November found all twelve SUSE advisories we follow posted there on the CVE page's day, not the release date's, so by the rule we fixed before that read the list supports 29 October (and 3 November for the later eight) as when SUSE announced them in public. The 24 October release date stays on the pages; what it marks isn't stated, whether the pages were public that day isn't shown, and no date here says when fixed packages reached anyone; the Red Hat errata we read are dated from 1 November, and Debian's and Ubuntu's dates are unread. Anthropic's policy page says it generally waits 45 days after a patch is available before publishing full technical details; the post came within 45 days of every candidate date before it, neither text defines full technical details, the page allows the wait to be shortened in two cases, and whether either applied isn't stated; the post describes the exploit in prose without publishing code. This version (0.4) has been through its evidence review, which cleared it on 21 November, and its reader review, whose two material points are resolved here; a fresh recheck cleared what the round changed on the same day; one revision follows after the core watch stops, by 13 January 2027.

## Findings

1. **The report date is not public in anything we could read** (observation). The nearest dated events are the CVE reservation (25 September) and the fix's author date (1 October); the brief rules out using either as a bound on the report, and this record doesn't.
2. **The fix was in stable kernels released four days before the CVE was published** (observation): 6.18.56 and 7.2.10, both 11 October, carry it; whether it was public earlier still, on the patch list or in mainline, isn't dated here; the CVE was published 15 October, minutes before the four older stable changelogs' Last-Modified times. Clocks side by side; no order of decisions is inferred.
3. **The post's 27 October date falls inside every candidate 45-day window** (derived; five candidate clocks: the brief's three, the last stable release, which the brief's comparison also computes, and the CVE's publication, added after the read, post hoc). Under the post's own wording the window can't be computed, since it follows 90 days from an unknown report. The record doesn't say whether the date matters under P1, since neither wording defines its terms and P1's page allows the buffer to be shortened in two stated cases.
4. **SUSE's public announcement list supports the later of its two clocks as the day SUSE announced every SUSE advisory we followed; it doesn't date when the fixes were released** (observation, with the amendment's rule applied as derived; version 0.4, evidence review cleared, reader review resolved, rechecked). Four SUSE kernel advisories naming the CVE give a Release Date of 24 October on their own pages (as read on 17 November), while SUSE's CVE page lists them as published on 29 October (LH036's read of 16 November; post hoc). SUSE's announcement list, read on 21 November under a rule fixed before that read, carries one message for each, posted on 29 October; the eight of 2 November were posted on 3 November, the CVE page's day for them too. So by the list none of the twelve followed SUSE advisories was announced in public before the post, and the earliest was announced two days after it. The list dates announcements, not releases, and within each day every list message came 3.3 to 3.5 hours before its CVE-page stamp, so the two look like one SUSE process seen twice rather than two independent witnesses (interpretation, post hoc: one look at one offset after the outcomes were seen, R-0106; no brief or amendment tests it): whether a SUSE fix was public before the post stays unknown. The messages list CVE-2026-98432, so the flaw was named in SUSE's public announcement of each; whether it was in anything public on 24 October isn't shown: the advisory pages and OSV records that carry that date were read weeks later, and nothing read records what was public on the day. The Release Date stays on the pages and in the messages; what it marks isn't stated, and neither date says when packages reached anyone. LH036's interim record says no tracker in its panel dates a fix on or before the post, and that Debian's and SUSE's pages give no dates; that holds for SUSE's product states, though the same page stamps each announcement. Debian's and Ubuntu's dates are unread, and 174 SUSE advisories weren't followed (their CVE-page stamps are all 29 October or later; their list dates weren't read).
5. **Red Hat's earliest followed errata came after the post** (observation): 1 November, the earliest date LH036 reads from Red Hat's tracker; twelve Red Hat errata weren't followed.
6. **The earliest oss-security message whose subject names the CVE came two to three days after the post** (observation), starting with Go's announcement, which cites the post.

## Method

The brief (protocol 0.1, 4 November) fixed the events, the clocks, the panel of 28 addresses and the follow rule before any timeline source was read; the policies were read and kept first. The one read, `LIGHTHOUSE_PROVIDER=anthropic python3 -I studies/LH038/scripts/collect.py --read 2026-11-17T1419Z --follow`, gated each address through scripts/robots_gate.py and kept raw bodies, gzipped, with status, Last-Modified, size and hash (read_log.json). The follow rule named 244 addresses and the ceiling left 52; they were filled round robin across the trackers in each tracker's own listing order (amendment of 17 November, made after the advisory lists were seen; it changes no comparison). This record's dates were extracted from the retained bodies by scripts/timeline.py, not copied from the checkpoint; the script asserts that each changelog's first commit is its release and that exactly one commit in each carries the fix, and counts the CVE's mentions in each followed advisory (all name it). The oss-security subject match was run after the read on the retained indexes (amendment of 17 November), and R-0091's independent check reproduces it. Elapsed days are UTC calendar-date differences. **Version 0.4.** The amendment of 20 November added one read, made on 21 November with scripts/suse_clocks.py (30 GETs, the ceiling exactly, robots.txt included; one more than planned because the host's front page only redirects to its list of lists; checkpoints/2026-11-21-suse-clocks.md). scripts/timeline.py now takes all three SUSE dates for each advisory from retained bodies (the Release Date from the advisory page of 17 November, the CVE-page stamp from LH036's page of 16 November, converted from CET to UTC, and the archive's date for the list message) and applies the rule as code, rather than taking the amendment's own list of clock days; the two agree. Where the rule needed reading, the choices were these. (a) A message's body repeats the advisory's Release Date; that is the clock under test, not a date the archive shows for the message, so each message shows one date and is classed. Each archived page's `Last-Modified` falls under a minute after the archive's date, on the same UTC day, so counting it too would change nothing. (b) "Supported by the list" is read as supported as the day SUSE announced the advisory in public, which is what the amendment says it compares; the other clock is kept, not called wrong, and not used to date anything else. (c) The CVE page gives its stamps in CET; they fall on the same calendar day in CET and in UTC, so the zone can't change a classing. (d) "Listed" means the identifier is in the message's list of references, as posted; what any advisory said on 24 October is not shown. (e) OSV's records name a file on SUSE's server as their source and say nothing that makes their dates OSV's own, so they are classed as SUSE's data relayed, as the amendment says, and are not in the rule. No clock was chosen, or compared, by its distance from the post; the post's date enters only the elapsed-day table afterwards. One choice was made after the bodies were seen: the post's account is quoted for its form (prose, no code), not for its mechanism, which the record gives only at the level the comparison needs.

## Limits

- **Unknown, not no.** T0; committer dates; the first mainline release; P2's and P1's texts on the dates they would have applied; linux-distros use; Ubuntu's and Debian's advisory dates; the Wayback bound on the post. Each is missing for a stated reason (a 403 from the session's own access layer for GitHub, robots.txt disallowed or unreachable, challenge pages, size ceiling).
- **Followed advisories only.** 192 advisory addresses were not followed under the ceiling, among them 174 SUSE, 12 Red Hat and 6 Ubuntu; earlier advisories may exist among them. The revision after LH036 stops may follow them.
- **SUSE's pages as read.** Each followed SUSE advisory lists many CVEs (129 for the four of 24 October); whether this CVE was listed at first release isn't shown. SUSE's Release Date and its CVE page's publication stamp differ by about five days for the four announcements of 24 October (about a day and a half for the eight of 2 November). What the Release Date marks is not stated in anything read, and no SUSE date read says when packages reached anyone. A message missing from an index would have been "no message found", not "not announced"; none was missing.
- **The fix's first public appearance** (patch list, mainline) is undated here.
- **Author dates are not release times**, and a page's Last-Modified is a server's clock, not a publication time.
- **Day-only dates.** Red Hat's dates and the post's datePublished carry no time, so differences of a day near midnight are uncertain by up to a day. The order of the post and SUSE's list announcements (two days apart) doesn't depend on this.
- **One case.** Nothing here generalises to other flaws, finders or vendors.
- **What can't be known from public sources:** what was said privately between reporter, kernel and distributions, why any date fell where it did, and whether anyone kept to a policy.
- **The amendment was post hoc by construction.** It was made after both SUSE clocks were seen, to settle which one marks public announcement; its rule was fixed and committed before the list or OSV was read, and the list's result was not known to its writer.
- **Model disclosure.** Written by an Anthropic model about Anthropic's post; the reviews check for wording easier or harder on Anthropic than the sources allow.
