# LH055 round 2 checkpoint, 17 November 2026

**In short.** We went looking for evidence outside the attackers' own records for Amazon's report that stolen AI keys paid for the hunt for more keys, and found none in seven more reports: none adds evidence of its own, and the one that repeats it only quotes Amazon. What Amazon's own sensors saw is the agents sending their working prompts to its decoys, which posed as AI services, as if to run on them; Amazon doesn't say how the decoys came into the agents' supply or what they sent back, so that isn't evidence of stolen keys paying for anything. The answer is still inconclusive. This round has had its evidence review and its reader review; a recheck is still to come. ## What it found

- **Amazon's campaign: no change.** All six links stay claimed without sufficient disclosed support. No Amazon statement on how its decoys came to be used was found, so that reading stays untested. The Stack quotes Amazon's CISO in words not in our copy of Amazon's post, origin unknown.
- **Dependence.** The Stack repeats Amazon with nothing of its own; Equixly's analysis of confirmed outcomes and Talos's survey (published half an hour after Amazon's post, revised on 16 November) don't mention the loop. No second origin turned up, in a thin check.
- **Microsoft, a different campaign.** Its own telemetry, as it reports it, shows agents searching GitHub for AI inference keys and compromised Azure AI services among stolen resources (coded supported for acquisition and access there). On use it says "abuse" in a summary and "might have attempted to abuse" in the detail, with no figure; coded claimed.
- **ARTEX's design.** Datadog says the user supplies keys for chosen providers and ARTEX moves to the next key when one is banned or cut off, with an /llm/pool route. That may be what Amazon meant by "the framework's own model gateway" (our reading, post hoc); it shows no stolen key loaded.
- **The reseller.** CrowdStrike says the Korean actor "likely accessed DeepSeek via the likely LLM API proxy/reseller" and nothing about buying. "Bought" was Censys's word. A reseller doesn't by itself tell paid capacity from resold stolen capacity (our reading, post hoc).
- **Other relays.** Check Point's case, via Equixly, of agents reporting cash-outs that never happened (agent-recorded false success, another campaign).
