## What round 1 found

- **Use and control.** Several actors since at least August 2026 (FBI synthesis); Microsoft's own observation of Storm-3358 from October; Amazon's decoy capture of ARTEX agents' requests, 3 October to 5 November. People set the objectives, targets and limits in every source that speaks to it; "minimal human intervention" is each publisher's own reading, and none measures it.
- **Scale and results** come in units that can't be combined (exposed hosts, console instances, targets, tenants with attempted sign-ins, records, individuals). Most results are relayed from reports not yet read; Amazon's are unverified agent records, and one of its campaigns failed in every attempt the agents recorded.
- **The loop.** Amazon is the only ARTEX source to describe it, and all six links are coded there as claimed without sufficient disclosed support. FBI, Microsoft, Censys and StepSecurity leave all six unknown. Nothing contradicts it. SANS (not ARTEX) directly observed an agent holding collected keys; its other five links rest on captured agent-side records.
- **Dependence.** The ARTEX loop has a single origin here (Amazon's sensors), with no repetition and no corroboration among the primaries read. The ConnectWise figure and the reseller purchase each have one origin repeated three times.
- **Pointing the other way.** One actor bought model access through a reseller (CrowdStrike, relayed); consoles hold the keys their users configure. Both fit the explanation that people supplied the inference while harvesting keys was an objective.
