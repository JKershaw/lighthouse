**In short.** Anthropic said on 27 October that its model wrote a working exploit for a serious Linux kernel flaw, CVE-2026-98432, fixed on 15 October; we watch the signals defenders rely on and where the fix has landed. To 16 November (interim; reviewed against the data and for how it reads, and rechecked): CISA had not listed the flaw as exploited in the latest catalogue read, of 13 November, which does not say whether anyone has exploited it; the exploit-prediction score's only one-day rise by a factor of two or more came on 25 October, a day after the kernel's CVE team updated the flaw's official record and two days before the post, and after the post smaller daily rises together about doubled it by 13 November; the dates do not say why the score moved. NVD finished its analysis on 12 November, by NVD's clock, without a score of its own. At our first read, five days after the post, Debian's security suites, its forky and sid, and 19 of the 112 SUSE products with a main kernel package had a fix, and no Red Hat or Ubuntu release did; no tracker dates a fix on or before the post, though Debian's and SUSE's pages give no dates. By 16 November Debian's security channels, the kernels of Red Hat's Enterprise Linux 8, 9 and 10 and Ubuntu's four newest releases carry a fix; SUSE lists a released kernel for 42 of the 121 products it names with a main kernel package, and Ubuntu's three oldest affected releases are still pending. Reads go on twice a week until 11 January 2027, or sooner if CISA lists the flaw.

## Answer

Interim, to the read of 16 November 2026; reviewed for evidence (R-0077, recheck R-0078) and for reading and inference (R-0079, resolved in version 0.2), and that resolution rechecked (R-0081). **Signals:** after Anthropic's post of 27 October, CISA did not list the flaw, no CISA container or score of NVD's own appeared, and EPSS rose without a step (the brief's rule: a day-on-day change by a factor of two or more), its two larger daily rises by factors of 1.15 on 30 October and 1.66 on 3 November; with two smaller ones they took the score from 0.00388 on 27 October to 0.00777 on 13 November, a factor of 2.00 over 17 days (derived; a summary over a window from E2 to the series' last change, chosen after the series was read, not one of the brief's measures). CISA's catalogue did not list the CVE in any of the five versions read (the latest 2026.11.13); the CVE record never gained a CISA ADP container, so no SSVC values; NVD moved from Received to Analyzed (12 November, NVD's clock) and added a weakness and affected version ranges but no score of its own; and the EPSS series has one step, on 25 October (0.00165 to 0.00388, a factor of 2.35), 1 day after E1 and 2 days before E2, rising afterwards only by smaller factors to 0.00777 (percentile 0.564) on 15 November (derived). A step near an event is a coincidence of dates; nothing here says why the score moved.

**Fixes:** at the first read, 5 days after E2, Debian's security channels for bookworm and trixie, and forky and sid, were already fixed, as were 19 of the 112 SUSE products that list a main kernel package; Red Hat's RHEL 8, 9 and 10 gained fixed releases between the first and second reads (Red Hat dates them 1 November); Ubuntu's stonking turned released between 5 and 9 November and jammy, noble and resolute between 9 and 12 November. On 16 November bionic, focal and xenial are pending, RHEL AI 3 is Affected, and 73 of the 121 SUSE products that list a main kernel package list only Affected for those packages (observation). No tracker in the panel dates a fix on or before E2; Debian's and SUSE's pages give no dates, so when their earliest fixes landed is not known here.

## Method

The brief (protocol 0.1, fixed 1 November 2026 before the first read, with its amendment of 8 November) defines the five measures, the events E0 to E4 and the observation clock. The collector made one GET per panel URL at each read, kept bodies gzipped under data/read-<label>/ with a read log, and from 9 November asked scripts/robots_gate.py first. tabulate.py, written after the baseline, implements the measures and writes tables.json. For this record the tabulator was rerun on a scratch copy of the six reads and its output was byte-identical to the committed tables.json; the collector was not run. Per-read summaries (Red Hat product counts, SUSE product classes, new references, EPSS day-on-day ratios) were computed by a short offline script over tables.json that is not kept in the repository; each count can be repeated from tables.json alone. NVD's weakness and configuration fields and the Ubuntu notice dates were read from the retained raw bodies of 16 November.

Two summary choices were made at the baseline checkpoint, after the first outcomes were seen, and are labelled post hoc: a page counts as changed when its extracted text changes (`text_sha256`), not its bytes, because markup varies between fetches; and SUSE's products are summarised by their main kernel packages. Both are summaries of the brief's measures, not changes to them; the full per-package SUSE states are in tables.json.

## Limits

- **Reviews so far.** This interim record has had an evidence review (notes/R-0077-lh036-interim.md) and a fresh recheck of its resolution (notes/R-0078-lh036-recheck.md), and a reader-and-inference review (notes/R-0079-lh036-reader.md) whose two must-fix findings version 0.2 resolves; a fresh recheck of that resolution (notes/R-0081-lh036-recheck2.md) cleared it with small wording changes. All were run on Anthropic's model. The brief asks those reviews to look for wording that is easier on Anthropic, or on the exploit's significance, than the sources allow, in either direction.
- **Unknown, not no.** The kernel announcement was never read. Catalogue versions and tracker states between reads were not seen; a change that came and went between two reads would be missed. Debian's and SUSE's fix dates are not given by their pages.
- **What a fix state means.** A tracker's released or fixed state says a package was published. Whether anyone installed it or rebooted into it is not observable here. Ubuntu's "pending" is Ubuntu's own state word; what lies behind it, and who can obtain the oldest releases' packages, was not read.
- **Not opened:** the oss-security post of 3 November, OSV's related advisories, Red Hat's RHSA pages, Ubuntu's notices beyond their ids and dates, Ubuntu's blog post of the proposed pocket, and SUSE's advisory pages.
- **What the study cannot say:** whether anyone has exploited the bug, how many machines run an affected kernel, or why any score or state moved. No claim is made that harm is happening or that any system is compromised. Whether Anthropic's publication kept to its stated policy is the timeline study's question, not this one's.
- **Self-interest.** The exploit and the post are Anthropic's, and this record was written on an Anthropic model.
- **Not independent of LH048** for NVD, EPSS and KEV on this CVE.
