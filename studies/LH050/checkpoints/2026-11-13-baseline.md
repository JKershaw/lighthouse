## What was retained, case by case

**Changi patrol robots (civilian comparison).** SPF's release of 15 June 2023 (PA-S05) says patrol robots have been deployed at Terminal 4 since April 2023, "capable of autonomous patrol", with cameras on a mast, two-way communication with the Airport Police Operations Room, and warning measures (blinkers, siren, speakers). A second configuration, GIBSON, has been on trial at the Terminal 4 Departure Hall since July 2025 (SPF, PA-S28; HTX, PA-S29): autonomous navigation by LiDAR, depth cameras and sonar, plus a ride mode officers summon by app. HTX reports people tried to ride it while it moved on its own and that it added stop sensors and a cordon. Unknown: armament (none described; not established either way), who triggers warnings or sets missions, AI involvement beyond automated navigation, status after July 2025, costs, and any complaint or harm. All Changi sources are from one government family.

**Foundation/Phantom.** Unavailable: the FT Gaza report and the Wired article (robots.txt disallows our tokens) and the company's own site (HTTP 404). Four third-party reports from the follow-up query (PA-S24 to PA-S27, December 2025 to June 2026) relay the company and a CNBC interview: a payload of about 44 pounds "including lethal weaponry" as a design aim; two MK-1 robots sent to Ukraine for logistics demonstrations "earlier this year"; $24 million in US research contracts, with no award record read; production targets that differ between reports; and an MK-1 described as lacking waterproofing and battery life. Control statements conflict over time: in December 2025, "will not have full autonomy" in combat decisions (PA-S24); in May 2026, the CEO, as reported, said human confirmation for some weaponised uses but fully autonomous decisions in some time-critical scenarios (PA-S27). Not established: any Gaza order, deployment or discussion; any armed Phantom fielded or fired; target selection, force authorisation or intervention mechanism; civilian consequences.

## Official-policy snapshot, before the conference

- UN Geneva, 10 November (PA-S15): the conference runs 16 to 20 November in Geneva; lethal autonomous weapon systems are "a central issue", and the conference will decide future work under the Convention.
- UNODA's meeting page (PA-S16) lists the draft final declaration (PA-S19), which holds paragraph 17 and Decision 1 as bare "[LAWS]" placeholders, and a one-page proposal (PA-S20, proponent not named) that the expert group "negotiate a new Protocol" and report a draft Protocol to the 2028 meeting. A proposal and a placeholder are not agreed text.
- Working papers WP.1 (ISU) and WP.2 (United States): listed, text unavailable (documents.un.org disallowed by robots.txt).
- ICRC FAQ, 8 October (PA-S11): calls for a binding instrument banning unpredictable and anti-personnel autonomous weapons and restricting others; says AI used only in navigation does not make a weapon autonomous. The joint UN and ICRC appeal of 25 August (PA-S17) was behind a challenge page and known only through the FAQ.
- General Comment 37, the law-enforcement standard: unavailable through all three routes (OHCHR challenge; OHCHR docstore and documents.un.org disallowed). The ICRC's armed-conflict material is not used in its place.
- UK JSP 936 publication page (PA-S13): readable; the directive itself not read.
