# What can public records establish about armed robots and human control?

**In short.** We asked what public sources we could read say about three kinds of robot: a humanoid its maker wants to arm, an armed non-humanoid robot used for comparison, and the patrol robots police run at Singapore's Changi Airport. So far, in unreviewed working material, the sources report how far each case has got, mostly in the words of the company or government concerned, and say almost nothing about who controls force. That gap mostly reflects which cases could be read: the two that could have tested it, the humanoid's proposed Gaza use and the armed comparison, could not be read at all, and neither readable case shows a robot fielded with a weapon. The airport robots are described in detail by the police, with no weapon mentioned: officers can summon one of the two types and take over its movement, but nothing read says who would decide on force. Next, the reviews' corrections get a fresh recheck, while a weekly watch of eight fixed pages runs to 24 December.

## Answer

Unreviewed. From what Lighthouse could read on 13 November 2026, public sources report the stage each case has reached, mostly in the words of the company or government concerned and as of their publication dates, much more often than they describe human control: of the 45 matrix cells (codings of this record, not robots, weapons or events), 7 are supported by a source, 23 are not established by any readable source, 14 are unavailable because the source could not be read, and 1 holds two reported accounts that conflict. No cell for target selection or force authorisation in practice is supported by a source in any case (derived count of this record's matrix codings). That absence cannot be read as public records being silent on control: 14 of the cells are the two whole rows lost because their only sources could not be read (the Gaza application and the armed comparison, the two cases that could have tested it), and in the four readable rows no source shows a robot fielded with a weapon, so a supported entry on force in practice was hardly possible there. Over the readable rows, the stage cells are supported in 4 of 4 and the control cells in 1 of 13, and that one concerns taking over a robot's movement, not force (derived, studies/LH050/review/r0074_matrix_split.out).

For Foundation's Phantom, everything readable is third-party reporting that relays the company: a design meant to carry weapons, a two-robot logistics demonstration in Ukraine, US research contracts and production targets, and two reported statements of intent about autonomy in combat decisions that do not agree. The proposed Gaza application could not be read and is neither established nor ruled out. The armed non-humanoid comparison is unavailable. Changi's two robot configurations are described only by the Singapore government: one said in June 2023 to have been in operational use since April 2023, and one said in July 2025 to be on trial since that month, with nothing read on either's status since, with automated navigation, warning measures and a manual takeover for mobility, and no weapon described. None of this is a finding that a robot is or is not armed, nor that anyone was or was not harmed.

What the supported cells say, with sources (read times are UTC on 13 November 2026):

- **Phantom's design.** Assembly (PA-S26, published 17 December 2025, read 14:58:02Z) reports a maximum payload of 44 pounds "including lethal weaponry"; TechCentral (PA-S24, 22 December 2025, read 14:57:58Z) reports more than 20kg of "equipment or weapons" (claims C022, C023). No source read says a Phantom has been armed, tested with a weapon or fired.
- **Phantom's stated control intentions, conflicting.** TechCentral (22 December 2025) reports "Phantoms will not have full autonomy when it comes to making decisions in combat" (C024); The Next Web (PA-S27, 30 May 2026, read 14:58:04Z) reports the chief executive said some weaponised uses will keep human confirmation in the loop and that in certain time-critical scenarios the robots will need to make fully autonomous decisions (C025). Both are relayed intentions for a configuration not shown to be fielded. Classing them as conflicting is this record's coding (interpretation); five months apart, they may record a change of position rather than a contradiction, and the record does not resolve them.
- **Phantom's maturity.** The Next Web reports $24 million in US Army, Navy and Air Force research contracts for feasibility testing in inspection, logistics and weapons handling, with no award record read (C030); production targets differ between reports and are kept apart (C032); a target of front-line US deployment within 12 to 18 months, as The Next Web puts it (C033); eWeek gives the same period as an aim to begin testing with the US military, a weaker claim. eWeek (PA-S25, published 3 June 2026, modified 12 September 2026, read 14:58:00Z) and The Next Web report two MK-1 robots sent to Ukraine "earlier this year" for logistics demonstrations with Ukrainian officials and US government support (C027), relay the company's claim of "the first known use of humanoid robots in an active combat theater" (C028, a supplier claim), and report that the MK-1 lacks waterproofing and battery life, eWeek calling the robots "far from battlefield-ready" (C029). That last report is evidence against maturity, kept as carefully as the claims for it.
- **Changi's 2023 patrol robot.** The Singapore Police Force (PA-S05, 15 June 2023, read 14:55:04Z) states patrol robots have been deployed at Terminal 4 since April 2023, "capable of autonomous patrol", with a mast camera and two-way link to the Airport Police Operations Room and "interim intervention measures (blinkers, siren, and speakers)", and plans to deploy more across Singapore (C001 to C004, C007).
- **Changi's GIBSON.** SPF (PA-S28, 21 July 2025, read 14:59:09Z) says GIBSON has been on trial in the Terminal 4 Departure Hall since July 2025, navigating autonomously with 3D LiDAR, depth cameras and SONAR (C010, C011). HTX (PA-S29, 31 July 2025, read 14:58:56Z) says officers summon and ride it, must arm and activate the handlebar to take manual control, that the public was largely unresponsive to a recorded "Excuse me" message, and that people tried to mount it while it moved on its own, after which stop sensors and a cordon were added (C012 to C014). These are takeover and stop mechanisms for mobility and safety, not for force.

What no readable source settles: in every case, who selects targets, who authorises force in practice, and whether any function is said to use AI. The Changi sources describe automated navigation, and the ICRC (PA-S11, 8 October 2026, read 14:55:07Z) says a weapon that uses AI only in navigation is not necessarily an autonomous weapon (C057); automated navigation is recorded as such, not as AI involvement. For the four Foundation reports, the AI-function cells rest on this record's reading of the retained text on 16 November 2026, which found no function attributed to AI; no ledger claim codes it, and a reviewer should check it.

What could not be read: the FT report on the proposed Gaza application (PA-S01) and Wired's article (PA-S02) are disallowed by robots.txt for our tokens; Foundation's own site (PA-S03) returned HTTP 404; the Marine Corps record of the armed comparison candidate (PA-S04) returned HTTP 403, and the brief's single replacement query (PA-Q01) found no first-party test record in its first ten results (C020, C021, C040).

## Method

This record reads only what the baseline and Amendment 1 retained on 13 November 2026; it collects nothing. It reads the brief, its amendments, the ledgers (reads.csv 39 rows, claims.csv 60 claims, events.csv 10 events), the baseline checkpoint and the retained bodies.

1. **Integrity check (derived).** The SHA-256 of each of the 26 retained artefacts with a hash in reads.csv (20 baseline bodies, 3 search records, 3 Amendment 1 bodies) was recomputed on 16 November 2026; all 26 match, none differ.
2. **Passage check (observation).** Text was extracted locally from the seven case-source bodies (PA-S05, S24 to S29) and the passages behind the matrix's supported cells were found in the retained text: C022 to C025, C027 to C030, C033, the SPF "autonomous patrol" and "interim intervention measures" passages. Not every claim was re-read; the evidence review should.
3. **Matrix coding (interpretation, rule-bound).** Each cell is built from coded claims and their reads; [case_matrix.py](case_matrix.py) holds the codings and writes the CSV. Each cell carries a status from the brief's classes (supported-by-source, not-established, conflicting, unavailable, not-applicable) and a label column saying whether it is an observation of what a source says, an observation of what sources do not state, or a class chosen by this record. Unavailable is used only where the source for that configuration could not be read; not-established where readable sources do not settle the field. Not-applicable is used nowhere: target selection and force authorisation might not apply to the Changi robots, but that would require knowing they are unarmed, which no source establishes.
4. **Derived arithmetic.** The revision differences and the ministry's "more than fourfold" statement: `python3 -c "print(16664-16663, 19969-19935, round(31398/7511,2))"` printed `1 34 4.18`. The ratio only confirms the ministry's own wording matches its own figures; it is not a Lighthouse growth estimate.

## Limits

- **Unreviewed.** No second agent has checked this record. Its matrix codings, especially the one conflicting cell and the AI-function cells for Foundation, are this writer's.
- **Selected cases.** The three cases were chosen after prior reading, and the Changi comparison partly because an official page described it without a weapon. Nothing here estimates prevalence, effectiveness, civilian harm or cost.
- **Access shapes the matrix.** Fourteen of the 45 cells are unavailable because of robots.txt, HTTP 403 or 404, not because of anything about the robots. The baseline read through Anthropic's agent tokens; the commissioning survey, under another provider, read Autonomous Weapons Watch, which is now closed to this route.
- **One source family per case.** Foundation's readable evidence is four third-party reports that relay the company and one interview (TechCentral's article ends with a Business AM credit, so it may be a syndicated copy); repetition across them adds no corroboration. Changi's is one government family (SPF and HTX). No independent or civil-society source was read for either.
- **Search coverage.** The three follow-up queries ran through a connector whose ordering is retained but whose completeness cannot be checked; no search-coverage measure is made.
- **Unopened leads.** The retained eWeek page (PA-S25) carries sidebar links headed "San Francisco Startup Pitches Armed Humanoid Robots to Trump Administration" and "Taiwan is testing armed robot dogs for remote island patrols". They are page furniture, were not opened and are not coded; they are leads only, and the Foundation case's one follow-up query is already spent.
- **Clocks.** Event dates are as sources give them, often missing ("earlier this year"); a source's date is not proof of first public visibility. The August ministry notice was read as it stood on 13 November; whether it was edited after 24 August cannot be told from this copy.
- **Not filled from retained evidence.** Armament, AI function, target selection, force authorisation, intervention and civilian evidence for the armed comparison and the Gaza application; who controls force for any Phantom or Changi robot; the post-conference policy comparison; the weekly change log (the panel was locked on 16 November 2026 at 14:25:29 UTC, after this baseline; no weekly read has yet been made); economics beyond reported totals (no unit price is derived, and support and supervision costs are unknown); collection and review effort and model cost, which are unknown, not zero.
