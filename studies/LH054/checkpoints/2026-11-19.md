# LH054 reads: 19 November 2026 (baseline)

**In short.** The first read of how a second Linux kernel TCP flaw, NoisyTCP (CVE-2026-99288), found and reported by a named person rather than a model, is scored and fixed, read on the same day and with the same collector as our main kernel watch. Four days after the detailed report, CISA's catalogue does not list it, CISA has added its own container to the CVE record (scoring it 5.9, with exploitation "none"), NVD has not yet analysed it, and EPSS puts it at 0.25 per cent. Debian's security channels for bookworm and trixie carry fixed kernels; Ubuntu, Red Hat and SUSE list none yet, though SUSE marks its 15 SP7 kernels in progress. This is a baseline and unreviewed; the next read is on Monday 23 November.

## Baseline in brief

No KEV listing (catalogue 2026.11.18). A CISA ADP container, dated 17 November 18:08 UTC: CVSS 5.9, SSVC exploitation none, automatable yes, technical impact partial. The CNA added a 7.5 score on 19 November. NVD "Received", no score of its own. EPSS 0.00252 on 18 November (percentile 0.168), first scored 11 November, no step. Fixed releases: Debian bookworm and trixie security (DLA-4906-1, DSA-6628-1, undated), plus forky and sid; none on Ubuntu, Red Hat or SUSE, SUSE's 15 SP7 kernels in progress. The post is unchanged.

Comparison note for the record, not a finding: at LH036's baseline (5 days after its E2) there was no CISA ADP container on that CVE, and there is still none at LH036's reads to 16 November; here one came 2 days after D. Two cases, one each side; the difference is a description, and the brief seeks no cause.

Not checked: whether any fixed package was installed or any machine rebooted into it, which the study cannot observe; the DLA, DSA and the repository the Debian page links, which were not opened; git.kernel.org, which is disallowed by its robots.txt. Lighthouse makes no claim that this flaw is exploited; CISA's SSVC value "none" is CISA's assessment as of 17 November 18:08 UTC.
