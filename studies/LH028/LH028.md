# The first public attack trace predates the PHP disclosure, but says little about AI use by attackers

**Study:** LH028, version 0.1, 22 October 2026. **Writer:** Codex, GPT-6 Sol in ChatGPT Work Mode. **Protocol:** local commit `a688fd2`, mirrored to draft PR #26 as `3e3d713` before direct reads. **Observation:** 22 October 13:42:19–13:42:36 UTC; exact times in [read log](data/snapshot-2026-10-22/read_log.json). **Instruments:** Python 3 urllib direct GET and an explicitly separate web reader for an inaccessible AWS page. **Overlap:** Cloudflare and the AWS organisation appeared in LH024; sources deliberately chosen after their visible outcomes. **Spend:** three direct GETs, 756,105 retained bytes, model spend unknown.

## Answer

The web reader showed Amazon Threat Intelligence's first-party account of an attempted exploit in its MadPot honeypot dated **17 October**, with further attempts from 02:00 to 08:00 UTC on 21 October and after the 14:00 UTC disclosure. Amazon says it does not know how the earlier actor obtained the information or whether attempts would have succeeded on real systems [S1]. The direct collector received a `Site Unavailable` placeholder for this article, so the Amazon text is **not** independently replayable from retained raw bytes here. Cloudflare's retained page says it observed attack traffic against its customers and changing payloads, but gives no count or rate for this episode [S2]. Neither observation shows a named system compromised or attributes an individual request to AI.

OpenAI's retained page says Calif identified one flaw using GPT-5.6 Sol, Wiz independently reported both, and PHP maintainers led the fixes, with OpenAI and partners assisting coordination [S3]. That is a disclosed use of AI in *defensive discovery* and response, not evidence of attackers' tools. Amazon argues that AI lowers the cost of developing exploits, an interpretation about the general mechanism rather than telemetry identifying AI in the 17 October request [S1].

The AWS article's separate figure of 200 billion events analysed per minute describes its general security operation, **not** PHP attack volume. News claims of millions of attempts were seen as leads but are not supported by an event-specific numerator and window in this fixed panel.

## Method

The [brief](brief.md) fixed three known first-party URLs, disclosure of their already-visible outcomes, one GET each and response limits before the direct requests. All three responded HTTP 200; the Amazon body was a 195-byte access placeholder and the other two were full pages. [Offline replay](scripts/replay.py) checks each retained byte length and hash. The web-rendered AWS first-party page was read after the direct attempt, outside the brief's raw-response instrument, and is separately marked; its exact text cannot be regenerated from this snapshot. [Sources](sources.md) record URLs, dates and instrument boundaries. No exploit was run and no site was probed.

## Limits

- Amazon's earlier date and actor interpretation are source-reported, retrospectively read on 22 October through a different reader. A honeypot is a decoy, not proof of an affected customer or successful execution. The apparent pre-disclosure activity complicates a simple patch-to-first-attempt clock but does not show how the actor learned of the flaw.
- Cloudflare's default protection is its claim for customers with WAF deployed. This panel did not access customer configurations, verify blocking of an attack or count exposed hosts.
- The OpenAI account is a participant's account of its and partners' roles. It cannot alone establish all discovery precedence or attribute independent offensive activity.
- The selected sources are visible because they report salient outcomes. No rate over all defenders, attacks or AI involvement follows. A later source revision needs a new dated snapshot.
