## Answer

A week after Node.js's security releases of 21 October 2026, the fixed release or a newer one in its line made up 53.9 per cent of line 22's downloads from nodejs.org on day 7, 72.2 per cent of line 24's and 64.7 per cent of line 26's (derived from the project's counts). All three lines first passed half on day 2. Lines the release does not fix took 14.7 per cent of version-attributed downloads that day.

Beside the comparators named in the brief, as a description: on day 1 the three Node.js lines were below all four Python security fixes' download shares (the same unit from a different index) and above all four PHP branches' Censys patched shares (a different unit); on day 7 line 22 was below the Python range and lines 24 and 26 above it. Lines 22 and 24 were higher in October's first week than after the July security release, on either way of aligning the two. A download is a request, not a machine, and nodejs.org is one channel of several.

## Method

The brief fixed, before any count was read, the source, day 0 (21 October, the UTC day the binaries were posted, which is also LH033's day 0), the fixed lines, the four measures and the comparators. Each read keeps the raw bodies gzipped with SHA-256 and UTC time, saves only HTTP 200 responses and never overwrites a read; a day's file is posted about 11:00 UTC the next day, so each morning's read fetched the day before yesterday. One reading of the brief, made after the version table's keys were listed and before any count was read: the `unknown` key is counted with requests that have no version (scripts/tabulate.py).

## Limits

- **A download is a request**, not a machine, an install or a user; a machine that builds often is counted often. Header tarballs fetched by node-gyp are among the requests and cannot be separated by version (nodejs.org/metrics, written for the historic logs and assumed to hold for the current summaries).
- **nodejs.org is one channel of several.** Distribution packages, Homebrew, built container images, corporate mirrors and caches, and runners that keep their own copy are not counted. A machine installed long ago that never fetches again is invisible here.
- **The file's day boundary** is taken as the UTC day of the downloads; the metrics page says only that a file is named for its last entry.
- **Different units and indexes.** The Python shares come from a different index and population; the PHP shares are services as last scanned. The comparison describes shapes; it does not rank the ecosystems or attribute a difference to severity, publicity, exploitation reports, AI or any cause.
- **Interim.** Measure 3 and the day 14 and day 30 points wait for the read holding day 30, on 22 November, when days 0 to 7 are also re-read once.
