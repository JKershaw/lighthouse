**In short.** On day 21 after Node.js's fix (11 November), 61.4 per cent of line 22's downloads from nodejs.org were at or above the fixed release, 70.6 per cent of line 24's and 94.5 per cent of line 26's, as the study counts them; 12.0 per cent came from lines the release does not fix. So Node.js very likely published new releases in all three fixed lines on or just before 11 November. The study hasn't read why, or what they fix: the list it would date them from can't be read under robots.txt, and no other source was opened. v27.0.0 is unlisted too (101,691 on day 21), outside the measured lines, as on 9 November.

By the amendment of 9 November, measure 1 counts only listed versions, so these downloads are left out of both the numerator and the denominator. They aren't counted as old. A side figure, worked out after the counts were seen and not a measure the brief fixes: if each one were counted at or above its line's fixed release, as its version number places it, day 21 would read 64.6 per cent for line 22 (against 61.4), 72.1 for line 24 (against 70.6) and 95.4 for line 26 (against 94.5). As those releases take over their lines, the listed-only denominator will shrink, and by days 28 to 30, which feed amendment 1's S30, it may leave out a large part of each line. **Flagged for the driving session:** whether to amend how measure 1 treats in-line versions above the fixed release that the kept list does not hold, decided before day 22 is tabulated.
