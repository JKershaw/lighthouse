## Answer, at the baseline

About seven hours after the time PHP stamps on its notice (10:12 UTC; its meaning is not documented, see LH029), none of the seven structured vulnerability records yet held a record for CVE-2026-115560: the CVE Program's API answered that the record does not exist, NVD returned no result, and OSV, Debian, Ubuntu, Red Hat and SUSE answered HTTP 404. Of the eight institutions, two named the issue at their fixed URLs. **CERT-EU's** advisory 2026-018, at version 1.2 ("Updates with information about a third vulnerability", 23 October; its release-date field reads 23-10-2026 14:36:26, timezone not stated), carries the instruction ("[New] CVE-2026-115560: Set session.upload_progress.enabled=0 and restart every worker until the next regular bugfix release, to fully protect from CVE-2026-115560"), qualifies the issue only in general terms ("it affects only web applications configured in an unusual way"), not with PHP's condition, makes no explicit claim about which releases are affected, and cites a news article, not PHP's notice, as its source for the new flaw. **Cloudflare's** page names the issue, links to PHP's notice and says it affects fully patched releases, including 8.5.12, without the instruction or PHP's condition, as LH028 had already seen. Four institutions' fixed URLs did not name it (NCSC, Fastly, AWS, Google Cloud) and two refused the read (CISA, Akamai). This is one left-censored read: it says what the panel's fixed URLs showed at 17:15 UTC, not when anything arrived.

## Method

The brief fixed the frame, classes and schedule, and the classifier was committed and tested on known answers before the read ([known answers](data/known_answers.txt)): the twelve pre-instruction pages of 22 October classed nothing (five were unavailable then); PHP's, Cloudflare's and SOCRadar's 23 October pages classed as already known on I, II, III and V, and IV misfired by proximity on PHP's and SOCRadar's, which is why the brief puts IV to a hand reading. The baseline ran the collector once, then the classifier, then tabulate.py. The hand decisions on IV are kept with their quotations.

## Limits

- One read, hours after PHP's notice: nothing here dates an arrival. CERT-EU's release-date field and Cloudflare's metadata are each source's own clocks.
- Fixed URLs see only what those pages say. An institution may have said it elsewhere, in a new advisory, a feed or a social post; an absence here is an absence at that URL.
- The classifier reads extracted text and links. It can miss an instruction worded otherwise ("turn off upload progress tracking") and cannot judge emphasis; II and III are pattern matches near an anchor, checked by hand at this read for the two entries that named the issue.
- Two institutions refused the read and access changed between days, so what is readable varies by day for reasons this study does not see.
