# How does PHP's 23 October mitigation instruction travel through a fixed panel of public notices and vulnerability records?

**In short.** On 23 October PHP told people to switch off one feature (session upload progress) until its next regular release, to protect against a newer flaw, CVE-2026-115560, and we followed that advice through 15 fixed public sources, seven vulnerability databases and eight security bodies' notices, to 23 November. In this final record (version 1.2, reviewed and rechecked), the instruction appeared on 2 of the 15 by the study's pre-set classifier and on 1, CERT-EU's notice, on a hand reading made after the outcome was seen; six of the seven databases answered that they had no entry until after PHP's releases of 19 November, the only earlier entry, SUSE's, didn't carry the advice, and Red Hat's later one says disabling the feature in its own older packages "provides a mitigation" and points to a security bulletin we didn't read. Five entries appeared between our reads of 19 and 23 November, the four that give a time, PHP's own CVE record (14:42 UTC) among them, dating themselves to 19 November; the study can't say why they waited. By 23 November three of the six entries opened (of seven databases watched) list fixed packages and two more end the affected range at those releases, while CERT-EU and Cloudflare keep their pre-release wording and PHP's news page calls the releases security releases without naming the flaw.

## Limits

- **Fixed URLs see only those pages.** An institution or tracker may have said something elsewhere, in a new advisory, feed or post; an absence here is an absence at that URL. After the stop, Lighthouse's LH037 read of 26 November (checkpoint studies/LH037/checkpoints/2026-11-26T1414Z.md, as summarised in notes/php-watch-log.md) reported that Google's GCP-2026-090 carried an update dated 23 November tying its patched runtimes to CVE-2026-115560; LH030 did not read that page after 23 November 14:21 UTC, did not check the claim, and counts nothing from it.
- **Wide intervals after 19 November.** Five records opened, and three retirements (five on the post hoc reading) fall, in one interval of about four days (19 November 14:18 to 23 November 14:21 UTC). The sources' own times suggest most published on 19 November, but those are their clocks, and Debian gives none. They are not first visibility: Ubuntu's `published` 2026-11-19T14:00:00 (no zone) is earlier than the 404 Ubuntu gave at 14:17:57 UTC that day. That the given times cluster around the CVE record's publication is described, not explained (Answer).
- **Retirement is the tracker's claim.** A record listing a fixed package says the tracker considers it fixed; whether the 19 November releases fix the flaw in fact was not tested and will not be.
- **The classifier reads patterns.** It can miss an instruction worded otherwise and cannot judge whether a match is advice; the Red Hat case shows the second.
- **Two institutions refused every read**, so nothing is known of CISA's alert page or Akamai's blog in this window. Ubuntu was unknown at seven reads.
- **Not checked:** whether any identifier was reserved while the APIs answered "no record" (beyond the reserved date the later CVE record gives); what the ChangeLog, the GHSA, any linked advisory or any mailing-list announcement says; whether byte changes on unchanged pages were only page furniture beyond the extracted-text diffs; whether robots.txt allowed this study's requests before 9 November (left to LH047); and other Lighthouse studies' retained PHP material, which was not searched for the gate question.
- **Reviewed once.** Everything first written in versions 1.0 to 1.2, and every read from 2 November onwards, has had one evidence review, one reader review and one recheck (header); the checkpoints from 2 November had no review of their own, and an agent's agreement is a review contribution, not independent confirmation.
- **Redacted bodies.** 96 bodies from the reads of 23 October to 5 November no longer hash to their logged SHA-256, because addresses were withheld for version 1.1 (Method); a replay that refetches compares against the logged hash of the bytes received.
