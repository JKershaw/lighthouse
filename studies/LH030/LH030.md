## Answer, at the interim (31 October 2026)

In 22 reads over the week from 23 October 17:15 UTC to 30 October 13:04 UTC, nothing in the frame changed class. None of the seven structured vulnerability records held a record for CVE-2026-115560 at any read in which it answered: at the last read, 30 October 13:04 UTC, about seven days after the time PHP stamps on its notice, the CVE Program's API still answered `CVE_RECORD_DNE` ("The cve record for the cve id does not exist"), NVD returned no result, and OSV, Debian, Ubuntu, Red Hat and SUSE answered HTTP 404. Of the eight institutions, the same two named the issue as at the baseline, with no change in their visible text: CERT-EU carries the instruction with a general qualification, not PHP's condition, and no link to PHP's notice; Cloudflare links to PHP's notice and says the issue affects fully patched releases, including 8.5.12, without the instruction. NCSC, Fastly, AWS and Google Cloud never named it at their fixed URLs, and CISA's and Akamai's refused every read (HTTP 403). So, within this frame and at its fixed URLs, PHP's instruction was on one institution's notice by the baseline read, about seven hours after PHP's stamped time, and on no further one in the week that followed, and no tracker opened an entry. No entry can yet be retired, because PHP's next regular release has not been seen.

This is an observation of a fixed panel, not a measure of reach: a tracker or institution may have said something elsewhere, and an absence at a fixed URL is not silence. That no record exists for a week is the record APIs' answer, read as given; this study did not read whether the identifier is reserved, or why no record has been published.

The baseline answer follows, unchanged.

## Method

The brief fixed the frame, classes and schedule, and the classifier was committed and tested on known answers before the read ([known answers](data/known_answers.txt)): the twelve pre-instruction pages of 22 October classed nothing (five were unavailable then); PHP's, Cloudflare's and SOCRadar's 23 October pages classed as already known on I, II, III and V, and IV misfired by proximity on PHP's and SOCRadar's, which is why the brief puts IV to a hand reading. The baseline ran the collector once, then the classifier, then tabulate.py. The hand decisions on IV are kept with their quotations.

## Limits

- One read, hours after PHP's notice: nothing here dates an arrival. CERT-EU's release-date field and Cloudflare's metadata are each source's own clocks.
- Fixed URLs see only what those pages say. An institution may have said it elsewhere, in a new advisory, a feed or a social post; an absence here is an absence at that URL.
- The classifier reads extracted text and links. It can miss an instruction worded otherwise ("turn off upload progress tracking") and cannot judge emphasis; II and III are pattern matches near an anchor, checked by hand at this read for the two entries that named the issue.
- Two institutions refused the read and access changed between days, so what is readable varies by day for reasons this study does not see.

- The interim's intervals are wide only on paper: with no class changing, every arrival in this frame is still after the last read, 30 October 13:04 UTC. The read scheduled for about 18:30 UTC on 30 October under amendment 2 was not made (no read directory or commit exists for it), so the interval for anything first seen on 2 November starts at 13:04 UTC on 30 October.
