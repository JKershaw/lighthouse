# LH044 baseline: 8 November 2026

**In short.** We asked whether the shared services the open internet runs on still answer a reader who doesn't log in and says who it is. At the first read, 13 of the 67 named services whose answer we could read put a wall in front of a single labelled request (a challenge page, a rate limit, a refusal), and 1 of the 26 randomly drawn repositories and mirrors did; that's unreviewed, and it's a snapshot, not a trend. The bigger surprise is what our own politeness hides: robots.txt told us not to look at 32 of the 161 services, kernel.org's git server and PyPI's JSON API among them, so we can't see any wall there. Weekly reads continue to 7 February 2027.

## What operators say (measure 7, the seed list)

Coded in [statements/coded.csv](../statements/coded.csv), each the operator's or reporter's account, quoted:

- **kernel.org** (29 August 2026): about 6 million daily requests for random commits at git.kernel.org; Anubis stops 66 per cent and 33 per cent solve it; legitimate requests "only about 2%" with "a bunch of generous assumptions"; 14 to 16 of 90 cores rendering commits for scrapers; "Expect to lose some functionality, at least when accessing our resources anonymously." It attributes the load to crawlers gathering training data ("feeding a learning model"), says "It's impossible to tell with certainty which of these are bots and which are real humans", and says what usually takes it down is badly designed CI, not scrapers. The gate's note that the post "speaks of scrapers, not of AI" should be revised: it speaks of both.
- **Wikimedia** (API rate-limit page): limits deployed in 2026 to cut "unauthenticated, automated API requests (about 33% at the end of 2025)" and load from "high-volume commercial consumers"; 10 requests a minute for clients known only by address, 200 with a compliant User-Agent. It doesn't mention AI. Its 2025 post (1 April 2025) attributes a 50 per cent rise in multimedia bandwidth since January 2024 largely to programs scraping Commons "to feed images to AI models", and says at least 65 per cent of its most expensive traffic comes from bots.
- **Read the Docs** (25 July 2024): abusive crawling "mainly from AI products and services"; one crawler took 73 TB in May 2024, costing over $5,000.
- **GitHub** (8 May 2025): lower limits on unauthenticated clones, REST API use and raw file downloads after "an increase in scraping activity targeting our API"; no AI named.
- **COAR** (2026): repositories blocking benign services, its own directory's robot among them; proof-of-work rated "Effective".
- **LWN** (10 July 2025, a reporter's account): Anubis in use at the LKML archive, SourceHut, FFmpeg and GNOME.
- Unread: CBC's report (its robots.txt timed out, so no request was sent) and the SourceHut founder's post (the URL read is a redirect stub).

Model disclosure: Anthropic runs crawlers named in measure 4 (ClaudeBot, Claude-User, Claude-SearchBot, anthropic-ai) and this study's requests leave from an address block registered to Anthropic. The operators above who attribute load to AI do so in their own words; nothing here attributes any traffic.

## Changes to the protocol after this read (labelled post hoc)

- **Amendment 2 (classifier 0.2).** Two faults, both found in this read: (1) github.com and api.github.com answered with a 403 written by this container's own GitHub policy ("GitHub access to this repository is not enabled for this session"), not by GitHub; version 0.1 called them `login` and `api_closed`. They are now `unread_proxy`. The message invited the session to request repository access; that was not done, since no task asked for it and the study does not route around a refusal. (2) Cloudflare injects its `/cdn-cgi/challenge-platform` bot-detection script into ordinary pages; version 0.1 counted that script as a challenge and so called readthedocs.org's robots.txt redirect a wall. It is now `robots_redirect`. `--classify` now also re-reads kept robots.txt responses. No other class changed between 0.1 and 0.2 (the 0.1 table is reproducible from the kept files at commit 1789e54's scripts).
- **Not changed, for the driving session to decide:** robots.txt redirects stopped 22 probes (8 named, 14 drawn), and with unreachable robots.txt files only 26 of the 60 drawn services could be read at all. RFC 9309 asks crawlers to follow robots.txt redirects; doing so would add a third request to those hosts, against the two-request cap. A dated amendment could allow one followed robots.txt redirect per host per read; it would be a change made after this read, labelled so.

## What this baseline does not say

It is one moment. It doesn't say any wall is new, that any operator is harmed, or that any traffic is AI. The named list is a judgement partly chosen for reports; the drawn strata are mostly unread here. A rate limit seen through a shared address is about that address's traffic.
