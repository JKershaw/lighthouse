# LH044: Which shared services of public infrastructure close their doors to an anonymous, identified reader, when, and what do their operators say about why? A weekly panel, 8 November 2026 to 9 February 2027

**In short** (added 8 November 2026; not part of the protocol). Operators of shared public services, kernel.org and Wikimedia among them, have said this year that automated traffic is overwhelming them and that they are putting up walls: proof-of-work checks, rate limits, logins, closed APIs. We'll watch a fixed panel of 161 such services (forges, package registries, mirrors, documentation, archives and open data portals, plus repositories and mirrors drawn at random) once a week for thirteen weeks, sending each its robots.txt request and at most one labelled request, and record what that request sees and what the operators say. Nothing has been measured yet; the baseline read follows this brief. Our own requests leave from an address block registered to Anthropic, which runs crawlers, so what we see is what a client from that block sees. From the first weekly read we also obey what each site's robots.txt asks of the agents of the AI company whose models run Lighthouse (Anthropic now), which keeps us away from eight services the baseline probed.

**Protocol 0.1, fixed 8 November 2026, before any panel service's probe URL or robots.txt was requested by this study, and before the panel was drawn.** Writer: a research subagent in Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5) for the driving session, from proposals.md P-0013, which the keeper agreed on 7 November 2026 with the gate's conditions (notes/horizon/2026-11-07T0853Z.md, Gate, P-0013); first study of [What AI activity costs the shared internet](../../investigations/ai-cost-and-spread.md), question 1. Before this brief, the writer read the scout file (notes/horizon/2026-11-07T0853Z/scout-7-wildcard.md), the gate, the investigation page, and the sampling frames and instrument tests kept under [data/prebrief-2026-11-08T1915Z/](data/prebrief-2026-11-08T1915Z/) with their [script](scripts/prebrief.py); no operator statement page was opened. The [classifier](scripts/classify.py), [collector](scripts/collect.py), [shared HTTP code](scripts/lh044lib.py), [draw](scripts/draw_panel.py) and [named list](panel/named.csv) are committed with this brief. One slip to disclose: while checking files, the writer ran draw_panel.py once before this commit (its `--help` is not implemented, so it drew); the panel/drawn.csv it wrote was deleted unopened, and the draw is deterministic, so the committed draw will be the same file. The keeper's caution of 8 November 2026 binds every step: a wall is the observation, and nothing here solves, evades or retries around one.

## Question

From one or two labelled requests a week, over thirteen weeks: which services in a fixed panel of shared public infrastructure answer an anonymous, self-identified reader with a wall rather than their content; which of them change, in either direction, and between which reads; what does the Wayback Machine's record say about when each wall went up, where it can be read; what does each robots.txt declare to named AI crawlers; and what do the operators themselves publish about what they withdrew and why? The unit is the service at a read: one host, one fixed probe URL, one UTC read.

What the question is not. It does not measure load, does not attribute any traffic to AI, and does not say that any operator is harmed. An operator's statement that AI scrapers caused its load is recorded as the operator's, with what it rests on, never as Lighthouse's finding. A wall is a fact about what one request saw; it says nothing by itself about why it is there.

## Lighthouse's own requests

This study adds Lighthouse's automated requests to services whose operators report being overwhelmed. It keeps them few, labelled, polite and finite (the gate's conditions):

- **Identified.** Every request names Lighthouse and this study's page: `Lighthouse-research/1.0 (+https://lighthouse.harbour.cat/studies/LH044/)` for robots.txt, APIs and files, and `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; Lighthouse-research/1.0; +https://lighthouse.harbour.cat/studies/LH044/)` for page probes. The second is the form self-identifying crawlers use on web pages; it claims to be no browser, and its `Mozilla` prefix means a wall set for browsers is shown to it rather than skipped, which is what a page probe exists to see. No email address, cookie, credential, key or token is sent; no JavaScript is run; no form is submitted; no account is made.
- **Few.** In a read, a panel host gets at most two requests: its robots.txt, then at most one probe. No two panel services share a host. No retries. A redirect is recorded and not followed. A probe that robots.txt disallows is not sent. Requests are sequential, paced 1.5 seconds apart.
- **robots.txt obeyed, RFC 9309.** The product token is `Lighthouse-research`; its group applies if present, else `*`; longest match wins, Allow wins a tie, `*` and `$` are honoured ([classify.py](scripts/classify.py), `robots_allowed`). A robots.txt that answers 5xx, fails to connect or redirects means no probe that read (`robots_unreachable`, `robots_redirect`); 4xx other than 429 means no rules (RFC 9309, "unavailable"). A robots.txt answered with a challenge or a 429 is itself the observation (`wall_at_robots`) and no probe follows.
- **Left alone when asked.** A probe answered with a Retry-After that runs past the next read's start skips that service at that read (`skipped_retry_after`).
- **Finite.** Fourteen reads (the baseline and thirteen weekly ones). The collector refuses to run after 2027-02-09T23:59:59Z (`END` in [collect.py](scripts/collect.py)).
- **Labelled in the record.** Every request is a row in a read's readlog.csv (URL, UTC time, status, bytes kept, sha256, User-Agent form, route), and each read's summary gives the request count, bytes retained and the most requests sent to any one host, beside the readings (charter: account for our own presence, measure our overhead).
- **Where they come from.** HTTPS requests leave this container through its egress proxy. Before this brief, rdap.arin.net (read 19:06:22 UTC) gave the address archive.org echoed back, 160.79.106.30, as part of NET-160-79-104-0-1 (160.79.104.0 to 160.79.111.255), registered to Anthropic, PBC. Hosts on the container's no-proxy list (pypi.org, registry.npmjs.org, proxy.golang.org among the panel) and plain-HTTP probes leave by a direct route whose address was not read. Each read records any address a response echoes (`egress_ip_echoed`).

## The panel

**Population.** Shared services of public infrastructure: services run for the public by an organisation, used anonymously by many people and programs, in six named classes: code forges and development infrastructure; package registries; mirrors and download services; documentation and wikis; archives, libraries and scholarly services; open data portals. Two further strata are drawn at random from public frames: open repositories and journals that speak OAI-PMH, and Debian mirrors.

**The named stratum** ([panel/named.csv](panel/named.csv), 101 services, fixed in this commit before any status is read). Rule: for each class, the services named in the scout file and gate as candidates or as having reported walls, and the largest shared public services of that class the writer could name, as the writer listed them on 8 November 2026 before any status read, one probe per host. It is a judgement list, not a sample, and it is partly chosen for its reports: column `prior` marks `reported` (an operator statement quoted in the scout file: git.kernel.org, Wikimedia's API, Project Gutenberg), `title` (named in a report title only: Weird Gloop's wiki), `candidate` (proposed as a panel member by scout 7), else empty. Classes: forge 19, registry 21, mirror 11, docs 15, archive 22, opendata 13.

**The drawn strata** ([scripts/draw_panel.py](scripts/draw_panel.py); panel/drawn.csv, written after this commit and before any status is read). Frames, both retained before this brief and read only from the retained files: the Open Archives Initiative's static dump of registered OAI-PMH base URLs at the registry's discontinuation (ListFriends_HISTORICAL_2025-10-08.xml, 7,103 entries, read 19:09:09 UTC), and Debian's mirror list page (351 distinct `/debian/` URLs, read 19:04:53 UTC). One URL per host (the lexicographically first), hosts in the named list and the frames' own hosts dropped, hosts sorted, then `random.Random(seed).sample` with seed the SHA-256 of `LH044 panel, fixed 8 November 2026`: 40 OAI-PMH hosts and 20 Debian mirror hosts. They are the comparison for the named stratum: chosen by no one for their outcome. The OAI frame is about a year old and its entries were never checked by anyone for being alive; a dead endpoint is `unreachable` or `gone`, not a wall.

**Probes.** Each service has one probe URL and a `surface`: `page` (a human-facing page; compatible User-Agent; Accept HTML), `api` (an anonymous API; plain User-Agent; Accept JSON or XML) or `file` (a static file on a mirror; plain User-Agent). Each has an optional content `marker`, a string the real content contains (for OAI-PMH, `<Identify>`; for Debian mirrors' README, `Debian`). Markers were written from the writer's knowledge, not from any response, so some may be wrong; a missing marker is a flag, not a wall.

**Fixed.** No service is added or replaced after the baseline, except by a dated amendment, labelled post hoc, for a probe that answers `gone` at the baseline (a probe the writer got wrong), with the replacement's first read as its baseline.

## Measures

1. **What a single request saw** ([classify.py](scripts/classify.py), version 0.1), from the status, headers and first 64 KiB of the probe's response, in this order: a network failure is `unreachable` (or `unread_proxy` when the container's own proxy refused, which is never a wall); a challenge signature (Cloudflare challenge or Turnstile, Anubis, go-away, AWS WAF, DataDome, HUMAN, Imperva, Sucuri, Vercel, Kasada) is `challenge` unless the response is a 2xx carrying the marker; 429, or 503 with Retry-After, or 403/503 with rate-limit wording, is `rate_limited`; 401 is `api_closed` on an API, `login` elsewhere; a redirect to a login path is `login`, to a challenge path `challenge`, otherwise `redirect_other`; 403 or 451 with a vendor's block page is `refused` (with the vendor), with authentication wording `api_closed` or `login`, otherwise `refused`; 404/410 `gone`; other 4xx `client_error_other`; 5xx `server_error`; a 2xx with the marker `open`, with a captcha widget and no marker `challenge`, with a password field on a page and no marker `login`, with no marker set `open`, with JavaScript-required wording and no marker `js_notice`, otherwise `open_unconfirmed`. **A wall** is `challenge`, `login`, `rate_limited`, `refused`, `api_closed` or `wall_at_robots`. `robots_disallowed` is not a wall: it is a declaration, counted under measure 4. `js_notice` and `open_unconfirmed` are not walls; each checkpoint lists them with what the retained body shows, and any reading of one as a wall is labelled post hoc.
2. **The tally:** services by class at each read, by stratum and by class of service, with the share behind a wall among those with a probe answered.
3. **Changes:** a **closing** is a service `open` at one read and walled at the next; it **holds** if it is still walled at the read after (a closing at the final read is unconfirmed). An **opening** is the reverse. Each closing and opening is listed with the two reads that bracket it and the classes either side.
4. **Declared walls:** from each robots.txt read, whether it allows this study's probe, whether `*` may fetch `/` and the probe, and whether each of 17 named AI crawler tokens (GPTBot, ChatGPT-User, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, Claude-User, Claude-SearchBot, anthropic-ai, CCBot, Google-Extended, PerplexityBot, Perplexity-User, Bytespider, Amazonbot, Applebot-Extended, meta-externalagent, cohere-ai, Diffbot) may fetch `/`. Counted at each read; changes listed.
5. **When the wall went up** (`collect.py --wayback`): for each service walled at the baseline or at its first walled read, the Wayback Machine's CDX list of captures of the probe URL since 1 January 2024 (deduplicated by digest), then archived captures read raw (`id_`) and classified with the same classifier, first the latest and the earliest and then by bisection, at most eight per service, giving the last capture not walled and the first walled. The bisection assumes a wall, once up, stayed up; a capture shows what the Wayback's crawler saw, with its own address and User-Agent, not what this study's request would have seen. A fixed check set (every tenth `open` service by id) gets a CDX listing only, as a check of the archive's coverage. At most 60 requests to web.archive.org per run, 4 seconds apart, after its robots.txt; the run stops at the first 429 or two network failures in a row, and whatever is unread is **undated**, never "no wall before". From this container web.archive.org reset every connection on 8 November; if that holds, measure 5 is unread here, and the script may be run under a later task from any machine that reaches it, with the same code and caps, its outputs kept the same way.
6. **Context: HTTP Archive** (`collect.py --httparchive`): one request to HTTP Archive's tech report API (cdn.httparchive.org/v1/adoption, the address its own report page uses, found on 8 November) for the monthly counts of origins on which its crawler detects each of: Cloudflare Bot Management, Cloudflare Turnstile, Akamai Bot Manager, DataDome, PerimeterX, Kasada, AntiBot.Cloud, AWS WAF Captcha, hCaptcha, reCAPTCHA and Friendly Captcha, from January 2024, all geographies and ranks. Read at the baseline, week 4, week 8 and the end. It counts a technology's presence on crawled home pages, not walls and not shared infrastructure; its rules detect no Anubis (scout 7, and its technology list read 8 November). Context only; nothing in it is compared with the panel.
7. **What operators say.** (a) At the baseline: the statement pages in [statements/seed.csv](statements/seed.csv), fetched once each after their robots.txt (`collect.py --statements`). (b) At each weekly read: for each closing or opening, and at weeks 4, 8 and 13 for each service walled at that read, a bounded search for the operator's own statement (at most two web searches per service, for the operator's name with "bots" and with "anonymous access" or the wall's kind, and at most two result pages opened per service), the queries, results and pages kept. Each statement is coded in statements/coded.csv: operator, URL, date published, read time, what was withdrawn or limited, the mechanism named, the reason stated and the words the operator uses for the traffic (for example "AI scrapers", "crawlers", "bots", "abuse"), quoted. The attribution is the operator's. A press report is coded as the reporter's account of the operator. Nobody is contacted.
8. **Our own load:** requests, bytes and the most requests to one host, per read (measure 1's summary).

## The comparison that matters

The named stratum against the drawn strata. The named services are the ones people talk about and were partly chosen because their operators reported walls; the drawn repositories and mirrors were chosen by a seed. If walls and closings are common in the named stratum and rare in the drawn ones, that is what selection on reports alone would also produce, and the record says so; if the drawn strata show them too, the closing reaches past the services that make the news, within the limits of two frames. The unit is the service; per-stratum counts are reported separately and never pooled into a rate for "the web".

**Pre-set readings, over the thirteen weekly reads.** For each stratum and for the panel: "the panel closed" if closings that hold number at least three and exceed openings that hold by at least three; "the panel opened" in the mirror case; otherwise "held". With about 160 services and thirteen reads, this can register only a few changes; "held" is not "nothing is happening", and a rate-limited service can flap from read to read with the load on a shared address.

## Missing evidence

- A read that does not happen in its window is missing, not filled.
- `unreachable`, `unread_proxy`, `robots_unreachable` and `robots_redirect` are unknown for that read; unknown is never open and never walled. A closing or opening needs both bracketing reads known.
- A Wayback run that is cut short leaves the rest undated. A statement not found by the bounded search is "none found", not "none made".
- An operator's silence says nothing about its reasons.

## Limits stated now

- One request, one address, one client. A wall may be set for data-centre addresses, for Anthropic's address block in particular, for unknown clients, for this User-Agent, or for everyone; one request cannot tell them apart, and the direct and proxied routes may leave from different addresses. A page that walls browsers but passes tools, or the reverse, shows the wall only on the matching surface.
- The named stratum is the writer's list and is partly selected on reports; the drawn strata come from two frames, one a year old. Neither is a census of anything.
- The probe is one URL per service. A wall on another path, at another time of day, or after more requests than one is not seen.
- Thirteen weeks is short against walls that went up over 2025 and 2026; the Wayback is the only reach back, and it may be unreadable from here.
- Markers and signatures can miss a new kind of wall (`open_unconfirmed`, `js_notice`) or a changed page.

Lighthouse runs on Anthropic's models; Anthropic runs web crawlers (ClaudeBot among the tokens in measure 4) and its address block is where this study's requests come from. ## Resource ceiling

Per read: at most 322 panel requests (two per service), retained bodies at most 64 KiB each. Statement fetches: at most two requests per statement host per run. Wayback: at most 61 requests per run. HTTP Archive: two requests per context read. Fourteen reads in all, so at most about 4,500 panel requests over the study. No account, key, credential, BigQuery or paid service; no contact with anyone. Model spend: about $3 a read on Sonnet or Opus for collection and the checkpoint, about $20 for each of the two interim checkpoints and $30 for the record, so about $110 at list rates before reviews.

## What would leave it inconclusive

- The container's egress changes between reads (a different address block echoed): classes either side are not compared; the record says so.
- More than a fifth of services `unreachable`, `unread_proxy` or `robots_unreachable` in a read: that read's tally is reported and kept out of measure 3.
- More than a fifth of answered probes `open_unconfirmed` or `js_notice` at the baseline: the classifier is not separating walls from content, and measures 2 and 3 are reported as the classifier's with that caveat.
- Measure 5 unread throughout: walls are undated beyond the reads themselves.

It cannot say, in any case, how much automated traffic any service receives, who sends it, whether any of it is AI, or whether any operator is harmed; those are operators' accounts, recorded as theirs.

RFC 9309 (section 2.3.1.2) says crawlers should follow at least five consecutive redirects when fetching robots.txt, so treating a redirect as unknown both departed from the standard this brief cites and set aside operators' rules. From read 1:
  - **robots.txt only:** the collector follows up to five redirects, same-site or cross-site, resolving a relative Location against the URL that gave it. Each hop is a request, logged in readlog.csv as `robots-redirect-<k>` and counted in the budget. The rules of the final robots.txt apply to the probe host, read by the same RFC 9309 matching. A chain still redirecting after the fifth hop is `robots_redirect` and no probe is sent. A hop that ends in a network failure, a 5xx, or a refusal by this container's own policy is `robots_unreachable`. A hop answered with a challenge or a 429 is `wall_at_robots`. 4xx other than 429 means no rules. In each case no probe is sent unless the final robots.txt allows it.
  - **Probes still follow no redirect.**
  - **The new ceiling:** at most seven requests to any one host in a read, enforced by the collector across all services (`HOST_CAP`). For one service that is its robots.txt, up to five redirects and one probe. A request that would pass the ceiling is not sent: the service is `robots_unreachable` (host cap) or `not_probed_host_cap`, both unknown and never walls.
  - **Resource ceiling, replacing "at most 322 panel requests" a read:** at most seven requests per service, so 1,127 a read in the worst case. On the baseline's pattern, one hop each for 22 services, about 271 is expected.
  - **Elsewhere:** the statement and HTTP Archive runs fetch robots.txt the same way. The Wayback run's single robots.txt request is unchanged and stays within its 60-request budget.
  - **Unchanged:** the panel, probes, classifier version 0.2, measures, pre-set readings, the thirteen weekly reads and the end date (no request after 2027-02-09T23:59:59Z). Offline checks are in [scripts/test_lh044.py](scripts/test_lh044.py), which makes no network request.
