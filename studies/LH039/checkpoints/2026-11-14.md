# Checkpoint 1: how far WordPress's fix had spread by 14 November 2026

**In short.** Seventeen days after WordPress fixed a flaw in its own updater, W3Techs's survey put 28.9% of the sites it sees on the 7.1 branch on the fixed release, and 14.0% on each of the 7.0 and 6.9 branches; each of those shares was higher at every daily read from 9 to 14 November. Composer downloads moved faster on 7.1 than on the older branches: on 12 November the fixed release took 88.3% of one package's 7.1 downloads and 83.5% of the other's, against 25% to 34% on 7.0 and 6.9. WordPress.org's own branch figures and release checks haven't been read since 9 November, because its API's robots.txt turns every crawler away. This is unreviewed working material, not a finding; the daily reads go on to 21 November, with a second checkpoint then.

## What was read, and what wasn't

- **Packagist checkpoint read, 14 November (observation).** All 52 requests (26 releases of 7.1, 7.0 and 6.9, for roots/wordpress and johnpbloch/wordpress-core) returned HTTP 200, from 14:15 UTC. Packagist's daily series in every body runs from 14 October to 12 November; **13 November, the day before this read, isn't in Packagist's history yet** and is missing, not zero. For a .0 release roots/wordpress answers under the key "7.1", "7.0" or "6.9"; johnpbloch under "7.1.0" and so on. Either way it is the same release.
- **W3Techs (observation).** The release pages for 7.1, 7.0 and 6.9 and their history pages were read with HTTP 200 at all six daily reads from 9 to 14 November; none failed. They weren't in the collector's list on 7 and 8 November (the brief added them on 8 November), so measure 1 starts on 9 November. Each page carried its own date, which matched the read's UTC date every time.
- **api.wordpress.org (observation).** From the read of 9 November on, every api.wordpress.org URL (branch shares, PHP, MySQL and locale shares, version-check, stable-check) was not fetched: its robots.txt (HTTP 200) disallows `/` for group `*`, and the gate logged each as not fetched (bodies say "not fetched: disallowed by robots.txt"; readlog status `error`). That is six missing days for each, from 9 to 14 November. These are missing readings, never negatives. The reads of 7 and 8 November, made before the keeper's rule, hold them.
- **wordpress.org per-branch download counters (observation).** Read with HTTP 200 at all six reads from 9 November; not in the list on 7 and 8 November.

## Measure 5: supply (observation)

- **7 and 8 November:** stable-check named 7.1.4 "latest", 7.0.8 and 6.9.11 "outdated", and every earlier release of the three branches "insecure"; version-check offered autoupdate to 7.1.4, 7.0.8 and 6.9.11, and upgrade to 7.1.4. No E3.
- **9 to 14 November: missing.** Neither stable-check nor version-check was fetched (robots.txt). The release feed isn't among the daily URLs. So the brief's instruments can't say whether a later release (E3) came out after 8 November, or whether W3Techs's 7.1.5 label is a published release.
- **Supplementary, not the brief's instrument:** the package-level Packagist stats (https://packagist.org/packages/roots/wordpress/stats.json and johnpbloch's), read daily, list versions; their highest on each branch was 7.1.4, 7.0.8 and 6.9.11 at all eight reads to 14 November. That's what the two Composer packagings had published, not WordPress's own word, so it is a lead only.

## What the robots.txt rule does to the question

The amendment of 9 November left this to checkpoint 1 (interpretation). The question rests on W3Techs (measures 1 and 2) and Packagist (measure 3); neither is affected, and neither lost a read. What's lost is context and supply: WordPress.org's branch shares (measure 4) after 8 November, and the stable-check and version-check entries (measure 5) after 8 November. The practical cost is in measure 5. "Fixed" includes any later release, and the tabulator counts any higher release on a branch as fixed, so an unseen E3 wouldn't be miscounted as unfixed; but whether 7.1.5 should move from "reported apart" to "fixed" can't be settled by the brief's instruments while api.wordpress.org stays closed. Nothing in the question, the measures or the pre-set reading changes here.

## Against the brief's inconclusive conditions

No W3Techs read has failed (0 of 6, against the brief's threshold of more than four). W3Techs's fixed share changed between every pair of consecutive reads on all three branches, so the "didn't move" condition hasn't arisen so far (observation; it says nothing about how often W3Techs visits a given site).

## Limits

- W3Techs gives shares to 0.1 percentage point; the ranges above carry only the "less than 0.1%" bounds, as the brief sets, not that rounding. Its shares are of the sites it detects on each branch, and it describes no per-site visit schedule on these pages.
- A W3Techs value that held between reads wouldn't show sites stood still; a share that rose doesn't show when the sites updated.
- A Packagist download is a fetch by a developer or a build system, not a site updating itself, and weighs each environment by how often it fetches. The two views count different things and are not combined.
- 13 November is missing from the Packagist series; checkpoint 2's read will hold it.
- No source here sees whether an update completed, or whether any site was exposed or attacked. No cause is claimed for any difference between branches, packages or views.
