# Checkpoint 2: how far WordPress's fix had spread by 21 November 2026

**In short.** We asked how fast the sites and builds that run WordPress moved to its fix of 28 October. By the last daily read, 24 days on, W3Techs's survey put 40.2% of the sites it sees on the 7.1 branch on the fixed release, and about a quarter on each of 7.0 and 6.9; set beside PHP's fix in the same survey at about the same ages, WordPress 7.1 sat inside PHP's range rather than above it, which is a description, not a cause. A further release on all three branches appears to have come out between 18 and 19 November, by the Composer package lists, but WordPress.org's own release checks have refused us since 9 November, so the study's instruments can't confirm it, and the developer-download figures from 18 November miss it. All of this is unreviewed working material; the record comes next, not before 22 November, with its reviews.

- **What the Packagist read didn't cover (observation).** packagist.py reads a fixed list of releases, 7.1.0 to 7.1.4, 7.0.0 to 7.0.8 and 6.9.0 to 6.9.11, and its `--extra` option for later releases wasn't used. - **7 and 8 November:** stable-check named 7.1.4 "latest", 7.0.8 and 6.9.11 "outdated"; version-check offered autoupdate to 7.1.4, 7.0.8 and 6.9.11, and upgrade to 7.1.4. No E3.

- **Supplementary, not the brief's instrument (a lead only):** the two Composer packagings' version lists (https://packagist.org/packages/roots/wordpress/stats.json and johnpbloch's) named 7.1.4, 7.0.8 and 6.9.11 as the highest on each branch at every read to 18 November (14:15 UTC), and 7.1.5, 7.0.9 and 6.9.12 at every read from 19 November (14:17 UTC). ## Limits

- W3Techs gives shares to 0.1 percentage point; the ranges carry only the "less than 0.1%" bounds, as the brief sets, not that rounding. Its shares are of the sites it detects on each branch.
- A W3Techs value that rose doesn't show when the sites updated; with each site visited about once a month (LH033), a day's figure blends a month of visits.
- Measure 2 sets two kinds of server software side by side on one instrument. The populations overlap (WordPress runs on PHP, so many sites are in both), the PHP figures come from reads two to five days off the matching day, and PHP's "at or above" includes any later PHP patch while WordPress's 7.1 figure leaves 7.1.5 out. A WordPress site updating WordPress doesn't update its PHP. No cause is claimed for any difference.
- A Packagist download is a fetch by a developer or a build system, not a site updating itself, and weighs each environment by how often it fetches. The two views count different things and are not combined.
- No source here sees whether an update completed, or whether any site was exposed or attacked.
