# After WordPress fixed CVE-2026-119977 on 28 October 2026, how fast did the public views of WordPress's installed base move to the fixed releases?

**In short.** We asked how fast the websites and the builds that run WordPress moved to its fix of 28 October 2026 for a flaw in its own updater. In W3Techs's survey of websites the fixed share of the newest branch, 7.1, rose at every daily read, from about 20% twelve days after the fix to 36% at day 21, read hours before WordPress shipped a further security release that afternoon; counting that release as fixed, as the brief's rule does once it is published, it was 43% at day 24 (38% for the first fix's release alone), against about a quarter on the 7.0 and 6.9 branches. Composer downloads, which are fetches by developers and build systems, moved much faster on 7.1, where the fixed release took most downloads within days, than on the older branches. Beside PHP's fix in the same survey at about the same ages, WordPress 7.1 sat inside PHP's range, not clearly above it; that's a description, not a cause, and none of this is reviewed yet.

## Method

The brief (protocol 0.1, fixed 8 November 2026) and its amendment of 9 November are unchanged. Daily reads ran from 7 November (the first two before the brief) to 21 November with scripts/collect.py, and Packagist's per-version series were read at the two checkpoints with scripts/packagist.py (52 requests each, all HTTP 200). From 9 November every request passed the robots.txt gate (scripts/lh_fetch.py, recurring rule, provider anthropic); api.wordpress.org's robots.txt (`Disallow: /` for `*`) refused its six URLs at all 13 reads from 9 November. W3Techs's and api.wordpress.org's bodies were replaced by derived files on 21 November under the keeper's decision of that day (data/REMOVED.md), and the tabulator's outputs from the derived files were found byte-identical to those from the bodies.

For this record, scripts/tabulate.py was rerun on all retained reads (record-1.0-tabulation.txt and record-1.0-packagist.csv, byte-identical to checkpoint 2's outputs), and scripts/checkpoint2_extra.py was rerun (its output rewrote checkpoints/2026-11-21-extra.txt identically). A new script, scripts/record_measures.py, applies the brief's 7.1.5 rule with E3 settled, sets out the post hoc treatments and measure 2 with the settled figure, and lists the Packagist shares of 17 to 19 November; from version 1.1 it also sets out measure 2's position against each PHP branch, the days below half counted to 17 November, and the weekend dips (record-1.1-output.txt, Parts E and F).

**The one-off read (after collection stopped).** The daily collector never read WordPress's release feed: it isn't in collect.py's list, and the feed was read on 7 November, before the brief, and not again until this read. With api.wordpress.org's stable-check and version-check refused from 9 November, nothing read during collection could have caught E3. The decision to make this read was taken on 22 November, after every outcome had been seen. That afternoon, scripts/oneoff_release_dates.py read WordPress's release feed (https://wordpress.org/news/category/releases/feed/, the brief's named instrument for E0 and E3, 15:14:54 UTC, HTTP 200) and its release archive (https://wordpress.org/download/releases/, 15:14:57 UTC, HTTP 200), once each, through scripts/lh_fetch.py with access='oneoff'. wordpress.org's robots.txt allowed both, and keep_decision allowed both bodies to be kept (no training, archiving or `*` refusal at those paths; no reservation on the pages), so the feed's body is kept in data/oneoff-2026-11-22T1514Z/ with the read log and the facts used (feed.facts.json, archive.facts.json); the archive page's body (1.3 MB) was not committed, for size alone, since archive.facts.json holds the facts used and the read log its hash (the read log's "yes" for the archive is keep_decision's answer, not what the repository holds; the folder's README.md says so); email addresses are withheld from the kept body. The read was made for the release dates only, which are supply metadata, not an outcome; it changes no measured share, only which label the brief's rule counts as fixed. api.wordpress.org's stable-check and version-check weren't attempted: its robots.txt refuses `*`, which the one-off rule also obeys.

## Limits

- **W3Techs's pace is partly its own.** Each site is visited about once a month, by LH033's reading of W3Techs's documentation (not re-checked here), so a day's figure blends a month of visits, and a share that rose doesn't show when the sites updated. Nothing here separates W3Techs's schedule from sites' behaviour.
- **What W3Techs's labels mean isn't known.** It listed a 7.1.5 before WordPress published one, and a 7.1.6 that WordPress hasn't published; how it detects a release isn't described on the pages read.
- **After 18 November two fixes are mixed.** From the read of 19 November, the fixed share counts a second security release whose post lists, among other fixes, an unauthenticated privilege escalation "that could lead to remote code execution" (release feed, read 22 November). Uptake from then on isn't uptake of the first fix alone.
- **Packagist** counts fetches by developers and build systems, weighing each by how often it fetches; it says nothing about sites. 20 November is missing, and 18 and 19 November are lower bounds.
