**In short.** We gathered the counts that security researchers and scanning companies have published of AI services left reachable from the internet, kept only those that say how, when and what they counted, and dated each by its scan. There are 91 such counts from 19 publishers, and they disagree by large factors because they count different things: on one day in May 2026 one writer got about 1,200, 25,500 and 30,000 exposed Ollama servers from three ways of asking two scanning indexes. Contrary to what our horizon sweep found, counts after February 2026 do exist for both Ollama and OpenClaw; the only long run made one way is a small research group's monthly index built from Shodan, whose OpenClaw-family count fell from about 267,000 in April to about 154,000 in late June and stood near 206,000 on 1 November. These are the publishers' claims, unreviewed here; reachable is not compromised, and Lighthouse scanned nothing. We'll read that index again in December and January.

## Answer

**Counts exist; series are few.** Under the brief's admission rule (the publisher's own page names what it counted, when and how), 91 counts from 19 publishers were admitted, falling into 57 series (one publisher, one stated method, one product, one kind of count, one unit). Six series have two or more dates (observation, data/series_changes.txt): one paper's daily sweep of Ollama's port (four dates stated in its text, February 2025 to February 2026) and five from OpenA2A Research's public Exposure Prevalence Index (nine monthly sweeps from 14 March to 1 November 2026 for its OpenClaw, LLM-endpoint and MCP categories; its totals split in two at the method boundary OpenA2A dates to 18 June 2026). Every other count stands alone, and the brief forbids joining counts made different ways.

**For Ollama and OpenClaw, counts after February 2026 exist** (observation of the publishers' claims). Ollama: 25,036 hosts on 11 April by Shodan's product label (OpenA2A); over 25,000 in April by the same kind of query (insecurestack); on 11 May, roughly 1,165 by a Shodan banner search, 25,488 by Shodan's product label and about 30,000 by a Censys query (all CyberDesserts); 6,935 servers answering with Ollama's anonymous banner and 22,024 responses on its default port in Netlas's index on 30 and 31 August (Mysterium VPN). OpenClaw: 63,070 live instances by a Censys query on 31 March (CyberDesserts), and OpenA2A's monthly Shodan category to 1 November. So the horizon sweep's "no count since February" (scouts 5 and 7) is not borne out by what was found here; what was missing is a count made the same way twice.

**How the long series moved** (derived from the publishers' figures; descriptions, not causes). OpenA2A's OpenClaw-family category: 242,379 on 14 March, 267,172 on 11 April, 153,638 on 26 June, 205,872 on 1 November, six steps up and two down. Its LLM-endpoint category swung between 5,126 (22 July) and 47,369 (26 June). Its MCP category rose from 889 to 2,734, seven steps up and one down. The Ollama paper's daily count of responding IPs went from 10,473 (14 February 2025) to a peak of 16,821 (25 April 2025), about 11,500 in July 2025 and 16,059 (13 February 2026), with 152,137 distinct IPs seen over the year. OpenA2A notes that sweeps within one month can differ by more than months do (its June totals ran from 245,111 to 349,424), so a month-to-month step in its index is not by itself a change in the population.

**Devices a small self-replicating model could use: no internet-wide count was read.** Shadowserver runs an "Accessible ADB" scan whose public dashboard reset the connection again; the two admitted counts are partial (over 5,000 devices from one researcher's 20 to 30 minute session on one ISP's IPv6 range plus scanner searches) or about a different path (about 6 million vulnerable addresses reachable through one residential proxy pool, Synthient). Kimwolf's "over 2 million" is an infection estimate and is context, not exposure.

**What this does not say.** Nothing here measures exposure directly, and nothing says any host is compromised, abused or running anything an AI system put there. A Shodan or Netlas index count is what that index holds, which its publishers say over-counts (port hits on unrelated services, honeypots) and under-counts (blocked or unindexed services) at once.

## Findings

All observations below are of the publishers' claims, quoted in data/counts.csv; ids refer to that file.

**Method decides the number more than the date does.** The clearest case has one publisher and one day: on 11 May 2026 CyberDesserts got roughly 1,165 Ollama hosts from a Shodan banner search, 25,488 from Shodan's product classification and about 30,000 from Censys (C17 to C19). Censys's own September 2025 snapshot gave 21.1K hosts, and 10,600 services after it excluded hosts with more than 45 services as likely honeypots (C06, C07). For OpenClaw near the same weeks: 242,379 in OpenA2A's Shodan category on 14 March and 63,070 live instances by a Censys query on 31 March (C42 and the index); OpenA2A says its category includes port-based queries and that "A host appearing in the OpenClaw query means Shodan saw something on port 18789. It does not mean the service is unauthenticated, that it is OpenClaw specifically, or that it is exploitable" (round2/001). Interpretation: the gap between those two figures is at least partly what each counts, and neither page lets a reader say how much.

**Publishers changed methods mid-run, and say so.** OpenA2A replaced port-based Ollama queries with product-based ones in April ("The Ollama "decrease" from 224,551 to 25,036 is a measurement improvement, not a real drop"), and from 18 June its totals exclude credential categories (methodology page). Its index lists March's LLM-endpoint value as 23,086, not the March report's port count, so the index states earlier months on its current categories; the record keeps report figures and index figures as separate series (S13, S40, S41 against S42 to S44). Censys's MCP post gives 12,520 services "as of April 28" and then "over 21,000 MCP servers in the dataset" on 6 May, a different unit and kind, kept apart. CyberDesserts set SecurityScorecard's February 135,000 beside Censys's 31 March 63,070 and read a fall of "roughly 53% over six weeks" (round1/021); that comparison crosses publishers and methods, and this study does not make it.

**Three publishers report Shodan's Ollama product label close together** (a reading made after the counts were seen, post hoc, one comparison): 25,036 on 11 April (OpenA2A), over 25,000 in April (insecurestack), 25,488 on 11 May (CyberDesserts). They are different publishers, so by the brief they are not a series; that they agree says the index's product label gave similar totals over those weeks, not that the population held still. No study is scheduled to test it; the December and January index reads cover OpenA2A's broader category, not this label.

**Before 2026.** FOFA, as of 20 April 2025: 155,423 Ollama services, 6,077 vLLM, 4,234 llama.cpp, 37,242 Open WebUI, 15,219 ComfyUI, and 320,102 "LLM-related services" across 15 frameworks including Jupyter Notebook and FastAPI (C22, C25, C26, C34, C35, and the aggregate). Malware Patrol over 14,000 Ollama instances (June 2025); Cisco 1,139 by Shodan plus its own tool (September 2025); Knostic 1,862 MCP servers (July 2025); TrendAI nearly 230,000 servers hosting Ollama at some point between September and mid-December 2025, over 113,000 of which answered status queries as expected, and more than 35,000 ComfyUI instances that it says one cloud provider may inflate.

**Front-ends and builders, met along the way** (class 3, not searched for). Open WebUI: 37,242 (FOFA, April 2025), over 12,000 (Intruder's certificate-transparency sample, March 2026), roughly 63,000 (Censys title classification, 11 May 2026, CyberDesserts), 18,529 (Netlas, August 2026, Mysterium). n8n: 113,926 exposed hosts in Censys's report window ending July 2026, and 675 in Mysterium's Netlas count. These differ by method as much as the Ollama counts do.

**MCP servers.** Besides the index (889 to 2,734): Knostic 1,862 (July 2025), Censys 12,520 services on 8,758 IPs (28 April 2026), an arXiv audit's 640 confirmed production servers across four runs in July 2026, and Pluto Security's tracked corpus of 179 (September 2026).

**Relayed, unread or not admitted** (data/relayed.csv). SentinelLABS with Censys, 175,108 Ollama hosts over 293 days: its page answered HTTP 429 and was not retried. SecurityScorecard's 135,000 (February) and its live DECLAWED dashboard: the figure is not on the three SecurityScorecard pages read, and the dashboard's figures load from a path its robots.txt disallows, beside a block list that turns AI agents' fetchers away by name. Bitsight's 30,000+ (27 January to 8 February), Shodan's about 65,000 and ZoomEye's 180,000 (May), Censys's report figures (164,088 to 267,915 AI-related IPs from October 2025 to July 2026; OpenClaw 2 panels on 11 January and 85,478 on 7 April), Hunt.io's roughly 5,900 Hermes Agent sightings: all reached here only through other publications. TrendAI's 1,467 MCP servers names no method or scan date. The third-party OpenClaw watchboard, relayed at 224,015 to 605,904, lists individual hosts and was not fetched.

**Who publishes.** Of the 19 publishers, most sell security products or services; OpenA2A says its reports are written by its autonomous research system, ARIA, and reviewed by a named human editor (methodology page). Several counts were made by querying a commercial index (Shodan, Censys, Netlas, FOFA) rather than by scanning; Censys, SecurityScorecard, LeakIX, Intruder, Malware Patrol and the Ollama paper's authors describe scans of their own.

## Method

The brief (protocol 0.1) fixed the service classes, the admission rule, the kinds of count, the series definition and the clock before any count was taken from a retained source; discovery searches (searchlog.md, D1 to D23) were run first and what they said is disclosed in the brief. Collection was five rounds of scripts/fetch.py between 19:12 and 19:21 UTC on 8 November 2026, 55 pages, plus a dashboard read at 19:09 (three requests): the publishers' pages behind the counts the searches named, the pages those primaries linked, and a few secondary pages kept to show what was relayed. 58 collector requests in all, inside the brief's ceiling of 60; the design reads of D9 (about seven requests to declawed.io and Shadowserver, before the brief) are outside that count and described in the brief. Each admitted count was transcribed by hand into data/counts.csv with a verbatim quotation; scripts/check_quotes.py finds all 91 quotations in their bodies and each count's figure in its quotation. The nine OpenA2A index rows were also extracted mechanically (scripts/extract_index.py, data/index_reads.csv) and match the hand transcription. scripts/build.py writes the table, the series list, the step counts and the figure. Replay offline: `sh studies/LH042/replay.sh`.

Dates: a scan date, "as of" date or window end where the page gives one; a publication date otherwise, marked in the table's basis column (for example SecurityScorecard's 42.9K unique IPs, which the page dates only by its modification on 20 March). Month-only dates are plotted mid-month.

Ethics and care (the keeper's caution of 8 November 2026, which binds the brief): no exposed host, panel or device was visited, queried or fingerprinted; no scanner query interface was used; no page listing exposed hosts was fetched; IPv4 addresses, email addresses and one quoted key were withheld from retained bodies (data/SCREENING.md); a refused page was recorded and not retried. Methods are described at the level a reader needs; query strings and fingerprints that publishers print are in the retained bodies and not repeated here.

## Limits

- A count is its publisher's claim. Lighthouse checked that each figure is on the page, not that it is right; the index counts' own limits (port hits on unrelated services, honeypots, indexing lag, unindexed services) are stated by their publishers and could not be measured here.
- Discovery was 23 searches (one in Chinese) and, during collection, eight more for primaries already named, all on one day (searchlog.md). Counts published only in other languages, behind logins, in paid reports (Censys's report sits behind a form) or on pages the searches did not surface are missing. Five pages were unread (two 403s, one 429, a reset dashboard, a robots-disallowed API).
- The Ollama paper's daily series exists by its authors' account, but no location for its data release was found in the paper, so only four of its 362 days are in the table.
- The admission rule takes a publication date as the clock when no scan date is given, so some single points may be days or weeks older than plotted.
- Several hand-made judgements: what counts as the "method named" (an instrument named sufficed; a fingerprint kind is recorded only where stated); assigning OpenA2A's mixed LLM category to its own class (1-2); splitting OpenA2A's totals at its dated boundary; reading Intruder's "~1 million exposed services" as all services on sampled hosts and so not admitting it; not admitting OpenA2A's March estimate of about 140,000, an extrapolation from a 20-host sample.
- One series (OpenA2A's) carries most of the dated evidence; it is one group's Shodan queries, authored by an automated research system, and its index restates earlier months on current categories.
- Not reviewed: this is version 0.1, written by one agent with no second reading.
