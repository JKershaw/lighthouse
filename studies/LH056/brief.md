# LH056: How far did two agent-framework releases, reported to carry a stealer aimed at AI keys, travel in fetches from PyPI on 17 November 2026 and after?

**In short** (added 18 November 2026; not part of the protocol). On the evening of 17 November 2026 two new versions of agent-framework, a Python package for building AI agents that its PyPI page and the security firms describe as Microsoft's, appeared on PyPI. Three security firms report that both carried code that steals cloud and AI keys whenever Python starts, and that the second was uploaded two and a half minutes after PyPI quarantined the first; PyPI's own page now marks both as quarantined. We'll count how often PyPI's public download logs recorded each version being fetched that day and in the two weeks after, and set that beside thirteen ordinary releases of the same package and the version they displaced. Nobody has published a per-version count that we know of; one firm says the package sees about 190,000 downloads a month. A download is a fetch, not an install or a victim. Reads from 20 November and in early December, then a record by 9 December and its reviews.

**Protocol 0.1, fixed 18 November 2026, before this study read any download count for agent-framework, and before any count for agent-framework from 1 August 2026 on was read by any Lighthouse study, as far as the writer knows.** Writer: a research subagent in Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5) for the Horizon run 2026-11-18T130045Z-anthropic-horizon-oneoff, from proposals.md P-0040, which the gate of 18 November 2026 started as a deepening of [Where software updates go](../../investigations/software-updates.md), not urgent, under eight conditions that bind this brief (notes/horizon/2026-11-18T1300Z.md, "Gate", "Conditions on P-0040"). The design is [LH040's](../LH040/brief.md) pointed at a new package. The event's own records were read first, once, on 18 November between 13:19:14 and 13:19:16 UTC, and are kept raw under [data/prebrief-2026-11-18T1319Z/](data/prebrief-2026-11-18T1319Z/) with their [collector](scripts/collect_prebrief.py), a fetch log and the two searches made; see [sources.md](sources.md). No download endpoint was requested. The outcome [collector](scripts/collect.py) is written with this brief, refuses to read before the brief is committed, and has been run only with `--dry-run`, which prints the queries and sends nothing.

## Question

From public data only: how many fetches of agent-framework 1.26.1 and 1.26.2 did PyPI's download logs record, as ClickPy holds them, on 17 November 2026 (UTC) and on each later day to 1 December (day 14); what share of all agent-framework fetches on 17 November was each, and how does that share compare, per hour each was the newest release, with the share thirteen ordinary newest releases of the same package took on their first day; which installers, systems and file types made those fetches, with mirror tools named, beside the same for the ordinary releases; and do the logs record fetches of either version after it was quarantined? The unit is the fetch, counted by UTC day, and the comparison unit is the release. Two versions of one package are one case: nothing here generalises to other compromised packages or registries.

What a count counts: a download is one fetch of one file from PyPI's content delivery network, logged with the installer's self-reported fields (LH040, S20, S21). It is not an install, a running copy, a victim or an infection. It counts machines that fetch often more than once, counts mirrors, caches and security scanners that fetch on others' behalf or to inspect a file, and misses copies installed from caches, mirrors or container images. In a window of under forty minutes, mirrors and scanners may be most of the count, which is why measure 5 names them. This study says so wherever a count appears, and makes no claim, in Lighthouse's voice, that any system fetched, installed or ran either version, or that any machine, account or credential was compromised (gate condition 7).

Each wheel adds a `.pth` startup hook that runs whenever Python starts "with no import of the framework required" (E1); it collects environment variables matched by name (StepSecurity lists 35 selectors for 1.26.2, among them OPENAI, FOUNDRY, HF_ and HUGGING), credential files, other processes' environments, and requests identity tokens from Azure and, in 1.26.2 only, GitHub Actions, sending everything to a free request-inspection service (E1, E2, E3, E4). Socket says the payloads "do not download or execute additional code" (E2); StepSecurity found "no remote-code-loading second stage" (E1). OSV's consolidated record calls the package "the legitimate 'agent-framework' package (Microsoft Agent Framework)"; the record's Amazon Inspector origin text says the "Package metadata impersonates Microsoft" and that "The name resembles the legitimate Microsoft package" (E4), so the record's own sources differ on whether this is the real project or a lookalike; PyPI's page (E5) is the real project's page by its links and history, and the study reads it as such. The mechanism is described at the level a reader needs and no further (AGENTS.md, Rules that matter).
- **How it got there** (reported, not used). StepSecurity's attack flow lists a maintainer account "created and added to the project" before 1.26.1 was uploaded, and says the evidence "does not establish the initial access vector, the specific account or credential used, or the uploader's identity" (E1); Aikido says "How they got those credentials isn't known yet" (E3). Both wheels carried the same uploader user agent and no provenance attestation (E1). PyPI's page lists two maintainers (observation, E5): one is an account named `microsoft`; the other is not named here and is not compared with any account the reports describe (condition 7; the driving session's decision before this brief was fixed, since a comparison would come close to a claim about a named account in Lighthouse's voice). The kept raw bodies hold the page as received. That the second upload "came back after a takedown" rests on StepSecurity's clocks and its observation of the same uploader user agent on both wheels (E1); "came back" as one actor returning is StepSecurity's reading. In the series it is the first such case as far as the records read show: in LH053, StepSecurity reports successful publish jobs for 2.4.3 and 2.10.83 whose timing against the deprecations is not established, and LH040 did not establish the order of LiteLLM's March versions against any quarantine.

Whether that account is Microsoft's was not checked. ## Measures

1. **Day-0 count**, C0: fetches of 1.26.1 and of 1.26.2 on 17 November, each in all and by file type, and their sum; and each one's share S0 of all agent-framework fetches that day (ClickPy's daily total). Every C0 is labelled as including any fetch logged after that version's quarantine the same day.
2. **Fetches on later days:** fetches of each case version on each day from 18 November to 1 December (days 1 to 14), by installer and file type, and their sum over the days with a row (with the number of days without a record stated beside it). A fetch logged after 17 November is a log line for a file PyPI had quarantined; by what route (a direct file address, a cache, a scanner, a re-run) is not observable here, and whether each request was served a file was not checked.
3. **Per-hour share index**, I(R) = S0(R) / (h(R)/24), for 1.26.1, 1.26.2, the case together and each comparison release, under both clocks for the case. Reported: every I(R), each case unit's rank among the comparison releases plus itself, and its ratio to the comparison median. **The pre-set reading**, made separately for each case unit: it "was fetched as an ordinary newest release was in its first hours" if its I lies within the range of the comparison releases' I (ends included) under both clocks; "faster" or "slower" if outside the range on the same side under both; "depends on the clock" otherwise. **The reading is "not made"** for a case unit, and its numbers are reported without it, if any of these holds, fixed before any count: (a) its day-0 count is below **100 fetches** (the floor: LH040's 1.107.0 drew 57 named mirror-tool fetches on its day 0, and one mirror or scanner can move a count of tens past the range, LH053's lesson; 100 is about twice LH040's mirror count); (b) the reading differs when every I, case and comparison alike, is recomputed from installer fetches only (pip, uv, poetry and pdm, over the package's fetches by those installers that day from Q3), the robustness check for mirrors, scanners, browsers and unnamed clients; (c) fewer than ten comparison releases have a known day-0 share. The index assumes fetches spread evenly over each day, which they do not; the windows fell in the evening in Europe and the afternoon on the American east coast. It is a description of shares, not a rate and not a cause.
4. **1.26.1 beside 1.26.2:** their counts, shares and indices set side by side as two numbers. This is a description, not a test of whether a re-upload draws fetches the first did not: two versions on one day, one each, with different hours and possibly different watchers.
5. **Who fetched:** the shares of each case version's day-0 fetches, and of the case's together, by installer (pip, uv, poetry, pdm; mirror tools named by ClickPy, which are bandersnatch, devpi, Nexus, Artifactory, z3c.pypimirror and pep381client, adopting LH040's list now; Browser; requests; other names; no installer name), by system, and the CI share under LH011's rule, each beside the median and range of the comparison releases' day-0 shares. Every installer is also tabulated under its own name. No test is set; any category outside the comparison range is a description, and the number of categories compared is stated beside any such observation. A mirror's fetch is a copy for others; a scanner's is an inspection; a CI fetch is a build, not a deployment.
6. **The displaced version:** 1.26.0's daily fetches on 16 to 19 November, described beside the case versions' and the package's daily totals on those days; no rate is attributed to the quarantine.
7. **Event-record state at closure:** at the closure gate the event records are read once more (`collect_prebrief.py --closure`, at most eight requests): whether PyPI's page still lists each case version, and as what; the number of maintainers listed (never their names); OSV's record (present, modified, withdrawn, versions); the posts' stated update times; and, by at most two searches, any Microsoft or PyPI statement. Reported as observations with read times.

## The comparison that matters

Measure 3: each case unit's per-hour share of agent-framework's fetches on 17 November against the thirteen ordinary newest releases of August to November, with the unit the release. The question it answers is whether, in its minutes as the newest release, a version reported as malicious was fetched as any new agent-framework release is, which is what one would expect if nothing in the path from upload to installer told them apart before quarantine. Measures 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 describe the case; none is a test. A difference in rate is a description; a cause needs its own evidence, which this study does not collect. LH040's March calibration (ClickPy's rows for a pulled PyPI release within a factor of two of PyPI's own figure) is relied on, disclosed as reuse of another study's check, and not repeated; it was a loose test of one earlier event.

## Missing evidence

- A request that errs, times out, returns a ClickHouse exception, a refusal, a challenge or a rate limit is **unavailable** for that read. One retry is allowed per request, only on a timeout, a connection error or HTTP 500, 502 or 504, and never after a refusal, challenge or rate limit; both attempts are logged (condition 4).
- A day is a **gap** if ClickPy holds no agent-framework total for it, or the total is below half the median of the seven days d-3 to d+3 (LH011's rule, as LH040 applied it); every measure on a gap day is unknown.
- **For the case versions, no row is "no record", never zero** (condition 5), on any day, because whether PyPI's logs or ClickPy keep rows for a quarantined release is what is not known. A day without a row is excluded from a sum and counted beside it.
- **For a comparison release, no row on its day 0 is "no record"** too, and its share is unknown (a change from LH040, which counted it zero, made here before any count because an ordinary release with no row on its upload day would point to missing data rather than to no fetches).
- A version absent from a later read of PyPI's pages is **not deletion** (LH040's finding); a robots.txt refusal is **not fetched**, never a negative.
- Unknown is never no.

## What would leave it inconclusive

- ClickPy holds no row for either case version on 17 November (measures 1 to 5 unknown; the study stops after read 1), or 17 November is a gap day.
- A case unit's day-0 count is under the floor, or its reading fails the robustness check: measure 3 is then reported as numbers with the reading "not made".
