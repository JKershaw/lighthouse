# How complete have GH Archive's hourly files been since September 2026?

**In short.** GH Archive keeps one file of GitHub's public events per hour, and several of our studies lean on it. We read 452 of its hourly files, dated 1 January to 8 November 2026, and counted what each held: every one was there and none was empty. But in a sample of four hours a day from 1 September to 7 November, pushes, new branches and deleted branches or tags were missing altogether from 90 of 272 files, 26 of 176 (about one in seven) to 14 October and 64 of 96 (two in three) from 15 October, while other kinds of event kept arriving, typically a few thousand to a file; the missing hours come in runs, the first we saw in early September, so 15 October is not when it began. Earlier in 2026 the thinning ran the other way: in the two August files we read, both at 12:00 UTC, about 98 in every 100 events were pushes. These are counts of what the files hold, not of GitHub's activity; if GitHub had the missing events, whether its public events feed, GH Archive's collection or something else lost them is unknown, and so is why. This is version 0.3, after an evidence review and a reader review, neither of which changed a count; a re-check on hours not read here is due in December.

## Answer

Every one of the 452 hourly files read, dated from 1 January to 8 November 2026, was present and none was empty (observation). But from 1 September to 7 November 2026, PushEvent, CreateEvent and DeleteEvent were absent, always together, from 90 of the 272 panel files (four fixed hours a day, 00:00, 06:00, 12:00 and 18:00 UTC): 26 of 176 (15 per cent) from 1 September to 14 October and 64 of 96 (67 per cent) from 15 October to 7 November, at every sampled hour of day (observation, derived share). They vanish and return in runs, of one file to 29 consecutive sampled files, and the edges fall inside an hour, not on file boundaries. In files without pushes, the other event types kept arriving (every file read had events in at least 57 of its 60 minutes) at roughly the levels they had in the same period's files with pushes. The reviewer's lead, that from 15 October many hours hold no pushes, holds as a description; that this began on 15 October does not (the 15 October split was taken from that lead, not found in the data), because September already had a four-day run of files without pushes (1 September 12:00 to 5 September 06:00) and two shorter ones.

Before September the thinning ran the other way. All 16 reference files, read at 12:00 UTC on the 1st and 15th of each month from January to August, held pushes, but the other types fell: in the two August files, pushes were 97.6 and 97.9 per cent of events, against 74.8 per cent on 1 January (observation; one hour of day, and two files for August).

The brief's fixed reading of the before-and-after comparison is **not applied**: the September baseline failed the brief's own test (19 of 120 September panel files, 16 per cent, had no PushEvent, above the 10 per cent limit), so by the brief comparison 1 is reported as description against the January to August reference hours, which all held pushes.

What the files hold is not what happened on GitHub, and no independent count of GitHub's activity was read. Whether GitHub's public events feed, GH Archive's collector or something else lost these events is unknown: neither GH Archive's page nor GitHub's changelog, docs or status incidents for September to November 2026, as read, say anything about it, and GH Archive's issue tracker could not be read from here (interpretation boundary; searchlog.md).

## Findings

Observations are counts of what the files held when read; derived measures are computed from them in data/results.txt.

1. **Present, readable and stable** (observation). All 452 distinct files answered HTTP 200 and decompressed; no line failed to parse; no file was empty, and no file held an event dated outside its hour. Six files re-read 2 to 14 minutes later had identical hashes; ten files the reviewer fetched 20 to 30 minutes earlier matched by size and hash prefix; LH002's 2026-04-02-14 still has 35,617,941 bytes and 153,261 events, and LH043's 2026-07-01-12 still has sha256 1c74717a.... Every file's Last-Modified header falls about 5 minutes after the end of its hour (5.0 to 5.4 minutes), which suggests (interpretation: a header is not a write history) that the files read were last written shortly after their hour and not replaced later.
2. **The file reported empty was not** (observation). The driver's task said 2026-11-05 hour 3 "came back empty". Read twice here (19:58 and 20:01 UTC), it held 12,045 events, 6,861 of them PushEvents, with the same hash both times. What the earlier reader received is not known.
3. **Pushes, new branches and deletions vanish together** (observation). In every file read, PushEvent, CreateEvent and DeleteEvent were either all present or all absent. In the panel: 19 of 120 September files, 49 of 124 October files and 22 of 28 November files held none. By hour of day, from 15 October: 17 of 24 at 00:00, 15 of 24 at 06:00, 16 of 24 at 12:00 and 16 of 24 at 18:00; before it, 8, 5, 5 and 8 of 44.
4. **Runs, not scattered hours** (observation, from data/results.txt). Consecutive panel files without pushes: 1 September 12:00 to 5 September 06:00 (16 files); 9 to 10 September (2); 21 September 18:00 (1); 7 October 00:00 to 8 October 00:00 (5); 9 to 10 October (2); 15 October 00:00 to 18 October 06:00 (14); 22 October 18:00 to 24 October 00:00 (6); 26 October 12:00 to 2 November 12:00 (29); 3 to 4 November (8); 6 November 00:00 (1); 6 November 12:00 to 8 November 06:00 (8, counting the latest files). On the whole days read: none of 24 hours without pushes on 15 September and 1 October; 24 of 24 on 7 October, 15 October and 7 November; 4 on 14 October, 13 on 26 October, 7 on 5 November.
5. **Edges fall inside the hour** (observation). On the whole days, pushes stop partway through an hour: on 14 October the last PushEvent fell at minute 45, 42, 39, 42 and 28 of hours 14:00 to 18:00 and at minute 7 of 19:00, with pushes present again from minute 0 of every hour, though at a rate that also fell from hour to hour (PushEvents in minute 0: 1,334 at 12:00, 1,837 at 14:00, 969 at 16:00, 735 at 18:00, 587 at 19:00); on 26 October at minutes 52, 37, 23 and 8 of 07:00 to 10:00; on 5 November at minutes 40, 39, 37, 35, 24, 18, 14 and 1 of 00:00 to 07:00. Pushes can also resume mid-hour (5 November 10:00 from minute 20, 18:00 from minute 10). Of 37 files with pushes and a run of 5 or more minutes without any, 33 had pushes from minute 0 and lost them later in the hour.
6. **The other types keep arriving** (observation; derived ratios). Every panel file without pushes still held pull request, review, issue, issue comment, star, fork, release, member and wiki events. From 15 October, files without pushes held a median 3,956 events (2,085 to 11,345), of which the four control types (pull requests, issues, issue comments, stars) a median 3,150; against the January to August reference median for those four types (6,397), that is a median ratio of 0.49 in files without pushes and 0.41 in files with them. On the two whole days with both kinds of hour, the control types per file were if anything higher in hours without pushes (14 October: 4,199 against 2,119; 26 October: 2,724 against 1,698), though those hours fall at different times of day. The control types in files with pushes from 15 October are themselves not shown complete.
7. **Files with pushes held fewer than earlier in the year** (derived). Panel files with pushes held a median 61,826 PushEvents from 1 September to 14 October and 35,700 from 15 October, against 124,358 in the 16 January to August reference hours. All events per file with pushes: median 83,422 and 47,088, against 158,693.
8. **Before September, the thinning ran the other way** (observation, comparison 6). All 16 reference hours (12:00 on the 1st and 15th of each month, January to August) held pushes, between 72,852 and 166,990. But the other types fell steadily: pull request events 11,989 at 1 January 12:00, 4,659 at 1 May, 442 at 1 June, 425 at 1 July, 95 at 15 August; the four control types together 21,503 at 1 January and 189 at 15 August, while PushEvents rose to 166,990 of 170,586 events. CreateEvents, between 14,559 and 16,058 in six of the eight reference hours from 1 April to 1 July, were 8,619 on 15 July and 2,818 and 2,348 in August. LH043's July hour held 425 pull request events, 204 issue comments and 113 stars.
9. **Payload shape held** (observation). Every PushEvent in all 278 files with pushes, January to November, carried exactly `before`, `head`, `push_id`, `ref` and `repository_id`, as LH002 found in April and GitHub's docs describe; none carried a commit list. Every one of 1,808,380 CreateEvents had `ref_type` "branch": no tag or repository creation appeared in any file read, though GitHub's docs list both. Mean uncompressed size per event changed little by month (PushEvent 626 to 643 bytes; pull request event 1,280 to 1,368).
10. **Not interpretable as planned** (derived). The brief's event-id span measure is kept in data/files.csv but not read: in files with pushes the ids span billions, in files without a few million, so the field mixes ranges.

**A reading made after the counts (post hoc, one of several shapes looked at, untested).** The within-hour pattern (pushes present from minute 0, stopping earlier in each successive hour, then starting again at the next hour) has the shape of a budget that resets each hour and runs out sooner as demand rises. Nothing read says so, nothing here tests it, and it does not say whose budget, GitHub's or the collector's. Two things in the counts sit less well with it: on 14 October the rate in minute 0 also fell from hour to hour, and four partial hours on 5 and 6 November had pushes starting mid-hour. A test would need a statement from GH Archive or GitHub, or a comparison with an independent copy of the feed for the same hours.

## Method

The brief fixed the sample: the panel (every day from 1 September to 7 November at 00:00, 06:00, 12:00 and 18:00 UTC, 272 files), eight whole days (15 September, 1, 7, 14, 15 and 26 October, 5 and 7 November; 160 more files), 17 reference hours (12:00 on the 1st and 15th of each month from January to August, plus LH002's 2026-04-02-14), the latest three files (8 November 00:00, 06:00, 12:00) and six re-reads. The whole days were chosen with the reviewer's scan in view and describe structure; no rate is computed over them. scripts/collect.py fetched each file once, sequentially, with at least one second between requests and the User-Agent `Lighthouse-research/1.0 (+https://lighthouse.harbour.cat)`; it hashed the bytes, decompressed and parsed them in memory, kept the counts and discarded the file. 459 requests, 7.56 GB; the one repeated non-re-read request was 2026-09-12-0, being counted when the run was stopped at 19:42 for amendment 1. scripts/analyse.py applies the brief's definitions; scripts/figure.py draws the figure (checked rendered in light and dark at 720 by 500; palette check scripts/check_chart_style.py passed). Replay: `sh studies/LH046/replay.sh` recomputes data/files.csv, data/results.txt and figure.svg from the retained counts; refetching (`python3 -I studies/LH046/scripts/collect.py <dir>`) would read the archive as it is then, comparable by hash.

What is retained: data/hours.csv (per file: response, size, sha256, headers, counts by type, payload keys, CreateEvent ref types, uncompressed bytes by type, minute coverage, id range), data/minutes.csv (per file and minute: events, PushEvents, CreateEvents), data/fetch-log.tsv (every request), data/files.csv and data/results.txt (derived), data/sources/ (documentation read before the brief).

## Limits

- **Files, not GitHub.** Every count is what GH Archive's file held. A file without pushes does not show that nobody pushed; a file with 95 pull request events does not show that only 95 were opened. No independent count of GitHub's public activity was read.
- **Cause unknown.** No source read states why. GitHub's status incidents of 7, 19, 21, 22 and 23 October and 4 November concern events or writes: the 22 October one (13:05 to 15:20 UTC) delayed "repository push events (12m)", and the 23 October one delayed Dependabot's processing of "events related to repository pushes". None names the public Events API. Most do not line up with the gaps: 7 October's write outages (15:06 to 15:16 and 16:52 to 17:01 UTC) and 4 November's delays fell inside runs of files without pushes that had begun earlier, and 19 and 21 October's fell while the files held pushes. But the run from 22 October 18:00 follows a 12:00 file with pushes, so the panel cannot say whether it began during the 22 October incident, and the 23 October incident falls inside that run. Nothing read links any incident to the gaps. GitHub retired email notifications for push events on 15 October 2026, the first day of a long gap; that is a different feature and nothing links the two. GH Archive's issue tracker was unread (HTTP 403 from this session's proxy), so a report or explanation there would be unknown to this record.
- **A sample.** Four hours a day, 16 reference hours over eight months and eight chosen whole days. Gaps shorter than six hours fall between panel files; the whole days show they exist. The September baseline was not clean, so no threshold-based gap rate is reported against it; the January to August references are hour 12 only, so hour-of-day variation is not in that baseline.
- **Thresholds.** "Gap" here is zero events of a type; shortfalls (fewer, not none) are visible in the counts, such as the non-push types in mid-2026, but no rule for them was applied beyond the brief's.
- **Read once, at one time.** Files were stable over the half hour between the reviewer's reads and ours, and their Last-Modified dates show no rewriting, but a later backfill would change what a later reader sees.
- **The lead shaped the protocol.** The panel's hour 12 re-reads the scan; the whole days include the days the scan showed. Hours 0, 6 and 18, September and the references are new.
- **Ceiling amendment.** The byte ceiling was raised after outcomes were seen, from 9 to 20 GB; the run used 7.56 GB, so the original ceiling would in the end have sufficed. The sample was not changed.

## What this means for the studies and pieces that rely on GH Archive

These answer the brief's questions; none is yet a correction of any piece, which needs review.

- **LH002** (2026-04-02-14): the file is unchanged and held pushes, new branches and deletions; the April reference hours held all types, though pull request events were already fewer than in January. LH002's one-hour reading is not touched by the September to November gaps.
- **LH043** (2026-07-01-12): the file is unchanged. It held pushes, but it falls in the months when the other types had thinned to a few hundred an hour (425 pull request events), and no 2026 file read held any repository or tag creation. LH043's format check, that much of what the incident report describes would leave little trace in that record, is if anything understated (interpretation): the July record was thin in the event types such actions would produce.
- **The map, row I28** (articles/who-watches-the-internet.md, released): all 452 hourly files read were present, so "hourly" holds as a cadence for them; but whole event types were absent from most sampled hours since 15 October and from runs of hours before it, and the mix of types swung widely through 2026. A proposed "Later evidence" notice is in for-driver.md; after both reviews, a "Later evidence" notice dated 9 November 2026 was added to the piece (version 1.1).
- **articles/what-we-can-see.md** (released): it says GH Archive "records every public event on GitHub, hour by hour". As a description of the files read, it did not hold for long stretches of 2026: at 12:00 on 1 and 15 August the four control types were under 1 per cent of their count at 12:00 on 1 January (212 and 189 against 21,503), and from 1 September to 7 November pushes, new branches and deletions were absent from about a third of sampled hours (90 of 272, four a day), two thirds from 15 October (64 of 96). That GitHub had events of those kinds in those hours which the files lack is an interpretation, a strong one but untested here, since no independent count of GitHub's activity was read. The mix also swung the other way: the file for 00:00 on 5 September, without pushes, held 30,009 pull request events, against 11,989 at 12:00 on 1 January. After both reviews, a "Later evidence" notice dated 9 November 2026 was added to the piece (version 1.1).
- **essays/what-the-watch-would-see.md** (draft): its line that a push "lands in GH Archive's hourly file" did not hold for 64 of 96 sampled hours from 15 October, nor for the latest two files read (8 November 00:00 and 06:00).
- **Investigation question 4 and any later use**: a study that reads GH Archive for 2026 should first count what each hour it uses holds, by type and by minute, as here; hours with a type absent, or the non-push types thin, cannot support a reading that needs them.
- **P-0006**: a cohort completeness study would now have to choose hours where the relevant types are present at all; this record's files.csv says which sampled hours those are.
