## Changes, measure 3

### D-Link (rule panel; first statement)

The announcements route lists a new item: "D-Link Security Assessment and Response Regarding the PHP2Shell Critical Vulnerabilities", Publication ID SAP10524, dated 2026-11-10 GMT, linking `security/publication.aspx?name=SAP10524` on the same host. It was added to panel.json under D-Link's `advisories` with `found_at_read` 2026-11-12T1416Z and fetched once with `--found` (HTTP 200, 28,411 bytes; the gate found the host's robots.txt answering 404, no rules). From the next read on it is read at every read.

**What the statement says** (published 10 November 2026 02:13 GMT, last updated 02:31 GMT, "Resolved Status: Yes"): it names CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, "collectively referred to as 'PHP2Shell'". It says D-Link's "currently marketed networking hardware, including enterprise and consumer switches, routers, wireless access points (APs), and network cameras, does not use PHP components", so "these products are not affected", and that its management tools, companion software, mobile apps and backend API "do not incorporate PHP components associated with these vulnerabilities". It then says "certain legacy web frontend components of the mydlink portal cloud platform previously utilized a PHP-based architecture"; that D-Link has "completed emergency remediation and security protection measures for the relevant cloud platform" and deployed web application firewall rules "to detect and block currently known automated attack vectors"; and that it is replacing the portal's PHP components, with the new architecture "scheduled for completion and deployment by the end of November 2026". It does not name CVE-2026-115560, any product version, any PHP version or branch, or a fixed release.

**Exploitation:** "To date, D-Link has found no evidence of unauthorized data access resulting from exploitation of these vulnerabilities." That is not a statement that its products or services were exploited, nor that they were not attacked; it says nothing about attempts.

**D-Link's class: *affected, fixed for all listed*** (set at review, [R-0068](../../../notes/R-0068-lh037-dlink-class.md); this checkpoint first recorded *not affected* for products and apps, with the portal noted separately). The statement heads its hardware and its applications "Not Affected", but heads the mydlink section "mydlink Cloud Services Emergency Risk Mitigation Completed", under the same "Security Assessment of Products and Services". It never says any product or service is affected, and never uses the word "fixed". The brief's *not affected* needs a statement "that no product, or that named products, are not affected, with nothing affected"; D-Link withholds "not affected" from the portal and reports PHP components there and "emergency remediation" of them, so that condition is not met. The classes are per vendor, with no exception for a service the vendor runs, and the panel has counted one before: Cisco's ISE Portal Builder ("Fix deployed, no customer action required") is among the listed products behind Cisco's *affected, fixed for all listed*. For the portal D-Link says it "has completed emergency remediation and security protection measures", alongside web application firewall rules, and the page shows "Resolved Status: Yes", so the portal is read as fixed; the replacement of its PHP components is a further step D-Link says is scheduled. That *affected* here is a reading of D-Link's report of remediation, not D-Link's word, and it is a class under the brief, not a finding that anything was attacked or entered. D-Link moves from *no statement found* to a statement naming the CVEs, which is what the count above uses.

**Clocks.** Vendor's clock: published 10 November (20 days after PHP's releases of 21 October). Lighthouse's clock: the item appeared after 2026-11-09T14:29Z (the read without it) and at or before 2026-11-12T14:16Z. The statement postdates the baseline, so the feed limit in the brief does not apply.

**Why the collector's count missed it.** The announcements body counted 0 for "PHP", because the brief's measure is the whole word, case-sensitive, and the listing names the flaws only as "PHP2Shell". It was found because every body, matched or not, was compared with the read before. The statement itself counts 5 and names both CVEs. For a reviewer: the name also appears in the Fortinet and Google bodies as "PHP2Shell" and in the Cisco and Splunk bodies as "PHP2shell"; a route that names the flaws only by that name would be missed by the count alone.
