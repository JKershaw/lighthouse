# Replay X3: old-versions-new-pythons

Stage one. Read: the full piece, its short form, and the README's "Current focus". Nothing else.

## What a reader would believe, written before reading the record

**Principal finding, said back.** In one September 2026 week, a typical heavily used Python library had 37 per cent of its downloads on versions older than its month-old newest. The obvious reason, Pythons too old for the newer version, holds for boto3, aiobotocore and numpy but fails for 32 of 37: most old-version fetches there came from Pythons that could have taken something newer. Also, a new version's download share settles within two days, confirmed by a pre-set test on 28 of 32 libraries.

**Observed.** One week of the PyPI download log, by version and reported Python, for 37 of the top 50 libraries; the limits the 500 most downloaded declare; day-1, -2 and -30 shares for 355 earlier releases; and, noticed afterwards, 55 of boto3's 88 million downloads on 23 September from Python 3.9.

**Explained, and what caused what.** Three libraries: old Pythons account for most older downloads, though not the exact version fetched (boto3's mostly took versions older still). Four: other libraries' pins. Pandas and urllib3: undecided. The other thirty or so: old Pythons ruled out as main cause; declared limits neither in nor out; the pattern (uv, build-server flags) fits environments rebuilt from frozen lockfiles, which the log cannot count. So most of it is described, not explained.

**Limits noticed.** Downloads are fetches, not machines, so repeat-fetching build servers dominate; one week; only 500 libraries' declared limits, anything else looks like a lockfile; the reported Python is the installer's, not the target's; the boto3 story was seen after the counts, not pre-tested; the pin arithmetic admittedly strains for botocore and s3transfer.

## Stage two: the record against the reader's belief

Read: studies/LH012/ in full (record 0.2, brief, amendments 1 to 4, sources, data, scripts, review/), investigations/software-updates.md, programme.md's Next. Calculations, all post hoc: studies/LH012/review/replay_x3.py, output replay_x3.txt (sections A to G cited below).

### What the round establishes

The counts stand: R-0016 recomputed every table and re-read samples live, and my recomputations agree wherever they overlap. Established, as far as one week can: older-version fetches are a large, spread and old part of the log, and the part fetched by Pythons a newer version admits is itself old (replay_x3 F: median age 278 days over projects, 39 per cent over a year, only 19 per cent from the 90 days before the reference, so not the week-scale lag of pins moving); the fresh-install mechanism, an old Python landing on the last version that admits it, is ruled out as the main mechanism for 32 projects and fits numpy, botocore and s3transfer (edge shares 57, 51 and 67 per cent, python.csv) but not boto3 or aiobotocore (7 and 0.6); an exact pin explains pydantic-core cleanly and s3fs explains fsspec without strain (tight_pairs.csv, ratio 0.53); a new release has most of its month's share by day 2. So: yes, the measurements can be correct while the explanation is misread, in three places, all wording laid over sound classes rather than errors of counting: P counts which Python fetched, not what chose the version; the boto3 hold on botocore and s3transfer is largely the same fetches P counts; and "settles" is true inside a ten-point band that a near-universal upward drift fits within. The residual stays consistent with frozen lists and unread bounds. A fourth candidate the brief never named, fetches that are not installs (mirrors, scanners, old pip's backtracking), is argued against for boto3 by the spread by upload year (replay_x3 A: versions of 2024 to 2026 carry 88 per cent of the Python 3.9 fetches, 2014 to 2021 almost none, where a bulk scanner would put weight) and is not separable elsewhere. Two points against the brief: a symmetric band cannot see a one-sided drift; and the dependents rule keys on the latest and week-newest versions, so awscli, whose older versions pin botocore exactly, never entered B for botocore or s3transfer because its 1.46 line names only urllib3 (replay_x3 G: 5.1 million read fetches, 3.6 per cent of botocore's older, an unstrained hold; no class changes).

### Material claim problems

1. **The short form asserts the mechanism for boto3.** Passage (articles/short/old-versions-new-pythons.md): "The obvious guess is old machines: on a Python too old for the new version, the installer takes the last one that still runs there. For boto3, the most downloaded Python library, that fits." and its summary line "For boto3 ... old Pythons are most of the answer." Evidence: data/driver/driver_checks.txt (95.4 per cent of boto3's Python 3.9 fetches below 1.42.97, 4.6 at it); data/analysis/python.csv, boto3 edge share 0.067; replay_x3 A (372.8 million fetches on 2,100 versions, largest 4.6 per cent, median age 535 days). The installer-takes-the-last mechanism fits at most one in fourteen of them. Resolution: "For boto3 the machines are old: 55 of 88 million ... came from Python 3.9. But they were not taking the last boto3 that runs there; 95 in 100 fetched versions older still, chosen by something else." The full piece's opening "old Pythons were the main reason for three of the 37 libraries" should read "accounted for most of the older downloads of three", as its own later paragraph says.

2. **"Settles within two days and holds for the month."** Passages: full piece, summary and "What this does" ("That a new version's share settles within two days and holds for the month is now a tested result"); short form ("settles within two days and holds for the month"); README, Current focus, likewise. Evidence: data/analysis/firstday_releases.csv via replay_x3 C: the day-30 share is above the day-2 share for 339 of 355 releases (95.5 per cent), median +7.2 points, quartiles 3.6 to 10.2, 97 releases above +10 and one below −10; the day-2 share is a median 0.79 of day 30; boto3 44 of 99 above +10. The band was fixed before reading and the verdict stands; "holds" does not: the share keeps climbing through the month in nearly every release, LH008's pins moving over weeks seen from the download side. Resolution: "a new version has most of its month's share by day 2, about four fifths, and the rest comes slowly: on day 30 the share was higher than on day 2 in 95 of 100 releases, by a median of 7 points, within the ten-point band we fixed for 257 of 355 releases and 28 of 32 libraries."

3. **The two explanations overlap for botocore and s3transfer, and the piece presents them as disjoint.** Passages (full piece): "For three libraries, it is mostly machines on Pythons too old for the new version. For four, it is mostly other libraries' limits."; "For 32 of the 37 libraries that was less than half ... In the typical library it was between 12 and 18 per cent"; "botocore and s3transfer, held by versions of boto3 from before May 2026". Evidence: python.csv, botocore P 47.1 to 49.6, s3transfer 44.8 to 47.9, fsspec 44.5 to 46.0; bounds_by_dependent.csv, botocore held by boto3's older versions, 214 million fetches; replay_x3 B, three quarters of botocore's admitted-Python older fetches are also at or below 1.42.97; record, post hoc: Python 3.9 environments fetch boto3 7.5 times as often as botocore. The boto3 versions that hold botocore are the ones the Python 3.9 machines fetch, so the two classes describe largely the same fetches; a reader takes old Pythons as minor for botocore. Resolution: name botocore, s3transfer and fsspec with pandas and urllib3 as "close to half"; in "Libraries holding one another back", say the holding versions of boto3 are those old-Python machines fetch, so the chain ends where boto3's does.

4. **"One kind of machine, fetching again and again."** Passage (full piece, "What this does"): "one kind of machine, fetching again and again, can decide what the most downloaded library's numbers say"; record, Findings, post hoc: "one large population of Linux machines running Python 3.9 fetches boto3 again and again". Evidence: replay_x3 A, the Python 3.9 fetches sit on 2,100 boto3 versions, 463 to reach 80 per cent, largest 4.6 per cent, uploaded across 2022 to 2026; replay_x3 E, boto3's older fetches fall less at the weekend (0.85 of a weekday) than its newer ones (0.68). One population would sit on few versions; this is many independently pinned Python 3.9 environments, mostly not build servers: a kind, not a population. Modest for the piece, which calls the population question untested; large for the programme, below. Resolution: "machines of one kind, on hundreds of different pinned versions, can decide ...", and drop "one large population" from the record's interpretation.

### Readability

- Pandas: the wheel measure the piece never mentions (data/analysis/files.csv, 67.8 per cent of older downloads on Python-and-platform pairs no newer wheel serves; numpy 72.0) tips pandas to "mostly could not take it" if platforms count; one clause would do.
- "For the rest the log cannot say": for annotated-types, grpcio-status and platformdirs it says more (residual.csv: 52 to 67 per cent of older fetches accounted for by neither explanation even at the upper estimates), the firmest sign of something else.
- The pooled 46 per cent is 42 without boto3 and 39 without the boto3 family (replay_x3 D); a clause would tell the reader the pooled figure is not boto3's.
