## Answer

For some projects, not as a rule, by the brief's convention; and the new release's early downloads carry the build-server flag less often than the old release's on the same days, by about seven percentage points.

Of the 37 projects among the 50 most downloaded that released a new newest version between April and August 2026, 26 had most of their releases reach half of the project's downloads, counting that release or any later one, on the first or second day after release: 70.3 per cent, with a project-resampled 95 per cent interval of 54.1 to 83.8 per cent (derived measurement). The brief's convention calls 75 per cent or more "as a rule" and below 50 per cent "not as a rule", so 70.3 per cent reads "for some projects"; the interval includes 75 and excludes 50. The split is sharp rather than graded: 21 projects had every release reach half, 11 had none, and five were mixed. Where it happened it happened fast, usually on the first full day. Where it did not, the new release's share stayed flat at between 2 and 34 per cent (project medians) and, for ten of the eleven, never reached half within 30 days (derived measurement). Pooled over releases rather than projects, only 127 of 424 releases (30.0 per cent) reached half within two days, because the three projects that release almost daily, boto3, botocore and litellm, account for 252 releases and none of theirs did.

For one release a project a month (104 releases, all 37 projects), a median of 32.2 per cent of a new release's pip and uv downloads over its first three days carried the CI flag, against 40.7 per cent of the replaced release's on the same days (medians of project medians). Every project's median difference was below zero; the median of project medians was -6.6 percentage points (95 per cent interval -11.4 to -5.0), and 96 of 104 releases had the new release below the old. The flag's meaning was read from pip's and uv's own source: both send true when one of four CI variables is set and nothing otherwise, so it is a floor on builds, not a measure of them. LH005's one case, `mcp` 1.27.0, had 10.6 per cent of its downloads flagged against 48.6 per cent of the previous release's, a gap of 38 points, larger than any project's median here (at most 30); it was measured over 3 to 22 April and over all installers, not over days 0 to 2 and pip and uv, so the two are not the same measure (interpretation).

## Findings

**The frame** (observation; brief amendment 1, data/projects.csv, data/releases.csv). The 50 most downloaded projects in ClickPy for August 2026, less setuptools (rank 9) and pip (rank 44), are ranks 1 to 52. 424 versions of 37 of them were uploaded between April and August 2026, were not pre-releases, and became the project's newest version when uploaded. The other 13 released no such version and have no outcome. 324 releases were patch releases, 95 minor and 5 major; 310 were followed by another newest version within seven days. Day 0 had a median of 4.5 hours left after the first upload. No gap was found under the brief's rule on any day read, so every one of the 424 releases has a known outcome for days 0 to 2 (derived measurement; data/analysis/gaps.csv is empty).

**Most projects' releases reach half within two days; the rest plateau far below** (derived measurement; data/analysis/project_outcomes.csv, release_outcomes.csv, release_days.csv; figure below).

- Every release reached half within two days for 21 projects: pygments, filelock (21 releases), aiohttp, tqdm, urllib3, charset-normalizer, packaging, certifi, typing-inspection, python-dotenv, rpds-py, annotated-types, pyjwt, typing-extensions, anyio, idna, click, cffi, requests, platformdirs and pathspec.
- Most did for five more: starlette (9 of 11), uvicorn (14 of 16), pydantic (5 of 6), pydantic-core (5 of 7), cryptography (6 of 7).
- None did for eleven, with their median day-1 at-or-newer shares: aiobotocore 1.9 per cent (6 releases), boto3 7.4 (103), litellm 9.4 (46), grpcio-status 10.2 (6), fsspec 21.3 (3), botocore 23.4 (103), protobuf 24.6 (4), pandas 27.7 (3), pytest 29.3 (3), numpy 29.5 (5), s3transfer 33.9 (7). For these the day-2 share was within a few points of the day-1 share, and no release of ten of them reached half by day 30 (or by 27 September, the last day held, for the six releases of late August); litellm had 6 of 46 do so, the median at day 25.
- Across the 37 projects, the median of the projects' median day-1 shares was 57.6 per cent, quartiles 29.5 and 69.0. Of the 139 releases that reached half at all within 30 days, the median day was day 1, and 23 reached it on day 0.
- The share of projects, 26 of 37, is 70.3 per cent (95 per cent interval 54.1 to 83.8, 10,000 resamples of projects, seed 20260927). Six of the 37 projects contribute a single release each, and all six reached half.

**Pooled over releases the picture reverses, because of three projects** (derived measurement). 127 of the 424 releases (30.0 per cent) reached half within two days. boto3, botocore and litellm, which released on most working days, account for 252 releases and none of theirs reached half in two days. The brief's unit is the project, so the pooled figure is reported beside the per-project one and not in its place.

**Described, not tested: kind of release and release frequency** (derived measurement; data/analysis/described.csv). Pooled over releases: 5 of 5 major releases (2 projects), 55 of 95 minor (57.9 per cent, 33 projects) and 67 of 324 patch releases (20.7 per cent, 26 projects) reached half within two days; 72 of 114 releases not followed by another within seven days (63.2 per cent) and 55 of 310 that were (17.7 per cent). Both contrasts are dominated by the three daily releasers, whose releases are almost all patches followed by another within the week, so they describe those projects as much as the kinds of release; nothing here says why a release was taken.

**The CI flag's meaning, from the installers' source** (observation; data/installer_source_excerpts.txt; sources.md K3). pip 26.0.1 and 26.2.1 set `ci` to true when any of BUILD_BUILDID, BUILD_ID, CI or PIP_IS_CI is present in the environment and to None otherwise, "so as not to give the impression that pip knows it is not being run under CI". uv 0.11.2 and 0.12.8 copy the same four variables and send true or null. ClickPy stores null as 'false'. So for pip and uv, 'true' is "the installer saw a CI variable" and 'false' is "CI not detected by that installer": a build in a service that sets none of the four, or a container built outside CI, counts as not flagged. Of the 5,765,627,058 downloads read for the CI subsample, pip made 59.8 per cent and uv 38.4 per cent; the other 1.7 per cent (Poetry 1.0 per cent, no installer recorded 0.5 per cent, then requests, Nexus, Bazel and others) are "flag not known" and are left out of both sides (data/analysis/ci_installers.csv). That is under the brief's one-third limit, so the CI part stands. No row held ClickPy's 'unknown' value.

**A new release's early downloads are flagged CI less often than the old release's** (derived measurement; data/analysis/ci_releases.csv, ci_projects.csv).

- Per release: the new release's CI share of its pip and uv downloads on days 0 to 2, minus the replaced release's over the same days. 96 of the 104 releases were below zero.
- Per project: all 37 projects' median differences were below zero. The median of project medians was -6.6 percentage points (95 per cent interval -11.4 to -5.0); pooled over releases, the median was -6.5. The largest gaps were pathspec (-29.8, one release), pydantic-core (-29.7, three), rpds-py, platformdirs, pydantic and aiohttp (about -17 each). The smallest were the daily releasers, whose replaced release was usually a day old: botocore -0.3, boto3 -0.4 and litellm -0.8, with per-release differences of both signs.
- Levels: the median project's new release had 32.2 per cent of its pip and uv downloads flagged CI in its first three days, the replaced release 40.7 per cent; pooled over all 104 releases' downloads, 33.9 and 40.0 per cent.

Interpretation. About a third of a new release's early downloads come from environments that say they are CI, and the release it replaces is fetched a little more often by such environments on the same days. That fits builds that hold a version, through a lockfile or a pin, for a while after a release, and people and unflagged automation resolving afresh; the record holds nothing that separates these readings, and a difference in share is a description, not a cause.

**Checks on the counts** (derived measurement; data/analysis/crosscheck_pypistats.csv, summary.csv).

- Against pypistats's "with mirrors" totals, on days 1 to 3 after the first qualifying release of the first five projects in SHA-256 order (grpcio-status, pygments, filelock, pytest, fsspec): 15 days, none differing by more than 5 per cent; nine identical; the largest difference 1.6 per cent, pygments on 19 August, where pypistats was higher on all three days. The brief's inconclusive condition is not met.
- ClickPy's own two tables: on 4,249 project-days both read, the by-version table's daily total equalled the per-day table's on 4,098 and differed by at most 1.0 per cent on the other 151.
- Three releases are yanked now and kept, flagged: charset-normalizer 3.4.8 and grpcio-status 1.82.0, and pandas 3.0.4, whose at-or-newer share fell from 22.7 per cent on day 1 to 0.2 per cent on day 2. When each was yanked cannot be read.

## Method

The brief (brief.md) fixed the question, population, measures, missing-evidence rules and reporting convention on 27 September 2026, before any count. Amendment 1 recorded the project list, the releases, the CI subsample, the installers' CI rule and ClickPy's table definitions, and was committed and pushed at f833a4b before any per-version or daily count was read; it also fixed six readings where the brief left a choice (the gap rule's "all-project total" as the sum over the 50 projects; exact version matching; "most" as more than half; percentile intervals; the CI share pooled over days 0 to 2; the cross-check's projects and days). Nothing was changed after outcomes were seen.

Scripts, in order: collect_frame.py (ClickPy's limits, catalogue, August totals and coverage; PyPI JSON for the 50 projects), frame.py (releases, replaced versions, kinds, the subsample and the SHA-256 order, offline), collect_installers.py (pip's and uv's source distributions and the lines that set the flag), collect_counts.py (G01 daily totals, V daily downloads by version per project, C per-download CI reads per subsample release, X pypistats), analyse.py (every table in data/analysis/, offline) and draw_figure.py (uptake.svg). `sh studies/LH011/replay.sh` reruns frame.py, analyse.py and draw_figure.py over the retained files into a temporary directory, compares every output byte for byte, and checks the reads against the ceilings; it passed when this record was written.

The at-or-newer share on day d is the downloads that day of the release or any higher non-pre-release version listed by PyPI, over all the project's downloads that day, both from `pypi_downloads_per_day_by_version`; versions outside the numerator sets were summed server-side into one row a day. The CI reads took `pypi.pypi` for the project, days 0 to 2, and the two versions only. Projects were read in SHA-256 order of their names; every qualifying release and every subsample release was read, so the stopping condition was met without reaching a ceiling.

Reads: 152 ClickPy queries (one of them a probe of the server's limits run by hand before the logger existed, logged as such) reading 5,848,644,945 rows in all by ClickHouse's own statistics, 5,808,627,712 of them for the CI subsample, against ceilings of 600 queries and 60 billion rows; 54 PyPI JSON requests of 300; four source distributions of four; five pypistats requests of 30. Scratch space used was under 100 MB. This agent's own model spend is not measured within the task; the driver session can price it from the transcript.

## Limits

**A download is not an installation or a run.** Each row is a file PyPI served to a client that named itself; mirrors, proxies and caches install without being counted, and scanners and `pip download` count without installing (LH005). The log is aggregate and anonymous, and ClickPy is a copy of it, not the original.

**The flag is a floor on builds.** pip and uv flag CI only when one of four variables is set; builds without them, including most container builds outside a CI service, are counted as not flagged. The rule was read for four versions; ClickPy drops the installer version, so older pip and uv versions were assumed to follow it. The 1.7 per cent of downloads by other installers were left out of the CI share.

**A day, not an hour.** ClickPy keeps the date. Day 0 had a median of 4.5 hours left after the first upload, so day-0 shares are not comparable between releases; the outcome rests on days 1 and 2.

**The unit decides the headline.** Per project, 70 per cent; per release, 30 per cent. Six projects contribute one release each, all of which reached half, and three contribute 252 releases, none of which did. The interval, 54 to 84 per cent, spans the convention's 75 per cent line, so "for some projects" could read "as a rule" in a larger or different sample of projects.

**Population.** The 50 most downloaded projects in August 2026, less two installers, over five months: libraries depended on by very many others. Nothing here says how less downloaded projects behave, and 13 of the 50 released no qualifying version in the window. The numerator uses the versions PyPI lists now; a deleted version's downloads, if any, count only in the denominator. The gap rule's total is over the 50 projects, not over all of PyPI (amendment 1).

**Why the eleven plateau is not measured.** The record says which versions were fetched, not why. Several of the eleven are commonly held back by their dependents' version bounds (boto3, botocore, s3transfer and aiobotocore constrain one another, and fsspec is often pinned by its filesystem plugins), which would fit the flat shares; that is an interpretation from general knowledge of those packages, and no dependent's requirements were read.

**Not checked.** BigQuery itself; linehaul's parsing of the User-Agent; installers other than pip and uv; the hour of any download; whether any release was announced; weekday effects; any project outside the 50.
