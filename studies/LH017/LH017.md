## Answer

**P1.** 395 of the 469 pairs, 84.2 per cent (95 per cent interval 79.7 to 88.3), keep at least one GitHub Actions or GitLab CI job at the snapshot: **as a rule**, by the brief's convention (derived measurement). In the repository view the share is 81.5 per cent (77.3 to 85.5), the same label; 271 of the 332 repositories keep CI at one of their snapshots and 270 at every one. 5 more pairs keep only another CI system's files (counted, not classed), and 69 keep none.

**P2.** Of the 293 pairs whose CI the rules decide, 282, 96.2 per cent (93.4 to 98.6), have **some job that installs L's version from a timed file**: **as a rule** (derived measurement); 48 pairs are undetermined, 14.1 per cent of the pairs whose CI installs the project's requirements or is undetermined, below the brief's one fifth, so the label stands. By pin class: exact pin 93 of 97, 95.9 per cent (90.1 to 100), **as a rule**; both 92 of 92 (100 to 100), **as a rule**; lockfile 97 of 104, 93.3 per cent (87.5 to 97.7), **no label**: undetermined pairs are 31 of 135 of that class's frame, 23.0 per cent, above one fifth, and the two undetermined bounds read "as a rule" (94.8 per cent) and "for some pairs" (71.9 per cent). The repository view gives 96.0 per cent overall (93.1 to 98.4) and 91.0 per cent for lockfile pairs.

**P3.** Of the 188 pairs with a timed lockfile whose CI is decided for the lock, 164, 87.2 per cent (80.2 to 93.3), have **some job that installs L's version from the timed lockfile itself**: **as a rule** (derived measurement). Undetermined pairs are 41 of 229, 17.9 per cent, below one fifth; with every one counted as not reading the lock the share would be 71.6 per cent ("for some pairs"), and as reading it 89.5 per cent. The repository view gives 87.5 per cent (81.8 to 92.8). **Half of these rest on commands that could re-lock:** in 83 of the 164 pairs every job that reads the timed lock does so through `uv run` or `uv sync` without `--locked` or `--frozen` (or a bare `pipenv install`, `pdm install` or hatch-pip-compile), which keep the locked versions unless the project's own constraints exclude them (LH015's D4); counted as resolving afresh, as the brief's sensitivity does, the share is 81 of 161, 50.3 per cent (38.6 to 60.9).

**Beside the container recipes (described, not tested).** For the pairs with a timed lockfile that both studies decide, a container build read the timed lock in 40 and did not in 25 (LH015's `reads_T_lock`); of those 25, a CI job read it in 20. Of LH015's 38 pairs "not from the pinned file", CI reads the pin in 21 of the 24 this study decides; of the 7 pairs whose recipe left a timed lock unused, CI reads it in 4 of the 5 decided. Of the 222 pairs with no recipe, 133 are decided and CI reads the pin in 127; 59 keep neither a recipe nor CI. Counting both kinds of build, a container build or a CI job installs from the pinned file at 321 of the 469 pairs (68.4 per cent, 62.2 to 74.7), and at 94 of the 415 pairs decided in both studies (22.7 per cent, 17.8 to 27.9) neither does, 59 of them with no recipe and no CI.

**What the classes mean.** A class is a reading of a workflow's instructions: what a job would be told to install, from which file, in the working directory the rules fix. A workflow is a recipe for a run, not a record of one: nothing here shows that a job ran, how often, on which events, with a cache that made no download, or against which index. "Reads the pin" means that some job's install step takes L's version from a timed file, whatever other jobs do; whether a job installs L at all (extras, groups, markers, another package) is not resolved. The study shows that a road runs from the pin to installs of the kind the download log's CI flag marks, for almost every one of these projects that keeps CI; it does not show how many of the log's older downloads come down that road, nor whether older downloads are frozen lists rather than unread bounds or an installer's resolution walk.

## Findings

## Method

The brief's order was followed. scripts/frame.py copied LH015's frame after checking its hashes. collect.py cloned the 332 repositories blobless and anonymously, eight at a time, and none needed its retry. For each pair it verified the snapshot, listed the tree and read every GitHub workflow file and `.gitlab-ci.yml` with its local includes, the local composite actions a step uses, and every file a classed step calls or names, one level deep, with README and CONTRIBUTING files at the root for S5. scripts/extract.py turns each workflow into jobs and variants (matrix include and exclude as GitHub documents them, G2; reusable workflows with their callers' inputs; composite actions inlined one level) and each task runner into the install commands it runs (tox's `deps`, `constraints` and package install, or `uv sync` with tox-uv's lock runner; nox's `session.install` and install commands; hatch's environment; Makefile and justfile targets with their prerequisites; shell scripts' install lines). scripts/ci.py applies LH015's installer rules to each job's steps in order, with the checkout and working directory in place of a recipe's build context, and the brief's precedence and override rules. collect.py ran the classification against the live tree and recorded every existence query and every relevant line it read (data/tree_paths.csv, data/aux_lines.csv); data/ci_lines.csv keeps each job's install-relevant lines, and scripts/classify.py replays the classification offline from those records alone. Its rules pass 45 synthetic cases (scripts/test_classify.py). Every clone, file read and request is in data/read_log.csv.

The read stage was run six times while the script was corrected, each time from the same clones; the files it fetched were cached and fetched once. Two readings made after workflows were seen are amendments (amendments.md): a shell's literal readings of variables, script directories and file listings (amendment 1: 51 jobs in 18 pairs, one pair's class), and Poetry's `--lock` flag (amendment 2: 112 jobs in one repository, no pair's class); scripts/amendment_counts.py compares runs with each switched off (data/amendment_counts.txt). The other corrections implement rules the brief states and are listed at the end of amendments.md; the largest in effect were that a program a step runs which is not a repository file at the snapshot is not a repository script, and that wheels from an artefact a needed job builds install the project from its manifest. The routine sensitivity counts a reusable workflow's jobs as routine when a workflow that calls it runs on `push` or `pull_request`.

The writer judged the "to judge" jobs in pairs where they could change the pair's class or its reading for the lock, 261 of 367, from the stored lines and the files at the snapshot (three read again from the clones, logged), and from two documentation pages read for the purpose (hatch-pip-compile's README and Poetry's commands, logged). Each reading is one entry in scripts/judge.py, which replaces the job's "judge" effects and lets the brief's precedence give the class. The other 106 stay class 6, flagged not judged. The blind sample (data/review_sample.csv) was drawn by scripts/analyse.py before it computed any measure. analyse.py computes every measure, view, sensitivity and description on one set of 10,000 resamples of the 332 repositories read, drawn with `randrange` from one `random.Random(20260930)`, with LH011's percentile; it applies the one-fifth rule to each primary measure's own frame.

Ceilings used: 332 clone attempts (of 664); 1.64 GB of clones (6 GB at once, 8 GB in all); 3,723 file reads (20,000); 27 PyPI requests (60); 2 further documentation pages (10), in 3 attempts; clones and reads within 31 minutes (two hours). Clones never entered the repository.

## Limits

- A workflow is not a run. Nothing here says which jobs run, how often, with what cache or against which index; a job under an `if:` is read as if it runs (6 pairs read the pin only through such steps), and jobs are counted alike whether they test, lint, publish or run once a year.
- The CI flag reaches pip's and uv's installs on a CI service; Poetry's and PDM's downloads are not flagged (LH011), installs inside container builds see the runner's `CI` only if passed, and a cache hit makes no download. So "reads the pin" says a road exists, not that any flagged download came down it.
- Half of the lockfile reading rests on commands that keep the locked versions unless the project's constraints exclude them (83 of 164 pairs); whether any such run re-locked is not known.
- 48 pairs are undetermined, most through calls to another repository's reusable workflow, local actions nested two deep, scripts called two deep, and values only a run supplies; 106 jobs left by the script were not judged because they could not change a primary measure, which the descriptions count as undetermined. For lockfile pairs the undetermined share is above one fifth, so that share carries no label.
- Whether a job installs L at all is not resolved; "reads the pin" is about the file a step installs from.
- Scripts, Makefile targets, tox, nox and hatch configurations and local actions are read one level; Python programs a step runs are not read; other CI systems are counted, not classed.
- The judged jobs rest on the writer's readings, which the blind check samples; the amendments and corrections were made after workflows were seen, and the pair-class tallies were seen while they were made.
- The frame is the repositories LH008 to LH010 timed, pinned dependents of 31 widely used libraries; the interval describes variation across repositories like these, not sampling error within the frame.

## Next

- **What the flag carries.** The road exists for almost every pinned project with CI; its volume is the open part. LH013's weeks (from 13 October 2026) read, among other things, how much of a library's older downloads falls in one combination of reported fields; the CI flag's share of the older-version downloads at each project's reference release, set beside this study's pairs by library, would say whether the libraries whose pinned dependents read their pins in CI show more flagged older downloads. It would be a description across libraries, not a count of these projects' runs.
- **The census, with installs beside it.** A census of lockfiles in public repositories, when a reader of public code at that scale can be used, should read the install commands in CI workflows beside each lockfile, as here, and count the commands that could re-lock separately.
- **Runs.** Whether a job ran, and with what cache, is recorded only in run logs, which this study did not read; a study of runs would need GitHub's API, which a task must name.
