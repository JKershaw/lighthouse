# LH041: The registry worms, dated. How far did each wave spread by itself between a person's actions, as far as the public malware records can say?

**In short.** Since September 2025 a family of worms has spread through the npm and PyPI package registries by stealing publishing tokens and pushing poisoned versions of their victims' other packages; every report read says people write and launch them. We dated thirteen waves that security firms name, using OSV's public malicious-package records and npm's own publish times. In this first reading (checked once against its sources, not yet released), and with two choices made after the counts were seen, the two biggest waves reached many separate groups of maintainers within hours (Shai-Hulud 2.0: 88 groups in under 15 hours on 24 November 2025; ChainDrop: 13 groups in under 4 hours on 4 August 2026), while others stayed inside one group or reached a handful; the records cannot say whether a person acted between those publishes, so these are upper bounds on spread without a person. Reviews come next, and a second read in December.

## Answer

As far as the public records can say, and before release:

1. **Seven of the thirteen named waves are identified in OSV's records by the indicators the vendors print** (W02, W03, W06, W07, W11, W12, W13); two more only by date window (W01, Shai-Hulud of September 2025; and W08, Miasma, under a reading of the rule taken after the counts were seen); W04 and W05 (April 2026) and W10 (July 2026) are not identified as the brief defined, though OSV holds records for their named first victims; W09 (Hades, PyPI) appears by window only: under the brief's rule as one npm record, which cannot be part of a PyPI wave, and post hoc as 38 PyPI records with no registry times. (Derived measurement.)
2. **Spread between maintainer groups, an upper bound on spread without a person's new act** (derived measurement; post hoc view where marked):
   - **Shai-Hulud 2.0** (November 2025): 788 npm packages; the 1,029 malicious versions with a registry time all went up on 24 November 2025 between 03:10 and 17:54 UTC, across 88 maintainer clusters; 87 after the first, the median one 7.1 hours in (post hoc view; the brief's view gives the same cluster counts but a span polluted by versions outside the wave).
   - **ChainDrop** (August 2026): 444 packages and 2,236 versions in the wave's window, 2,127 registry-dated between 09:35 and 13:19 UTC on 4 August; the first cluster holds 11 packages, and 12 more clusters holding 416 packages followed within 3.7 hours; 17 packages have no packument (post hoc view). StepSecurity's own split of the same day is 11 carriers and 433 packages "republished automatically", 444 in all (w19): OSV's 444 packages equal its total and the first cluster's 11 equal its carriers, a comparison of counts, not of package lists.
   - **Shai-Hulud** (September 2025, by window only): 203 packages, 589 versions from 14 to 17 September, 17 clusters over 71 hours.
   - **TanStack wave** (May 2026, mostly by window): 13 clusters in 30.0 hours; the first is a single package that is not TanStack's, then TanStack's 42 packages at 19:20 UTC, then 11 more clusters.
   - **Miasma** (June 2026, post hoc only): 106 packages; the first cluster holds 32 packages from 10:54 UTC on 1 June, and 7 more clusters followed 60 to 87 hours later.
   - **AntV wave** (May 2026, by window): 316 of its 359 npm packages are one cluster published from 01:39 UTC on 19 May; one cluster of 6 packages followed in 1.2 hours and one of 37 nearly seven days later, so the brief's pre-set rule reads the wave as spread across clusters. One maintainer hash is on every one of the first cluster's 316 packages (29 hashes in all; the reviewer's count, R-0057), which fits Snyk's account of one compromised account publishing the packages it maintained (w10); that one account published them is Snyk's finding, not something these records show (interpretation), and the two later clusters may be window sweep-ins (Limits).
   - **MemTensor** and **Tensorlake** (September and October 2026): one cluster each, no further spread in the records, as their sources reported.
3. **Clocks.** Where a wave has registry times, OSV's first record followed the first malicious publish by 0.5 hours (Shai-Hulud 2.0) to 18 hours (Shai-Hulud), and the median version waited 4 to 12 hours for its record. The registry clock agrees with the vendor clock to the minute for the TanStack and AntV waves and Tensorlake, and matches StepSecurity's ChainDrop timeline to the second: keyv 6.0.0 at 09:35:00 UTC, then the first automated republish at 09:38:13, the time the brief took as the wave's first publish. PyPI's clock is wholly unknown: every one of the 85 malicious PyPI releases read is gone. (Observation and derived measurement.)
4. **Residue.** Across the waves as assigned, OSV holds 1,992 records and 5,126 malicious versions under the brief's rules, and 2,137 records and 5,550 versions in the post hoc view. That is about 1.2 to 1.3 per cent of the 163,919 npm malicious-package records OSV published from 1 September 2025: most malicious-package records are not these worms. (Derived measurement.)

What this cannot say: whether any person acted between publishes (sources describe operators acting by hand in several waves), who infected whom, installs or infections, or anything about packages OSV never recorded. No reading here is about AI activity: by every source, these worms are run by people, and "by itself" means software publishing without a person's new act, as the sources describe the mechanism.

## Method

As the brief fixed it, with these notes:

- **Collection.** One run of [scripts/collect.py](scripts/collect.py) with the label 2026-11-08T1910Z. Its first attempt stopped on a record whose `database_specific` is null, after the two exports had been downloaded and before anything else; the bug was fixed (null fields read as empty) and the run repeated on the same downloaded bytes with `--skip-download`; the export headers were then taken from a HEAD request whose MD5 matched the bytes (data/2026-11-08T1910Z/exports.json). Neither change touches the waves, indicators, windows or measures.
- **Retention.** Each selected OSV record is kept whole, gzipped (osv-selected.jsonl.gz); an index of every MAL- record (osv-mal-index.csv.gz); extracts of the npm packuments (publish times, current maintainers as salted hashes, dist-tags, version list, and for the record's surviving versions the hashed publisher and whether it has an attestation), with each body's sha256 in requests.csv; PyPI read statuses; the OpenSSF spot checks. The exports themselves (258 MB) are not kept; their sha256, size and GCS generation are. Raw packument bodies are not kept for size; the extract is the retained form.
- **Email addresses** are withheld from everything retained (the keeper, 8 November 2026): [scripts/redact_gz.py](scripts/redact_gz.py) applies scripts/redact_emails.py inside the gzipped files, and the collector now redacts before saving. Requests.csv keeps the hashes of the bytes as received. The vendor pages committed with the brief at 147e953 held addresses before this rule and were redacted in 1cccfb2; history keeps the earlier bytes.
- **Tabulation** was written after the read, from the brief: joins, per-wave counts, clocks, maintainer clusters (union of packages sharing any hashed current maintainer; packages with a 404 packument, no maintainers or only a `-security` placeholder version are "maintainers unknown"), seeds, cross-wave packages and the spot check. The two post hoc readings are a flag (`--posthoc`) writing to tables/posthoc/. The brief's reading rule ("spread across clusters" at two or more later clusters) is applied as written.
- **Replay.** `sh studies/LH041/replay.sh` re-derives both views and the figure from the retained files, offline.
- **Safety.** No package, tarball or payload was fetched, installed or run; records and packuments were parsed as JSON. Prose names packages only where a source names them, and no maintainer.
- **Spend.** Model spend for this subagent is not available from inside it; the driving session's `harbour/hb tokens --latest` prices the transcript.

## Limits

- **Clusters are today's maintainer lists.** Maintainers added or removed since a wave, and company accounts shared across packages, change the clusters. A cluster is not an account, a person or an organisation.
- **An upper bound, not a measurement.** A later cluster is a publish the sources' mechanism attributes to the worm; an operator holding stolen tokens could have made it by hand. Nothing in these records separates the two.
- **OSV's coverage.** OSV holds what its sources reported; ChainDrop's 444 packages equal StepSecurity's count at 18:10 UTC on 4 August, not Aikido's 868 or BleepingComputer's "more than 1,300" (w03, w19), and whether OSV's set came from one feed was not checked. A wave's count here is a floor of unknown depth.
- **Text indicators.** Joining depends on OSV's text naming what the vendors print. For W01, W04, W05, W08 and W10 it mostly did not; window joins, the weaker class, carry W01, W06, W07 and W08, and a window can sweep in an unrelated package (W06's first, W07's last cluster).
- **One record per package** merges waves that hit the same package, hides re-infection (no package joined two waves by construction), and lets out-of-wave versions into a wave's list.
- **PyPI is undated** (all malicious releases read are gone), so W09 and the PyPI parts of W03, W06, W07 and W12 have no registry clock.
- **84 npm packuments answered 404** (deleted packages), and their versions have no registry time.
- **Vendor counts and clocks** are the vendors'; three pages were refused (w05, w06, w08) and are unread, not negative.
- **Post hoc readings** (two) are labelled where used; the brief's view is reported beside them.
- **Reviewed once.** One evidence review (R-0057) re-derived the counts with its own code and re-read six vendor pages; a second agent's agreement is not independent confirmation, and the reader-and-inference review has not happened.
