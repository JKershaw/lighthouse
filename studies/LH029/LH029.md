**Study:** LH029, version 0.1, 23 October 2026. **Writer:** a Claude Code driving session (Claude Opus 5.5). **Protocol:** [brief](brief.md), commit `a41f896` (header time corrected in `7ef696d`), committed at 16:59:44 UTC and pushed by 16:59:50 UTC by the session's own log, before the read. **Observation:** one direct GET, 23 October 2026 17:00:11 to 17:00:12 UTC ([read log](data/snapshot-2026-10-23/read_log.json)). **Instruments:** Python 3 urllib, the reader LH028 uses; [offline replay](scripts/replay.py), `python3 studies/LH029/scripts/replay.py`. **Cites:** [sources](sources.md) S1 to S3. **Overlap:** the page is one of LH024's fixed daily selections (this read is not LH024's 23 October observation and fills none of its cells); S2 and S3 are earlier retained reads, reused; the notice's content was known before the read, from a web reader that kept no bytes (brief). **Spend:** one GET, 102,923 bytes retained; model spend in the close-out.

## Answer

PHP's own archive entry, dated 23 October with a `datetime` attribute of 10:12:28 UTC and first read directly by Lighthouse at 17:00 UTC, suggests that users set `session.upload_progress.enabled=0` "until the next regular bugfix release, to fully protect from an unlikely RCE vector" [S1]. It says the issue can occur only under "an unusual combination of configuration and application code": an application sets `session.serialize_handler` to `php_binary` or `php_serialize` in its configuration, switches it to `php` at runtime with `ini_set()` before `session_start()`, and leaves session upload progress enabled. Applications that never change the handler at runtime can keep the feature enabled. The setting is the one PHP listed on 21 October as a partial measure against CVE-2026-109619; those who applied it then should keep it in place, and need do nothing more. PHP will handle the issue in the regular release cycle and publish a full advisory for CVE-2026-115560 with the next release.

The entry names no affected version. By asking users to keep the setting until the next regular release in order "to fully protect", it implies that the current releases, including the 21 October fixed ones, do not close this path; that is our reading, not PHP's words. Cloudflare's revised page, which links to this entry, states it outright, "It affects fully patched PHP releases, including 8.5.12", and does not carry PHP's unusual-combination condition or the setting itself in its update paragraph [S3]. So for a reader of the 22 October account, "fixed" holds for the two emergency flaws and needs one qualification: PHP says a further, separately numbered, unlikely remote code execution path remains in the current releases for applications that switch the session serialise handler at runtime, until its next regular release. Nothing here counts how many applications do that.

## Limits

- **The clock.** The `datetime` attribute's meaning is not documented on the page. The five 21 October entries for the security advisory and releases carry values between 00:05 and 00:35 UTC, hours before the 13:30 UTC release reported by CERT-EU and the 14:00 UTC embargo lift reported by Amazon (LH024, LH028), and the RC4 entry was absent from a copy read after its value. The attribute is a time PHP attaches to the entry; the page does not say what it marks, and the evidence shows it is not when the text became public: the five 21 October entries carry 00:05 to 00:35 UTC against a 13:30 UTC release, the RC4 entry's 15:41 UTC preceded by fifteen hours a copy that lacked it, and the 20 October entry's 20:32 UTC is an hour after the 19:33 UTC Date of the php.announce message for the same notice (LH024). The interval this study can bound is from Lighthouse's side only: the entry was not in the 22 October 06:24 UTC copy and was in the 23 October 17:00 UTC copy; Cloudflare's page claims an edit at 15:09 UTC on 23 October and LH028 first saw that edit at 16:06 UTC.
- **A claim, not a measurement.** The conditions and the "unlikely" judgement are PHP's. This study observed no installation, application, configuration or exploit attempt, and cannot say how many applications switch the serialise handler at runtime, whether any are exposed, or whether the next release will close the path.
- **The implied scope.** Reading the entry as saying the current releases are affected is an inference from its wording; the full advisory, promised with the next release, has not been read.
- **One reader, one read.** A later revision of the entry needs a new dated read. The GitHub advisory was not read, by the brief's rule.
