## Answer

The study does not settle the question. Before the advisory, pinned dependents moved to announced fixes at a higher pooled rate than to silent ones under every clock and every restriction tried, but with the library as the unit the brief's classes cannot be told apart under any of them (only the strict class under a clock chosen after the fact reaches p < 0.05; "How many comparisons" below), and under the brief's own clock the pooled lead's uncertainty spans no difference. The top 500 projects gave 43 libraries with a qualifying event, not the 40 or more the design needed: 22 announced and 21 silent, of which 18 announced and 12 silent had pinned dependents to follow (observation; brief.md amendment 1, data/summary_by_class.csv). Their 209 repository and event pairs gave 142 moves by 27 September. Version 0.3 reanalysed the retained evidence after every outcome was known (outcome-aware and post hoc, not preregistered; Findings, "Reanalysis of retained evidence, version 0.3"); the first three bullets are version 0.2's figures, unchanged, with one interval added.

- **Pooled over pairs, by the brief's clock** (derived; data/hazard_by_class.csv). From the release to the event's advisory, 2.30 moves per 100 repository-days at risk for announced fixes (35 moves over 1,522.4 repository-days) against 1.57 for silent ones (20 over 1,273.4), a ratio of 1.46; LH009 had 2.36 against 1.36. A library-clustered bootstrap (10,000 resamples of libraries within each class, seed 20260927; added in version 0.3) puts the ratio's 95 per cent interval at 0.52 to 3.96. Leaving out any one library, the announced rate stays between 2.04 and 3.73 and the silent between 1.41 and 1.95. In days two to seven after the release, stopping at the advisory, 3.34 (11 moves) against 2.29 (5), ratio 1.46, interval 0.43 to 6.17.
- **With the library as the unit, by the brief's clock** (derived; data/library_unit.csv). The median of the libraries' own rates from the release to the advisory is 3.21 for the 18 announced libraries and 2.74 for the 12 silent ones; shuffling the class labels over the 30 libraries 10,000 times gives a difference at least that large, either way, in 89 per cent of shuffles (p = 0.89). In days two to seven the median announced library had no move (median 0.00 over 16 libraries, nine of them with none) against 1.46 for 8 silent libraries (p = 0.68).
- **Share before the advisory** (derived). 35 of 96 moves to announced fixes (36 per cent) and 20 of 46 to silent ones (43 per cent) came before the event's advisory.
- **The clock** (derived, version 0.3; data/reanalysis/clocks.csv and comparisons.csv). Taking as the clock the earliest GitHub-reviewed, unwithdrawn GHSA record in the retained OSV records that names the event's fixed release changes three events with pairs (nltk and pip, whose earliest such record came six days before their release, and tornado, one day after it) and reproduces the reviewer's hand computation in notes/R-0010.md: medians 3.26 (17 libraries) against 1.30 (11), p = 0.47; pooled 3.42 (27 moves over 788.5 repository-days) against 1.42 (18 over 1,270.4), ratio 2.42, interval 1.00 to 4.90, and 3.04 to 4.02 against 1.25 to 1.74 leaving one library out; 27 of 96 and 18 of 46 moves came before that clock. Taking also the NVD publication times those reviewed records carry, which came first for 17 events, widens the pooled lead further (4.32 on 23 moves against 1.22 on 14, ratio 3.53, interval 1.44 to 6.86) while the medians still do not separate (3.71 against 0.76, p = 0.47). Neither clock is the first warning anywhere: only these retained records were searched.
- **Notes that could be read** (derived, version 0.3; data/reanalysis/notes_evidence.csv). By a rule written before it was applied, 22 events' fixes show an announcement, 12 had notes that were read at release time and carry none, and 9 had no notes in any source read at release time; "silent" in version 0.2 covers both of the last two. Among the 30 libraries with pairs, 18 are announced, 8 silent with notes, and 4 silent without (litellm, mcp, pydantic-ai-slim, fastmcp). Keeping only fixes whose notes could be read drops those four libraries, 31 pairs and 8 of the silent class's 20 moves before the advisory: medians 3.21 against 2.47 (p = 0.87), pooled 2.30 against 1.41 (ratio 1.63, interval 0.47 to 4.90). With the earliest reviewed clock as well: medians 3.26 against 0.76 (p = 0.37), pooled 3.42 against 1.18 (ratio 2.91, interval 0.96 to 6.19).
- **Days two to seven** (derived). Under no clock or restriction do the classes separate in this window: the medians are 0.00 against 1.46 throughout (p = 0.64 to 0.68) and the pooled ratios 1.43 to 1.69, with intervals from about 0.3 to between 5.7 and 10.8.
- **Other readings** (derived; version 0.2's, and version 0.3's comparisons.csv). Security words only (strict class), by the brief's clock, the library medians are 3.71 against 0.76 (15 libraries each; p = 0.28). With idna's and aiohttp's security words, which concern an earlier release, not counted, the medians are 3.17 against 4.18 (p = 0.78) and the pooled rates 2.17 against 1.74; the pooled lead in days two to seven rests on aiohttp's three moves and turns to 2.61 against 3.30 without them. Within the four libraries where both an announced and a silent fix have followed dependents, the announced fix drew moves faster in three (cryptography, aiohttp, gitpython) and slower in one (pip), on 0 to 4 moves a side; under the earliest reviewed clock pip's silent fix has no time before a counted advisory, leaving three.
- **How many comparisons** (derived, version 0.3). The reanalysis makes 36 overlapping comparisons (three clocks, two case sets, three groupings, two windows). Two of the 36 permutation p values fall below 0.05, both for the strict class under the clock with NVD times (0.035, and 0.024 with the notes restriction); seven of the 36 pooled-ratio intervals exclude 1, all of them in the window from the release to the clock, under the earliest reviewed clock with the strict class or under the clock with NVD times. No correction for multiple comparisons was made, every one of these readings was chosen after the outcomes were known, and none is claimed as a finding.

What this says (interpretation, written after every result above was known). Pooled over pairs, moves before the advisory ran faster to announced fixes than to silent ones under every reading, and the pooled gap grows as the clock moves to earlier public records and as the silent fixes with no readable notes are set aside. But a pooled rate follows the libraries with the most waiting repository-days and the few quick small ones: under the brief's clock its interval spans no difference and under the earliest reviewed clock its lower end sits at no difference. With the library as the unit, the typical announced library cannot be told from the typical silent one under any reading, and in days two to seven, where LH009 found its difference, nothing separates. So the study neither shows that announced fixes draw pinned dependents sooner before the advisory nor that they do not: version 0.2's "not found with the library as the unit" is a non-detection by a test of little power (30 libraries, most with a handful of pairs and no move in a window), not evidence that the notes make no difference, and the pooled lead is consistent with a modest difference without establishing one. What the reanalysis adds is where the uncertainty lies: much of it is the clock (which public record counts as the advisory) and which fixes had notes anyone could read, and both move the pooled comparison further than the classification rule does. A move is a request in a repository, not an installation or a run, and no project here is said to have been exposed.

## Method

The brief (brief.md) was written before any list of projects, advisory, note, dependents list or repository was read for this study, with the library and event rule (LH008's widened to every severity and the top 500), LH009's classification rule as amended, the order, frame, kept and move rules, the measures, the reuse of LH008's and LH009's reads, and the ceilings. Then, in order: select_libraries.py (ClickPy, OSV, PyPI release lists; the events); classify.py (PyPI's description and the libraries' changelogs and tags for 94 fixes); date_head_entries.py (the one entry first written after its tag); the flaw-named judgements, made by this agent against each fix's advisory summaries and written to data/flaw_judgements.csv; classes.py (data/fix_classes.csv, last written 10:02:16 UTC); amendment 1, whose tables (data/fix_classes.csv and data/frame_defs.csv) were written before the first dependents list was requested at 10:03:13 and whose text was written after the dependents were read (brief.md was last written at 10:20:43; the file times are the working tree's, as the reviewer found them); osv_ranges.py (the frames and their advisories' ranges); collect.py frame, screen and moves (LH009's, with 20 packages a frame, a pair's own fix from the range that holds it, and the Co-authored-by names read in place); reuse.py (the twelve reused frames' first 20 packages, re-tested against LH010's advisory ranges, which kept every earlier kept package); analyse.py; summarise_reads.py. Every table in data/ is written by them; classes.py, reuse.py and analyse.py need no network. The clones are not kept.

Choices made where the brief left room: the library-unit windows are the two the brief names; each library's rate is its own pooled rate, not a mean of its pairs; the permutation counts a shuffle when its absolute difference of medians is at least the observed one (to 1e-12); a third grouping (incidental security words counted silent) is named in amendment 1, and analyse.py, which carries it, was last written at 10:05:01 UTC, after the dependents were listed and cloned and before data/moves.csv was written at 10:18:25, so on the file times it was fixed before any move was known; the reused frames keep their own end of observation (their clone times), so the pairs end between 07:24 and 10:05 UTC on 27 September.

Version 0.3 (27 September 2026) added a reanalysis of the retained evidence, outcome-aware and post hoc, by a research subagent that had read version 0.2, the review and the tables: scripts/reanalyse.py (no network, standard library only; it imports analyse.py's definitions) writes data/reanalysis/, and scripts/test_reanalyse.py checks its two rules on synthetic fixtures and its agreement with version 0.2. scripts/common.py gained an output directory (the `LH010_OUT` environment variable; without it every table is written to data/ as before) and no longer needs `requests` to be installed for the offline scripts. `sh studies/LH010/replay.sh` regenerates analyse.py's eight tables, classes.py's, reuse.py's and summarise_reads.py's into a temporary directory and compares them with data/ byte for byte (all identical on 27 September 2026), regenerates data/reanalysis/ the same way, and runs the checks; REPLAY.md says what it needs and what cannot be replayed (the collection, which needs the network and would read today's sources, and the moves, read from clones that were deleted). The clock rule and the notes-evidence rule are stated in Findings; the earliest GHSA or NVD clock was added during the reanalysis, after the retained records showed NVD times earlier than the GHSA records' own for several events, and before any comparison was computed with it.

## Limits

- **Too few libraries.** The design asked for 20 libraries a class with pinned dependents; the top 500 projects and five months of advisories gave 18 and 12. Many fixes followed their release before by days, and Open Source Insights lists few or no dependents for such a release, so the frame rule loses exactly the libraries that release often.
- **Low power.** Thirty libraries, most with 2 to 14 pairs and 0 to 5 moves in a window. A permutation p of 0.89 says the typical libraries could not be told apart here; it does not show that a modest real difference is absent. The brief ran no test of the pooled rates; version 0.3's library-clustered bootstrap intervals for their ratio are wide (0.52 to 3.96 by the brief's clock) and resample only 18 and 12 libraries, too few for a percentile interval to be more than a rough guide.
- **Outcome-aware reanalysis.** Every reading in version 0.3 (the two new clocks, the notes restriction, the bootstrap) was chosen after version 0.2's outcomes were known, and the reanalysis makes 36 overlapping comparisons with no correction; the few intervals and p values that exclude no difference are all under readings chosen after the fact. The notes-evidence rule was written after the retained texts had been read for it.
- **The judgement matters in places.** Four events are announced on the flaw named alone (mako, uv, authlib, mistune), two of them borderline; dropping the ground moves them to silent, where they contribute no move before the advisory, and the library medians part further (3.71 against 0.76, p = 0.28). The word rule itself counts security words about something else (idna, aiohttp 3.13.4); not counting them reverses the medians (3.17 against 4.18) and removes the pooled lead in days two to seven. Both are reported beside the class; neither is significant.
- **Silent in the sources read.** Eight silent events have no notes in the changelog at the tag, the description or the annotation, and a ninth (fastmcp) had notes only 64 days after its release; version 0.3 separates these nine (state c, insufficient accessible notes) from the twelve silent events whose notes were read and carry no announcement (state b). "Silent" in version 0.2 and in every table before the reanalysis covers both. GitHub's release pages were not requested, since LH009 found every one refused, and such projects often write their notes only there, so a c state is missing evidence, not silence. Security mailing lists, blogs and social media were not read.
- **Class is still partly library.** The within-library comparison has four usable libraries with 0 to 4 moves a side; it points the same way as the pooled rates in three and cannot separate class from library.
- **Short windows.** Where the advisory came within two days of the fix (cryptography, pip, anthropic, pypdf, pydantic-ai-slim and fastmcp; mako's an hour after), there is no days-two-to-seven window and the release-to-advisory rate stands on a few repository-days.
- **Advisories the window does not see, and which clock counts.** The rule takes the first advisory published in the window whose fix came a day or more before it; an earlier GitHub-reviewed advisory naming the same fix, published before 27 March or before the fix, is not the event's advisory. That happened for three libraries with pairs (nltk, tornado, pip; Findings), so nltk's 691 repository-days and four of tornado's five moves "before the advisory" came after a reviewed advisory for the same release, and nltk's weight in the pooled announced class is the rule's artefact. Version 0.3 computes the earliest reviewed record in the retained OSV records, and the earliest GHSA or NVD time, as clocks (data/reanalysis/clocks.csv); for 17 events an NVD time came before any reviewed record's publication, by up to 23 days among events with pairs (awscli), and one unreviewed record (starlette's) came earlier still. Neither clock is the first warning anywhere, and a record's fields are as read on 27 September, not as first published. The comparison before "the advisory" depends on which public record is taken as the advisory more than on any other choice tried here. A later study should fix the clock before reading outcomes, and search more than OSV for it.
- **Not independent of LH009.** 12 of the 43 events, ten of them among the 30 libraries with pairs, are LH008's releases with LH009's class and LH008's or LH009's frames, so LH010 is a widening of LH009, not a replication of it.
- **Frames cut to 20.** The reused frames lose the pairs beyond their first 20 packages in hash order that LH008 and LH009 read, so the pooled figures for those libraries differ from LH009's.
- **Three readings on one day.** LH008's frames were cloned at 07:24, LH009's at 08:49 and LH010's at 10:03 UTC on 27 September; each pair ends at its own clone time.
