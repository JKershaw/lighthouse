## Answer, at the first read

The inventory contains 751 PHP-related entries, including runtimes, extensions, application frameworks, tools and hosted products. It is broader than a count of vendors or products bundling PHP. It gives a status per entry for each of the two original flaws (Investigation, Vulnerable, Fix, Workaround or Not vuln, each defined in its README by the kind of source behind it) and, for nearly all entries (749 of 751), a link. At its latest commit it holds **751 entries**. For CVE-2026-109618: **109 Fix, 222 Vulnerable, 389 Investigation, 30 Not vuln** and one blank (PHP itself); CVE-2026-109619's counts differ by one or two. So by the list's account, two days after PHP's release, about one entry in seven had a recorded fix and about half were still being assessed. These are the list's statuses, not vendors' or customers' states.

## Findings

- **How the list grew.** 615 of the 751 identities surviving at the observed head first occur in one mainline commit at 11:20 UTC on 21 October, before PHP's release, mostly as "Investigation"; the rest were added in smaller batches over the next two days.
- **When fixes were recorded.** Of the 141 surviving identities ever recorded as Fix or Not vuln for either original CVE, the first such mainline commit is within 12 hours of PHP's release for 50 entries (13:30 UTC by CERT-EU, LH024), 18 between 12 and 24 hours, 1 between 24 and 36, and 72 between 36 and 48, chiefly in batch commits; none precedes that release clock. These are mainline committer timestamps, not observed public availability. First records already carrying a resolved label are left-censored; they do not date when a vendor fixed the issue. Fix and Not vuln are different source labels, combined only for this explicitly defined timing count.
- **Against the third party already read.** Palo Alto Networks' PAN-OS and Panorama are listed Vulnerable with fixed releases "not yet announced", as SOCRadar said. Cisco Expressway and Expressway Select are listed Vulnerable with "affected release range not specified", where SOCRadar named a fixed release; the two records differ, and neither vendor page has been read. Fortinet's entries are Not vuln or Investigation, where SOCRadar said investigating; the list has no Splunk entry at this commit, where SOCRadar said Splunk was investigating.
- **The related flaw.** The list's tables have status columns only for CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619; CVE-2026-115560 has no column at this commit.

## Measurement audit, 23 October

Fourteen authoritative JSON fragments contain 2,286 graph elements; they match the compiled inventory semantically. All 751 generated table identities, their two per-CVE statuses and version fields match the individual source packages. The source supplies an HTTP(S) link for 749 entries; 202 entries have a link explicitly typed `securityAdvisory`. These are link metadata counts, not 202 advisories verified by Lighthouse.

The original all-branches traversal treated merged side-branch trees as successive list states. First-parent reanalysis changes 117 first Fix/Not vuln clocks and 66 first-listed clocks, and changes the number of surviving identities with status transitions from 328 to 308. The original claim of one pre-release Not vuln record is withdrawn. Twelve links were missed by splitting escaped Markdown pipes; correcting the splitter changes 737 to 749, without changing the 751 entries or current CVE status totals. [The exact derived differences](review/replay-results.json) are retained.

A [late bounded advisory check](review/advisory-check-2026-10-23/findings.md) supports three selected entries through two readable first-party notices (Arch's two package entries share one page; BookStack's remedy is updating its hosting PHP runtime). AWS returned a placeholder. A case-sensitive selection error substituted a Bref issue for aaPanel; that unintended response is retained but unclassified, aaPanel remains unattempted, and no fifth GET was made. This check is incomplete and cannot validate past states or estimate the full list's accuracy.

## Limits

- The mainline commit clocks date edits incorporated along the captured first-parent history, often in batches. Git alone does not date a public push or first reader access; a later commit can record an earlier vendor statement. No commit-clock reversal occurs in this pinned mainline, but future reversals must be reviewed.
- 389 entries in "Investigation" are unknowns, not negatives; a product absent from the list is not an unaffected one.
- Supplier/product identity can change on rename. The history refers to recorded identities, not permanent real-world product identities. Multiple rows may scope vulnerable and fixed variants of the same product.
- The selected head is checked against the source JSON. Historical clocks follow generated Markdown along the mainline; a stale generated table could lag its source JSON edit. They describe that table history, not a reconstruction of every source-field change. Malformed rows and conflicting table identities now stop the analyser.
