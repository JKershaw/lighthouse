# How do fixes for the October 2026 PHP flaws reach products that embed PHP, as one national CERT's public list records them?

**Study:** LH032, version 0.1 (first read), 23 October 2026. **Writer:** a Claude Code driving session (Claude Opus 5.5). **Protocol:** [brief](brief.md), commit `5b4710b`, before the list was read. **Observation:** a full clone of `NCSC-NL/php2shell` at about 18:50 UTC on 23 October, head commit `2fdc314ded28` (committed 23 October 12:50:49 UTC), 120 commits from 21 October 10:21:41 UTC. **Instrument:** [analyse.py](scripts/analyse.py), which parses every software-list table at every commit; replay with `python3 studies/LH032/scripts/analyse.py <clone> 2026-10-23T1850Z` on a clone at that head. **Retained:** [summary](data/read-2026-10-23T1850Z/summary.json), [per-entry data](data/read-2026-10-23T1850Z/entries.json) and a copy of the list's Markdown files at the head commit with its EUPL licence ([list/](data/read-2026-10-23T1850Z/list/)).

## Answer, at the first read

The list is what CERT-EU described: a table of third-party products that embed PHP, with a status per product for each of the two original flaws (Investigation, Vulnerable, Fix, Workaround or Not vuln, each defined in its README by the kind of source behind it) and, for nearly all entries (737 of 751), a link. At its latest commit it holds **751 entries**. For CVE-2026-109618: **109 Fix, 222 Vulnerable, 389 Investigation, 30 Not vuln** and one blank (PHP itself); CVE-2026-109619's counts differ by one or two. So by the list's account, two days after PHP's release, about one product in seven had a recorded fix and about half were still being assessed. These are the list's statuses, not vendors' or customers' states.

## Findings

- **How the list grew.** 615 of the 751 entries arrived in one commit at 11:20 UTC on 21 October, before PHP's release, mostly as "Investigation"; the rest were added in smaller batches over the next two days.
- **When fixes were recorded.** Of the 141 entries the list has moved to Fix or Not vuln, it recorded 49 within 12 hours of PHP's release (13:30 UTC by CERT-EU, LH024), 18 between 12 and 24 hours, 1 between 24 and 36, and 72 between 36 and 48, chiefly in batch commits; one "Not vuln" was recorded at 13:01 UTC, before that release time. These intervals are the list's maintainers' clock.
- **Against the third party already read.** Palo Alto Networks' PAN-OS and Panorama are listed Vulnerable with fixed releases "not yet announced", as SOCRadar said. Cisco Expressway and Expressway Select are listed Vulnerable with "affected release range not specified", where SOCRadar named a fixed release; the two records differ, and neither vendor page has been read. Fortinet's entries are Not vuln or Investigation, where SOCRadar said investigating; the list has no Splunk entry at this commit, where SOCRadar said Splunk was investigating.
- **The related flaw.** The list's tables have status columns only for CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619; CVE-2026-115560 has no column at this commit.

## Limits

- The list's commit times date its maintainers' edits, often in batches, not vendors' releases; a later commit can record an earlier vendor statement.
- 389 entries in "Investigation" are unknowns, not negatives; a product absent from the list is not an unaffected one.
- The brief's sample check of up to five vendor advisories was not made at this first read; it is due at the next.
- Parsing reads Markdown tables; an entry whose row breaks the table format would be missed. The JSON fragments the README calls the source of truth were not parsed.
