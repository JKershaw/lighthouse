# LH061: What does published evidence measure about whether containment and recovery work, and what does it only assert?

**In short.** We asked how much of what's said about containing and recovering from internet incidents has actually been measured. For containment (taking down domains and botnets, sinkholing, removing or revoking leaked keys), six documents were read and included, and 18 of their 20 outcome claims were measured by the authors, mostly in the field, though the measures differ too much to add up and the results run in every direction. For recovery exercises, only two reports could be read and included, and neither measures anything: their twelve claims are participants' own answers or the organisers' statements, and three of the five named exercise series couldn't be read at all. This record is unreviewed; an evidence review, a reader review and a recheck come before anything is said elsewhere.

## Answer

**Part A, containment.** Six documents were included, and their authors' 20 outcome claims are 18 measured outcomes, 1 modelled and 1 asserted (derived, tally). Of the 18 measured claims, 16 are in the field and 2 in a laboratory; 2 rest on a security vendor's product telemetry; 10 rest on data that isn't public, 6 on partly public data and 2 on public data. What they report runs every way, each in its authors' terms (observations): lookups that stopped on the day of one botnet takedown and mixed results at two others (Nadji and others, CCS 2013); domains that stayed down about two years on average, more than half later released for sale (Alowaisheq and others, NDSS 2019); 81 per cent of leaked secrets not removed within 16 days (Meli and others, NDSS 2019); exposed credentials halved 14 days after notification, exposures rising again by day 172 while revocations kept falling (Demir and others, arXiv 2603.12498); one malware operation growing during a sinkhole operation and two falling after seizures (Ife and others, arXiv 2104.01631); and laboratory loss after a damaging database statement from none to 15 per cent by engine (Kaya and others, arXiv 2611.30483). The measures, outcomes, populations and windows (14 days to about six years) are too different to set side by side, so each is reported alone, as the brief directs (interpretation). Nothing here says whether any containment measure works in general or now.

**Part B, recovery exercises.** Inconclusive under the brief on two counts: fewer than three documents were included, and none of their claims is a measured outcome. The two included reports, SIFMA's Quantum Dawn VII after-action report and Public Safety Canada's Cyber-Physical Capstone after-action report, carry 12 outcome claims: 8 self-reported and 4 asserted (derived, tally). Neither gives a measured time to restore a service; Quantum Dawn VII says its design took in lessons from Quantum Dawn VI but reports no follow-up of their implementation, and the Capstone, a pilot, states none (observations). This is a result about the two reports read, not about exercise reporting in general, and an exercise's result is never evidence of real recovery. Three of the five named series (Cyber Storm, Cyber Europe, Locked Shields) couldn't be read at all, so the readable set may lean towards openly published reports (the brief's statement, applied).

## Method

The brief's eight queries (QA1 to QA5, QB1 to QB3) were run once each, verbatim and in order, on 25 November 2026; none errored, none was reworded or rerun (searches.md). QA5 returned only public-health results, so isolation and quarantine are **not found by this search**, not untested. Candidates were screened on the title and URL alone, since the tool showed no venue or year. Part A had 9 candidates (7 known documents and 2 from searches); Part B had 11 slots (five named series by two editions, plus one admitted series, Public Safety Canada's Cyber-Physical exercise, the first other series in QB1's results). No other QB result met the admission rule.

Each document was read by one gated one-off GET through scripts/lh_fetch.py, with the robots gate applied before the request and any redirect. From fetchlog.csv (derived, tally): 13 document GETs were made (Part A 8, Part B 5), 10 answered HTTP 200 and 3 met walls (A04 with HTTP 301; B01 and B06 with HTTP 403); 3 documents were refused by robots.txt before any request (B02, B03, B08); 7 of the 20 GETs were unused. Five locator rows were logged: three requests with a status (two 200, one 403), one refused before any request (ENISA) and one request whose redirect to a disallowed path wasn't followed (Bank of England), within the brief's eight. No body was kept: no read document but A07 states a licence allowing redistribution, and A07's body wasn't kept because its PDF's compressed streams can hold email addresses the redaction can't reach. Each read document has a derived file with its URL, read time, status, SHA-256, the facts used and short quotations. Inclusion was decided on the document read, by each part's three conditions, and each claim was classed once, with setting and vendor labels, by the reading subagent; the classes have not yet been checked by anyone else.

**The stop.** The brief stops reading when the eight searches are run and the twenty GETs are spent or both candidate lists are exhausted, or at the end of 15 December 2026. The eight searches are run. Both lists are exhausted in the sense the brief allows: every candidate was read, walled, refused by robots.txt, or not locatable within the rules (A06 had no URL and the brief allows locator reads only for exercise series; SIMEX's and Locked Shields' one locator read each was spent without finding a report). The order of reading never bound, since neither part's list outran its GETs. I checked whether any candidate remains readable within the seven unused GETs before 15 December, against the brief and fetchlog.csv, and under the brief as the reader applied it none does: the brief allows a retry only on a timeout, connection error or HTTP 500, 502 or 504, so A04's 301 and the two 403s stand as walls; the robots refusals at ENISA and at Quantum Dawn VIII's host can't be read around, and fetching the same Quantum Dawn VIII file name from SIFMA's main host would route around a refusal; and the brief counts a GET against a document read and then excluded. So the stop is met, and this record is version 0.1, not an interim record (derived from the brief and fetch log; one reading flagged below).

One reading could change that. A03's GET returned a project page rather than the paper, and the brief's exclusion list names "a wall, refusal or error" for full text unavailable. If the evidence review reads the brief as allowing one GET of A03's full text, a candidate would remain readable before 15 December, and this record should then be relabelled interim until that read is made or the date passes.

**Safety conditions as applied.** The research safety assessment's narrowing ([the keeper's decision of 24 November 2026](../../notes/keeper/2026-11-24-defensive-side.md)) was followed in the reads and in this record: outcomes and durations only; where authors give reasons a measure failed or an effect faded (A01-2, A05-2, A07-3, A09-1, A09-2), the record says only that they give reasons and tallies none; no host, domain, credential or indicator was enumerated, resolved or visited, and no dataset, sinkhole or dashboard was fetched; no participant, operator or system is named beyond a report's own level (A01-5's "one technology company", B11-8's unnamed organisations, A07's organisations described generally); A07-4 names providers as its authors do; no dependency map is compiled; A08 is recorded only for recoverability; the technical package the Capstone page mentions was not used; nobody was contacted; robots.txt was obeyed under the one-off rule; email addresses are withheld.

## Limits

- One run of eight fixed queries, with a search tool that showed no venue or year, is a narrow window on the literature; what it didn't surface is not found by this search, not absent (the brief's rule).
- Part A's documents are few and varied: three peer-reviewed papers from 2013 and 2019 and three arXiv texts from 2021 and 2026, two of which state peer-reviewed publication. Their observation windows run from 14 days to about six years, from 2011 to 2026; most of their data aren't public, and the three vendor-based claims rest on two documents' vendor relationships.
- The reads are one agent's, on one day, and no body is kept. Classes, directions and paraphrases are the reading subagent's and are unreviewed; the evidence review needs the derived quotations or a gated reread of the same URLs.
- Part B rests on two reports, both of 2023 exercises, both inconclusive by the brief's own conditions. Three named series were unread and SIMEX's only readable page was excluded, so the brief's statement applies: the readable set may lean towards openly published reports. Walls and refusals among document reads numbered six, at the brief's threshold but not more than it.
- Self-reported and asserted claims in Part B are about plans, perceptions and organisers' conclusions; they say nothing about how quickly anything was restored, in an exercise or in reality.
- The derived files' "body_removed" text is a fixed sentence giving robots or page reservation as the reason, while their "body_kept" field and fetchlog.csv give the absence of a redistribution licence; and fetchlog.csv's body_kept column reads "pending (keep.py)" for the three walls, where nothing beyond a short error page was received. Neither changes a figure; both are for the evidence review.
- Nothing here says whether any containment measure works in general or now, whether any organisation could recover from a real incident, why a measure did or didn't work beyond what its authors measured, or anything about containing AI activity beyond A08's laboratory outcome.
