# LH061 source register

**In short.** This is the list of every document the study's searches and named series put forward, and what happened to each when we tried to read it, as of the search-and-read row of 25 November 2026 and the read of one more paper on 26 November 2026. For containment (Part A), seven documents were read in full and included: six peer-reviewed papers or preprints that say they were published, plus one preprint; six measure outcomes in the field and one, a laboratory test of recovery that the brief named for this part, is the study's only measured recovery outcome. Part A admitted only documents from its listed venues that report an outcome, with vendors' pages and blogs as leads only, so most of its claims being measured follows largely from that rule and isn't a finding about containment claims in general. For recovery exercises (Part B), two organisers' after-action reports were included, and neither reports a measured outcome: their claims are participants' own answers or the organisers' statements, so the gap between the parts is partly one of source type. Three of the five named exercise series gave nothing to read: two were walled or refused, and for the third we found no public report, so the readable set may lean towards openly published reports. An evidence review (R-0158) and a reader review (R-0160), both of 26 November 2026, have been applied; a fresh recheck of what their resolutions wrote, and any line on the investigation page, come later, as the brief sets out.

Compiled by a reading subagent in Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5) on 25 November 2026, from searches.md, fetchlog.csv, derived/ and claims.csv in this folder; revised on 26 November 2026 by a Claude Code subagent on Claude Opus 5.5 after the evidence review (notes/R-0158.md), which added A03's read and corrected the entries marked so; and again on 26 November 2026 by a Claude Code subagent on Claude Opus 5.5 after the reader-and-inference review (notes/R-0160.md), which changed the In short and relabelled A01's claims vendor under the rule stated in the record's Method. Every read was one gated one-off GET through scripts/lh_fetch.py (access 'oneoff', redirects checked, provider anthropic). No body is kept: each document read has a derived file in derived/ holding its URL, read time, status, SHA-256, the facts used and short quotations. The one document with an open licence (A07, CC BY 4.0) is a PDF whose compressed streams hold author email addresses that the redaction can't reach, so its body was not kept either. Nothing from LH060's register overlaps (its register.md and searches.md, read on 25 November, name none of these documents).

## Part A, included

**A03. Kim, Kwon, Kozák, Gates and Dumitraș, "The Broken Shield: Measuring Revocation Effectiveness in the Windows Code-Signing PKI".** USENIX Security 2018, peer reviewed (venue as the authors' project page names it; the PDF is the authors' copy). Read 26 November 2026 at http://users.umiacs.umd.edu/~tdumitra/papers/USENIX-SECURITY-2018a.pdf (the project page's "Paper" link; final URL under the same host; HTTP 200, 365,567 bytes, sha256 d6216cd4...98f73079). Search result QA3 result 9 had led to the project page on 25 November 2026, when it was excluded as full text unavailable; the evidence review (R-0158, M1) ruled that exclusion had no ground in the brief. Because no link was retained, the project page was fetched once more on 26 November (same bytes as on 25 November) to find the paper's URL. Measure: certificate authorities' revocation of code-signing certificates that were compromised or used to sign malware. Population and unit: a corpus of 145,582 code-signing certificates (most from a security vendor's internal sample repository, the rest from public prior datasets), 215 revocation lists, and the vendor's customer telemetry for signed samples; the certificate. Outcome: delay from first detection of signed malware to revocation, whether revocation ended trust in malware signed earlier, whether revocations stayed on the lists, and whether revocation reached clients. Window: revocation lists monitored 16 April to 10 September 2017. Data public: partly (the authors say they released three of their seven datasets; not checked, and nothing was fetched). Funder: the US National Science Foundation and the Department of Defense, partly, as stated. Interest: one author at the vendor's research laboratory. Claims A03-1 to A03-4, measured, field; A03-1, A03-2 and A03-4 vendor. The paper's text and summary table give different counts for A03-2 (45 certificates and 250 samples; 62 and 402), not reconciled. The paper also reports problems in how revocation information is published, by certificate authority; under the brief these aren't restated and no authority is named. Derived: derived/A03-kim-usenix2018-paper.json (and derived/A03-kim-usenix2018-page.json for the page read). Licence: none stated, so no body kept.

**A01. Alowaisheq, Wang, Alrwais, Liao, Wang, Alowaisheq, Mi, Tang and Liu, "Cracking the Wall of Confinement: Understanding and Analyzing Malicious Domain Take-downs".** NDSS Symposium 2019, peer reviewed (24 to 27 February 2019). Read 25 November 2026 at https://cse.buffalo.edu/faculty/xmi/files/ndss19_domain_takedown.pdf (an author's copy; QA2 result 1; known document). Measure: domain takedown by sinkholing or delisting. Population and unit: 625,692 seized domains (608,557 sinkholed, 21,757 delisted), the domain. Outcome: how long domains stayed taken down, how many were released for purchase again, re-registration and confirmed reuse. Window: passive DNS over about six years to early 2018; registrar availability queried twice weekly from October 2017 to May 2018. Data public: no (commercial passive DNS, WHOIS from an industry partner). Funder: US National Science Foundation grants, as stated. Claims A01-1 to A01-5, all measured, field, vendor (from 26 November 2026, after R-0160: the domains' takedown status and dates rest in part on commercial passive DNS, a company's data product, under the vendor rule in the record's Method). The paper also reports weaknesses in how some takedowns were run; under the brief these aren't restated. Derived: derived/A01-alowaisheq-ndss2019.json.

**A02. Meli, McNiece and Reaves, "How Bad Can It Git? Characterizing Secret Leakage in Public GitHub Repositories".** NDSS Symposium 2019, peer reviewed. Read 25 November 2026 at https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/ndss2019_04B-3_Meli_paper.pdf (the symposium's copy; known document; the URL was the reader's, not a search result's, and answered 200). Measure: the repository owner's removal of a leaked secret, which the study counts as taking content down; it doesn't measure providers' revocation, as the gate recalled. Inclusion is a judgement for review. Population: secrets found through GitHub's Search API and monitored from 4 April 2018 (no total count in the section read; the paper says the 19 per cent "amounts of tens of thousands of secrets", per R-0158's reread). Outcome: share removed over time, and whether removal ended exposure. Window: hourly for a day, then daily to 16 days. Data public: inputs public, derived data not stated. Claims A02-1 and A02-2, measured, field. Derived: derived/A02-meli-ndss2019.json.

**A05. Nadji, Antonakakis, Perdisci, Dagon and Lee, "Beheading Hydras: Performing Effective Botnet Takedowns".** ACM CCS 2013, peer reviewed (4 to 8 November 2013). Read 25 November 2026 at https://coeus.ece.gatech.edu/articles/rza-ccs2013.pdf (QA1 result 4; known document). Measure: botnet takedowns by domain seizure and sinkholing, three studied after the fact, and a recommendation system. Population: aggregate daily successful DNS lookups at one large North American ISP; 45 active botnets for the recommendations. Window: 14 days around takedowns of 26 September 2011, 25 March 2012 and 11 September 2012. Data public: no: the experiments ran on a security company's internal passive DNS database built from the ISP's resolutions, as the paper says (R-0158's reread). Interest: one author's employer was that company, which also tracked the 45 botnets. Claims A05-1, A05-3 and A05-4 measured, field, vendor (from 26 November 2026, after R-0158 S2); A05-2 asserted; A05-5 modelled, laboratory (after R-0158 S1), vendor. Derived: derived/A05-nadji-ccs2013.json, left as read on 25 November 2026; its paraphrase of the second takedown ("lookups to sinkholed domains dropped") is superseded by claims.csv's A05-3, corrected after R-0158 M3.

**A07. Demir, Vekaria, Smaragdakis and Durumeric, "Keys on Doormats: Exposed API Credentials on the Web".** arXiv 2603.12498, v3 of 14 September 2026 (screened as a preprint on search metadata; the v3 text states publication in the proceedings of ACM CCS 2026).
