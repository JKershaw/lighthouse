## Answer

Yes, in part: a published container image is a public record of installed state, and it reaches past the download to the installation, though not to a run. Inside each image's layers the installer's own record, a `site-packages/mcp-<version>.dist-info` directory, says which version of `mcp` was installed, and the image's configuration says when it was built. For the images read here that record could be read anonymously from the registry without pulling the image, by streaming the image's upper layers and stopping at the directory, between 25 and 211 MB of compressed data per image (observation; data/image_mcp.csv).

The population is thin. Of the 37 packages in the frame (LH002's ten projects and the 34 direct dependents of `mcp` 1.27.0 that Open Source Insights lists), 7 name a published image of their own, and 6 of those could be read (observation; data/candidates.csv). In those six, 38 images were read to a result: 37 held an installed `mcp` and one, jesse's, held none (derived; data/image_timeline.csv). Three things came out of them that neither the download log nor git shows:

- **The installed version follows how the image is built, not the project's pin alone.** agentcrew's repository locked `mcp` 1.26.0 until 24 April, and LH002 counted it among the dependents that did not move. Its images held 1.27.0 from 8 April, 5.5 days after the release, because its Dockerfile installs from an open range (`mcp>=1.24.0`) rather than the lockfile. serena pins exactly and mcp-snowflake-server installs from its lockfile, and their images moved only when the pin did: serena 2.9 days after its git pin and 24.9 days after the release, mcp-snowflake-server 11 minutes after its pin and 34.1 days after the release. keboola's package pins exactly and it has no public repository; its images moved between two builds on 17 April, 14.9 days after the release, in a build of the package version whose PyPI release three days later is the first to require 1.27.0, so its move with its pin is inferred, not seen. octobot's images still held 1.26.0 in its next image, on 30 July (observation for versions and times; derived for the lags; interpretation for the cause).
- **The same version, even the same tag, can hold different installations.** agentcrew 0.12.3 exists as two images: one built by its CI at 04:37 UTC on 2 April, ten hours before the release, holding 1.26.0; and one built on 8 April and pushed under the tag `v0.12.3`, which the CI's rules had already given to the first, holding 1.27.0 (observation; the tag's history is inference from the workflow). keboola has built 24 commits twice since May 2025, under two tag prefixes, and each time its `sha-<commit>` tag now names the later build (derived; data/keboola_double_builds.csv).
- **A registry keeps what a tag used to point at only by accident.** Neither Docker Hub's nor ghcr.io's anonymous interfaces list the earlier digests of a tag. An earlier build stays visible only if another tag still names it, as agentcrew's first 0.12.3 build does; builds that only ever carried a moving tag, such as serena's `main`, leave nothing listed (observation of every field returned; derived).

The limits are as sharp. An image is an installation made to be run; nothing in a registry says whether, where or how often it ran, and a pull count or last-pulled time counts requests, including sweeps by some client that pulled 612 of keboola's tags on one day. Docker Hub's anonymous allowance, served here as 100 pulls an hour shared across the proxy's addresses, was exhausted by other traffic from 18:43:57 to at least 18:55:26 UTC, and with the study's own budget of 30 pulls it bounded what could be read (observation).

- *Exact pins and lockfiles held the images back* (observation; interpretation). serena installs with `uv pip install -r pyproject.toml -e .` against `mcp==1.26.0`, then `==1.27.0`; its 1.0.0 image, built on 3 April, a day after the release, holds 1.26.0. keboola's images and PyPI releases pin exactly. mcp-snowflake-server's images held 1.14.0 while its `pyproject.toml` asked for `mcp>=1.0.0`, which is what its `uv.lock` held from at least 21 April, the first lockfile commit in the span read; the image history shows a virtual environment copied from a build stage, and the Dockerfile committed on 6 May runs `uv sync --frozen`. ## Method

The brief (brief.md) was written before any candidate was searched or any image read, and amended before any Docker Hub manifest or layer was read, when the tag listing showed Docker Hub's allowance to be hourly and shared (brief.md, amendment).

1. **Selection** (scripts/select_candidates.py, scripts/classify_candidates.py). The frame from Open Source Insights' dependents endpoint and LH002's cohort; each package's repository by LH002's rule; PyPI's JSON for the listed and the latest version; a blobless clone of each repository, and from it only Dockerfiles, compose files, CI workflows, root Markdown files, READMEs and MCP registry manifests at two commits. Every matching line is in data/candidate_hits.csv; the decision and its reason for each package in data/candidates.csv. The decisions were made by reading those lines and, where needed, the named file, before any registry was read. One decision goes beyond the letter of the rule and is stated there: hal0ai names published images but they are not images of its package.
2. **Tags** (scripts/collect_tags.py). Every tag from the registry's `tags/list`, and on Docker Hub every tag record from the hub API.
3. **Images** (scripts/collect_images.py, scripts/registry.py, scripts/plan_layers.py). One row per distinct image; for ghcr.io, a HEAD and a GET of every tag's manifest and configuration. The images whose installed `mcp` was read were chosen by the priority order of the amendment (data/layer_plan.csv, with each image's tier and reason): for each Docker Hub repository the last image before the release, the first after it, the last in the window and the last in the period; then the nearest outside the period on each side and the first in it; then images midway between two read images whose `mcp` differed. All twelve serena images in scope were read. Each chosen image's linux/amd64 manifest was fetched by digest (on Docker Hub, one counted pull), then its configuration, then its layers from the top down, skipping base-image layers, each streamed, gunzipped and walked as tar entries until an entry under `site-packages/mcp-<version>.dist-info/` appeared (data/layer_reads.csv, one row per layer with the bytes read and why the read ended).
4. **Pins** (scripts/collect_pins.py). For each qualifying project, every first-parent commit from 1 January to 30 June 2026 that touched a manifest or lockfile, with the `mcp` requirement and locked version after it, and `requires_dist` of every PyPI release in the same span.
5. **Analysis** (scripts/analyse.py, scripts/draw_figure.py). The `reading` column of data/image_timeline.csv is the classification used: an `mcp` dist-info found; no `mcp` in any layer read to the end; not determined because a byte cap stopped the read; or configuration only. In data/image_mcp.csv, `method` says "no dist-info; requirements or lock file seen" wherever a lock or requirements file was seen on the way without an `mcp` dist-info, including files in installer caches; none of them pinned `mcp`, and the classification does not use them (version 0.1 called this "lockfile only"). Build times are the configuration's `created`, normalised to UTC; lags are derived from them; LH005's share is looked up by UTC day. data/context_reads.csv holds the Docker Hub search count, the repository records and the referrers read (scripts/context_reads.py).

Running the scripts in that order, with `LH006_SCRATCH` set to a scratch directory outside the repository, reproduces data/*.csv and images.svg, subject to the registries' state at the time and to Docker Hub's allowance. Manifests and configurations are cached in the scratch directory so that a rerun does not repeat a counted pull; layers are never written to disk. The documentation pages in sources.md were fetched with curl and are not in data/read_log.csv; their read times are in sources.md.

## Limits

- **Docker Hub could not be read in full.** Of 109 Docker Hub images in scope (in the period or nearest outside it), 30 had their manifest and configuration read, 28 had layers read and 25 gave an installed `mcp` version; 79 were not read (data/images.csv, data/image_configs.csv, data/image_mcp.csv). The allowance served was 100 manifest GETs an hour per egress address, shared with other traffic: 8 remained at 18:37:46 (a HEAD made with curl before the scripts' logging began, recorded here only), 18 at 18:40:19, none from 18:43:57, HEAD itself answering 429 until at least 18:55:26, and 91 at 19:02:22. This study made exactly 30 counted pulls, its amended budget. So the first image with 1.27.0 is bracketed, not pinned, for agentcrew's CI builds (its first CI image after the release, 0.12.4 on 12 April, was not read) and for keboola (one image unread in three and a half hours).
- **Byte caps cut two repositories short.** agentcrew's installed environment sits in a 500 MB layer in which `mcp` appears after about 110 MB, and jesse's first image had to be read to the end (786 MB) to show it held no `mcp`. Both reached the 800 MB per-repository cap: agentcrew's 0.11.1 image and jesse's 1.13.9 are recorded as "not determined", jesse's 1.13.11 and 2.1.3 as configuration only. jesse 2.1.3, whose PyPI release requires `mcp==1.26.0`, would have been the one jesse image expected to hold it. The total, 2,960,812,225 bytes, stayed under the 3 GB cap.
- **One environment per image.** A read stops at the first `mcp` dist-info seen from the top layer down, so an image holding a second Python environment with another `mcp` below it would be misreported. In every image read, the `mcp` found was in the project's own virtual environment or `/opt/venv`, the environment its entry point uses (data/layer_reads.csv, `mcp_paths`; the entry point was not read).
- **Base layers were not read.** Layers whose history line is an operating-system or Python-runtime step with no package installation were skipped. `mcp` in such a layer would be missed; by their history lines, the skipped layers install the operating system and the Python runtime only.
- **Tag history cannot be read.** What a moving tag named during the period, and whether a replaced, untagged manifest is still served by digest, could not be tested: no anonymous interface lists an earlier digest, and GitHub's package-versions API needs a token. The agentcrew and keboola cases are seen only because other tags kept the earlier images.
- **Configurations and labels are the builder's word.** `created` is written at build time and was within 32 minutes of Docker Hub's push time for every image but one. Build tools that fix timestamps for reproducibility would make it meaningless; none of the images read shows that. Labels were not checked against the repositories, except that keboola's `revision` labels match the commit named in each tag.
- **The pin tables follow one line of each repository.** scripts/collect_pins.py walks the default branch's first-parent commits. A release or image built from another line, as jesse's 1.13.x were, is set against a pin that line never had; the jesse row above is read with that in mind, and no other project's images showed the mismatch.
- **The selection reads current metadata and two commits.** A project that published an image in the period but removed every mention of it from both commits read, and from PyPI's current and listed versions, would be missed. keboola's repository is not linked from its package metadata, so its image moves cannot be set against its git history. cognirepo's image could not be read.
- **Not a run, and not a population.** Nothing here says an image was started. The six repositories are the images of a selected neighbourhood; frame A is selected on having taken 1.27.0 up at some point (studies/LH002/brief.md), and nothing here estimates how images at large take a release.
- **Not checked:** architectures other than linux/amd64; the attestation manifests in each index; quay.io and other registries (none qualified); whether a replaced Docker Hub manifest is still served; the hub per-tag `images` endpoint's layer lists beyond one response; the pip and uv documentation for how each writes dist-info; this subagent's own spend, which is in its transcript and was not measured here.

## Next

- **Read the images LH006 could not, from an address with its own allowance.** Docker Hub's anonymous limit is per address; a run from an address not shared with other traffic could read every image in scope, including agentcrew's CI builds between 2 and 22 April and jesse 2.1.3, in about 90 counted pulls. That would turn the brackets into first moves.
- **Ask how common range-resolving images are.** agentcrew's images moved 16 days before its pin because its Dockerfile ignores the lock. A sample of images of a release's dependents, classified from their history lines as lock-installing or range-resolving and read for the installed version, would say how much of the installed population moves with the resolver rather than the maintainer. It is the image counterpart of LH005's installer split, and it is a question about the wider population the 21,873 "mcp" repositories belong to, not about this neighbourhood.
- **Watch a moving tag.** What `latest` or `main` named over time is not kept by the registries read. Recording a handful of moving tags' digests on a schedule, with a HEAD each time (not a counted pull on Docker Hub), would build the history the registries do not keep, and would show how often an image is rebuilt under the same name with a different installation.
- For the programme: images reach installation and not a run. The next step past installation would be a record of containers started, which no public registry holds; whether any public source records runs of published software at all is the question left.
