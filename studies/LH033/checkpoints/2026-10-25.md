## Censys: the tail no fix reaches

Services on branches PHP did not fix (8.1 and older, as Censys classes `affected_nofix`) were 23.3 to 23.5 million each day, 57.2 per cent of version-reporting services on day 0 and 55.1 per cent on day 3; most of that share is the unchanged size of the old branches, and the fall is the fixed branches growing as more services are seen. The largest are 7.4 (7.9 million services in the current snapshot), 8.1 (2.7 million), 7.2 (2.3 million), 5.6 (2.1 million) and 5.2 (2.0 million). Services reporting no version rose from 16.2 million on day 0 to 53.6 million on day 2 and 56.3 million on day 3, from 28 to 57 per cent of all PHP services Censys counted; the total grew from 56.9 to 98.9 million. That jump is in Censys's detection, not in the internet, and is why the brief's measures use shares within the version-reporting services.

## Beside the Python figures (derived; different units)

The comparators named in the brief, from LH011's and LH008's retained tables:

- LH008's four Python security fixes reached between 53.8 and 60.8 per cent of their library's downloads on day 1 and 52.7 to 63.7 per cent on day 2 (at that version or newer); the median across the 37 top projects' releases was 57.6 per cent on day 1 and 57.2 on day 2.
- Of 102 pinned repository pairs on those four fixes, 18 had moved to the fix within 7 days and 50 within 30.

So, as a description only: on day 1, the Censys patched share in each PHP branch (22 to 36 per cent) was below the download share the Python fixes had on their day 1, and on day 2 branch 8.4 (58.5 per cent) was within the Python fixes' day-2 range, 8.5 (70.5) above it, and 8.2 and 8.3 (41 to 42) below it. A download share counts fetches in a day; a Censys share counts standing services as last rescanned; the two are not the same quantity, and the Censys figures are floors for the reasons above. No cause is read into either. The Python curves rose slowly over the following month (to 57.3 to 72.3 per cent on day 30 for the four fixes); whether the PHP curves level off similarly is what the remaining reads measure.
