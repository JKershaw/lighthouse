**In short.** After PHP's emergency fix of 21 October, how many internet-facing PHP services were counted as having it? By Censys's count of services (an address and a port) that report their PHP version, 30 days on (20 November) the share counted patched was 78 per cent in branch 8.2, 75 in 8.3, 87 in 8.4 and 92 in 8.5. Nearly all of that came in the first two weeks; after day 14 the shares crept up mostly because Censys counted fewer services in three of the branches, not more patched ones; that share is a floor, because some distributions fix PHP without changing its version number. Over half the version-reporting services, 22.5 million, ran older branches PHP no longer fixes. This version is unreviewed; its reviews come next, and the comparison of ordinary PHP releases follows the 1 December point.

## Answer

Thirty days after PHP's emergency releases, Censys counted most version-reporting, internet-facing services in each fixed branch as patched: on day 30 (20 November), 78.2 per cent in 8.2, 75.0 in 8.3, 86.5 in 8.4 and 92.2 in 8.5 (observation, Censys's classification). The two newer branches passed half on day 2, the two older on day 6, and the two older stayed below the two newer on every day from day 1. The patched counts rose by 18.5 to 24.3 per cent of each branch from day 7 to day 14, then barely moved: from day 14 to day 30 they rose by 0.5 to 0.9 per cent of the branch in 8.2, 8.3 and 8.5, whose branch totals fell, and by 9.0 in 8.4. So the shares still rose by 2.5 to 5.5 points after day 14, but in three branches mostly because the count of services in the branch shrank (derived, from Censys's counts; why it shrank isn't known). One minus those shares, 21.8, 25.0, 13.5 and 7.8 per cent, is the part of each branch not counted patched a month on; most of it sits five or more releases behind the fix in 8.2 and 8.3. Beside them, 22.5 million services, 51.8 per cent of those reporting a version, ran branches PHP did not fix, as their versions report them. All of this is among services that report a version: 62.3 per cent of the PHP services Censys counted on day 30 reported none.

Set beside the Python comparators named in the brief, as a description in different units: on day 1 every PHP branch's share was below every Python security fix's download share, and on days 14 and 30 every PHP branch's share was above every Python fix's. That crossing is what two such units would be expected to show, a stock that starts near zero against a flow that starts high, so it is not a contest. A Censys share counts standing services as last scanned, and is a floor on protection; a Python share counts a day's downloads. Neither says which ecosystem is faster or why.

On 30 October, after seeing the first week, we wrote down two predictions to check at day 30 against LH034's Node.js figures. Both held as written: more of PHP's day-30 share arrived after the first week than of Node.js's (test A), and the older PHP branches sat more than five points below the newer ones (test B). Test A's PHP side rests mostly on days 7 to 14; its second part, a rise of a point or more in the last week, held on shares in all four branches but in patched counts only in 8.2 and 8.4. They are tests of readings made after the first week was seen, in different units across the two ecosystems, and they say nothing about cause.

## Method

The brief fixed, before any outcome was read: the sources, day 0 (21 October, PHP's release day), the measures and the Python comparators; amendment 2 fixed tests A and B on 30 October, after day 8 was read and before any later day. Each read retains the raw Censys body gzipped with SHA-256 and UTC time (read_log.json) and never overwrites a read; a completed day's value is taken from the earliest read that holds it, and scripts/record_v1.py compares every later read with it field for field. Measure 1 is `patched / targets` per branch and day from Censys's `branch_history`; measure 2 is `affected_nofix` over `affected + affected_nofix + patched` from `history`, with `unknown` over all targets beside it; measure 3 groups `branch_versions` by patch number against the fixed release, over all the branch's rows. W3Techs's bodies were replaced on 21 November by derived files holding the facts used (data/REMOVED.md), and tabulate.py's outputs were checked identical before removal. Collection closed at the brief's stop on 22 November (checkpoints/2026-11-22-closure.md).

## Limits

- **A Censys share is not protection.** The dashboard's notes say figures "can lag real-world changes by hours, and by up to about a week for some websites", that "Status is based on reported versions, so counts are an upper bound", and that "Linux distributions often backport security fixes without changing the PHP version" (read 25 October 2026; discovery.md). So the patched shares are floors: Ubuntu's fixed package for its 24.04 release reports itself as 8.3.6 (studies/LH018), and services that hide their version, 62.3 per cent of those counted on day 30, are outside both the count and its denominator.
- **Measure 1's denominator is Censys's branch count**, which leaves out services in a fixed branch whose patch number Censys can't place (its `unknown` status within the branch, 253,987 in 8.2 in the snapshot of 21 November); they are neither patched nor unpatched here.
- **"Patched" probably includes services first seen already fixed.** Censys's notes define patched as seen on an affected version and later on a fixed one, but in 8.5 the day-30 patched count (3,423,244) is larger than the whole branch's day-7 count (3,381,503), which the definition allows only if a large share of the services that joined the branch after day 7 were each seen first on an affected version and later on a fixed one. We think it more likely that services first seen on a fixed version are counted patched too (interpretation, not checked with Censys); either way the patched share is a share of services seen fixed, not of services that changed.
- **A service is an address and a port**, not a machine, a website or an organisation.
- **Different units.** The Python comparators are shares of a day's downloads from the Python Package Index, the Node.js figures shares of a day's downloads from nodejs.org, the Packagist figure a month's installs, the W3Techs figures a month-blended survey of websites, and the PHP figures a stock of services. The comparisons describe shapes; they don't rank the ecosystems, and severity, publicity, exploitation reports and AI aren't examined as causes.
- **One third-party instrument.** Censys's detection changed on day 2; its methods are not fully published; how it treats a service first seen already fixed was not checked. Day 29's fall in service counts coincides with PHP's regular releases and can't be separated from a rescan.
- **Measure 3 is from the snapshot**, refreshed on 21 November, half a day after day 30 ended, not from a day-30 bar; the branch totals agree with the day-30 bars to within 0.3 per cent.
- **W3Techs lists two releases before their dates**: 8.4.28 at "<0.1%" on 1 October and 1 November and 8.5.13 at "<0.1%" on 1 November, though php.net's release lists, read on 21 November, date both to 19 November. Counted as 0, they change no figure; W3Techs's version detection was not checked.
- **Amendment 2's readings were made after the first week was seen**, and test A's Node.js side depends on which of LH034's two rules is used (both reported).
- **Not reviewed.** This version has had no evidence or reader review yet.
- Not checked: Censys's terms of use; W3Techs's version detection; why 8.3.8 holds so many services.
