## Answer

A week after PHP's emergency releases, Censys counted between a little over half and nearly four fifths of the version-reporting, internet-facing PHP services in each fixed branch as patched: on day 7, 53.0 per cent in 8.2, 52.4 in 8.3, 67.8 in 8.4 and 78.8 in 8.5 (observation, Censys's classification). 8.4 and 8.5 first passed half on day 2, 8.2 and 8.3 on day 6. Beside them, 24.4 million services, 54.3 per cent of those that reported a version, ran branches for which PHP released no fix.

Set beside the Python comparators named in the brief, as a description in different units: on day 1 every PHP branch's share was below every Python security fix's download share; by day 7 the two older PHP branches were below the Python range and the two newer ones above it. A Censys share counts standing services as last scanned, and is a floor on protection; a Python share counts a day's downloads. Neither says which ecosystem is faster or why.

## Method

The brief fixed, before any outcome was read: the sources, the day 0 (21 October, PHP's release day), the measures and the Python comparators. Each read retains the raw bodies gzipped with SHA-256 and UTC time (read_log.json) and never overwrites a read; a completed day's value is taken from the earliest read that holds it. Measure 1 is `patched / targets` per branch and day from Censys's `branch_history`; measure 2 is `affected_nofix` over `affected + affected_nofix + patched` from `history`, with `unknown` over all targets beside it. Censys calls a service patched when it was seen on a fixed release, or seen earlier on an affected one and later on a fixed one.

## Limits

- **A Censys share is not protection.** The dashboard's own notes say figures "can lag real-world changes by hours, and by up to about a week for some websites", that "Status is based on reported versions, so counts are an upper bound", and that "Linux distributions often backport security fixes without changing the PHP version" (read 25 October 2026; discovery.md). So the patched shares are floors: Ubuntu's fixed package for its 24.04 release reports itself as 8.3.6 (studies/LH018), and services that hide their version are outside both the count and its denominator.
- **A service is an address and a port**, not a machine, a website or an organisation.
- **Different units.** The Python comparators are shares of a day's downloads from the Python Package Index, a flow weighted towards environments that fetch often; the PHP figures are a stock of services. The comparison describes shapes; it does not rank the ecosystems, and severity, publicity, exploitation reports and AI are not examined as causes.
- **One third-party instrument.** Censys's detection changed on day 2 (above); its methods are not fully published.
- **Interim.** Measures 3 to 6 and the day 14 and day 30 points wait for the read holding day 30 (21 or 22 November) and W3Techs's monthly points.
- Not checked: Censys's terms of use; how Censys treats a service first seen already fixed; W3Techs's version detection.
