## Answer

Thirty-four candidate observatories were considered; seventeen have a full row in sources.md's included table and seventeen are excluded there with a stated reason, an observation. Of the seventeen included, most see only one of LH F02's layers from one narrow population: repository-event archives and dependency resolvers see residue (changed structure), API-broker rankings and vendor usage reports see activity (inference calls), and vulnerability databases see a specific kind of residue, a disclosed flaw, rather than its exploitation. No source in this reading is classed as observing propagation directly. That is Lighthouse's classification, not an observation about the sources: LH F02 defines propagation as a derived link between an earlier residue record and later activity, not a surface any instrument reads off directly, so under that definition no source could be counted as seeing it. The empirical question is which of these records support identifying and testing such links. A propagation claim has to be built by joining two sources' records and testing the join, which is what LH002 is designed to attempt on a small scale for one such join (a dependency change and a dependent's later change) and what this study does not attempt at all. The strongest supported finding, an interpretation resting on the rows above rather than on any single one, is that these seventeen sources are partial views whose relationships to one another are unmeasured in anything this survey read: each is scoped to its own population (one forge, one broker, one vendor's product tiers in a given report, one company's network), no source read here states how much its population overlaps another's, and this study took no joint sample, so their counts cannot simply be added into a total. Distinct boundaries do not establish little overlap, and the table itself records connections, an observation: Open Source Insights links packages in seven ecosystems to their GitHub, GitLab and Bitbucket projects and to OSV advisories; OSV.dev aggregates GitHub Security Advisories, PyPA and RustSec among its upstream sources; GitHub's unreviewed advisories come from the NVD feed and are published in OSV format; NVD enriches records that CVE Numbering Authorities assign (this last per a search summary; see the fourth Finding). Where the populations overlap, and by how much, is a question for a joint sample, not something this reading establishes. What stays invisible, or visible only in part, is specific. No source read here can reliably tell a person's download or code change from an agent's. Cloudflare's AI Crawl Control does attribute crawler traffic to named AI operators, and its page lets a site owner group that traffic by crawler, by operator and by "Category: Analyze crawlers by their purpose or type" (an observation, from notes/R-0001.md's raw read of the page, last updated 23 April 2026); but it covers only crawlers Cloudflare has already identified, and because the category belongs to the crawler rather than to any one request, it cannot say why a particular page was fetched (an interpretation). The conversation figures do not say whether a person or a program was typing. No source states at what layer an AI system operated, and usage that happens off all these particular surfaces goes unseen, for instance direct provider API traffic outside a broker, a private repository, or a deployment that never triggers a public event. The first of those examples holds for the sources as read here; the first review note says later Economic Index reports, which this survey has not read, include one vendor's first-party API traffic.

## Findings

**Every included source is scoped to its own population; how those populations relate is unmeasured in anything read here, and the table records some connections between them.** GH Archive sees one forge's public events (sources.md's GH Archive row); Open Source Insights sees seven package ecosystems' declared manifests, not what any of them actually runs; OpenRouter's rankings see one broker's own routed traffic; the Anthropic Economic Index, in the introductory report of 10 February 2025 that its row describes, excludes its own vendor's API, Team and Enterprise tiers and says "we don't argue that the uses in our dataset are a representative sample of AI use in general" (the page's words as notes/R-0001.md read them; the quotation in versions 0.1 and 0.2 was not on the page), and the first review note says later reports widened that scope, which this survey has not checked (sources.md's Anthropic Economic Index row and its note); Cloudflare's two products each see only what crosses that one company's network. A repository event, a dependency bump, a broker-routed inference call and an identified crawler hit could all describe the same underlying task, a scenario. Some of these sources do carry one another's identifiers, an observation from the table: Open Source Insights links package versions to GitHub, GitLab and Bitbucket project metadata and to OSV advisories; OSV.dev draws its entries from GitHub Security Advisories, PyPA, RustSec and other upstream databases; GitHub's unreviewed advisories come straight from the NVD feed and are published in OSV format. For the broker, the vendor reports and Cloudflare's two products, the documentation read states no identifier shared with any other source here. Version 0.1 read these distinct boundaries as populations "almost disjoint by construction"; distinct boundaries do not establish little overlap, this study measured no overlap, and that reading is withdrawn (see Corrections). The defensible statement, an interpretation, is that these are partial views whose relationships need investigating and whose counts cannot simply be added.

**Under LH F02's classification no source is counted as observing propagation directly; the empirical question is which records support identifying and testing it.** LH F02 states propagation "is not a surface... it is a typed link between a residue record and later activity." Saying that none of the seventeen sources observes propagation therefore restates how Lighthouse classifies evidence; it is not a limitation this study found in the sources, and no source was tested for it. The empirical question is which of their records carry what such a link needs: a dated residue record, a later dated record elsewhere, and an identifier or other evidence connecting the two. GH Archive and Open Source Insights both record residue (repository and dependency change), and Open Source Insights already links packages to their forge projects; joining a dependency-graph change in one to a later repository change in the other, and testing that join against the alternative explanations LH002's own design section lists (dependency-update bots, release trains, shared maintainers), is exactly LH002's task, not this one. This study's own contribution is prior to that: it says which raw materials for such a join exist and what population each covers, not whether any particular join holds.

**Two kinds of "model usage report" exist, and they differ in what a reader can check.** OpenRouter's rankings are a live, queryable API over a named population (that broker's own routed traffic), licensed CC BY 4.0 for reuse. The Anthropic Economic Index and OpenAI's "How People Use ChatGPT" are each a periodic or one-off published study built from a vendor's own internal conversation logs, filtered and aggregated before release; the Anthropic Index's introductory report (10 February 2025) states its scope limit (Free and Pro only); a privacy floor earlier versions attributed to it (any cell under 15 conversations or 5 accounts suppressed) is not on the page by notes/R-0001.md's raw read and is unconfirmed, possibly from the accompanying paper, which this survey has not read; the first review note says later reports widened the scope; OpenAI's paper was readable in this study only through a WebSearch summary of press coverage and the abstract, because the primary PDF's text could not be extracted by the tool available here and the companion web page returned HTTP 403 (sources.md's fetch log). A reader comparing these three needs to know that the first is a continuously re-derivable API and the other two are single snapshots whose underlying logs a reader cannot re-query.

**The vulnerability-database row shows a source split between assignment and enrichment that a single citation would hide.** A WebSearch summary read on 26 September 2026 states that over 400 CVE Numbering Authorities, under MITRE and CISA as top-level roots, assign CVE identifiers directly from allocated blocks, while NVD separately enriches those same identifiers with CVSS scores; both nvd.nist.gov and cve.org's own pages returned only a bare header to this study's fetch tool, matching what F02 already records for S15, so this split rests on a secondary summary and should be checked against a primary CVE Program page before a study leans on it.

**Two rows in the included table sit outside F02's three layers rather than fit awkwardly inside one.** The AI Incident Database records harm attributed to deployed AI systems, closer to F02's separate Outcomes category (scored against a declared purpose) than to activity, residue or propagation, and it is built from voluntary submission with no denominator of total deployments to turn its count into a rate. Epoch AI's workplace-adoption polling dataset, bundled alongside its otherwise Residue-layer model and compute catalogue, is self-reported survey data, not a direct observation, and the row says so rather than labelling it as a reading.

**Fetch failures cluster on JavaScript-rendered pages and are specific, not general.** Six of the twenty-two URLs attempted for this study failed outright or returned unusable content (sources.md's fetch log): nvd.nist.gov, cve.org and its own /general/News/change-timeline sub-page each returned only a bare page header, matching a pattern F02 already recorded for nvd.nist.gov's S15 entry; the Anthropic Economic Index's own top-level page returned a "Loading data" placeholder where a research sub-page one level down did not; the OpenAI usage paper's PDF could not be parsed for text and its companion openai.com page returned HTTP 403. Each failure is recorded against the specific row it would have filled, and the row is left with a blank or caveated cell rather than a guess.

**Where this study rested on a WebSearch summary rather than a direct page read, sources.md says so per row.** This applies most to the OpenAI usage-report row, the Hugging Face download-concentration figures, the CNA/NVD split, and the Shodan/Censys and Stack Exchange exclusion reasons. Two rows, GitHub Security Advisories and the npm download-count API, were first found by search and then confirmed by a direct fetch; the notes under sources.md's included table say which rows carry which confidence.

## Method

Started from LH F02's own Sources section (S1 to S19) and LH F04's LH003 design section, which names GH Archive, Cloudflare AI Crawl Control and Radar, Software Heritage, Open Source Insights, OpenRouter's rankings and the vulnerability databases as the seed candidates. Read Lighthouse_Founding_Charter.md, Lighthouse_Initial_Research_Programme.md and Lighthouse_Study_Templates.md in full before drafting the table, then studies/LH000/ for file layout and prose conventions to follow.

For each seed candidate and each further candidate this study found, fetched the source's own public documentation with WebFetch, or, where a direct fetch was not attempted or did not resolve to readable content, used WebSearch and recorded that the row rests on a secondary summary. Every fetch attempted is listed in sources.md's fetch log with its URL, read date and outcome; every row in the included and excluded tables cites the URL or S number the row's cells came from. No source's live API was queried for a data sample; this ticket is reading only, and no request beyond fetching a documentation page was made to any of these services.

New candidates beyond LH F02's seed list (OSV.dev, GitHub Security Advisories, the Anthropic Economic Index, OpenAI's usage paper, Common Crawl, PyPI and npm download statistics, Epoch AI, the AI Incident Database, and Hugging Face Hub) were found by following the seed sources' own stated relationships (OSV.dev names GHSA and NVD as partner sources; deps.dev's own advisory data links to OSV) and by searching directly for the kinds of source the task's framing question named: repository activity, model usage reports and crawler measurements. The excluded table's non-S-number rows (the Stanford AI Index Report, the State of AI Report, Shodan/Censys, SimilarWeb-style estimators, W3Techs, and the Stack Exchange data dump) were considered because each is a commonly cited source for some part of internet-scale measurement; each is excluded with a stated reason rather than silently dropped.

## Limits

Six of twenty-two attempted fetches failed or returned unusable content; sources.md's fetch log names each one and the row it would have filled. This study cannot say whether NVD, CVE.org or the Anthropic Economic Index's top-level page would have answered the missing cells, because the tool available here could not render them; a person with a browser, or a differently configured fetch tool, may see more than this study did.

Several rows rest on a WebSearch summary of a source's documentation rather than a direct read of the primary page; sources.md flags each one. A WebSearch summary is a secondary account of what a page says, produced by a different model than the one writing this study, and it can compress or misstate a detail the primary page states precisely; none of those rows should be treated as more reliable than the direct-fetch rows until someone reads the primary source.

This survey states each source's population and boundary as its own documentation describes them; it does not test any of those claims against the source's actual behaviour (for instance, whether GH Archive's payload really does vary the way its docs warn, or whether OpenRouter's CC BY 4.0 licence covers the exact figures a study would want to cite). Confirming a claimed licence or boundary against the source's actual output is a further task, not done here.

This study cannot compare any two sources' populations quantitatively (how much they overlap, or how much of the wider population that "what can we actually see of AI activity on the internet" asks about, either one covers) because no source read here states its own overlap with another, and estimating one would require a joint sample this reading-only ticket did not take. Version 0.1's Findings called the populations "almost disjoint by construction", an interpretation drawn from each source's stated boundary; that reading did not follow and is withdrawn in version 0.2 (see Corrections). The Findings now describe partial views whose relationships are unmeasured: this study measured neither an overlap fraction nor its absence, and did not look for anyone else's measurement.

The publication-date and observation-window columns added to sources.md in version 0.2 are filled only where this survey's record of a page states them. Most cells are blank with a note: for most rows the read recorded no page date, and for several no period of coverage. Only the Anthropic Economic Index page was read again for the correction, to date the report its row describes; the other blanks may be fillable by a fresh read of each page, which this correction did not make.

LH F04's own design section for LH003 asks for "a register entry for each source that could serve as a Lighthouse instrument" in addition to the table and report; this dispatch's rules restrict its writes to studies/LH003/, so no such entries were added to registers/instruments.md. This is recorded as a gap for a follow-up ticket, not silently dropped; see Next.

This subagent was not given Harbour's take record for the LH003 dispatch (unlike LH000's subagent, which had `take.json`), so the brief's "initiated by" field, and any dispatch-level identifiers, are recorded as unknown from here rather than guessed; the driver session may be able to fill them from what it holds when it posts feedback.
