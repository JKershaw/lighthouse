# An emergency patch had several public kinds of protection

**Study:** LH024, version 0.1, 22 October 2026. **Writer:** Codex, GPT-6 Sol in ChatGPT Work Mode. **Protocol:** local commit `a7fdc47`, mirrored in PR #26 before panel search. **Observation:** 22 October UTC, direct source timestamps in [the read log](data/snapshot-2026-10-22/read_log.csv). **Overlap:** the same two-CVE PHP episode studied by LH018–LH023; the source and Docker records are reused context, not independent events. **Model spend:** unknown in this harness; no paid external API.

## Answer

The fixed public panel exposes different sorts of response: PHP's release notice, public-agency advice, and network providers' claims about traffic rules. Of ten selected institutions, six supplied directly readable relevant pages in this bounded snapshot (PHP, UK NCSC, CERT-EU, Cloudflare, Fastly and Akamai). CISA refused a direct request; AWS and Google Cloud returned `Site Unavailable` pages with HTTP 200; the Azure site-restricted search supplied no selected relevant page. Those four are instrument outcomes, not evidence of inactivity. Three selected media outlets supplied article metadata, not operator-action evidence. The panel is purposive and supports no percentage about all operators or protected sites.

## Findings

The [13-entry event sheet](data/events-2026-10-22.json) keeps each classification, source clock and limitation. The upstream php.announce message has a 20 October 19:33:47 UTC Date field for the advance notice. NCSC's page has a 21 October 12:00 UTC time element and recommends inventory, updating, temporary mitigation and considering taking unprotected systems offline. CERT-EU gives 21 October 15:20:18 as its release time and advises patching or temporary isolation where immediate patching is infeasible. These are source-reported publication fields seen retrospectively on 22 October, not clocks observed as they happened.

Cloudflare's page, published at 21 October 13:56:45 UTC by its metadata and later modified, says WAF protections became default for its free and paid WAF customers at **03:02 UTC on 21 October**. That earlier time is the company's retrospective claim. Fastly's blog and changelog describe a CVE-specific virtual patch available to activate; the blog says later updates reach those who opt in. Akamai's page says App & API Protector security updates and rule `3000991 v1` were released. None of the three pages exposes a denominator of applicable customer sites, a verified rule activation per site, or a controlled measure of efficacy. All still recommend applying the upstream PHP fix.

The Register's two retained article metadata records have 21 October publication fields at 15:08:51 and 23:02:57 UTC; Ars's article says it was originally published on 20 October but its extracted `article:published_time` is 21 October 02:12:18, a conflict this snapshot cannot resolve. BleepingComputer's selected page supplied no precise timestamp to this reader. These articles mark a bounded sample of coverage; their full text is not republished in the repository.

## Method

The [brief](brief.md) fixed ten institutions, three outlets, query strings, a daily window and ceilings before this panel search. One bounded site-restricted search per entry was attempted, with three queries repeated after the search tool's output was truncated. [The chosen URL manifest](selection-2026-10-22.json) contains at most two results per entry; the direct reader attempted 17 URLs. It retained 1,997,296 bytes in total, including error bodies, and logged retrieval times, URLs, status, hashes and extracted metadata. Sixteen requests returned HTTP 200 and one 403, but four of the 200 responses were unavailable placeholders. The [offline replay](scripts/replay.py) checks panel sizes, source mapping, retained primary byte hashes and access-failure bodies. Media response hashes were recorded but their full bodies were discarded, so those hashes cannot be independently replayed offline.

**Protocol deviation:** the search connector output was not itself retained with exact query invocation times and full ranked results. The selected URLs, frozen queries and direct reads are retained; nonselected search hits and precise search times cannot be replayed. This weakens any comparison of a missing result or search rank. Future collection must persist its full bounded search response before treating such a comparison as complete.

## Limits

- This was observed on 22 October after many pages had already been published or revised. A source's published field cannot by itself establish when a particular sentence was first visible. Cloudflare's claimed 03:02 protection predates its article; this reader did not observe the rules then.
- HTTP 200 alone is not access. The four `Site Unavailable` bodies leave AWS and Google Cloud's advisory contents unknown in this instrument; a search snippet is discovery, not the retained page.
- A vendor's claim of a deployed rule, an available opt-in rule, advice to take a system offline, and an actual patched deployment are different kinds of record. None counts hosts, attack attempts or avoided compromises here.
- One search per organisation and two pages per entry can miss releases, social posts, customer advisories and revisions. The institutions were chosen by role with several known pages disclosed in the brief, and are not a representative sample. Media publication clocks may change upon revision.
