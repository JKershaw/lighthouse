## Answer

In this sample, the images that resolve an open range took the new release within days, and the images built from a lockfile or an exact pin took it only when the pin moved. For most of the lockfile and exact-pin repositories that was a month or more after the release, or had not happened by 15 August: of 12 such image repositories, 9 had no pin on the new release within four weeks of it, and the largest group of pin moves, four of the nine found, came within 28 hours of a security advisory published a month after the release rather than in the days after the release itself.

The sample is the published images of the direct dependents of `mcp` that Open Source Insights lists around release 1.28.0 (16 June 2026), fixed by a hash-order rule before any image was read: 22 of 120 screened packages name an image of their own, and 13 image repositories gave an installed `mcp` version, 49 images in all (observation; data/candidates.csv, data/image_timeline.csv).

- **Open range** (the installer resolves a specifier such as `mcp>=1.26` at build time, with no lockfile): 5 of the 6 images read that were built in the four weeks after the release held 1.28.x, the first 2.6 days after it; 3 of the 4 repositories held it in every image read after the release. The fourth, chemgraph, whose specifier names no version at all, held 1.16.0 before and after, so something else in its resolution held `mcp` back; that was not investigated (observation; derived shares, data/share_by_class.csv).
- **Lockfile or exact pin:** 7 of 10 images built in the same four weeks held 1.28.x, but all seven came from two projects whose pin had already moved: better-telegram-mcp, whose lockfile a Renovate bot moved 16 hours after the release, and mcp-mesh, whose package pinned 1.28.1 from day 21. The others held what their pin or lock said: mcp-atlassian 1.26.0 at days 23.7 and 24.3, browser-use 1.26.0 at day 3.9 (observation; derived).
- **Every image read held what its own lockfile or pin said:** all 20 lockfile images their lock's version, all 9 exact-pin images their pin. Of the 20 open-range images, 10 held a newer `mcp` than a lockfile their own repository kept but the image did not use: mcp-proxy-for-aws's images held 1.28.0 from day 2.6, 27.8 days before its repository's lockfile moved; mcp-pinot's held 1.28.1 8.8 days before its lock did (observation of versions and files; derived lags). This is the case LH006 found in one project, agentcrew; here it is in all three open-range repositories that keep a lockfile (the third, alation's MCP server, only in an image from January), and in each the image installs with `pip`, not from the lock.
- **More first pins moved in the day after the advisory than in the four weeks after the release.** Of 13 projects whose first commit pinning or locking 1.28.x was found, six made it between 0.9 and 27.6 hours after GHSA-vj7q-gjh5-988w, a high-severity advisory fixed in 1.28.1, was published on 16 July at 20:14 UTC, 29.9 days after 1.28.0. It was the third high-severity advisory against `mcp` published that evening; the two before it, at 19:56 and 19:58 UTC, concern flaws fixed in 1.27.2 (data/osv_mcp_all.csv, read by the review). Four of the six commits were changes Dependabot wrote: three committed under its name and one merged by a person from its branch. One was made under a project's own bot account, with a person as co-author, in a change whose message says it upgrades FastMCP to version 3 and regenerates the lock; the sixth is a person's, citing the three advisories (observation of commits and the advisories' publication times; the causal link is interpretation from timing and commit messages). Three first pins moved in the four weeks after the release (days 0.7, 7.1 and 20.8) and four between days 40 and 57. Five sampled projects had no commit pinning or locking 1.28.x by 15 August (browser-use, hybrid-groups, modulector, alation's MCP server and mcp-standards-server).

By comparison, PyPI's download log (ClickPy's copy) shows 1.28.x at 46.7 per cent of all `mcp` downloads on the day after the release and between 43.2 and 62.0 per cent on each day of the four weeks after (observation; data/clickpy_mcp_daily_share_128.csv). The open-range images kept pace with that; the pinned ones did not.

The limits are sharp. The sample is small and clustered: 49 images from 13 repositories, many of them from better-telegram-mcp (10), mcp-proxy-for-aws (6) and mcp-mesh (7). The byte ceiling was reached before most Docker Hub images were read: of the seven Docker Hub repositories, including the largest projects (langflow, browser-use, jesse), only browser-use gave an installed version. Images are installations, not runs, and a published image is not the installed population.

## Method

The brief (brief.md) was written before the frame was read and before any tag list, manifest, configuration or layer; it was amended before any manifest of a sampled image was read (the mirrors, Docker Hub's 1,000-tag listing, ghcr.io dating) and again before any Docker Hub manifest (the byte ceiling). Its clock times were corrected against the read log afterwards; nothing else was changed (brief.md, note on times).

1. **Choice** (scripts/probe_choice.py). The most recent minor or major `mcp` release at least 28 days old with no other minor or major release within 28 days after it: 1.28.0. `fastmcp` was considered and not chosen.
2. **Frame and screening** (scripts/select_candidates.py, scripts/extract_images.py, scripts/classify_candidates.py). The direct dependents listed for 1.27.2, 1.28.0 and 1.28.1, in SHA-256 order; the first 120 screened as in LH006 (repository by LH002's rule, PyPI metadata, blobless clone, image references in Dockerfiles, workflows, READMEs and MCP manifests at the head and at the last commit before 14 July). extract_images.py lists the image names each pushing workflow names; the decisions and reasons are in classify_candidates.py and data/candidates.csv. The sample is every qualifying image repository, 22, fewer than the brief's limit of 24.
3. **Tags and images** (scripts/collect_tags.py, scripts/collect_images.py `meta`). Tag lists; on Docker Hub the hub API's tag records (push times, digests); on ghcr.io and public.ecr.aws a HEAD of every tag and the manifest and configuration of every distinct image, to date it by `created`.
4. **Plan and layers** (scripts/plan_layers.py, scripts/collect_images.py `layers`, scripts/registry.py). At most 10 images per repository by the brief's tiers; the linux/amd64 manifest and configuration of each, then its layers streamed from the top down, skipping base-image layers, until a `site-packages/mcp-<version>.dist-info/` entry appeared (data/layer_reads.csv). Non-Docker Hub images were read first, then Docker Hub images by tier across repositories, within 25 counted pulls an hour and 40 in all.
5. **Build commits and pins** (scripts/collect_builds.py, scripts/collect_pins.py, scripts/pin_movers.py). For each image, the commit (revision label, else the last first-parent commit before the build), the Dockerfile's install lines and the project's `mcp` specifier and lock there; every change of `mcp` in a manifest or lockfile from 1 April to 15 August; PyPI's `requires_dist` for each sampled package and for the two packages images install by name; for the first 1.28.x commit, the kind of author (a bot account's name, or "a person"; personal names are not recorded) and any co-author trailer naming a bot or an AI model.
6. **Analysis** (scripts/analyse.py, scripts/clickpy.py, scripts/draw_figure.py, scripts/summarise_reads.py). The install class per image by the rules in analyse.py's `classify`, which are the brief's; the agreement of each image's installed version with the lock or pin at its commit; shares by class and bin; the first image with 1.28.x per repository; ClickPy's daily shares; the OSV advisory (read inline and written to data/osv_mcp_1_28_0.csv).

Running the scripts in that order with `LH007_SCRATCH` set to a scratch directory outside the repository reproduces data/*.csv and installed.svg, subject to the registries', forges' and indexes' state at the time and to Docker Hub's allowance. Manifests and configurations are cached in the scratch directory so a rerun does not repeat a counted pull; layers are never written to disk. E-mail addresses found in READMEs and workflow labels are removed from the tables when they are written.

## Limits

- **The byte ceiling, not the pull budget, cut Docker Hub short.** The non-Docker Hub images were read first and used 2,471,129,240 bytes of the 3 GiB total; chemgraph, aurapro-webui and mcp-mesh's runtime each reached the 400 MiB cap (brief.md, amendment 2). The ceiling was not raised. Of the seven Docker Hub repositories, one langflow image consumed its 400 MiB cap without reaching `mcp`, browser-use gave two versions and jesse one partial read; the other 21 Docker Hub images read have a configuration but no installed version. The total read was 3,221,286,098 bytes, 60,626 bytes over 3 GiB, because a stream stops at the first 64 KiB chunk past the cap. The brief says "3 GB" and "400 MB"; the scripts count 3 GiB (3,221,225,472 bytes) and 400 MiB, as LH006's did.
- **Docker Hub's limit headers were not recorded for most of the reads.** The scripts checked `ratelimit-remaining` before the first counted GET and before the tenth and twentieth (reading went on, so it was at least 10 each time), but did not write the values to the log; that was added afterwards. A HEAD at 21:26:45 UTC showed `100;w=3600` with 72 remaining from one egress address (seen in the session's output, not in the log), and one at 21:52:06 showed 11 remaining from another (in the log). The study made 25 counted pulls, its hourly budget, and stopped there; two further GETs by an empty digest (langflow tags with no linux/amd64 image) answered 404.
- **The sample is small, clustered and selected.** 49 images from 13 repositories, three of which give 23 of them. The frame is what Open Source Insights lists, 100 per version at most: up to 68 direct dependents it counted were not returned (46 of 1.27.2's and 22 of 1.28.1's; some may be the same package), and 79 returned packages were beyond the screening limit. Projects that publish images are a selected group, and dependents of 1.28.x are selected on having taken it (the 1.27.2 list was added to soften this; data/frame.csv records each package's lists).
- **Install class is the builder's word and the nearest commit's.** 46 of 75 build commits are the last commit before the build, not a labelled revision; a build from a branch or an uncommitted change would be misread. For mcp-proxy-for-aws and seclab-taskflow-agent, which install their package by name from PyPI, the class follows the release current at the build, not a file in the repository. chemgraph's open specifiers resolved to 1.16.0, and why was not investigated; cloudsmith-cli's image downloads a zipapp built elsewhere and is classed unknown.
- **One environment per image, upper layers only**, as LH006: a read stops at the first `mcp` dist-info from the top down, and base-image layers are skipped.
- **Tag history cannot be read.** Images that only ever carried a moving tag and were replaced are invisible; the in-period images are those still tagged on 26 September.
- **The advisory link is timing.** Six pins moved within 28 hours of the advisory; the Dependabot commit messages say "Bump mcp from ... to 1.28.1" and do not say whether they were security updates, though serena's was authored within four minutes of the third advisory; openroad-mcp's cites "3 high-severity CVEs" and names alerts matching the three advisories of that evening; mcp-atlassian's is a FastMCP migration whose regenerated lock carried `mcp` to 1.28.1. Three advisories were published within 18 minutes, so "the advisory" here means the set, and the one fixed only in 1.28.1 is what made 1.28.x, rather than 1.27.2, the version that cleared them all.
- **Not a run, and not a population.** Nothing here says an image was started, or estimates how the installed population of `mcp` takes a release.
- **Not checked:** architectures other than linux/amd64; the Docker Hub copies of the ghcr.io images read; public.ecr.aws's and ghcr.io's documentation on anonymous limits (public.ecr.aws answered 429 eleven times until requests were paced); git blobs fetched on demand from the blobless clones, which are not logged request by request; whether any project is exposed to the advisory; this subagent's own model spend, which is in its transcript and was not measured here.
