## Answer

Partly, and only as far as the download. PyPI's public download record, read through ClickHouse's copy of it, connects a release to the files fetched from PyPI by version, day, installer and a self-reported CI flag. For `mcp` 1.27.0 it shows the release taken almost at once: 22.3 per cent of all `mcp` downloads on its release day, 1,639,539 of 7,351,271, in the nine hours after publication; 48.1 per cent the next day; between 45 and 71 per cent of every day's downloads from then to the window's end; 95,672,297 of the window's 219,899,536 `mcp` downloads in all (observation and derived measurement). By the time the first of LH002's dependents was seen pinning it, six days after publication, the release had been about half of all downloads for five days. What carried it to the downloaders was install-time resolution of open version ranges, mostly by `uv` (78.6 per cent of the window's downloads), not the dependents' pins (interpretation).

The record reaches the download and stops there. A row is a file served by PyPI to a client that names itself. It is not an installation: mirrors, proxies and caches install many times from one download, and CI runs, `pip download` and scanners download without installing. It is never a run. It is aggregate and anonymous, so it cannot link one package's download to another's. lean-lsp-mcp 0.26.0 and 0.26.1, which pin `mcp==1.27.0` exactly, were downloaded 35,024 times from 8 to 29 April, 0.03 per cent of `mcp` 1.27.0's 109,023,441 on those days. The second hop LH002 found is real in the metadata and invisible in the downloads (derived measurement; interpretation).

## Findings

**Three public copies of one dataset, none of them the original, agree closely on most days they share** (derived measurement; data/cross_copy_daily_totals.csv, data/cross_copy_pepy_clickpy.csv). The original, BigQuery's `bigquery-public-data.pypi.file_downloads`, needs a Google Cloud account and was not read (brief.md). Its coverage statement is PyPI's own. The packaging guide says download counts are "Highly inaccurate": "pip's download cache (lowers download counts)", "Internal or unofficial mirrors (can both raise or lower download counts)", "Unofficial scripts or attempts at download count inflation (raises download counts)" (sources.md L2, updated 22 September 2026, read 26 September).

- ClickPy's daily `mcp` totals equal pypistats.org's "with mirrors" totals exactly on 17 of the 25 window days both hold, 29 March to 22 April. On the other eight they differ by 653 to 104,651 downloads, at most 1.0 per cent (7,788,556 against 7,711,168 on 1 April). Summed over the 25 days, ClickPy is 0.18 per cent higher. Over all 181 days both hold, the totals are identical on 120 and differ by at most 2.7 per cent.
- pepy.tech's anonymous route reaches back only to 27 June 2026, so it cannot be compared in the window. From 27 June to 25 September its daily totals equal pypistats's on 72 of 91 days and ClickPy's on 57; 4,139 of 6,062 version-days match ClickPy exactly. It holds only part of four days (207,746 downloads on 6 August against ClickPy's 11,708,293; also 13 July, 14 August and 8 September) and runs 2.4 to 7.6 per cent above ClickPy on 15 days between 22 July and 20 August, when pypistats is also above ClickPy by up to 2.0 per cent.
- ClickPy's own tables disagree with each other on a few days. On 31 March its per-day table and its per-download table give 6,459,869 downloads, while its by-version table gives 6,524,343, 1.0 per cent more. ClickHouse says it has found days missing from its copy and re-ingested them (L4), so any of these figures can change after the read.

At the one-per-cent scale of these differences, the shares and timings below do not change. Which copy is right on a day they differ cannot be told without BigQuery (interpretation).

**pypistats's "without mirrors" leaves out more than mirrors** (derived measurement). On all 22 days from 29 March to 29 April on which ClickPy and pypistats agree on the total, pypistats's "without mirrors" equals ClickPy's total less the four installers pypistats names as mirrors (bandersnatch, z3c.pypimirror, Artifactory, devpi) and less every download with no installer name recorded. The second exclusion is not in pypistats's own description (L3). Over the window, downloads with no installer name were 255,315 and the named mirrors 2,957, of 219,940,356. Nexus, a repository manager, is not on pypistats's list and fetched `mcp` 47,834 times in the window.

**One ClickPy table misreports file types, because of how it is built** (observation and derived measurement). In `pypi_downloads_per_day_by_version_by_installer_by_type`, every one of pip's 940,901 downloads of `mcp` 1.26.0 on 27 March is labelled a source distribution. pip's 1.26.0 downloads that day are otherwise ordinary in size, and the file-type table gives 1,188 source distributions for all versions that day. The table's sorting key is (project, version, date, installer), so `type` is outside it, and ClickHouse's documentation says such a column keeps "an arbitrary value ... from the existing ones" when rows merge (L6). File types here come from the file-type table: 99.99 per cent of the window's `mcp` downloads were wheels.

**The release was taken within hours, faster than any dependent moved** (observation, ClickPy by-version table; derived shares; data/mcp_daily_by_version_share.csv, data/uptake_thresholds.csv).

**Which installer took it, and which did not** (derived measurement; data/mcp_installer_and_file_type_window.csv). Over the window, `uv` made 172,783,051 of the 219,940,356 downloads (78.6 per cent) and pip 44,872,032 (20.4 per cent). Poetry and PDM made 0.8 per cent, and every other name less than 0.2 per cent. From 3 to 22 April, 1.27.0 was 58.9 per cent of `uv`'s `mcp` downloads, 53.1 per cent of pip's, 21.2 per cent of Poetry's and 0.95 per cent of Bazel's (234 of 24,660). `uv` reached 50 per cent on day 6, pip on day 1. Tools that normally install from a lockfile, such as Poetry and Bazel, took the new release far less than tools resolving afresh, which is what lockfiles are for (interpretation).

**Downloads flagged as CI mostly stayed on older versions** (observation of ClickPy's `ci` column; derived shares; data/mcp_ci_by_version_class.csv). 34.6 per cent of the window's `mcp` downloads carry the CI flag (75,971,844 of 219,695,030). Only 10.6 per cent of 1.27.0's downloads from 3 to 22 April do (9,940,759 of 93,961,053), against 48.6 per cent of 1.26.0's and 72.9 per cent of every older version's. Among downloads not flagged as CI, 1.27.0 passed 50 per cent on day 1; among those flagged, it never passed 25 per cent in the span read, peaking at 22.7 per cent on 29 April. The flag is what the client reports, and ClickPy stores an unreported value as "not CI" (L4). So "not flagged" includes CI systems whose installer does not say so (observation of ClickPy's transformation; consequence derived).

**People or machines: the record does not say, and the week hints at both** (derived measurement for the ratios; interpretation for the reading). Over three whole weeks, 6 to 26 April, the mean weekend day's downloads were 0.79 of the mean weekday's for `mcp` as a whole. For 1.26.0 the ratio was 0.52; for CI-flagged 1.27.0, 0.66. For 1.27.0 as a whole it was 0.88, and for 1.27.0 fetched by `uv`, 0.92. Traffic that stays on the old release keeps working hours, as scheduled builds and pinned deployments would. Traffic on the new release barely dips at weekends, and the busiest share of the window fell on Sunday 12 April. That fits environments created afresh whenever something runs, day or night, as well as people working at weekends; the download record holds nothing that would tell them apart (interpretation). One day stands out: on 26 March, `uv` fetched 1.26.0 7,656,950 times, against 2,911,462 the day before (observation). What drove it is not recorded.

**The second hop cannot be seen in downloads** (observation, ClickPy per-download table; derived measurement; data/lean_lsp_mcp_daily.csv, data/lean_lsp_mcp_0_26_x_breakdown.csv). lean-lsp-mcp 0.26.0 went on PyPI at 18:49:02 UTC on 8 April and was fetched 967 times that day and 2,480 the next. It was superseded by 0.26.1 on 10 April, which peaked at 4,099 on 17 April. The two were fetched 38,953 times from 8 April to 3 May, the day before 0.26.2: 97.6 per cent by `uv`, 3.5 per cent flagged as CI, 98.9 per cent as wheels. Each of those installed with its dependencies, not from a cache or proxy, would have fetched `mcp` 1.27.0 as a separate row (interpretation, from how installers resolve an exact pin). Those rows are indistinguishable from the other 1.27.0 downloads. ClickPy's per-download table keeps the date, country, installer name, Python minor version, operating system, libc, CI flag and file name, and nothing that identifies a client or a session (L8), so no row can be tied to another. Even as a count, lean-lsp-mcp's 35,024 downloads from 8 to 29 April are 0.03 per cent of 1.27.0's 109,023,441, smaller than the day-to-day swing in 1.27.0's own downloads. Across the other cohort packages the same holds: in the window, mistral-vibe was fetched 2,074,948 times and every other dependent fewer than 43,000, against `mcp`'s 219,835,062 (data/cohort_window_totals.csv). dartlab, whose published requirement is `mcp>=1.0`, was fetched 17,103 times; what those installs resolved is not recorded anywhere read.

**Some of these readings will expire** (derived from the sources' stated retention; not checked beyond the read). pypistats keeps about 180 days: on 26 September its `mcp` series began on 29 March, so LH002's window leaves it day by day and is gone after about 20 October 2026. pepy's anonymous route keeps three months and already holds nothing from the window. ClickPy holds `mcp` from 2016 and was current to 25 September, but ClickHouse re-ingests days it finds wrong (L4), so its figures can change. The original, BigQuery, keeps rows it does not delete (not stated). A re-reading by anyone with a Google Cloud account is the durable check.

## Method

The brief (brief.md) was written before any count was read, and set the access decision: no Google account and no credential, so BigQuery was read through public copies only. The documentation pages were read first (sources.md, L1 to L7), then ClickPy's catalogue of tables, engines and definitions (L8), then the counts. scripts/collect_clickpy.py holds every scripted ClickPy query (C01 to C12). scripts/collect_other_copies.py reads pypistats and pepy; scripts/collect_pypi_releases.py reads release upload times from PyPI's JSON API, because ClickPy's copy of PyPI's metadata holds no row for the current `mcp` project. Each read appends its UTC time, endpoint, query text and status to data/read_log.csv. scripts/analyse.py derives every table and data/headline_figures.csv, and scripts/draw_figure.py draws uptake.svg. Running the five in that order reproduces data/, subject to the copies' own retention and corrections.

Before the scripts, eleven exploratory queries inspected ClickPy's tables, limits, time zone, the list of installer names and one day of the CI flag; every count they returned that is used here is repeated by a scripted query. 26 ClickPy queries in all, every one filtered to named projects and dates; none was refused or rate-limited. The largest, C07, read the 318,422,940 per-download rows of `mcp` for 42 days.

Choices made. Days are UTC, as ClickPy's server is. A version's release day is day 0. Shares use the ClickPy by-version table's own total as the denominator, so that numerator and denominator come from one table. Installer classes group ClickPy's installer names: `uv`; `pip`; Poetry and PDM; mirrors and proxies (bandersnatch, devpi, Artifactory, Nexus); Bazel; Browser; requests; none recorded; and other names (Homebrew, OS, setuptools, conda, and a "pip" spelled with an invisible character, 30 downloads). The weekday comparison uses three whole weeks, 6 to 26 April, to keep the release week out.

This agent's own token spend is not measured within the task; the driver session can price it from the transcript.

## Limits

**A copy, not the original.** Every count is ClickPy's, pypistats's or pepy's, not BigQuery's. The copies agree with each other to about one per cent in the window, which is evidence that none has drifted far, not proof that any matches the original.

**Download, not installation, not run.** Each row is a file PyPI served to a client that named itself. The record does not see installs served by mirrors, proxies or caches, and it counts downloads that were never installed. Where a proxy sends its own name (Nexus, Artifactory, devpi) the fetch is visible and what it served afterwards is not. Nothing in any source read says whether a downloaded package was imported or run.

**Self-reported fields.** Installer name and CI flag are what the client sends. How each installer decides them was not read: linehaul's source on github.com was refused (403), and pip's and `uv`'s sources were not read. An installer that does not report CI counts as "not CI" in ClickPy.

**Aggregate and anonymous.** No field ties two downloads together, so co-installation, the second hop, and the share of downloads made by any one dependent's users cannot be measured.

**Day, not hour.** ClickPy keeps the date of each download, not its time. BigQuery keeps the timestamp; it was not read.

**Not checked.** BigQuery itself; which CDN's logs PyPI streams and what they exclude (not stated on the pages read); pip's and `uv`'s cache behaviour; the Python-version and operating-system series read from pypistats, retained in data/ but not analysed. LH002's dependent moves are taken from its tables as published, not re-derived. The figure was rendered in headless Chromium by the driver session, which widened its margins so that no label is clipped; its data did not change.
