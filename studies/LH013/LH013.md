## Answer

The study ran. Its eligibility condition was met: on 2 November 2026 at 15:30 UTC, LH012's coverage query found 1 November 2026 as the last day held for every one of the 37 projects in all five per-day tables, and the preflight of 22 October had already found 21 October, so ClickPy held 12 October well before 31 October. When it first held that day the source cannot say (Method). Every day of both weeks was held for every project, no day was a gap, and W0's totals, read again, equalled LH012's to the download for all 37.

What stays behind held from week to week, as a rule, on every measure the brief tests (derived measurements, counts of fetches). A project's older share O, at the reference release R0 held fixed from LH012, stayed within 5 points of W0 in both new weeks for 32 of 37 projects (86.5 per cent, project-resampled 95 per cent interval 75.7 to 97.3); the five that moved further were aiohttp, fsspec, litellm, numpy and pydantic-core, all downwards. The median project's O was 37.3, 36.6 and 36.7 per cent in W0, W1 and W2. The class of Python exclusion P held for 35 of 37 (94.6 per cent, 86.5 to 100): boto3, aiobotocore and numpy stayed "accounts for most" in both weeks (boto3 76.9 to 78.3 per cent across the weeks' estimates), and the two that changed, pandas and urllib3, were the two "undetermined" in W0 and moved to "cannot be the main reason" as their upper estimates slipped under one half.

Downloads are concentrated in few combinations of reported fields. The largest cell of six fields (Python minor, installer, system, libc, CI flag, country) held the median project's 10.2 and 10.1 per cent of downloads in W1 and W2, and nine cells together carried half for the median project; boto3's largest cell, Python 3.9, pip, Linux, glibc, no CI flag, US, held 61.1 and 61.3 per cent of all its downloads, and with country set aside 64.9 and 64.8 per cent. C held, same cell and within 5 points, for 33 of 37 (89.2 per cent, 78.4 to 97.3), and C5, without country, for 29 of 37 (78.4 per cent, 64.9 to 91.9); where the largest cell changed, the two largest were within about 2 points of each other in at least one week in ten of the twelve cases, read after the counts; the other two are litellm's, whose largest cell passed from pip to uv. A cell is not a machine, an owner or a population.

All four tests of LH012's post hoc readings held on weeks no one had read. boto3's downloads reporting Python 3.9 were 65.3 and 65.1 per cent of its downloads (test: above one half); Python 3.9 fetched boto3 7.16 and 8.04 times as often as botocore, against 2.73 and 2.92 over all Pythons (test: at least twice); 95.2 and 95.5 per cent of boto3's Python 3.9 downloads were below 1.42.97 (test: above three quarters); and boto3's Python 3.9 profile below 1.42.97 stayed flat, flatness 0.0007 and 0.0005 against 0.001 in W0 (test: below 0.05). The staircase kept its shape, near 860,000 to 900,000 a version for about a hundred versions, then near 535,000 to 550,000, then near 110,000 to 126,000, and 1.42.87 stayed at about half its neighbours (0.53 and 0.55, 0.55 in W0). That these are a resolver's walk stays an interpretation: the log cannot say which installer versions made them, and whether it counts requests for metadata files is still not known. Holding is not explanation; nothing here says who or what makes these downloads.

## Limits

**A download is a fetch, and a cell is not a machine.** Every share weights environments by how often they fetch. A cell that holds its values and size from week to week has not been shown to be the same machines; many unrelated machines can share one, and one machine can fall in several.

**Two adjacent weeks.** W1 and W2 follow W0 directly. Holding over three consecutive weeks says nothing of longer stretches, of other seasons, or of what a change in one large population would do.

**R0 is fixed, so O drifts.** As releases accumulate above R0, downloads move from older to at-or-newer without anything changing in the environments that fetch; most projects' O fell a little (post hoc), and the five that left the band all fell. The secondary at each week's own reference shows how much the choice of reference moves O (up to about 15 points).

**The tests' bands.** For C, a 5-point band is half the size of a typical largest cell, so C's holding rests mostly on the same cell staying largest, and the failures are near-ties. P's class changes for pandas and urllib3 come from estimates on the one-half line. The walk threshold of 0.05 sorts some profiles differently from week to week (grpcio-status, litellm, fsspec), so the walk-shaped share of a project with a profile near the line is unstable; boto3's and aiobotocore's are far from it.

**Unknown stays unknown.** When ClickPy first held 12 October 2026 cannot be read from the source; the record knows only that it was held by 03:59 UTC on 22 October. Whether the download log counts requests for PEP 658 metadata files is not stated on either page read, so which pips can leave a walk in the log is not settled; which installer versions make the flat profiles needs the installer's version, which ClickPy does not hold.

**Country.** A country code is where an address is placed; the US in every largest cell may reflect where cloud machines and mirrors are, not where anyone is. Which service assigns it was not read.

**Not checked.** BigQuery; W0's cells (the brief does not read them, so C has no W0); any project outside the 37; days after 11 October, except for the gap rule; whether ClickPy will re-ingest W1 or W2 later. This record has had no review: it is version 0.1, written and checked by the agent that ran the reads, and a second agent's agreement would be a review contribution, not confirmation.
