**In short.** We asked how often PyPI's public download logs recorded the poisoned LiteLLM 1.107.0 being fetched on 21 October 2026, the day it was up for about an hour and three quarters, and afterwards. So far, unreviewed: the logs as ClickPy holds them record 49,043 fetches that day, 1.4 per cent of all LiteLLM fetches, and for the hours it was the newest release that's within the range of 17 ordinary LiteLLM releases of the same weeks, though low in it unless the window ended early. The logs go on recording a trickle of fetches of it after it was pulled, 1,431 from 22 October to 8 November. A download is a fetch, not an install or an infection; a second read after 20 November and a reviewed record by 4 December come next.

## Answer (to date, interim)

To 8 November 2026, unreviewed. PyPI's download logs, as ClickPy held them on 9 November, record 49,043 fetches of litellm 1.107.0 on its upload day, 21 October 2026 (observation), which is 1.42 per cent of litellm's 3,465,646 fetches that day (derived). Beside the comparison releases, per hour as the newest release, 1.107.0's index lies inside the range of all 17 under each of the three window ends, so the brief's pre-set reading applies: it "travelled as an ordinary newest release did in its first hours" (derived, by the rule fixed before the counts). Within that range it sits low under the maintainers' end of 13:23 UTC (17th of 18, at 0.68 times the comparison median) and near the middle only under Aikido's 12:47 (8th of 18, 1.04 times). The logs keep recording fetches of 1.107.0 on every day read after it was pulled, 1,431 from 22 October to 8 November (observation). ClickPy's rows for the March 2026 versions, 120,633 fetches from 24 March to 30 April, are within a factor of two of PyPI's own "over 119k" (R5), so the brief's calibration holds (derived).

A download is one fetch of one file. It is not an install, a running copy, a victim or an infection, and nothing here says any system fetched, ran or was compromised by 1.107.0. StepSecurity's count of "about 49,000" (R9) is theirs, unchecked, and set beside ours below rather than merged with it.

## Findings

Each figure is from tables/ as written by scripts/tabulate.py. "Comparison" is the brief's 17 ordinary newest releases of 1 October to 7 November 2026; "day 0" is a release's UTC upload day. ClickPy keeps no time of day, so nothing here places a fetch inside the window W (11:39:51 to 13:23 UTC): every fetch logged on 21 October, in W or after it, is a day-0 fetch.

### Checks before the measures (observation and derived; tables/daily_totals.csv, checks.json)

- No day from 17 March to 8 November 2026 is a gap by the brief's rule (derived). ClickPy returned more than one daily-total row for six dates (9 April, 2 August, 19 October, 2 November, 5 November, and five rows for 8 November); they are summed (see Method).
- ClickPy's daily totals against pypistats.org's "with mirrors", 14 October to 4 November: 0 of 22 days differ by more than 5 per cent, the largest difference 1.1 per cent on 19 October (derived). The brief's inconclusive rule (more than a fifth of days) is not met.
- ClickPy holds a 1.107.0 row on 21 October, and 21 October is not a gap, so measures 1, 3 and 5 are known for 1.107.0 (observation). All 17 comparison releases have a row on their day 0, so measure 3 has more than the ten known shares it needs (observation).
- 8 November, the last day held, came in five rows totalling 2,366,947, which matches pypistats.org's figure for that day exactly; it may still be re-ingested (S21), and read 2 will show whether it changed (unknown for now).

### Measure 1: the day-0 count (observation and derived; tables/m1_day0.csv)

- C0 = 49,043 fetches of 1.107.0 on 21 October (observation, ClickPy by version). By file type: 48,683 wheel and 360 source distribution (observation, ClickPy by file type; the two tables agree on the total).
- S0 = 49,043 / 3,465,646 = 1.42 per cent of litellm's fetches that day (derived).
- Set beside StepSecurity's "about 49,000 times from PyPI before removal" (R9, read 8 November 14:14 UTC, attributed to StepSecurity, method and interval not stated): C0 is 43 more (derived, a difference only). The brief does not judge which is right, and this record doesn't either. C0 counts the whole UTC day, including any fetch after 13:23, which ClickPy can't separate; StepSecurity's says "before removal". How close they are doesn't show that they counted the same thing; nothing read can show that (interpretation). A search summary of 8 November attributed "about 49,000" to Wiz, whose post was not opened.
- The source distribution: the reports read describe seven poisoned wheels (R1, R2). StepSecurity (R9) also describes a 1.107.0 source distribution with a tampered build configuration and, by its account, no payload. Whether that is so was not checked; the 360 source-distribution fetches are reported as fetches only.

## Method

- The brief's measures, implemented as written by scripts/tabulate.py, which reads only data/pre-brief-2026-11-08T1414Z/pypi_project_json.raw (L1) and data/read-2026-11-09T1432Z/ and makes no network request. Replay: `python3 -I studies/LH040/scripts/tabulate.py --read 2026-11-09T1432Z`; it rewrites tables/ (CSV, checks.json and summary.txt) and stops if a retained body does not match the SHA-256 its read logged.
- S0(R) is R's day-0 count over litellm's daily total from `pypi_downloads_per_day` (all versions). h(R) is built from a timeline of every final release in L1 by first-file upload time, with 1.107.0 added at 11:39:51 and removed at the window end. Missing evidence is classed as the brief says: on a non-gap day, no row is zero for a comparison release and "no record" for 1.107.0 or the March versions (none arose).

Choices where the brief was open, each in a comment in the script. All were made on 10 November after the writer had looked at the raw files' shape, and so after seeing 1.107.0's day-0 row, the March rows and the duplicate dates: they are decisions made after outcomes were seen, though none changes the question, comparison set, measures, pre-set reading or missing-evidence rules.

1. Duplicate daily-total rows for one date are summed, read as unmerged parts of ClickHouse's summing table; Q1 did not sum. The sums agree with pypistats.org's "with mirrors" on those dates: exactly on 9 April, 2 August, 2 November and 8 November, within 0.4 per cent on 5 November and within 1.1 per cent on 19 October.
2. The gap rule's seven days around d are d-3 to d+3, d included (LH011); at the ends of the series, the days held in that span are used.
3. "Mirror tools named by ClickPy" are bandersnatch, devpi, Nexus, Artifactory, z3c.pypimirror and pep381client. Every installer is also tabulated under its own name (tables/m5_installer_by_name.csv).
4. Each alternative window end applies to 1.106.2's second spell as well as to 1.107.0's h, so each end is one consistent timeline for all 18 releases.
5. Ranks count 1 as highest; a value's rank is one more than the number of releases above it. "Within the range" includes its ends.
6. The CI share is day 0 only, as measure 5 says, pooled over pip and uv fetches of the release; LH011 pooled days 0 to 2, which this brief does not ask for.
7. Measure 6 sums 24 March to 30 April, the end of the collector's March window; the brief says "each later day held".
8. The tail runs from 22 October to the last day held at the read (8 November).

## Limits

- A download is a fetch. It counts machines that fetch often more than once, counts mirrors and caches that fetch for others, and misses copies installed from caches, mirrors or container images (including LiteLLM's Docker images, R1). Nothing here measures installs, running copies, victims, credentials taken or who fetched.
- ClickPy keeps the day, not the time: no fetch is placed inside W, and the per-hour index rests on an even-spread assumption that is false in its details.
- One release is one case; nothing generalises to other compromised packages.
- The figures are ClickPy's copy of PyPI's logs as held on 9 November; ClickPy re-ingests days it finds wrong (S21), and read 2 re-reads day 0.
- Not checked: how StepSecurity, Wiz or PyPI counted; whether ClickPy attributes every 1.107.0 file to the version string "1.107.0"; whether logged tail fetches were served a file; the CI rule for pip and uv versions other than those LH011 read; whether 1.107.0's source distribution carried a payload; and whether robots.txt allowed this study's pre-brief requests (that is for LH047's re-audit). deps.dev was not read (measure 7).
- Unreviewed: no evidence review or reader review has read this record.
