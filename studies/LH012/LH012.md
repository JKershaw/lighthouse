## Answer

Pythons the newer releases no longer support are ruled out as the main reason for older downloads as a rule: for 32 of the 37 projects (86.5 per cent, project-resampled 95 per cent interval 75.7 to 97.3), less than half of the downloads on versions older than the release of a month before came from a Python that every newer release excludes, even counting downloads with no Python reported as excluded (derived measurement, on the Python the installer reports). For three projects such downloads are most of the older downloads, the brief's class "accounts for most": boto3 (76.3 to 77.0 per cent), aiobotocore (63.0 to 63.5) and numpy (62.5 to 66.7); the question stays open for pandas and urllib3. That class counts fetches that could not have been of R or later; it does not say why they were of the versions they were. For boto3 and aiobotocore few were at the highest version their Python admits, where a fresh install lands (6.7 and 0.6 per cent; numpy 56.7). Read after version 0.2 was released (post hoc, below), their count-by-version profiles are flat for long runs of versions below each Python's edge (for boto3 on Python 3.9, about a hundred at one level and several hundred at the next), the shape an installer's resolution walk leaves when it fetches each version it tries and rejects; on that reading most of those downloads were versions inspected and discarded, not installed (interpretation, untested). Identified bounds, the requirements that the 500 most downloaded projects declare, are in the brief's class "accounts for most" for four tightly coupled projects: pydantic-core (held by pydantic's exact pin), fsspec (by s3fs), and botocore and s3transfer (by boto3). B is an estimate under the co-fetch assumption, that each fetch of a held dependent version brings one fetch of the project, and the log does not observe that, since it counts each project's fetches separately; so the class says what identified bounds could account for, not what they did. The log's own ratios strain the assumption for botocore and s3transfer, boto3 being fetched 2.94 and 3.88 times as often as they are (7.51 times botocore on Python 3.9), and are most consistent with it for pydantic-core, whose dependent is fetched about as often (1.05 times) and whose older fetches sit largely at the one version pydantic's newest releases pin. Only for pydantic-core do the dependent's current versions carry a large part of the hold (53.2 per cent); for fsspec, botocore and s3transfer the hold comes from s3fs's and boto3's older versions, which moves the question to why those are fetched. On the same assumption, identified bounds are ruled out as the main reason for seven projects; they are undetermined for 26, mostly because requirements the log cannot resolve (extras, platform markers, versions not read) leave the upper estimate near one. Environments rebuilt from frozen lists cannot be counted in the log, as the brief said in advance; 18 of the 37 projects show their trace (most older downloads off every version a fresh install on the identified constraints would land on, and more often flagged CI than new downloads). So for most projects the public record rules out the Python explanation, cannot rule identified bounds in or out, and leaves what remains unmeasured, consistent with frozen lists but not shown to be them.

The first-day observation holds as a rule on releases no earlier study read: for 28 of the 32 projects with a release between November 2025 and March 2026 (87.5 per cent, interval 75.0 to 96.9), most releases' at-or-newer share on day 30 was within ten points of day 2. Within the band the share went on rising: it was higher on day 30 than on day 2 in 339 of the 355 releases (median 7.15 points, raised by the 238 releases of the three projects that release on most working days; 3.02 without them), and the median release rose for 31 of the 32 projects, by 3.2 points for the median project, so to settle is to stay within ten points, not to stop (a reading made after the counts, found by a replay in studies/LH016; version 0.4, review/v04_checks.txt). The band is absolute: by the brief's secondary descriptor, the ratio of the day-2 to the day-30 share, boto3's and aiobotocore's median releases held only 0.60 and 0.58 of their day-30 share on day 2, their shares being small (reported from version 0.3; firstday_releases.csv, summarised in review/r0018_check.txt, B).

Every share here is a share of downloads, which count fetches: an environment that fetches often without a cache counts many times (brief, "What each measure counts").

## Method

The brief (brief.md) fixed the question, the population, the read week, R, the measures, the dependents rule, the coverage rule, the conventions and the missing-evidence rules before any count, with the driving session's six decisions, and was snapshotted on issue #14 at 06:35 UTC. Phase 1 (collect_meta.py, collect_docs.py, frame.py) read metadata only, through a gate in common.py that refused any ClickPy query able to return a count. ## Limits

**A download is a fetch, not an installation or a run.** Every share weights environments by how often they fetch; the older share of boto3 is mostly downloads reporting one Python, and on the one day broken down one installer and system (post hoc reading), which may be one population fetching many times or many machines alike in those fields, and the log cannot say which. Mirrors and caches install without being counted.

**A download can be a version the installer tried and rejected.** A resolver that fetches each candidate it tries counts every one as a download; the log marks none of them. The profiles of boto3's, aiobotocore's and grpcio-status's older downloads have that shape (post hoc, above), so their older shares, and P for boto3 and aiobotocore, may count inspections rather than installations.

**B rests on the co-fetch assumption, and the log strains it.** B treats each fetch of a held dependent version as bringing one fetch of the project. The log records each project's fetches separately and never links one fetch to another, so B is a modelled estimate, never an observed count of held fetches, and its classes say what identified bounds could account for. Where the dependent is fetched far more often than the project (boto3 is 2.94 times botocore and 3.88 times s3transfer, and 7.51 times botocore on Python 3.9), the lower estimate can reach one without the dependent's environments carrying that many of the project's fetches; the guard checks only that the project's older fetches sit at versions the held dependent admits. "Accounts for most" for botocore and s3transfer should be read with that ratio.

**B's upper estimate saturates.** Requirements the log cannot resolve count as holding in the upper estimate: dependent versions beyond the coverage rule, Python-marked requirements on downloads with no Python, platform markers, and extras (brief, decision 3). Summed over dozens of dependents they exceed the older fetches of 26 projects, which are therefore undetermined; the record cannot rule identified bounds in or out for them.

**Identified bounds only.** Requirements declared by the read versions of the 500 most downloaded projects, directly or one step through another of the 37. Bounds in less downloaded projects, in applications and in private code are not read; "cannot be the main reason" for B always means among identified bounds.

**The Python is the client's.** pip reports the interpreter running it and uv the marker environment it is given; a cross-target fetch (`pip download --python-version`, a lock for another platform) reports the wrong Python, which the file pass also shows (Python 3.13 clients fetching cp310 wheels, in small numbers). ClickPy's documented import drops rows with no Python or system; whether its live ingestion does was not checked.

**One week.** 21 to 27 September 2026, a week LH011 also read; R is the release of 21 August or earlier. Another week, or a reference release closer to the day, could give other shares.

**Frozen lists are not measured.** The trace is a description consistent with frozen lists and with unidentified bounds and pins; nothing here counts environments rebuilt from lists.

**Classes in the grouped pass.** Versions ClickPy holds and PyPI does not list fall into "older" in the `pypi.pypi` passes (amendment 1); their volume is at most 6,832 downloads a project over the week, so the descriptors are not affected at the precision reported.

**Not checked.** BigQuery itself; linehaul's ingestion beyond its parser; country (not read); the hour of any download; whether any release was announced; any project outside the 37 and their dependents in the top 500; whether a yanked version was yanked before W.
