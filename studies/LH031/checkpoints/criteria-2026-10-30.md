## Classes

**Criteria: stated, on a model's rendering.** The catalogue page gives three thresholds: an assigned CVE ID; "reliable evidence that the vulnerability has been actively exploited in the wild"; and "a clear remediation action". The BOD 26-04 guidance, updated 25 August 2026, adds a fourth: "Carries significant risk to U.S. government information systems." In the rendering of the catalogue page, active exploitation is "reliable evidence that execution of malicious code was performed by an actor" "on a system without permission of the system owner". It counts attempts as well as successes. Code run on a honeypot is classed as attempted exploitation, and scanning, research and proof-of-concept code do not count. Those definitional sentences reached us paraphrased, not quoted, so their wording is unverified. The guidance, as rendered, quotes CISA saying it "also uses commercial threat intelligence platforms that provide data (i.e., honeypot detections and malware activity)" and that it "internally tracks unconfirmed reports until more evidence emerges".

**The alert: no sentence on observed exploitation, on the version rendered.** That version is the one "Last Revised October 26, 2026". The rendering finds no sentence saying CISA had observed exploitation, or had no evidence of it. The only related sentence is "CISA added CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 to its KEV Catalog on Oct. 26, 2026." The text of 21 October was not read, so SOCRadar's report that CISA said on 21 October it had not observed exploitation (brief, "What is already known") is neither confirmed nor contradicted.
