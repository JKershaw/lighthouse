**In short.** We asked how often npm's public download counts record the compromised bee-js 13.3.0 being fetched on 9 November 2026, the day it was on the registry for about 75 minutes, and in the two weeks after. Under the rules we fixed before reading any count, npm's figures put 202 fetches on 9 to 12 November together and none on each of 13, 14 and 15 November, but they can't say how many fell on the first day, because the windows that would show it don't add up; npm's per-version figures for the two later windows we read, ending 16 and 23 November, carry no record of the release at all. A download is a fetch, not an install or a victim. An evidence review has checked these figures against the retained reads; a reader-and-inference review and a recheck of whatever it changes come next.

## Answer

By the brief's rules as written (evidence-reviewed; reader review pending). npm's per-version counts record 202 fetches of 13.3.0 on 9 to 12 November taken together, and none on each of 13, 14 and 15 November (derived). How the 202 split between 9 November and the three days after it is unknown: the windows that would separate them fail the brief's clock rule, so 13.3.0's day-0 count is bounded, at most 202, and the brief's lower bound can't be formed because the window ending 16 November carries no record of 13.3.0 (derived). At most 202 is at most 0.1766 of the package's 1,144 fetches that day, 17.7 per cent, and the per-hour index is at most 3.386 (derived; no lower end). Fetches on 16 November, and in the week 17 to 23 November, are unknown: npm's answers for the windows ending 16 and 23 November hold no key for 13.3.0, which the brief classes as no record, not zero. Whether Socket's guess of "on the order of ten" fits can't be read: the bound, 0 to 202, reaches both inside and outside the brief's range of 4 to 31. No comparison release was published, so measure 5 isn't made. So the study is inconclusive on its central figure, the day-0 count: the window ending 9 November was read on time but fails the clock rule, which leaves the count only bounded, with a bound too wide for measure 6. The brief named this as inconclusive for a day-0 window not read, and named per-version totals that don't sum to the point total as leaving a window unfixable; reading the first as covering a window read but unfixable is Method's reading 1.

**The amendment question, decided.** Version 0.1 left open whether to use the three windows that fail the sum check. It is decided here: the brief's rule as fixed governs, no amendment is made, and the Answer above is the study's answer. The brief has no amendment other than the operational one of 14 November, which changes no scientific rule. Using those windows would change the clock rule after the counts were seen. The readings below stay outside the protocol, labelled as such, and no measure uses them.

**Outside the protocol (observation, and one post hoc reading).** Every per-version window npm answered with 9 November in it, including the three the rule sets aside, gave 13.3.0 exactly 202; if those windows could be used, the brief's formulas would give 202 on 9 November and none on 10 to 15 November. And in the window ending 23 November, npm's per-version answer left out 66 versions that the registry still lists, while no answer at any read held a zero value and that window's per-version totals still added up to the package total. Read after the counts were seen (one reading, post hoc): npm's per-version answer appears to leave out a version it counts no fetch for, so 13.3.0's absence from the windows ending 16 and 23 November is most simply read as no fetch of it counted by npm in those windows; the reads can't tell that apart from npm dropping a removed version's fetches from both its tables alike. The brief's rule stands, and this reading changes no figure in the Answer.

A download is one HTTP 200 response serving a tarball (E9). It isn't an install, a running copy, a victim or an infection; mirrors fetch each new version once, usually on its publication day (E9), and nothing here says any system fetched, ran or was harmed by 13.3.0. That 13.3.0 was malicious is StepSecurity's account (E3) and Socket's (E8), with the advisory OSV carries (E6); this study quotes it and doesn't test it.

## Findings

Every figure is from data/record_v1_output.txt, the output of scripts/tabulate.py over the nine retained reads under data/read-*/.

### Checks before the measures (observation and derived)

- All nine reads made all six requests, and every one answered HTTP 200; no retry was needed (observation, read logs). Collection stopped at the brief's stop with no gap (checkpoints/2026-11-24-closure.md).
- npm's per-version answer states no dates: it carries only `package` and `downloads` (observation). Each window is fixed by the brief's rule from the two point answers of the same read.
- **Clock rule.** At every read the two point answers agreed on start, end and total, and each stated window is seven days (observation). The per-version totals summed to the point total at the six reads of 13 to 17 November and 24 November, so the windows ending 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 and 23 November are held (derived). At the reads of 10, 11 and 12 November they did not: the per-version totals summed to 4,236, 4,643 and 4,999 against point totals of 4,026, 4,433 and 4,789, each 210 more (observation). Under the brief those three windows, ending 9, 10 and 11 November, are unknown, with every difference that uses them.
- The 210 excess is the same in the three windows that contain 5 November and absent from the first window that doesn't (6 to 12 November) (observation of a pattern). Nothing read says why. At every read the package's daily totals, summed over the stated window, equal the point total (4,026, 4,433, 4,789, 5,310, 5,144, 5,772, 6,298, 5,911 and 2,645), so the excess is on the per-version side.
- The package's daily totals for every loaded day were identical at every read that held them (observation). Each read's answer for its own UTC date was 0, the day not yet loaded (E7), and is set aside.

### Measure 1: the day-0 count and its share (derived)

- d(9 Nov) isn't exact, since the window ending 9 November is unknown by the clock rule. Bound by the brief: at most V of the earliest window held, V(12 Nov) = 202. The brief's lower bound, V(15 Nov) minus V(16 Nov), can't be formed: the window ending 16 November is held but has no key `13.3.0`, which the brief classes as no record, not zero. Only the trivial floor of zero remains.
- The package's total on 9 November: 1,144 (observation, request 4, the same at all nine reads).
- S0 is at most 202 / 1,144 = 0.1766 (17.7 per cent); its lower end is unknown.

### Measure 2: fetches after removal (derived)

- d(13 Nov) = d(14 Nov) = d(15 Nov) = 0, each from two held windows (202 minus 202 each time).
- d(10 Nov), d(11 Nov) and d(12 Nov) are unknown: each needs a window the clock rule leaves unknown. d(16 Nov) is unknown: the window ending 16 November has no record of 13.3.0, and d(9 Nov) isn't exact.
- The sum of d(10) to d(16) is unknown. The sums that can be formed, as the brief directs: d(9) to d(12) = 202, d(9) to d(13) = 202, d(9) to d(14) = 202, and d(9) to d(15) = 202.
- **The tail.** The weekly read of 24 November holds the window ending 23 November (17 to 23 November; point total 2,645, per-version sum 2,645). V(23 Nov) is **no record**: the answer has no key `13.3.0`. Fetches of 13.3.0 recorded from 17 to 23 November are therefore unknown, not zero.
- Whether npm's counts record fetches of 13.3.0 on any day after 9 November is unknown, not no. The pre-set sentence "npm's counts record fetches of 13.3.0 on days after it was removed" is not reported, since no day after 9 November has a count above zero that the rules can form.

### Measure 3: per-hour index (derived; a description, not a test)

- The minutes share is 75.1 / 1,440 = 0.05215. I = S0 / 0.05215 is at most 3.386; its lower end is unknown. The brief's caveats hold: fetches don't spread evenly over the day, and a mirror's single fetch isn't spread at all.

### Measure 4: the package's day (observation and derived)

- 9 November: 1,144 fetches of the package. The other days 2 to 16 November that the reads hold, fourteen days: median 697, range 181 (8 November) to 1,070 (12 November). d(9 Nov), beside them, is at most 202. Reported as numbers; no excess is attributed to 13.3.0.
- Daily totals, 1 to 16 November: 245, 614, 516, 394, 549, 853, 389, 181, 1,144, 923, 750, 1,070, 687, 1,017, 707, 757.
- Description only, not a measure: 17 to 23 November, 372, 410, 460, 441, 288, 335, 339 (range 288 to 460, sum 2,645, equal to that window's point total).

### Measure 5: comparison release

- None qualifies: at no read did the registry document hold a `time` entry for any version other than 13.3.0 after 9 November, final or pre-release (observation). Measure 5 isn't made; measures 1 to 4 describe one case with nothing like it in the same windows.

### Measure 6: Socket's estimate (derived)

- Socket's "on the order of ten" (E8) beside d(9 Nov): the pre-set reading needs d(9 Nov) from 4 to 31, or a whole bound inside or outside that range. The bound is 0 to 202 (and the brief's own lower bound couldn't be formed), which is neither, so **the reading is not made**. Socket's estimate stands as reported and unchecked.

### Measure 7: registry and OSV at each read (observation)

- At all nine reads, 10 to 17 November and 24 November: 13.3.0 not listed under `versions`; its `time` entry kept (2026-11-09T00:09:50.630Z); `latest` 13.2.0; no version deprecated; no `unpublished` entry; `time.modified` 2026-11-09T01:24:58.830Z; 177 versions listed; no version added or removed between reads.
- OSV: MAL-2026-18773, alias GHSA-hh7j-5m3j-23pf, present at every read, never withdrawn. Its `modified` moved from 2026-11-09T02:30:04Z (reads of 10 and 11 November) to 2026-11-11T21:15:15Z (reads of 12 November on, the 24 November read included), when a second origin, credited to Amazon Inspector, was added beside the GitHub advisory's; the added text describes the install hook in more detail, which this record doesn't repeat.

### Outside the protocol (observation; no measure uses it)

- 13.3.0 as answered in every per-version window, by stated end: 202 in each window ending 9 to 15 November; no record in the windows ending 16 and 23 November.
- Per-version keys against the registry's 177 listed versions: 178 keys (the 177 plus 13.3.0) at the reads of 10 to 16 November, 177 at 17 November, and 111 at 24 November, when 66 listed versions had no key. No answer at any read held a zero value; the smallest value was 1 to 5 at each read. The window ending 23 November balanced (2,645 against 2,645).
- The post hoc reading of these, in the Answer, is one reading made after the counts were seen. Nothing read for this study can test whether npm drops a removed version's fetches from both tables. A later npm study, of the compromised WPPConnect releases, classes an absent key in a balanced window under a rule its brief fixed before its own counts were read (studies/LH053/brief.md, "Missing evidence"); that applies the rule to a new case but doesn't test it.

## Method

The tabulator, studies/LH049/scripts/tabulate.py, was written at the checkpoint of 17 November, after the counts had been seen, to implement the brief's rules as written; it changes none of them. It reads only the retained bodies and logs and makes no request. For this version it was extended, on 24 November after the weekly read had been seen, in two ways, changing no rule: measure 2 now reports V(23 Nov) from the held window ending 23 November, which the 17 November version left as waiting for the weekly read; and four descriptive lines were added, used by no measure (each read's package daily totals summed over its stated window beside its point total, which version 0.1 reported without printing; each per-version answer's key count against the registry's listed versions; the package's daily totals 17 to 23 November; and 13.3.0's `time` entry at each read). Every other line of output is unchanged from 17 November, with the ninth read added.

Readings taken where the brief's wording leaves room, each marked `READING:` in the code or listed here:

1. **"Never read" includes "read but unknown".** The bound for d(9 Nov) is written for the case where the window ending 9 November is never read. Here it was read, but the clock rule makes it unknown. The tabulator applies the bound, since the missing-evidence section makes such a window unknown with every difference that uses it, so it can't give d(9) = V(9). The other reading (that the bound applies only to a window never fetched, leaving d(9 Nov) simply unknown) gives the same upshot: no exact day-0 count.
2. **"Earliest window held"** is taken as the earliest window that passes the clock rule and carries a 13.3.0 value, W(12 Nov).
3. **A held window with no key `13.3.0`** (W(16 Nov), W(23 Nov)) is "no record" by the brief, so V(16) minus V(15) is unknown and so are d(16 Nov), the brief's lower bound on d(9 Nov), and the tail for 17 to 23 November. The brief says in general that no record is not zero, and the tabulator follows that. Version 0.1 said the reads gave no sign of whether npm drops a version with no fetches from its answer; the weekly read now gives one (Findings, outside the protocol), read post hoc and not used.
4. **Measure 6 with only an upper bound.** The tabulator uses the trivial floor of zero where the brief's lower bound can't be formed; the reading isn't made either way, since 0 to 202 isn't wholly inside or outside 4 to 31.
5. **Package daily totals.** Each read's answer for its own date is set aside as not yet loaded; for other days the newest read's value is used, and the tabulator reports any disagreement between reads (there is none).
6. **Measure 5** checks every read's registry document for a final release above 13.2.0, other than 13.3.0, first timed 10 to 16 November. None was found, so the tabulator has no code for d0(R); it says so and must be extended before use if a release ever qualified.
7. The clock rule is applied with strict equality of totals, as written. The brief leaves no tolerance.
8. **No post hoc amendment.** The question version 0.1 left for the driver (whether to use the three windows that fail the sum check) is settled by keeping the brief as fixed; see the Answer.

## Limits

- **The clock rule removes the day-0 window.** The study's design rested on the read of 10 November, which it made on time; the per-version answer of that read doesn't add up to the point total, so under the brief it can't fix d(9 Nov). This is the main limit of the result. The brief foresaw totals that don't sum as a way a window could be lost, but not that it would take the day-0 window while later windows balanced.
- **The 210 discrepancy is unexplained.** Nothing read says whether it comes from the per-version table, the point table, or a correction on npm's side between 12 and 13 November. Lighthouse doesn't contact npm (a third party; AGENTS.md).
- **No record is not zero.** 13.3.0's absence from the windows ending 16 and 23 November fits no fetch of it counted by npm, and also npm dropping a removed version from its per-version answer and its package totals alike; the reads can't tell these apart, and the brief forbids reading the first into it. The weekly read's 66 absent listed versions make the first the simpler reading (post hoc, outside the protocol), not a measured one.
- **Windows ending 17 to 22 November weren't read**, by design; the tail is one seven-day sum, and its days can't be separated.
- **What a count counts.** A fetch, by npm's 2014 account (E9), not checked since. Mirrors and scanners are counted; copies from caches, mirrors or lockfile-pinned stores aren't. Nothing here says who fetched, installed or ran 13.3.0.
- **One case.** No comparison release was published; the package's other days are the only context, and no excess is attributed to 13.3.0.
- **Review so far.** An evidence review (notes/R-0129-lh049-v1-evidence.md) reproduced every figure with its own code from the retained reads; a second agent's agreement is a review contribution, not independent confirmation. The tabulator was written, and extended, after the counts were seen; its readings of the brief are listed above so a reader can judge them. The reader-and-inference review hasn't been done.
