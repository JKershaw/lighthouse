**In short.** We asked how often npm's public download counts record the compromised bee-js 13.3.0 being fetched on 9 November 2026, the day it was up for about 75 minutes, and afterwards. So far, unreviewed: npm's per-version counts give 13.3.0 exactly 202 fetches in every seven-day window that includes 9 November, and no record of it at all in the first window without that day; but under the rule the brief fixed, the three earliest windows can't be used, because their per-version totals don't add up to npm's own package totals, so the study can say only that 202 fetches fell on 9 to 12 November together and none on 13, 14 or 15 November. A download is a fetch, not an install or a victim. One more read on 24 November, then a reviewed record by 1 December.

## Answer (to date, interim)

To 16 November 2026, unreviewed, by the brief's rules as written. npm's per-version counts record 202 fetches of 13.3.0 on 9 to 12 November taken together, and none on each of 13, 14 and 15 November (derived). How the 202 split between 9 November and the three days after it is unknown: the windows that would separate them fail the brief's clock rule, so 13.3.0's day-0 count is bounded, at most 202, and the brief's lower bound cannot be formed because the window ending 16 November carries no record of 13.3.0 (derived). At most 202 is at most 17.7 per cent of the package's 1,144 fetches that day, and the per-hour index is at most 3.39 (derived). Whether Socket's guess of "on the order of ten" fits cannot be read: the bound, 0 to 202, reaches both inside and outside the brief's range of 4 to 31. No comparison release was published, so measure 5 is not made.

Outside the protocol, as an observation only: every per-version window npm answered with 9 November in it, including the three the rule sets aside, gave 13.3.0 exactly 202. If those windows could be used, the brief's formulas would give 202 on 9 November and none on 10 to 15 November. They can't be used under the brief as fixed, and this record does not use them; whether to is a decision for a dated, post hoc amendment, not this checkpoint (see Next).

A download is one HTTP 200 response serving a tarball (E9). It is not an install, a running copy, a victim or an infection, mirrors fetch each new version once, usually on its publication day (E9), and nothing here says any system fetched, ran or was harmed by 13.3.0.

- **Clock rule.** At every read the two point answers agreed on start, end and total, and each stated window is seven days (observation). The per-version totals summed to the point total at the five reads from 13 to 17 November, so the windows ending 12, 13, 14, 15 and 16 November are held (derived). Under the brief those three windows, ending 9, 10 and 11 November, are unknown, with every difference that uses them.
- The 210 excess is the same in the three windows that contain 5 November and absent from the first window that doesn't (6 to 12 November) (observation of a pattern). Nothing read says why; this record does not guess. The package's daily totals from request 4 sum exactly to each point total, so the excess is on the per-version side.
- The package's daily totals for every loaded day were identical at every read that held them (observation). Each read's answer for its own UTC date was 0, the day not yet loaded (E7), and is set aside.

## Method

The tabulator, studies/LH049/scripts/tabulate.py, was written at this checkpoint, after every count had been seen, to implement the brief's rules as written; it changes none of them. It reads only the retained bodies and logs and makes no request. Readings taken where the brief's wording leaves room, each marked `READING:` in the code:

1. **"Never read" includes "read but unknown".** The bound for d(9 Nov) is written for the case where the window ending 9 November is never read. Here it was read, but the clock rule makes it unknown. The tabulator applies the bound, since the missing-evidence section makes such a window unknown with every difference that uses it, so it can't give d(9) = V(9). The other reading (that the bound applies only to a window never fetched, leaving d(9 Nov) simply unknown) gives the same upshot: no exact day-0 count.
2. **"Earliest window held"** is taken as the earliest window that passes the clock rule and carries a 13.3.0 value, W(12 Nov).
3. **A held window with no key `13.3.0`** (W(16 Nov)) is "no record" by the brief, so V(16) minus V(15) is unknown and so are d(16 Nov) and the brief's lower bound on d(9 Nov). The brief does not say whether such a window may still serve as evidence that nothing was fetched; it says the opposite in general ("no record ... not zero"), and the tabulator follows that. Every one of the 177 listed versions had at least one fetch in every window, so the reads give no sign of whether npm drops a version with no fetches from its answer.
4. **Measure 6 with only an upper bound.** The tabulator uses the trivial floor of zero where the brief's lower bound cannot be formed; the reading is not made either way, since 0 to 202 is not wholly inside or outside 4 to 31.
5. **Package daily totals.** Each read's answer for its own date is set aside as not yet loaded; for other days the newest read's value is used, and the tabulator reports any disagreement between reads (there is none).
6. **Measure 5** checks every read's registry document for a final release above 13.2.0, other than 13.3.0, first timed 10 to 16 November. None was found, so the tabulator has no code for d0(R); it says so and must be extended before use if a release ever qualified.
7. The clock rule is applied with strict equality of totals, as written. The brief leaves no tolerance.

## Limits

- **The clock rule removes the day-0 window.** The study's design rested on the read of 10 November, which it made on time; the per-version answer of that read does not add up to the point total, so under the brief it can't fix d(9 Nov). This is the main limit of the interim result and was not foreseen by the brief.
- **The 210 discrepancy is unexplained.** Nothing read says whether it comes from the per-version table, the point table, or a correction on npm's side between 12 and 13 November. Lighthouse does not contact npm (a third party; AGENTS.md).
- **No record is not zero.** The disappearance of 13.3.0 from the window ending 16 November is consistent with no fetch of it on 10 to 16 November, and also with npm dropping a removed version from its per-version answer; the reads cannot tell these apart, and the brief forbids reading the first into it.
- **What a count counts.** A fetch, by npm's 2014 account (E9), not checked since. Mirrors and scanners are counted; copies from caches, mirrors or lockfile-pinned stores are not. Nothing here says who fetched, installed or ran 13.3.0.
- **One case.** No comparison release was published; the package's other days are the only context, and no excess is attributed to 13.3.0.
- **Interim and unreviewed.** No reviewer has read this record or rerun the tabulator. The tabulator was written after the counts were seen; its readings of the brief are listed above so a reviewer can judge them.
