# LH047 brief: the rules of the road

**In short** (added 8 November 2026; not part of the protocol). Websites and data publishers ask automated readers to behave in particular ways: stay out of some paths, don't train on this, slow down, prove who you are. We'll write down each of these requests from its own specification, say who enforces it, if anyone, and how anyone could tell it was being followed; then gather what others have published about how well crawlers follow them; then check every request Lighthouse itself made on the evening of 8 November against the rules that applied, by a rule fixed here before any request is checked. We already know of one overstep that evening (two requests to a dashboard's API that its robots.txt asks agents not to make), and we know of features of our own collectors that may turn up others; the audit asks whether there are.

## 1. The question

Three parts, as the task sets them.

- **A. The catalogue.** Each mechanism by which a site or publisher states what automated readers may do: what it asks, of whom, whether it is advisory or enforced and by whom, how a reader can tell it is being heeded, and which of P-0022's classes it puts a source in (a rule followed voluntarily; a route not considered; paid; licensed; private; ephemeral).
- **B. How well each is heeded, by public evidence.** Published measurements of compliance, each attributed, with what it rests on and its window. No claim in Lighthouse's voice that a named crawler or company breaks a rule.
- **C. How well we heed them.** For each request in the retained fetch logs of this evening's studies and essay reviews, the rule that applied and whether the request complied, judged by section 5's rules.

Disclosure, carried into every output: Lighthouse runs on Anthropic's models, its requests leave from an address block registered to Anthropic (studies/LH044, checkpoint of 8 November), and Anthropic runs crawlers and fetchers (ClaudeBot, Claude-User, Claude-SearchBot and others) that are subjects of part B. Evidence about Anthropic is reported exactly as about anyone else, and every source found in part B that measures or reports on Anthropic's crawlers is included, whichever way it reads.

## 5. The rules, fixed before any request is checked

Each request gets a verdict under each rule: **complies**, **does not comply**, **unknown** (the rule in force could not be read) or **not applicable**.

- **R1, robots.txt, reading A (own token).** RFC 9309 as implemented in `scripts/robots9309.py`: the group(s) naming the product token `Lighthouse-research` exactly (case-insensitive), else the `*` group(s), combined; longest match wins, allow wins a tie; `*` and `$` as specials; percent-encoded unreserved octets unencoded; the path with its query; `/robots.txt` always allowed. A robots.txt answering 4xx allows everything; one that answered 5xx or not at all at the time of the request (as logged) means complete disallow, so a request sent then does not comply; a robots.txt redirect is followed through retained copies, else through the current re-read. A group whose name is a prefix or extension of the token (for example `Lighthouse`) is reported, not obeyed. Applies to every automated request, crawling or not: Lighthouse's ground rules say to respect robots.txt, without an exception for API calls. Where the operator's own documentation, read for this study from a host that is not audited, invites programmatic use of a disallowed path, the request is still "does not comply" under R1 and is flagged "documented API path" so the tension is visible.
- **R2, robots.txt, reading B (Anthropic tokens).** As R1, and in addition, for each of the seven Anthropic tokens in ai.robots.txt's list as read for this brief (anthropic-ai, ClaudeBot, Claude-Web, Claude-User, Claude-SearchBot, Claude-Code, Claude-Agent), the group naming that token exactly, with no fallback to `*`; the request does not comply under R2 if R1 fails or any such group disallows its path. Whether a group addressed to an Anthropic token applies to Lighthouse, whose requests are made by Claude agents in Anthropic's cloud under a Lighthouse User-Agent, is open and has been put to the keeper (the coordinator, 8 November 2026). This brief does not decide it: both readings are tabulated and reported side by side.
- **R3, context only.** Whether any of the 204 tokens in ai.robots.txt's robots.json has a group that disallows the request's path: a count of requests to places some AI crawler is asked to stay out of. No verdict.
- **R4, usage preferences.** From the R1 group's `Content-Signal` (Cloudflare) and `Content-Usage` (AI Preferences drafts) lines, read against Lighthouse's use. `ai-train=no` or `train-ai=n`: complies (Lighthouse trains no model; Limits says what this does not cover). `ai-input=no` or `ai-use=n`: does not comply if the body was read by a language model (pages fetched for an agent to read: articles, blog posts, statements, reports), complies if it was only parsed by a script into counts. `search`: not applicable. Cloudflare's experimental `use=` field: `use=reference` or `use=immediate` with the full body kept in the public repository does not comply (keeping and republishing a copy is beyond "index, excerpt, and link back"); `use=full` complies. A use the line does not mention carries no preference. With no such line, not applicable.
- **R5, rate.** Per host, the largest number of requests in any 60-second window and the shortest gap, per study. Does not comply if (a) the R1 group sets `Crawl-delay: N` and two consecutive requests to that host by one study were less than N seconds apart (Crawl-delay is not in RFC 9309; reported as advisory), or (b) a documented limit known under section 4 was exceeded by Lighthouse's own requests. Separately, and not a site's rule: whether each study kept the pacing its own brief or collector states.
- **R6, walls.** A wall is a response of 429, 401, a 403 or 503 carrying a challenge marker, a challenge or proof-of-work page, or a redirect to a login or challenge path. Does not comply if, after the wall, any later request in the window went to the same URL (beyond a single retry a study's brief states, after any Retry-After), or to the same host with a different User-Agent or route, or if a challenge was answered.
- **R7, terms and licences.** Where section 4 gives the terms: does not comply if what was kept or published goes beyond them. Elsewhere: "terms not read", never complies by default.
- **R8, identification.** Complies if the User-Agent names Lighthouse, or is a tool's default that does not claim to be a browser (labelled "unlabelled"); does not comply if it claims to be a browser; unknown where the tool's User-Agent is not visible (WebFetch).

A request's overall verdict under reading A is "does not comply" if any of R1, R4, R5, R6, R7 or R8 says so, "unknown" if none does but R1 is unknown, and "complies" otherwise; under reading B, the same with R2 in place of R1. The known overstep is counted and reported as known before this brief.

## 6. Missing evidence

A robots.txt that cannot be read for the time of the request, and cannot be re-read now, is **unknown**, never compliant. A current re-read stands in for the file in force at the time only with that label; a file that has changed since is a limit, not a finding. An unlogged request whose response is not visible counts as sent, with its rule verdicts where the rule does not depend on the response. A refused or unreadable part B source is recorded as unread, not as a negative.

## 7. Part A and part B method

- **A.** One row per mechanism: robots.txt (RFC 9309); AI-specific user-agent tokens and shared block lists (ai.robots.txt); Crawl-delay; the robots meta tag and X-Robots-Tag; Content Signals; AI Preferences (Content-Usage, and the training-signal draft and policy token Anthropic says it honours); TDMRep and the EU text-and-data-mining reservation it serves; RSL licences; terms of service and data licences; rate limits and HTTP 429; challenges and proof-of-work walls; login walls and API keys; bot authentication and verified-bot schemes (Web Bot Auth, published IP lists and reverse DNS, Cloudflare's verified bots); pay-per-crawl (HTTP 402); llms.txt, which offers rather than forbids; and others found. Columns: what it asks; of whom; advisory or enforced, and by whom; how a reader can tell it is heeded; its status (standard, draft, vendor feature); P-0022 class. Each row cites the retained primary source or the operator's own documentation.
- **B.** A bounded search (about ten WebSearch queries, logged with time and links in searchlog.md; a result is a lead, not a source), then each cited source opened with `scripts/fetch.py` and retained. Inclusion: a published measurement or operator's report of whether automated readers heed one of part A's mechanisms, saying what it rests on. For each: who published it, when, its window, its method and sample, the figure as they state it, what it rests on, and its limits as they or we see them. Figures are not pooled across sources. A named company appears only inside an attributed claim. Every source found on Anthropic's crawlers is included. At most about 40 requests, at most three per host, none to an audited host.

## 8. The comparison that would matter

For part C: the share of requests that comply under reading A and under reading B, by rule, by study and by kind of reader (collector script, ad hoc command, tool fetch), and whether the faults cluster in any one collector or practice. Described, not tested: this is one evening's census, not a sample.

## 10. Resource ceiling and stopping

One robots.txt re-read per audited host that has no retained copy (at most 248 requests, one per host, two seconds apart, following redirects up to five hops), and nothing else to any audited host. At most about 40 part B requests. The study stops when every request in the population has a verdict under every rule, or "unknown". The record is version 0.1, unreviewed.

## 11. What would leave it inconclusive

If most requests' robots.txt cannot be read for the time, part C would say what was compliant on current files only. If the transcripts are incomplete, the unlogged count is a floor. If part B finds no measurement with a stated method, it says so rather than citing claims.
