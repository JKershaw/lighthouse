# The rules of the road

**In short.** Websites ask automated readers to stay out of some pages, not to train on what they read, to slow down or to prove who they are; most of these asks are requests that nothing enforces, and a site can only tell whether they were heeded from its own logs. Published measurements disagree about how well crawlers heed them, partly because they count different things, and the operators of AI assistants' fetchers say robots.txt may not apply to them at all. Checking all 3,497 requests Lighthouse made on the evening of 8 November 2026, we found that 185 broke a rule as our brief fixed it, against 2 we already knew of: mostly reads of PyPI's documented JSON interface, which PyPI's robots.txt asks crawlers to avoid, and one reviewer's requests sent under a name that did not say they were Lighthouse's (unreviewed). Next: a review of this record, then a short paper for readers who have never thought about robots.txt.

## Answer

**Part A (catalogue.md).** Twenty mechanisms by which sites and publishers state what automated readers may do, each from its own specification or operator. Fetching rules (robots.txt and its AI tokens, Crawl-delay) are advisory and visible in a site's logs; use rules (Content Signals, the IETF's AI Preferences, TDMRep, RSL) govern what happens after the fetch, which no site can see; enforcement, where it exists, comes from servers and intermediaries (rate limits, challenges, paywalls, verified-bot lists), not from the rules. Interpretation, unreviewed.

**Part B.** The published measurements read do not agree, and they do not measure the same thing. TollBit, which sells licensing and paywall tools to publishers, reports that about 15 per cent of AI scrapes on its European and North American publishers' sites in the first half of 2026 fetched pages the publisher had disallowed to that bot by name, up from 3.3 per cent in the last quarter of 2024 by its own earlier reports. University researchers who ran their own test sites from September 2024 to March 2025 found seven of nine AI crawlers that visited, ClaudeBot among them, obeyed robots.txt, and most of 23 third-party assistant fetchers did not. Cloudflare reported in August 2025 that Perplexity's declared crawlers were blocked and an undeclared one carried on; Perplexity's reply could not be read (it sat behind a challenge). Each figure is its publisher's, from its own data. Operators' own documents explain part of the gap: OpenAI, Google and Perplexity say robots.txt may not apply to fetches a user asks for, which a measurer counting by robots.txt counts as bypassing.

**Part C (derived measurement, unreviewed).** Of 3,497 requests (3,446 in the studies' fetch logs, 51 found only in the agents' transcripts), under reading A (the groups naming Lighthouse's own token, else `*`) **3,301 complied, 185 did not and 11 are unknown**. Under reading B (also any group naming one of seven Anthropic tokens) 204 did not; restricted to Anthropic's agent tokens (Claude-User, Claude-Agent, Claude-Code), 190 did not. The 185 are:

- **89 reads of PyPI's JSON interface** (`/pypi/<name>/json`), which PyPI's robots.txt disallows to every crawler and PyPI's own documentation describes as an interface for programs: 85 by LH041's collector, which reads no robots.txt, and 4 by the essay's three reviews and its drafting agent. A tension between two of the operator's own statements, not a hidden act; under the brief's rule it does not comply.
- **61 requests by the essay's reader review** under the User-Agent `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; research-reader/1.0)`, which names no one; 2 of them are also among the PyPI reads.
- **24 requests that broke a Crawl-delay** (advisory, not in RFC 9309): 6 by LH042 to arXiv, 2 seconds apart against a 15-second delay; 18 by LH044, where the only earlier request was LH044's own read of robots.txt 2 seconds before.
- **5 requests to two hosts whose robots.txt could not be read** (web.archive.org, dashboard.shadowserver.org), which RFC 9309 treats as "complete disallow"; none of the five was answered.
- **5 pages kept whole in the repository where Cloudflare's experimental `use=reference` asks for no more than an excerpt**: declawed.io's front page (LH042) and four LH044 bodies.
- **2 requests to declawed.io's `/api/`**: the overstep already known from the incident note.
- **1 read of `/api/` on www.githubstatus.com by LH046**, disallowed for `*` in the current file (the one in force at the time was not kept).

No request was found that retried a wall, answered a challenge or changed its name after being refused. No documented rate limit that this study could read was exceeded. The rule as it now stands (the keeper, 8 November 2026: obey groups naming Lighthouse's token or an agent token of the provider running the session, else `*`) would also forbid the requests that only reading B catches: under Anthropic's agent tokens, 5 more (three ad hoc reads of declawed.io, whose file disallows Claude-User; LH044's probes of wiki.gentoo.org and ftp.eq.uc.pt); counting all seven Anthropic tokens, 19 more, most of them groups addressed to ClaudeBot, Anthropic's training crawler.

**The faults, stated plainly.** Lighthouse's own collectors and agents did not all read robots.txt before reading a site: LH041's collector, LH043's, LH044's and LH045's pre-brief reads, LH046's source reads, all three essay reviews and every ad hoc command did not, and every robots.txt breach except one came from these. LH042's collector read robots.txt, could not reach it, and fetched anyway, against RFC 9309. LH044's collector cut robots.txt at 64 KiB, below RFC 9309's 500 KiB floor. One reviewer sent 61 requests under a name that did not identify Lighthouse. 51 requests are in no fetch log in the repository, the known overstep among them. None of these is a claim about intent; the transcripts show what was sent, not why.

## Method

1. **Brief first.** The rules R1 to R8, the population and the sources were fixed and committed (0fbec8c) before any request was checked or any part B figure gathered. The keeper's decision on Anthropic's tokens arrived after the brief and before tabulation and is recorded as a dated note; it changed no rule.
2. **Population.** `normalise_logs.py` reads the 26 fetch logs into one table; a templated row in the reader review's log is expanded into its 38 requests. `transcript_requests.py` lists every Bash command and WebFetch call in the session's 19 transcripts, up to 20:27 UTC, that could reach the network (196 collector runs, 39 WebSearch calls, 30 git operations, and 57 other calls: 48 curl or inline-client commands, 6 that sent no request to a third party (the local proxy's status, a local server, a file write), 2 runs of scratchpad scripts that read local files, 1 WebFetch); the other calls were read by hand and their requests written to adhoc-requests.csv with transcript and line; those also in a fetch log within 15 seconds were dropped as duplicates.
3. **robots.txt.** `robots_index.py` indexes the 194 copies the studies kept and the 62 current re-reads; `audit.py` picks a copy per request in the brief's order, evaluates it with `robots9309.py` under both readings, and records the rule that decided. A copy answered by this container's GitHub policy counts as unread.
4. **Other rules.** Usage preferences from the applicable group's `Content-Signal` and `Content-Usage` lines, read against what each request's body was used for (read by a model, or parsed by a script; inferred from the log and the URL) and whether the body is in the repository; Crawl-delay from the same group; walls from status codes and LH044's classes; terms from LH045's quoted clauses; identification from collectors' code and the transcripts.
5. **Clarifications made after the first tabulation (labelled).** (a) R4's `use=reference` rule counts only 2xx responses as collected content, after it was seen to catch two 429 error pages; this moved two LH044 requests from "does not comply" to "complies". (b) Requests answered by the container's GitHub policy are counted as not reaching the site, with robots.txt "unknown". (c) A copy kept by the same study after the request is labelled as such rather than as another study's. (d) The reader review's User-Agent class (F7).
6. **Part B.** Eleven WebSearch queries for leads (logged in searchlog.md, with the six read for part A before the brief), then each cited source fetched once with `fetch.py`, at most three per host and none on an audited host; 12 sources requested, 8 read, 2 behind challenges, 2 not sent because their robots.txt disallowed this reader.
7. **Replay.** `sh studies/LH047/replay.sh` rebuilds every part C table offline.

## Limits

- **The transcripts are this session's only.** Requests made by earlier sessions that evening, by OpenAI Codex (which wrote and revised the essay *It was only a test* earlier on 8 November), or inside scripts outside the repository are not seen; the unlogged count is a floor.
- **robots.txt in force at the time.** 184 verdicts rest on a file re-read after the event; a file changed in between would change them. Copies another study kept are up to about 25 minutes from the request (web.archive.org's, used for LH044's pre-brief reads).
- **What a body was used for is inferred** from the log and the URL; an agent may have read machine data, or a script parsed a page.
- **Crawl-delay counting the robots.txt read** as a request is the brief's rule; most sites probably mean the gap between page requests, under which 6 rather than 24 requests break it.
- **"Lighthouse trains no model"** covers Lighthouse; whether Anthropic uses content read through its products is governed by Anthropic's terms, not read here. Keeping full bodies in a public repository also makes them available to others' crawlers.
- **Terms were not read** for 3,466 requests; "terms not read" is not compliance.
- **Part B** is a bounded search; the evidence on Anthropic is what was found, and absence of a finding is not evidence of compliance or of its lack. TollBit's per-bot figure for Anthropic's bots was not read.
- **One evening.** This is a census of one night's requests by one project, not a rate for agents in general.
