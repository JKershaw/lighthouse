# The rules of the road

**In short.** Websites ask automated readers to stay out of some pages, not to train on what they read, to slow down or to prove who they are, and most of these asks are requests that nothing enforces. Published measurements of how well crawlers heed them disagree, partly because they count different things and partly over what robots.txt covers: OpenAI's, Google's and Perplexity's pages say it may not apply to fetches a user asks for, Anthropic's page says its bots honour it, and the published evidence on Anthropic's bots (Lighthouse runs on Anthropic's models) is mixed. Of the 3,497 requests we could find that Lighthouse made on 8 November 2026 between 18:57 and 20:26 UTC (a floor), 113 broke a rule as our brief fixed it, against 2 we already knew of; 59 more, all from one reviewer whose User-Agent named no one, we classed as breaches only after seeing them (post hoc, one choice), for 172 (a derived measurement, after an evidence review and a reader-and-inference review). These count requests that broke a stated rule, not incidents or harm: 90 of the 172 were answered with HTTP 404, among them 88 of the 89 requests to PyPI's documented JSON interface, which PyPI's robots.txt asks crawlers to avoid, and 146 came from two practices, one collector that read no robots.txt and that reviewer's User-Agent; next comes a short paper for readers who have never thought about robots.txt. A later week, 16 to 22 November, after Lighthouse put its collectors behind a shared robots.txt check on 9 November, was re-audited under rules fixed before it was judged: of 1,649 logged requests, 2 broke a robots.txt rule under our own name (3 adding Anthropic's names), and 53 broke some rule, mostly by coming sooner than a site's requested delay or by going back on a later day to a page that had answered with a wall (unreviewed). That week's count of requests no log recorded is a floor of zero, because no session kept an extract of its transcript, so the two audits can't be compared as a trend.

## Answer

**Part A (catalogue.md).** Twenty mechanisms by which sites and publishers state what automated readers may do, each from its own specification or operator. Fetching rules (robots.txt and its AI tokens, Crawl-delay) are advisory and visible in a site's logs; use rules (Content Signals, the IETF's AI Preferences, TDMRep, RSL) govern what happens after the fetch, which no site can see; enforcement, where it exists, comes from servers and intermediaries (rate limits, challenges, paywalls, verified-bot lists), not from the rules. Interpretation, after both reviews.

**Part B.** The published measurements read do not agree, and they do not measure the same thing. TollBit, which sells licensing and paywall tools to publishers, reports that about 15 per cent of AI scrapes on its European and North American publishers' sites in the first half of 2026 fetched pages the publisher had disallowed to that bot by name; its earlier reports gave 3.3 per cent for the last quarter of 2024 and 13.26 per cent for the second quarter of 2025, across all its sites (TollBit's own comparison runs only to 2025; setting the 2026 figure beside them is this record's, across cohorts that differ). University researchers who ran their own test sites from September 2024 to March 2025 found seven of nine AI crawlers that visited, ClaudeBot among them, obeyed robots.txt, and most of 23 third-party assistant fetchers did not. Cloudflare reported in August 2025 that Perplexity's declared crawlers were blocked and an undeclared one carried on; Perplexity's reply could not be read (it sat behind a challenge). Each figure is its publisher's, from its own data. Operators' own documents may explain part of the gap (interpretation): OpenAI, Google and Perplexity say robots.txt may not apply to fetches a user asks for, which a measurer counting by robots.txt counts as bypassing. The evidence on Anthropic's bots, read the same way, is mixed: the university test found ClaudeBot obeying robots.txt, and TollBit's 2025 test saw Claude-User read robots.txt before fetching its page; but TollBit could not match 12.7 per cent of the 2026 scrapes naming Claude-User to Anthropic's published addresses (0.65 per cent for ChatGPT-User, 0.33 for Perplexity-User), 27 per cent of those from an address that appears to be Anthropic's but was not yet listed, and Cloudflare wrote in August 2025 that Anthropic "still lags in verification", "leaving its compliance effectively unclear". The spoofing figure says how far a site can trust the name, not whether Anthropic's own bots obeyed; no source read measures how often Anthropic's bots bypass a disallow (TollBit's per-bot figure was not read); and Anthropic's statement that its bots honour robots.txt is its description of itself, as the other operators' pages are of themselves.

**Part C (derived measurement; after an evidence review and a reader-and-inference review).** Of 3,497 requests (3,446 in the studies' fetch logs, 51 found only in the agents' transcripts), under reading A (the groups naming Lighthouse's own token, else `*`) **3,315 complied, 172 did not and 10 are unknown**. Under reading B (also any group naming one of seven Anthropic tokens) 191 did not (133 under the brief as fixed); restricted to Anthropic's agent tokens (Claude-User, Claude-Agent, Claude-Code), 177 did not (118). The 172 are:

Evidence about Anthropic, which is mixed: one controlled test found ClaudeBot compliant (2024 to 2025); TollBit's 2025 test saw Claude-User read robots.txt before fetching its test page; in 2026 TollBit could not match 12.7 per cent of scrapes naming Claude-User, and 5.87 per cent naming Claude-SearchBot, to Anthropic's published addresses (under 2 per cent across Anthropic's bots; 27 per cent of the Claude-User share, it says, from an Anthropic address not yet published), the Claude-User figure the highest of the eleven bots in its table; its per-bot bypass figure, which would show Anthropic's bots, was not read, so the text read neither singles out nor clears them; Cloudflare called Anthropic's compliance "effectively unclear" for want of verification (August 2025); Reddit alleges unlicensed access in court, which Anthropic disputes. ## Method

1. **Brief first.** The rules R1 to R8, the population and the sources were fixed and committed (0fbec8c) before any request was checked or any part B figure gathered. The keeper's decision on Anthropic's tokens arrived after the brief and before tabulation and is recorded as a dated note; it changed no rule.
2. **Population.** `normalise_logs.py` reads the 26 fetch logs into one table; a templated row in the reader review's log is expanded into its 38 requests. `transcript_requests.py` lists every Bash command and WebFetch call in the session's 19 transcripts, up to 20:27 UTC, that could reach the network (196 collector runs, 39 WebSearch calls, 30 git operations, and 57 other calls: 48 curl or inline-client commands, 6 that sent no request to a third party (the local proxy's status, a local server, a file write), 2 runs of scratchpad scripts that read local files, 1 WebFetch); the other calls were read by hand and their requests written to adhoc-requests.csv with transcript and line; those also in a fetch log within 15 seconds were dropped as duplicates.
3. **robots.txt.** `robots_index.py` indexes the 194 copies the studies kept and the 62 current re-reads; `audit.py` picks a copy per request in the brief's order, evaluates it with `robots9309.py` under both readings, and records the rule that decided. A copy answered by this container's GitHub policy counts as unread.
4. **Other rules.** Usage preferences from the applicable group's `Content-Signal` and `Content-Usage` lines, read against what each request's body was used for (read by a model, or parsed by a script; inferred from the log and the URL) and whether the body is in the repository; Crawl-delay from the same group; walls from status codes and LH044's classes; terms from LH045's quoted clauses; identification from collectors' code and the transcripts.
5. **Clarifications made after the first tabulation (labelled).** (a) R4's `use=reference` rule counts only 2xx responses as collected content, after it was seen to catch two 429 error pages; this moved two LH044 requests from "does not comply" to "complies". (b) Requests answered by the container's GitHub policy are counted as not reaching the site, with robots.txt "unknown". (c) A copy kept by the same study after the request is labelled as such rather than as another study's. (d) The reader review's User-Agent class (F7). (e) The overall verdict is "unknown" when R8 is unknown as well as when R1 is, a departure from the brief's rule that moves one request (the WebFetch call) from "complies" to "unknown"; kept, and disclosed by the evidence review. (f) Corrections of version 0.2, made by the evidence review after every figure was seen: R5 judges the later request of a pair, a robots.txt answered with a 2xx challenge page is unread, and a request for /robots.txt is always allowed (Corrections).
6. **Part B.** Eleven WebSearch queries for leads (logged in searchlog.md, with the six read for part A before the brief), then each cited source fetched once with `fetch.py`, at most three per host and none on an audited host; 12 sources requested, 8 read, 2 behind challenges, 2 not sent because their robots.txt disallowed this reader.
7. **Replay.** `sh studies/LH047/replay.sh` rebuilds every part C table offline.

## Limits

- **The transcripts are this session's only.** Requests made by earlier sessions that evening, by OpenAI Codex (which wrote and revised the essay *It was only a test* earlier on 8 November), or inside scripts outside the repository are not seen; the unlogged count is a floor.
- **A class made after the result was seen.** The reader review's User-Agent is classed "does not comply" post hoc (F7); 59 of the 172 rest on that choice alone, and the count under the brief as fixed is 113. Of the six choices made after the first tabulation (Method 5), it is the only one that added breaches.
- **robots.txt in force at the time.** 184 verdicts rest on a file re-read after the event, 9 of them among the 172 (4 among the 113); a file changed in between would change them. Copies are up to about 25 minutes from the request (web.archive.org's, LH044's own later read, used for LH044's pre-brief reads). The declawed.io file quoted in the incident note was not indexed; the current re-read stood in, with the same verdicts.
- **What a body was used for is inferred** from the log and the URL; an agent may have read machine data, or a script parsed a page.
- **Crawl-delay counting the robots.txt read** as a request is the brief's rule; most sites probably mean the gap between page requests, under which 2 rather than 11 requests break it. The brief's separate check, whether each study kept the pacing its own brief or collector states, was not reported.
- **403 bodies outside LH044 were not checked for challenge markers** by the audit; the review found three challenges among them (F9).
- **"Lighthouse trains no model"** covers Lighthouse; whether Anthropic uses content read through its products is governed by Anthropic's terms, not read here. Keeping full bodies in a public repository also makes them available to others' crawlers.
- **Terms were not read** for 3,466 requests; "terms not read" is not compliance.
- **Part B** is a bounded search; the evidence on Anthropic is what was found, and absence of a finding is not evidence of compliance or of its lack. TollBit's per-bot figure for Anthropic's bots was not read.
- **One evening.** This is a census of one night's requests by one project, not a rate for agents in general.
- **Week 47's unlogged requests weren't seen.** No session in the week committed a transcript extract, so requests no log recorded (ad hoc reads by drivers and reviewers, the Horizon sweeps of 17 and 18 November, the robots.txt surveys behind the keeper's decision of 21 November, LH032's reads) are outside the count; it is a floor, and the floor of zero isn't comparable with 8 November's 51.
- **Week 47's robots.txt at the time** is unknown for 596 page requests, judged on files of 23 November; a file changed since is a limit, not a finding. Amendment 2 sat in the repository during the week it names, so sessions could have read it.
- **Week 47's post hoc choices** (listed in its README, each labelled in its code): classing rules for the candidate logs, reclassing LH039's gate refusals (which removed 36 apparent R1 failures), counting one gate call's duplicate rows once, resolving LH044's robots.txt redirects to their last hop, the strictest of several Crawl-delays (2 study-host pairs), body use for R4, and the same-day or later-day split of R6. None changes a rule.
- **Week 47's replay depends on clone depth for one label.** In the subagent's shallow clone the collector version of reads counted from code was the clone's boundary commit (cc80694, 12 November); in a full clone it is each collector's last commit before the run (for example 4c9b77c, 9 November). No verdict, table or count changed (checked by the Research run of 23 November, comparing requests-judged.csv column by column: only `collector_version` and `note` differ).
- **The keeper's decision of 21 November** came during week 47 and is not part of its protocol; context columns only.
