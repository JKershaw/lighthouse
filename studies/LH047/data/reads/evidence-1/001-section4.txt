## **Section 4: Robots.txt and the Bypassing Problem**

<UserStory>
  - Robots.txt is not protecting European publishers: ~15% of AI scrapes on
  European sites in H1 2026 ignored an active disallow instruction. - The median
  amount of bypassed scrapes for a European site is 2.8x that of a North
  American site. - For the major training crawlers, European sites disallow more
  than North American sites — CCBot 63% vs 46%, GPTBot 60% vs 41%, ClaudeBot 53%
  vs 43%. - ChatGPT-User, Bytespider, and Youbot bypass robots.txt instructions
  on nearly 50% of all European sites that have explicitly disallowed them.
</UserStory>

### 4.1 Disallow Adoption

<InfoBox title="Robots.txt & Bypass Definitions">

**Robots Exclusion Protocol (robots.txt)** — a voluntary handshake agreement,
in place since the early days of the Internet, by which a website uses a
robots.txt file to signal which automated user agents are permitted to
access it. It has no enforcement mechanism and depends entirely on the
goodwill of those operating the bots.

**Disallow** — an instruction in a publisher's robots.txt file naming a
specific user agent that the publisher does not permit to access its
content. AI developers note it may take 24-72 hours for disallow to be
logged and reflected by their bots.

**Bypass** — when an AI bot scrapes a publisher's content despite that
publisher having explicitly disallowed that bot in its robots.txt file.

</InfoBox>

Disallowing a bot in robots.txt only matters if the bot obeys the guidance,
but a measurable share of AI bots do not. Across European and North American
sites in the first half of 2026, ~15% of AI scrapes bypassed a disallow
instruction.

European publishers have disallowed the following bots on robots.txt at a
higher rate than North American sites. 63% of European sites disallow CCBot,
against 46% in North America. The same holds for GPTBot (60% vs 41%),
ClaudeBot (53% vs 43%), PerplexityBot (50% vs 40%), and OAI-SearchBot (46% vs
34%).

For the newer real-time user agents, the ones that fetch pages live to
answer a prompt, European disallow adoption drops off and North America
moves ahead, often by more than double. Only 9% of European sites disallow
Claude-User, compared to 26% in North America. Perplexity-User is 13% versus
26%. Most of the newest agents sit in the single digits in Europe.
ChatGPT-User is the exception among the AI user agents, and is blocked at a
rate closer to the established crawlers, most likely because it is the most
widely recognized.

### 4.2 The Bypass Problem

For the median (typical) European site, bypassed scrapes are 2.8x times a
typical North American site. These bypasses represent the website being
accessed against the publisher's stated wishes, consuming server resources
in the process, and, for AI bots, rarely returning a human visitor in
exchange.

Given that European publishers are scraped at a 4x higher rate by AI bots
than North American sites, it's not too surprising that their median
bypassed scrapes would appear higher.

### 4.3 Bypass by AI Bot

This brings us to another metric: the bypass rate, which is the percentage of
all scrapes that ignored explicit robots.txt guidelines and successfully
scraped content anyway.

ChatGPT-User had the highest bypass rate, ignoring explicit robots.txt
instructions on 54% of all its scrapes. It is followed by Bytespider with a
bypass rate of 48% and PerplexityBot with a bypass rate of 42%.

For the AI bots that have a smaller bypass rate, there often appears to be an
interesting trend of the bypass rate increasing between Q1 & Q2. For example,
the data shows GPTBot, CCBot, and DuckAssistBot all bypassing more often than
in Q1, an early sign that bypassing behaviors may be spreading beyond the
handful of agents that dominate it today.

Robots.txt files were designed for an era when honoring it cost a crawler
little, and defying it gained it little and cost the site little. As AI
tools and traffic explode, that balance no longer holds. AI bots take
publisher content, cost the sites yet serve them, yet send back very few
human visitors in return.

Because the robots.txt protocol is not active enforcement and rests on the
goodwill of the operator, European publishers who rely on it alone today are
protected only if AI developers choose to cooperate, and for the bots doing
the most scraping, abidance of robots.txt remains thin.
