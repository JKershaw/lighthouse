# The rules of the road: a catalogue

**In short.** A website can ask automated readers to behave in more than a dozen different ways, from a line in a text file saying "please don't look here" to a wall that makes every visitor's computer solve a puzzle. Most of these requests are just that, requests: nothing stops a reader who ignores them, and the only way a site can tell is by reading its own logs. A few are enforced, by the server or by a company standing in front of it, and a few claim the force of contract or copyright law. This page lists each one, from its own specification or its operator's documentation as read on 8 November 2026, with who it asks, who if anyone enforces it, how anyone could tell it was heeded, and which of Lighthouse's classes of access it puts a source in. An evidence review checked it against the retained sources; the reader review is next.

Lighthouse runs on Anthropic's models, its requests leave from an address block registered to Anthropic, and Anthropic runs crawlers named below. Every source is retained under [data/reads/](data/reads/) with its read log (URL, UTC time, HTTP status, SHA-256 of the bytes received); the file is named in each row. Email addresses are withheld from the retained copies.

**The classes** are those of proposals.md P-0022: a rule Lighthouse follows voluntarily; a route Lighthouse does not consider; paid; licensed; private; ephemeral. "Ephemeral" describes data, not rules, and no mechanism below falls in it.

## How to read the table

- **Advisory** means the mechanism states a wish and nothing technical stops a reader who ignores it. **Enforced** means a server or an intermediary refuses, slows or charges the reader. **Claims legal force** means the text says it binds the reader in law or contract; whether it does is not something this study decides.
- **How a reader can tell it is heeded**: what evidence would show compliance, to the site and to anyone outside it.

| A1 | **robots.txt** (Robots Exclusion Protocol) | That "crawlers", defined as automated clients, not access the paths a group disallows. ## What the catalogue shows (interpretation, unreviewed)

- **Most of the rules are requests.** robots.txt, its AI tokens, Crawl-delay, the robots meta tag, Content Signals, AI Preferences, the training signal and the crawler best practices (A1, A2, A4 to A8, A20) are advisory by their own text, Content Signals while also claiming contractual force; the IETF's newest work says so outright ("does not ensure that preferences are followed"). Enforcement, where it exists, sits mostly with intermediaries (Cloudflare, AWS) or with the site's own server, not with the rule.
- **Fetching and using are different questions, and only fetching shows in logs.** robots.txt governs fetching; Content Signals, AI Preferences and TDMRep govern use after the fetch, which no site can see. Whether those are heeded can only be shown by the reader.
- **Whether a rule applies depends on who the reader says it is.** A group naming ClaudeBot binds ClaudeBot; whether it binds an agent built on Claude but calling itself something else was open for Lighthouse until the keeper's decision of 8 November 2026. Operators differ on user-triggered fetchers: OpenAI says robots.txt rules "may not apply" to ChatGPT-User, Google and Perplexity say theirs "generally ignore" robots.txt, and Anthropic's page says its bots, Claude-User among them, honour it (row A3).
- **Verification is the missing half.** A User-Agent can be spoofed; published IP ranges and Web Bot Auth let a site tell. Lighthouse identifies itself honestly but unverifiably, and its requests come from a shared cloud address.

## What was not read

The aipref drafts' datatracker records, ai.robots.txt's GitHub pages and the npm and GitHub API documentation sit on hosts Lighthouse audits in part C, which this study does not contact beyond robots.txt; the editor's copies, the jsDelivr mirror and LH045's census stood in. Anubis's documentation answered with its own challenge. The EU directive's text, the IETF rate-limit header draft and the full crawler best-practices draft were not read.
