**In short.** Two security firms report that from 10 November 2026 someone used stolen GitHub credentials to open about 62,000 to 63,000 harmless-looking probe pull requests, then planted a script that steals build secrets where it would run. Counting GH Archive's public hourly files from 9 to 26 November, we find 595 events opening a pull request from the probe's branch in the hours of one firm's search window and 606 in the other's, under 1 per cent of what each firm's GitHub search counted (655 in all, the first in the hour from 13:00 UTC on 10 November), and over the following two weeks 6 closing events and 21 branch deletions, in the two hours in five whose files record deletions at all. The low share says more about the files than about the campaign: in one firm's window the files hold fewer pull request events of every kind than that firm's probe count, so they could never have held most of it, and at this coverage the closing counts can't show how fast the probes were cleaned up. This is version 1.2 of the record: both reviews' fixes are made, and a recheck comes next, and nothing here is released before them.

## Answer

Derived measurement, unreviewed. GH Archive's files hold **a small fraction** of the probe pull requests the vendors' GitHub searches counted, by the reading the brief fixed before any count was read (under 10 per cent): 595 opened events against Aikido's 61,839 (0.96 per cent) and 606 against StepSecurity's "more than 63,000" (0.96 per cent, an upper estimate). The first probe pull request event is in the hour 2026-11-10-13 (branch creations are unknown in the start hour and in 28 earlier gap hours), the hour after the vendors' stated start; the start hour itself holds no probe pull request event. From 10 to 25 November the files hold 6 closing events against 655 openings (0.92 per cent cumulative) and 21 probe branch deletions in the hours where deletions are recorded. No opening is held after 12 November; at this coverage that is weak evidence that new probes stopped (StepSecurity reported new ones every few minutes at 07:40 UTC on 12 November). Post hoc context, one comparison (R-0153): over Aikido's 27 hours the files hold 41,014 pull request events of every kind, so Aikido's 61,839 is 1.51 times everything of that type the files hold, and the share was capped well below the brief's 50 per cent before any probe was matched; over StepSecurity's 56 hours they hold 85,894, so a share of 50 per cent there would have needed probes to be over a third of all pull request events held. What sets the files' hourly volume is not known from this study. None of the brief's four conditions for an inconclusive result was met, though measure 4 came close to the fourth (gap hours throughout).

What this cannot say. It counts events in a public feed as GH Archive collected it, not pull requests, repositories, accounts, thefts or victims. It cannot say how many repositories ran the stealer, which secrets were taken, who closed a probe or why, how many distinct repositories or accounts were touched, or why the files hold so few of the probes, beyond the post hoc context that they hold few pull request events of any kind. It cannot say how fast the probes were cleaned up: with about 1 in 100 probes held, few closing events would appear in the files whatever happened on GitHub. The vendors' "about 90%" and "92%" still open describe GitHub's search state at their times; the files' 0.92 per cent closed describes events held in the feed. They are set side by side, not compared as rates. Most of the campaign's first day and a half falls in hours with no branch or push events, so creations, deletions and pushes there are unknown, not zero.

Beside the brief's stated expectation (a few thousand to some tens of thousands of openings for 10 to 12 November; closing events in the low thousands): both counts are below it.

## Method

1. Replay, from the repository root:

        python3 -I studies/LH051/scripts/tabulate.py > studies/LH051/record-output.txt
        python3 -I studies/LH051/scripts/compare_posts.py > studies/LH051/record-posts-output.txt

   Both read only retained files and fetch nothing. The tabulator reads data/hours.csv and data/counts.csv, which hold aggregate counts and no identifier.
2. The tabulator of 17 November was run unchanged. It takes the last held hour as the end of the range and treats a UTC day with all 24 hours held as complete, so it needed no copy or edit for the full window: 25 November is the last complete day.
3. The implementation choices are the checkpoint's seven, kept unchanged, since the brief contradicts none of them: (1) distinct counts are per hour, marker, type and action, reported as written and never added; (2) review events keep their own actions (created, updated), and measures 3, 5 and 7 use PullRequestEvent only; (3) onset is the first held hour with any M1 event, which in gap hours means a pull request or review event; (4) measure 4 sums both series over the non-gap hours from onset to 23:00 on 12 November, without pairing hours; (5) a complete day has all 24 hours held, "closed" is GH Archive's action and includes merged pull requests (the merged flag is not read), deletions count all ref_types and only non-gap hours, and the cumulative ratio runs from 10 November; (6) measure 6 leaves unknown hours out of the median and range rather than counting them as zero, and "after" runs from 2026-11-10-12 to the last held hour; (7) measure 7's per-hour means and the split at 13 November are descriptive additions that test nothing.
4. One choice added for this record: the vendor posts are compared by the figure strings and the set of figure-bearing prose sentences, after dropping code and markup, as scripts/compare_posts.py says.
5. Collection followed the brief: one collector, five reads, robots.txt asked per URL, each file streamed and discarded after counting, no identifier written, no held hour re-fetched. Collection closed on 26 November (checkpoints/2026-11-26-closure.md).

## Limits

- **Gap hours.** 257 of 421 hours, including most of 9 to 11 November, carry no branch or push events. Creations, deletions and pushes there are unknown, which leaves measure 4 on 3 events, makes every deletion count a lower bound and means the late M1 pushes have no earlier comparison.
- **Lower bound.** Every count is a lower bound on GitHub's public events of that kind. The files carry no commit message or pull request title, so the stealer's commits, reverts and run deletions are not visible at all, and private repositories are outside the source.
- **The search counts are vendor reports.** 61,839 and "more than 63,000" are the vendors' accounts of GitHub searches at their times, not checked here and not a census; the shares in measure 3 are only as good as them.
- **Windows approximate.** The hour files cover Aikido's window from 45 minutes early and StepSecurity's to 20 minutes late.
- **Collection-time filing.** GH Archive files events by the hour it collected them; `created_at` was not used, so an event can sit in a later hour than it happened.
- **No re-read.** A held hour was fetched once. A later rewrite or backfill of any file would not be seen; no announcement was looked for.
- **Merged counted as closed.** A merged probe pull request would count as closed; the merged flag was not read.
- **Distinct counts are per cell.** No count of distinct repositories or accounts across hours exists or can be formed under the brief's retention rules.
- **No cause.** Why the files hold under 1 per cent of the vendors' counts is not measured. Interpretation: the record rules out only LH046's gap, the missing branch and push events, as the explanation for pull request events (measure 7). LH046's other finding, pull request events at roughly half their earlier level, cannot be tested here.
- **One case.** Measure 3 is one marker in one campaign; it is not a general coverage rate for GH Archive.
- **Unreviewed.** No second agent has reproduced any figure yet.
