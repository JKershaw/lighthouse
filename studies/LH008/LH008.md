## Answer

In this sample, pins and locks moved after both, and neither drew most of them. Across four libraries in the top 50 by downloads (urllib3, cryptography, pyjwt, starlette), 102 kept repository and library pairs held a version below a fix when it was released; 73 moved to the fix or later by 27 September, a median 18.7 days after the release (and, for the 54 that moved after the advisory, a median 10.5 days after it), 28 had not moved and one stopped pinning the library. The rate of moves rose in the two days after the release (8 moves, 4.1 per 100 repository-days) and again in the week after the advisory (8 moves in its first two days, 5.1; 14 in days two to seven, 4.3), against 1.3 to 1.7 per 100 repository-days from the third day after the release to the advisory and in the three weeks after the advisory's first week (derived measurement; data/hazard.csv, figure rates.svg). The release's spell lasted two days: from day two to day seven, stopping at the advisory where it came first, the rate was 1.5. No burst like LH007's appeared: 4 of the 102 moved within a day after the advisory, where LH007 found 6 of 13 `mcp` projects within 28 hours (observation; LH007 is one library and one evening).

- **Release and advisory, side by side** (derived; data/summary_by_frame.csv). Within two days of the fixed release, 8 of 102 moved; within two days after the advisory, 8 of 102. 19 of the 73 moves came after the release and before the advisory; 54 after it. For the two libraries whose advisory came weeks after the release (pyjwt 25 days, starlette 23), 13 of 30 moves came before the advisory.
- **A release no advisory named moved pins more slowly.** In the comparison frames (the same four libraries, an earlier release of the same kind that no advisory named as fixed), 5 of 91 moved within two days, 21 within 30 days (23 per cent, against 50 of 102, 49 per cent, for the fixes), and the rate fell to 0.6 per 100 repository-days from day 7 to day 30. Of the 45 comparison moves, 21 came after the next advisory against the library was published, 14 of cryptography's 20 after an advisory 13 days after its comparison release (derived). Part of the difference is activity: 28 of the 43 comparison repositories that had not moved made no commit to their default branch more than 30 days after the release (interpretation: dormant, not waiting).
- **Who moved them** (observation of git authorship, as classified in the brief; data/by_authorship.csv). Of the 73 moves to a fix, 22 were commits authored by a bot (Dependabot 17, Renovate 5), 16 were a person merging a bot's branch (Dependabot 13), and 35 were a person's. Of the 8 moves in the two days after the advisory, 3 were bot commits, 2 merges of a bot's branch and 3 a person's; of the 8 in the two days after the release, 4 were bot commits. No bot commit message in the two days after an advisory calls itself a security update: their messages have the same "Bump X from a to b" and "Update X to v" form as the bots' moves outside that window in this sample (so the bot's word is silent here, not negative). Two of the three people's commits in that window cite an advisory or "dependabot security alerts".

What this does not say: which moves the advisory caused. The advisory's two days and the release's two days carry eight moves each, a count at which the difference between 4.1 and 5.1 is noise. A fixed release may say in its own notes that it fixes a security flaw before any advisory is published; this study did not read release notes, so "before the advisory" is not "before anyone knew" (interpretation). A move is a request in a repository, not an installation or a run, and no project here is said to have been exposed.

## Method

The brief (brief.md) was written before anything but ClickPy's table list was read, with the library, comparison, frame, pin and authorship rules; the first amendment records the libraries the rule chose, before any dependent was read; the second records three changes to how the plan was carried out, made after the screening and before any commit after a snapshot was read; the third corrects a time in the first and a pattern in the authorship classifier, found after the moves were read. The scripts, in order: select_libraries.py (ClickPy's top 200, OSV for each, PyPI release times, the events), osv_ranges.py (the five advisories' ranges), collect.py frame (Open Source Insights' lists, SHA-256 order, first 60), collect.py screen (repository, blobless clone, snapshot, pins and locks at the snapshot), collect.py moves (first-parent commits after the snapshot that touch a pin or lock file, found with `git diff-tree --stdin` against each commit's first parent, read in order to the first move), trailers.py, analyse.py (lags, summaries, rates, authorship), summarise_reads.py and draw_figure.py. Every table in data/ is written by them; analyse.py, trailers.py and draw_figure.py need no network, but trailers.py needs the clones in /tmp/lh008, which are not kept in the repository.

Choices made where the brief left room: a lockfile that locks the library twice counts at its lowest version; a requirements file counts as a lockfile if its first 3,000 characters say it was generated by pip-compile, `uv pip compile` or an export command; a bare Poetry version counts as an exact pin; at most 80 pin or lock files per repository were read at the snapshot, shallowest first; the rates use the clone time as each repository's end of observation.

## Limits

- **Four libraries and one advisory each.** The rule took the four most downloaded with a qualifying advisory; three of the four advisories were published within 48 minutes of each other, among ten GitHub advisories against those three libraries that evening, so one evening's publishing weighs heavily, as in LH007. A repository reacting to any of that evening's advisories against its library is counted against the one the rule chose.
- **The frames are Open Source Insights' lists**, capped at 100 listed entries (urllib3's two frames, pyjwt's and starlette's comparison frames listed 100 of 233 to 288); 110 of the 436 screened packages (25 per cent) named no repository and 47 clone attempts (35 repositories) were refused; how Open Source Insights chooses its listed sample was not read.
- **Pairs are not independent.** 102 fix pairs come from 88 repositories; a repository that moves several libraries in one commit (for example a lockfile refresh) counts once per library.
- **Authorship is read from git alone.** A squash or rebase merge by a person of a bot's pull request shows the bot as author and is counted as a bot; a person's commit that copies a bot's text, or a bot running under a person's name, is misread; two merged branches counted as a bot's were release or sync branches whose tip github-actions wrote (vantage6, grooveshop-django-api), not dependency updates. A first run of the classifier also took a person's branch named for Snyk (`fix/snyk-high-severity-deps`) as a bot's; the pattern was corrected and the moves re-read before any table here was written. Pull-request bodies and labels, where Dependabot marks a security update, were not read, because GitHub's API is refused here. The "bot's word" was therefore read only from commit messages, where neither Dependabot nor Renovate said "security" in the window after an advisory.
- **Times are git's.** The committer time of a merge or GitHub squash is close to when it reached the default branch; a rebased or cherry-picked commit's may not be; pushes are not in git. Author and committer times differ by more than a day for one move (geonode), which does not change which window it falls in, but does change its reading: the change was authored 14.8 hours before the advisory and reached the default branch 18 hours after it, so the lags from the advisory are lags of the merge, not of the bot's proposal. Where a squash merge rewrites the author time, as in cytomine's, git keeps no trace of when the bot proposed the change.
- **The advisory time is OSV's `published` of the GHSA record.** GitHub's own pages were not read; pyjwt's NVD and PYSEC records a week after its release are a second public time, reported alongside. Release notes and changelogs, which may have named a security fix before any advisory, were not read.
- **Small counts.** Eight moves in each two-day window; the brief ran no significance test, and the rates should be read with Poisson noise of that size in mind.
- **A move is a request.** It changes what the repository's next build or install would take; nothing here says what was installed, deployed or run, or whether any project was exposed to an advisory.

## Next

The candidate question this raises: how much of a fix's uptake in pinned software happens before its advisory, because the release itself says it is a security fix? Reading the release notes and changelog entries of these four fixed releases (and a few more) for security language, and setting the moves before each advisory against them, would test whether "release versus advisory" is really "announced versus unannounced". A second: reading Dependabot's pull requests themselves, where they are public, would separate security updates from version updates, which git alone cannot.
