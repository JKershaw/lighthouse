## Answer

Not as a rule in either band, by the brief's convention; the share of projects whose new releases mostly reached half of downloads within two days falls from the top fifty to each band below it. The share of a new release still settles by day 2 as a rule in band A, and for some projects in band B.

For projects ranked 51 to 500 in ClickPy's August 2026 downloads (band A), 25 of the 52 drawn projects with a release between April and August 2026 had most of their releases reach half of the project's downloads, counting that release or any later one, on the first or second day after release: 48.1 per cent, with a project-resampled 95 per cent interval of 34.6 to 61.5 (derived measurement). For projects ranked 501 to 5,000 (band B), 9 of 35: 25.7 per cent, 11.4 to 40.0. The brief's convention calls a share below one half "not as a rule", so both bands read "not as a rule"; band A's interval includes one half, so in another draw it could read "for some projects". Beside them, as a description and not a test, LH011's top fifty: 26 of 37, 70.3 per cent, 54.1 to 83.8. Pooled over releases, 114 of 295 releases in band A (38.6 per cent) and 38 of 196 in band B (19.4 per cent) reached half within two days, against 127 of 424 (30.0 per cent) in the top fifty, where three near-daily releasers weigh on the pooled figure.

LH012's settling test (the at-or-newer share on day 30 within ten points of day 2 for most of a project's releases) held for 39 of 50 projects in band A, 78.0 per cent (66.0 to 88.0), "holds as a rule", and for 23 of 35 in band B, 65.7 per cent (51.4 to 80.0), "holds for some projects" (derived measurement). Beside them, LH012 found 28 of 32 top-fifty projects settling on November to March releases, and 35 of 37 on LH011's April to August releases as a description.

Described only: in the median project of each group, a new release's early pip and uv downloads carried the build-server (CI) flag less often than the replaced release's on the same days, by 14.2 points in band A (interval 18.3 to 12.3 points below) and 7.1 in band B (13.3 to 0.0 below), against LH011's 6.6 for the top fifty; 50 of 52 projects in band A and 23 of 35 in band B were below zero. Mirror tools named by pypistats (bandersnatch, Artifactory, devpi) made a median of 148 downloads of a band A release and 62 of a band B release on its first two days, a median 0.01 and 0.23 per cent of the release's own downloads; leaving them out changed no project's outcome (derived measurement). A difference between groups is a description: rank is not a cause, and nothing here says why a release was fetched.

## Method

The brief fixed the question, the bands, the draw, the release rule, the measures, the conventions, the missing-evidence rules and the ceilings on 29 September 2026, before the ranked list or any count was read, and was snapshotted at 353f17f. Amendment 1 recorded the frame (the list, the draw, the releases, the CI subsample, the mirror installer names and the days held) before any count, at 514f4bd, and three cases the brief had not foreseen, each handled before any count: `pypi.pypi`'s own coverage was not read, since its rows-read statistic would be a download count; an installer named `pip` followed by a zero-width space is not `pip`; two packaging tools outside LH011's list were drawn and stay. Nothing was changed after counts were seen; the one reading made after them is labelled post hoc above.

Scripts, in order: collect_schema.py (phase 1: the `system` database only), collect_frame.py and frame.py (phase 2: the ranking, the PyPI walk, the installer names, the days held; the frame offline), collect_counts.py (phase 3: G01 daily totals, V per-version counts, M mirror counts, C per-download CI reads, in the brief's order with projects alternating between the bands), analyse.py (every table in data/analysis/, offline) and draw_figure.py (uptake.svg). common.py's gate refused every count before phase 3 and every query naming a day after 27 September; every query on the `pypi` database set `read_overflow_mode = 'throw'`, because the `demo` user's default returns a partial result without error past a billion rows read. `sh studies/LH014/replay.sh` checks the brief and amendment 1 against their posted hashes, reruns the frame, the analysis and the figure over the retained files into a temporary directory, compares every output byte for byte, and checks the reads against the ceilings and the cut; it passed when this record was written. The release review's own recount of every tested and quoted figure from the retained files, and its eight live re-reads of ClickPy, are kept with their output in review/ (notes/R-0019.md).

The at-or-newer share is LH011's, from `pypi_downloads_per_day_by_version`, with versions below each project's earliest qualifying release folded server-side into one row a day. The mirror readings come from `pypi_downloads_per_day_by_version_by_installer_by_type` on each release's days 0 to 2, with and without the mirror class, so both shares of a difference come from one table. The CI reads took `pypi.pypi` for each subsample release's days 0 to 2 and its two versions, batched per project. Intervals are percentile intervals over 10,000 resamples of a band's projects with a known measure, from one generator seeded 20260929 and drawn in the brief's order.

Reads: 293 ClickPy queries of 800, reading 1,371,500,991 rows of 60 billion by ClickHouse's own statistics (the largest single query 101,818,368; 1,097,596,928 of them for the CI reads); 160 PyPI JSON requests of 400; two documentation pages of five. ## Limits

**A download is a fetch.** Each row is a file PyPI served; an environment that installs from scratch many times a day counts as often as it fetches, and one behind a cache or mirror once or not at all. A low share is not by itself slow uptake by people or projects (LH011, Limits).

**Two samples beside a census.** The top fifty is every project with a release among the fifty most downloaded; each band is 80 projects drawn at random, of which 52 and 35 released. The intervals, thirteen to fourteen points either side, carry the sampling; band A's primary share, 48.1 per cent, sits just below the one-half line its interval crosses, and its settling share, 78.0 per cent (75.6 without the overlap projects), just above the three-quarter line. The groups differ in many ways besides rank: the gradient is a description, not a cause.

**The unit decides the pooled view.** A few projects carry many releases: cyclopts 28, transformers 26, langchain 23 and cfn-lint 23 in band A; djlint 33, zensical 26 and dagster-shared 22 in band B. Per project each counts once; pooled figures lean on them.

**Mirrors and proxies.** Only the four installer names pypistats gives were separated. Repository managers such as Nexus, which appears among the installers here, caches and scanners were not, so the mirror readings are of those four tools only.

**Projects that are tools or very young.** The frame kept LH011's tooling list unchanged, so hatch, editables and pre-commit's dependencies (identify, cfgv, nodeenv) stay in, as do three projects whose downloads began in 2026 (httpcore2, nab-index, pydantic-ai-shields); the post hoc reading above shows what leaving them out would do.

**Overlap with LH012's reads.** 48 of band A's 80 projects had their 21 to 27 September downloads read by LH012; 19 of their releases have later days in that week, including day 30 for those of 22 to 28 August. The primary outcome (days 1 and 2) touches none of those days.

**The build-server flag** is a floor on builds: pip and uv flag CI only when one of four variables is set (K5), older pip and uv versions were assumed to follow the same rule, and 3.0 and 4.3 per cent of the subsample's downloads came from installers whose flag is not known. `pypi.pypi`'s own coverage was not read (amendment 1).

**A day, not an hour; a lenient band.** ClickPy keeps the day, so day-0 shares are not comparable between releases. LH012's ten-point settling band is lenient for small shares: band B's median day-2 share was 0.81 of its day-30 share.

**Not checked.** BigQuery itself; why any release was or was not fetched; the hour of any download; weekday effects; any day after 27 September 2026; any project outside the 160 drawn beyond the top fifty's retained analysis.
