# Who decides when a robot may use force?

Trade reports passing on what its maker says describe Phantom, a humanoid robot, as designed to carry up to 44 pounds "including lethal weaponry". On who controls it, two reports disagree. In December 2025 TechCentral wrote that Phantoms "will not have full autonomy when it comes to making decisions in combat"; in May 2026 The Next Web reported the company's chief executive saying that in certain time-critical situations the robots would need to make fully autonomous decisions. Both are intentions, for a version nothing we read shows in the field with a weapon.

None of the sources we could read on 13 November 2026, about Phantom or about the police patrol robots at Singapore's Changi Airport, says who would pick a target or decide to use force. They say how far each robot has got, mostly in the maker's or the government's words. The airport sources even explain how officers take over a robot's movement. They describe no weapon.

So the question stays open. A week later, by its own account, the Geneva weapons conference left the subject with its expert group, to draw up "an instrument, without prejudging its nature" and report in 2028.

The main limit: the two cases that might answer it, a reported Gaza proposal for Phantom and an armed comparison robot, couldn't be read. One was closed to us, by the site's robots rules and then by the site itself; the other's official page returned an error, and one search found no replacement.
