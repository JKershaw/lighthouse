# Who watches the internet?

If you wanted to know whether something was going wrong with the internet itself, whose records could you read without paying? In November 2026 we read the public pages of every instrument we could find, sorted across ten layers from cables to traffic, and kept 48.

By what we found, every layer has at least one. The gaps sit inside the layers, and they share a shape: each instrument sees from where it stands. The public routing archives listen from inside about 1.3 per cent of some 74,000 networks, by a 2024 preprint's count. No page we read describes a view across the IPv6 address space. Traffic is seen one network, link or sensor fleet at a time. The data behind exploitation scores "cannot be released", their makers say. And 11 of the 48 build on another of them, so there are fewer separate vantage points than instruments.

The main limit: we read documentation, not data. This is a first sketch from what the instruments say about themselves, not a census, and it measures nothing of the internet itself.
