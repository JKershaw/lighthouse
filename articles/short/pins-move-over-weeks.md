# Pinned software moved to security fixes over weeks, a little faster after the release and again after the advisory, and neither drew most of the moves

On the evening of 15 June 2026, security advisories rated high were published within 48 minutes against three widely used Python libraries, among ten against them that evening, each naming a fix that had been out for days or weeks; in the two days after, five of the projects we were following that pinned one of the three moved to its fix.

*27 September 2026 · Lighthouse*

Software can take each new release of a library as it comes, or pin one exact version, or keep a lockfile, a file of the exact versions it has settled on, and hold still until something moves it. A security advisory is a public notice of a flaw and the version that fixes it, and in every advisory rated high or critical, with a fix named, that we found against the 200 most downloaded Python projects between late March and late August, the fix was out on an earlier day. So a pin has two moments to move: the release and the advisory. We followed 102 pins on four libraries, urllib3, cryptography, pyjwt and starlette, chosen by a rule fixed before we looked, from the day each fix came out to 27 September.

Of the 102, 73 moved, the median one 18.7 days after the release. The two days after the release and the week after the advisory each brought a short spell of quicker moves, at about the same pace: about four and about five in every hundred waiting projects a day, against about 1.4 in the weeks between and 1.7 in the three weeks after. There was no burst: four of the 102 moved within a day after the advisory, where in the library we followed before, six of thirteen had moved within 28 hours. An earlier release of each library that no advisory named moved about half as many pins within 30 days, 23 per cent against 49, though part of that gap is quiet projects. About half of the moves to a fix were written by a bot, mostly Dependabot, or merged from one, and the bots' messages do not say whether they were security updates.

So the earlier finding holds only in a smaller form: in these four libraries a fix, with its advisory, moved pins faster than an ordinary release, but the advisory did not draw most of the moves. The counts are small, three of the four advisories fell on one evening, we did not read the releases' own notes, and a move is a change to what a project asks for, not an installation; nothing here says any project was exposed.
