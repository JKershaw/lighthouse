# Lighthouse

Lighthouse watches the internet for what AI systems do there and what their activity leaves behind, and publishes what it finds. The question the watch is kept for is whether such activity could sustain and spread itself in ways that degrade the infrastructure everyone shares. Nobody knows yet, and Lighthouse means to be able to tell. The charter says why. This file says how a session works, for any agent in any harness.

## How a session goes

1. Read the section above, the investigation page and programme.md's Next. Read pieces, records and observations.md as the task needs; nothing else is required. The open issues on the repository are the queue; `harbour/hb status` lists them.
2. Do the next open issue: a study the programme describes, or the piece a finished study earns. Use subagents freely: Opus 5.5 for research and writing, Fable 5.1 for review, Sonnet 5 for collection and tabulation. Give each the task, the repository path and this file. The review is where money buys the most: the errors in the first drafts came from a research pass on Sonnet, and the review that caught them ran on Opus. Every subagent writes about 45,000 tokens of harness context before it starts, a floor of about $0.11 on Sonnet, $0.22 on Opus 5.5 and $0.56 on Fable 5.1, and a Fable review costs about twice an Opus one. About twenty dollars of measured subagent spend a session is the bound, and Claude spend is a measure at list rates rather than a bill.
3. Write what was found: a study as a record, and a piece if the record earns one, in the forms below. A study need not produce a full article; a line in the investigation page and the current account may be enough.
4. Have a second agent on the strongest model review the piece against its sources. The reviewer reads the primary sources rather than summaries of them, reproduces the important calculation or says why it could not, keeps any consequential calculation it makes as code and output in the record, checks the consistency rule above, and writes what it checked, found and changed as a note in notes/; a second agent's agreement is a review contribution, not independent confirmation. When the review finds nothing that must be fixed, release it: set `status: released`, complete the colophon, add a line to releases.md, and put it on the front page. When later evidence changes how a released piece reads, add a dated notice near its opening, "Correction" where it said something wrong and "Later evidence" where a follow-up changes how its main claim reads, and a line in the investigation page.
5. At a checkpoint in an investigation (a question answered, left unsettled or stuck), compare three options: continuing the question, repairing a measurement, and another outward question. Write the choice and why it is worth asking in a few sentences in programme.md's Next, after a bounded check of the primary literature (a few searches, with URLs and dates read) where it could change the design or the reading. An investigation may rest.
6. Commit once at the end, with a message that says what was learned and what the session spent, and put it on main; the site at https://lighthouse.harbour.cat rebuilds from main. Close the issue with a comment naming the record and the commit (`harbour/hb close`), and file an issue for a question the work raised (`harbour/hb add`). Close out in a few lines, in the report and, when the current account changed, as notes/closeout-<date>-<topic>.md: what was learned, what changed in the current account, what remains uncertain, the next outward question, and the spend from `harbour/hb tokens` and `harbour/hb drivercost`, labelled incomplete where it is. Say nothing about process unless it failed.

Fix small things where you find them, without filing, listing or reporting them. Decide routine questions yourself and note the choice in a sentence where it was made. Nothing waits for a person except the three things below.

## Rules that matter

- Every claim says where it came from and when the source was read. Label a statement as observation, derived measurement, interpretation or scenario wherever a reader could confuse them. Never invent a timing, a count or a source. Say what was not checked.
- Three things wait for a person: a claim that harm is happening now, a claim that a named system is compromised, and anything irreversible outside this repository, such as contacting a third party. Everything else is the session's to decide.
- Do not read or use credentials from the environment unless the task names them. Never write a token, a session id or a key into the repository.
- No piece is released without a review that found nothing that must be fixed. Commit with a message that says what changed and why, and never rewrite history on a shared branch.

## Evidence

These are the lessons of the reviews so far (notes/R-0010.md, R-0011.md), for research and review alike. Unknown stays unknown: a missing reading is not a negative, and a fix whose notes could not be read is not a silent fix. The event or clock matches the question: for "before the warning", the earliest public notice naming the fix, not the first in a window. Reused data is disclosed with its overlap; a widening that reuses its predecessor's cases is not a replication. A difference in timing or rate is a description; a cause needs its own evidence. Pooled and per-unit views are distinct views, each reported with its uncertainty, and a test of little power that finds nothing has not found an absence. A reading chosen after outcomes were seen is labelled post hoc, with the number of comparisons. Any consequential calculation, a reviewer's included, is kept in the record as code and output, and where the analysis is offline the record has a replay command, as studies/LH010/replay.sh.

## Writing

Records are for checking: studies and notes. Pieces are for reading: articles, short forms, the investigation page and the front page. Both in British English, with no em or en dashes in prose, and no invented figures.

A study lives in studies/LHnnn/ with its brief, its sources and its write-up. The brief fixes, before any outcome is read: the question; what its writer already knows of the outcome; the population and unit; the sources and each event's definition, including which clock counts (which notice is "the advisory"); how missing evidence is classed; the comparison that would matter, and its unit; overlap with earlier studies; the resource ceiling; the stopping condition; and what would leave it inconclusive. Commit the brief, or keep another retained snapshot, before any outcome is read; a protocol commit before collection is encouraged. Later changes are dated amendments, and a decision made after outcomes were seen says so; studies/LH011/brief.md is an example. The write-up has a header (the question as its title, version, date, who wrote it and on which model, the commit the figures were taken from, the observation period, the instruments and versions used, what it cites, and any overlap to disclose) and then Answer, Findings, Method, Limits, Next and Corrections, where each entry says what changed, why, whether the conclusions changed, and where the earlier edition is. Cite a source by its S number in sources.md or by URL with the date read, and give its publication date and observation window beside it. Leave unfillable fields blank and say what could not be filled.

A piece is written for a curious person who has never heard of Lighthouse. It carries the small header site/README.md describes ("What the site reads": published, summary, status, investigation, revised). Its title states the finding, or, where the finding is uncertain, the question or the tension. A short opening gives a reason to care, then a discreet date and byline. Open with the thing itself, a person, a moment or a surprise, never with Lighthouse or its method. One idea a reader could say back in a sentence, developed one thought at a time; say it, then qualify it once, where the qualification matters. Plain words; define a term where it is first needed and only if it is needed. No internal references in the prose: no document names, study numbers, tickets or roles; the piece speaks as "we". Honesty lives in the verbs: the record shows, nobody has measured, we think. An illustration drawn, not described, with a one-line caption. The colophon is a short line of who wrote, reviewed and released it, with version and date, then named links: Methods, Sources (with dates read), Corrections and Full record, and one line on what Lighthouse is. After the facts are checked, rewrite once for the reader; recheck any change of meaning against the record. Read it aloud and rewrite anything that trips. The short form in articles/short/ is written separately, as a standalone piece of about 150 to 250 words: a reader's question and one concrete observation, then the consequence and the main limit. The worked examples are [the synthesis](articles/where-software-updates-go.md) and [the latest piece](articles/the-lead-was-a-few-libraries.md).
