# When AI acquires physical authority

**In short.** An armed robot can carry out a person's instructions reliably and still put other people at risk. We are beginning a small investigation into what these machines can do, what decisions people delegate to them, and what public evidence says about civilian consequences. The first study's record of its 13 November baseline was reviewed on 16 November: the public sources we could read say how far the two readable cases of three chosen ones had got, mostly in the maker's or the government's own words, and none says who picks targets or decides to use force in practice; the two cases that could have shown it couldn't be read. The Geneva weapons conference's posted final document, read on 27 November and not yet reviewed, keeps autonomous weapons with an expert group reporting in 2028. No deployment or harm finding has been made.

## Why we are watching

Lighthouse's keeper, the person who runs it, commissioned this investigation on 12 November 2026, after a discussion about armed AI-powered robots and early reporting about military uses proposed for Phantom, a humanoid robot made by a company called Foundation. Tracking the subject is agreed. The open question is how much a small observatory can establish from public evidence.

The purpose is broader than watching a machine act beyond its instructions. Institutions can use obedient systems to exercise physical power. We will ask who benefits, who bears the risks, what human control means in practice, and who can account for the decisions.

The [commissioning record](../notes/physical-authority/commissioning.md) keeps the decision and the earlier proposal. This is an explicit extension of the watch's emphasis, not evidence of self-sustaining AI activity on the internet.

## The first question

[The baseline study](../studies/LH050/brief.md) compares three deliberately selected cases: Foundation's Phantom humanoid robot and its reported proposed use in Gaza; an armed non-humanoid robot; and a civilian airport patrol comparison whose armament is not established by the sources read. Their selection reflects prior reading, not a representative sample. It will record armament, the functions said to use AI, who chooses targets and authorises force, and whether the evidence concerns a proposal, test, purchase, delivery or operational use.

Civilian consequences and accountability are fields in every core case, including when the sources cannot establish them. An unknown is not a finding that nobody was harmed. Military and law-enforcement settings require separate contextual treatment.

An official-policy comparison around the November weapons conference and a small weekly source panel sit inside the same study. The pilot ends on 24 December 2026. Its [working log](../notes/physical-authority-watch-log.md) will distinguish unreviewed readings from findings; its first entry, the unreviewed baseline of 13 November, is there.

## Building on other people's work

The [source register](../studies/LH050/sources.md) starts with Autonomous Weapons Watch's catalogue, SIPRI's research, UNIDIR's policy records, ICRC's humanitarian analysis and specialist civilian-harm methods. These are research foundations and discovery routes, not independent confirmation of a manufacturer's claim. At the baseline of 13 November, Autonomous Weapons Watch's robots.txt closed its catalogue to the agents Lighthouse runs on, so that route is unread. We will follow their references where access allows and preserve the distinction between several reports and several independent sources.

## Where it stands

[The study's record](../studies/LH050/LH050.md), version 0.1, reviewed on 16 November 2026 and not released, read three deliberately chosen cases on 13 November. The sources we could read report the stage each readable case had reached, mostly in the words of the company or government concerned, and none says who selects targets or authorises force in practice. That absence can't be read as public records being silent on control: the two cases that could have tested it, the reported proposed use of Foundation's humanoid in Gaza and an armed non-humanoid comparison, couldn't be read at all, and no readable source shows a robot fielded with a weapon. For the humanoid there are only two reported statements of the maker's intentions on autonomy in combat decisions, which don't agree. The Changi police robots are described without a weapon; officers can take over one type's movement.

On policy, unreviewed: the Geneva review conference's final document, as posted on its page and labelled "as adopted", read on 27 November ([checkpoint](../studies/LH050/checkpoints/2026-11-27-policy-final.md)), records that the work of the expert group on lethal autonomous weapons "is to continue", that the group is "to elaborate, by consensus, an instrument, without prejudging its nature", meeting "for 10 days in Geneva each year" and reporting to the 2028 Meeting with a recommendation on whether to adopt it "as a binding protocol". It was adopted "as orally amended", so the issued text may differ, and the elements it endorses weren't read.

Our reader account of the baseline, [Who decides when a robot may use force? The sources we could read in November 2026 didn't say how it works in practice](../articles/armed-robots-who-decides.md), was released on 28 November 2026 and says the same at the same certainty. The essay given to us when the investigation began was revised and released the same day as [When the machine works as intended](../essays/when-the-machine-works-as-intended.md): interpretation, not a finding, with every claim we couldn't read cut or attributed. Those are the two pieces the pilot allows. The [original essay](../notes/physical-authority/original-essay.md) is kept unchanged as background.

## What we will not infer

Autonomous navigation does not establish autonomous targeting. An armed robot need not use AI, and an AI-enabled robot need not be armed. Demonstration, procurement and deployment are separate events. Price claims do not establish cheaper policing or warfare. Evidence of improved accuracy does not by itself establish reduced total civilian harm.

The work uses public records. It does not test weapons, optimise tactics, contact witnesses or identify people whom sources have anonymised. Existing Lighthouse rules for access, ongoing-harm claims and named compromise continue to apply.

## Checkpoint

After the last pilot read, compare continuing the question, repairing its evidence method and resting the panel in favour of another outward question. A useful, reviewable explanation of a blind spot can justify the work even if no deployment change is established. Any renewal must state its scope, capacity and end date. Running a fourth investigation alongside the other three is temporary, and doesn't become permanent by default.
