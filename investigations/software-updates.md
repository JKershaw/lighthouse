## How the inquiry developed

We started by following one release of mcp, a Python library AI agents use, into nine projects built on it ([where a software update went](../articles/where-a-software-update-went.md)); the histories showed where it went but not why, or whether anyone ran it. The download log **extended** the trail past the projects and showed the new version at half of all downloads within two days, stopping at the download ([what a download shows](../articles/what-a-download-shows.md)). Published images **extended** it again, to the installation, and showed a project's software holding a version its own lockfile did not name ([what an image holds](../articles/what-an-image-holds.md)). A sample of images around a later release confirmed two paces, ranges within days and pins waiting, and **branched** into what moves a pin, where a security advisory seemed to ([what moves a pin](../articles/what-moves-a-pin.md)). Four libraries **revised** that: pins moved over weeks, after both release and advisory, with no burst ([pins move over weeks](../articles/pins-move-over-weeks.md)). Fixes' own notes then **narrowed** the question to whether a fix that says so draws pins sooner, which fourteen libraries suggested ([fixes that said so](../articles/fixes-that-said-so.md)). Thirty libraries **revised** that to unsettled: pooled it leans that way, library by library we could not tell ([the lead was a few libraries](../articles/the-lead-was-a-few-libraries.md)). Back on the fast half of the road, 37 of the most downloaded Python libraries **tested** the two-day pace: for 26 a new release was half of the downloads within two days, and for eleven no release was, but in almost all a new version took its share on its first full day and older versions kept the rest for the month ([the reading of the pace](../articles/two-days-for-most.md)). One week of September 2026 then **tested** what keeps those older versions downloaded: downloads from Pythons too old for a newer version were most of it in three of the 37 libraries and fewer than half in 32, though an old Python does not say which older version is fetched, and for boto3 and aiobotocore most of those downloads look, on a reading made after the counts, like versions an installer tried and discarded; other libraries' version limits could account for most of it in four, if each download of a library that sets a limit brings one of the library it limits, which the log cannot check; and for the rest the log could not say. A new version's share settling within two days held as a tested result on releases we had not read ([the reading of what stays behind](../articles/old-versions-new-pythons.md)). While the next reading waited for its weeks, 160 libraries drawn at random from further down the rankings **bounded** the two-day pace: most new versions reached half of the downloads within two days for 25 of the 52 that released, of 80 drawn from those ranked 51 to 500, and 9 of the 35 that released, of 80 drawn from those ranked 501 to 5,000, against 26 of 37 at the top, and further down a typical new version held a smaller share from its first full day ([the latest reading](../articles/rarer-further-down.md)). [Where does a software update go once it is released?](../articles/where-software-updates-go.md) draws the whole together, and is the place to start.

The reading order above puts that account first and then the pieces in the order they were written, so that a reader who starts with the whole is led on through the steps that built it, and the last piece is the latest reading.

## Where it changed direction

- From projects to downloads: the histories stopped at what projects asked for, so we looked for a record on the far side of the release.
- From downloads to images: the log could not say what was installed, so we read the installations some projects publish.
- From installation to what moves a pin: images showed that pinned software waits, so the question became what ends the wait.
- From advisories to the fix's own notes: fixes came out before their advisories, so a move "before the advisory" was not a move before anyone knew.
- From fourteen libraries to thirty: two libraries carried the lead, so each library had to count once.
- From the notes back to downloads: five months of advisories held too few libraries, so the next reading returned to how fast releases reach downloads.
- From the pace to what stays behind: in almost every library a new version took its share on the first day and older versions kept the rest for the month, so the question became what keeps older versions downloaded.
- From the top fifty to the rest of the rankings: the pace had been measured only for the libraries almost everything depends on, so while the next reading waited for its weeks, it was measured on libraries drawn from further down.
- From what stays behind to whether it holds: old Pythons and other libraries' limits could account for the older downloads of few libraries, and downloads reporting one Python made nearly two thirds of boto3's in the week read, so the question became whether what stays behind is steady from week to week.

## Where the evidence stands

Attention says where we are looking; this says how firm the findings are. That a new release reaches half of a library's downloads within two days held for most of the most downloaded Python libraries, 26 of the 37 that released between April and August 2026 (70 per cent, and anywhere from 54 to 84 per cent when the libraries are resampled), and for eleven no release did. Below the top fifty it was not the rule: of 80 libraries drawn at random from those ranked 51 to 500, 52 released and 25 of them had most new versions reach half within two days (48 per cent, anywhere from 35 to 62 when resampled), and of 80 drawn from those ranked 501 to 5,000, 35 released and 9 of them did (26 per cent, 11 to 40), on new versions of the same months. A typical new version's share on its first full day was smaller further down, and on a reading made after the counts, for new versions that replaced one at least two weeks old, it was about the share the version it replaced had held the day before, in every group, so what differs down the rankings seems to be how much of a library's downloads follow its newest version at all; mirror programs made few of those downloads, and why the share is smaller was not measured. That a new version's share is set early and holds for the month was first seen after the counts were read, from the first full day; measured from the second day to the thirtieth, it then held as a tested result on 355 releases not read before, for 28 of 32 libraries, within a ten-point band that is lenient where shares are small: for boto3 and aiobotocore the typical new version's share still grew by about two thirds after the second day. Further down the rankings it held for 39 of the 50 libraries with a known result among those drawn from ranks 51 to 500, and 23 of 35 among those drawn from ranks 501 to 5,000. In one week of September 2026, a median 37 per cent of each library's downloads were of versions older than its newest of a month before. Downloads from Pythons that no newer version supports were most of those in three libraries and fewer than half in 32, so old Pythons could not be the main reason there; where they were most, the old Python rules out the new version for those downloads without saying which older one is fetched, and for boto3 and aiobotocore few were where a fresh install would land: read again after publication, their counts by version are nearly the same for long runs of versions in a row, the mark of an installer trying one version after another and fetching each, so most of them may never have been installed. Other libraries' declared limits could account for most in four, as an estimate that assumes each download of a library that sets a limit brings one of the library it limits, which the log does not record and its own numbers strain for two of the four; they could not be settled in 26. Environments rebuilt from old lists, which fit what remains, cannot be counted in the download log, and downloads that share the details they report are not thereby one machine or one population. A download counts fetches, so a low share is not by itself a slow spread. In those releases' first three days, downloads flagged as builds were a smaller share of the new version's than of the replaced version's, in every library's typical release; the flag is a floor on builds. That software built from open ranges took a release within days while pinned software waited showed in the published images of one library's dependents, and that pins move to security fixes over weeks held across four libraries. Whether a fix whose own notes say so draws pins sooner is not settled. No reading checked whether any project was exposed, and none can see software run.

## What is next

The question of fixes that say so rests unsettled: five months of advisories against the 500 most downloaded Python projects did not hold enough libraries to press it further, and a longer window may one day. What keeps older versions downloaded has been read as far as the download log reaches ([the reading of what stays behind](../articles/old-versions-new-pythons.md)): old Pythons are most of it for a few libraries without saying which older versions are fetched, other libraries' limits could account for a few more on an assumption the log cannot check, and for most a remainder fits environments rebuilt from old lists, which only a count of public lockfiles could test, once a reader for public code at that scale can be used. The next question is whether what stays behind is steady from one week to the next, and how much of a library's downloads falls in one combination of the details clients report, as downloads reporting Python 3.9 made nearly two thirds of boto3's in the week we read, something we noticed only after the counts were in. Such a combination is not one machine or one owner, and the study will not say who makes it; it also measures the shape of each library's older downloads by version, to see whether the installer's searches seen in boto3's hold. A watch can call a change unusual only against a baseline that holds. Below the top fifty, where new versions took half of the downloads within two days for fewer libraries ([the latest reading](../articles/rarer-further-down.md)), a reading made after the counts points at the same thing, how much of a library's downloads follow its newest version at all; the libraries drawn there can be read the same way once the weekly question is answered. Its two weeks, 28 September to 11 October 2026, are fixed, and it waits until the download log's public copy holds them, from about 13 October.
