## What a reader would believe, written before reading the record

**The finding, said back.** A new version of a big Python library takes most of its share of downloads on its first full day, gains a few points over the month, and older versions keep the rest. For 26 of 37 libraries most releases passed half within two days; for eleven, boto3, numpy and pandas among them, none did that month. In a later week older versions were 37 per cent of the typical library's downloads, mostly, in 32 libraries, from Pythons a newer version supports.

Observed: shares by version and day for 424 releases, April to August 2026; one September week by version and reported Python; the first-day pattern, noticed after the counts, then holding on 355 unread releases, 28 of 32 libraries, though still rising. Explained, hedged: the first day reaches the machines that take whatever is newest, so a low share is a small pool, not a slow spread; build-flagged downloads leaning to the replaced version fit builds holding pins. Caused: an old Python rules out the new version but does not pick the old one; boto3's fifty versions fetched almost equally from Python 3.9 read as pip's resolver trying and discarding them, most never installed. Other libraries' limits could account for four libraries' older downloads, pydantic-core's most plausibly, on an unchecked assumption; the rest fits lockfiles without showing them.

**Limits.** A download is a fetch, not an install or a run; build servers count many times, caches never; the log keeps the day, not the hour; the reported Python is the installer's. Thirty-seven big libraries, five months, then one week; limits from the top 500 only. The installer-search reading is post hoc, untested, and whether pip's metadata reads since 2022 appear in the log is unknown. By release, only 30 per cent crossed half. Two corrections, no figure changed, leave the counts more trusted than the readings.

The walk reading, post hoc and labelled, with LH013 named as its test, gains one thing from my check (r0025_walk_dip.txt). The record's one exception in boto3's Python 3.9 run, 1.42.87 at 55 per cent of its neighbours, has the same metadata as they do (not yanked, Requires-Python >=3.9, one wheel and one sdist) and dips only on 3.9 (0.99 and 0.91 of its neighbours on 3.10 and 3.11). Nothing on the index explains it; pip's resolver's use of an already-installed distribution as the candidate for its version, without fetching it (as I know pip's source, not re-read here), would: environments that hold 1.42.87 installed would fetch every version they try except that one. That is a further post hoc reading, so I state it as a prediction: the dip stays at exactly 1.42.87 on later weeks until those environments are rebuilt. Under the walk reading, about 885,000 walks a week, about 400 versions deep on average; only 5 per cent of the edge's 17.1 million fetches are walk starts, so about 16 million are fresh installs that stop at the edge: for boto3 the old-Python mechanism is real and small (4.6 per cent of its 3.9 fetches), as the piece says, and for aiobotocore the edge (486,492) barely exceeds the plateau (473,700), so nearly all of its old-Python fetches are walks. No class moves: with the walk-shaped fetches set aside, boto3's at-or-newer share in the week goes from 10.0 to 34.5 per cent and aiobotocore's from 2.8 to 15.2, still under half; the pooled older share goes from 45.6 to 41.7 without boto3 and 38.8 without its family.

So the measurements could be right while an explanation is wrong, and where that matters the pieces already say so. The two places a reader is led further than the evidence are below; neither changes a figure or a class.

### Material claim problems

1. **The pace piece's later-evidence box gives the old-Python result for boto3 without the reading that changes its meaning.** articles/two-days-for-most.md, the box of 29 September: "downloads from old Pythons were most of the older downloads in three of the 37 libraries, boto3, aiobotocore and numpy, and fewer than half in 32, though an old Python does not say which older version is fetched"; articles/short/two-days-for-most.md, its box: "downloads from Pythons too old for a newer version were most of the older versions' downloads in three of the 37 libraries". Coming after the section that names old Pythons as one of three explanations for "what keeps older versions downloaded", a reader takes this as that explanation holding for boto3: machines on an old Python installing older versions. Evidence: LH012 record, "Read after version 0.2 was released"; review/r0018_walkshare.txt N (walk-shaped fetches 78.9 per cent of boto3's older downloads, 84.2 of aiobotocore's); data/analysis/python.csv edge shares 6.7 and 0.6; r0025_walk_dip.txt 3 (16 million fresh installs at the edge against 356 million below it). The second piece says this; the first's box, on the more-read piece, does not. Resolution: one clause in each box, after "which older version is fetched": "and for boto3 and aiobotocore most of those downloads, on a reading made after the counts and not yet tested, look like versions an installer tried and discarded rather than installed".

2. **The date from which pip stops fetching each version it tries is given a year early, so a reader places the walks on older installers than the evidence needs.** articles/old-versions-new-pythons.md, "What an old Python rules out": "since October 2022 it reads instead a small file of the version's requirements where the index offers one"; LH012 record, Findings ("since 22.3 (15 October 2022)") and Next ("resolution walks by pips older than 22.3"); programme.md, Next ("pips older than 22.3"). Evidence: r0025_pip_metadata_sources.txt. PEP 714 (June 2023): PyPI "did not support PEP 658 until just recently", and pips from 22.3 on "hard fail" on the metadata key in the JSON index they request, so PyPI emits it only under a renamed key; pip's NEWS puts the parse fix in 23.2 (15 July 2023). So the pips that fetch every candidate in full from PyPI are those older than 23.2, and PyPI itself served nothing before mid-2023: a wider and more recent set than "before October 2022", which makes the walk reading easier to hold and the "could vanish overnight" scenario rest on installers a reader might still expect to meet. Small, and it changes no figure. Resolution: "since 2023 it can read instead a small file of the version's requirements, which the index began serving that year and pips from 23.2 can read"; the same in the record's Findings and Next and in programme.md, and by dated amendment in LH013's brief if it carries the date.
