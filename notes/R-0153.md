# R-0153: reader-and-inference review of LH051 record 1.1

## What a reader would believe, written before reading the record

Written from lines 1 to 23 of studies/LH051/LH051.md only (header, In short, Answer).

**Principal finding, said back.** Two security firms say someone used stolen GitHub accounts to open about 62,000 to 63,000 probe pull requests from 10 November. GH Archive's public hourly record holds only about 600 of them, under 1 per cent, and almost no trace of their clean-up: 6 closings and 21 branch deletions over two weeks. So the public archive is a poor witness to this campaign.

**Observed.** Counts of events in GH Archive's files: 595 or 606 openings depending on which firm's window is used, 655 in all, the first at 13:00 UTC on 10 November, none after 12 November; 6 closings; 21 deletions; many hours with no branch or push events at all. The firms' totals and "about 90%" or "92%" still open are their reports, quoted.

**Explained.** Nothing. The record says outright that it can't say why the files hold so few probes.

**Caused.** The firms' account is that stolen credentials caused the probes and the planted script. The record claims no cause for the low share or the few closings. A reader may still drift towards "the archive misses things" as the cause, and may read "6 closings" as "the probes were barely cleaned up", which the text warns against: the 0.92 per cent is not comparable with the firms' 90 per cent.

**Limits a reader would notice.** Events, not pull requests, repositories or victims; gap hours make early creations and deletions unknown, not zero; deletions only in two hours in five; one firm's figure is an upper estimate; unreviewed; counts fell below the brief's own expectation.

## Stage 2: against the record

Read for this stage: all of studies/LH051/ (record, brief, sources, both checkpoints, data, scripts, both outputs), investigations/ai-cost-and-spread.md and programme.md's Next. I read no other note, no releases.md and no commit message, and made no network read. One calculation, kept as code and output: [R-0153_context.py](../studies/LH051/review/R-0153_context.py) and [its output](../studies/LH051/review/R-0153_context-output.txt), which reads only data/hours.csv and data/counts.csv; two runs gave byte-identical output. It's a reviewer's comparison made after the counts were seen (post hoc, one comparison).

**What the round establishes.** The counts hold up and the record states them carefully. The files hold 595 and 606 opened probe events in the two windows (0.96 per cent of each vendor's search count), the first in the hour 2026-11-10-13, and 6 closings and 21 deletions to 25 November. Measure 7 supports the one explanation the record rules out: LH046's gap removes branch and push events, not pull request events. The record says plainly that it can't say *why* the share is low. The stage 1 reading (the archive is a poor witness to this campaign) is right as far as it goes. The evidence goes further than the summaries do, and that is where the problems are.

### Material claim problems

1. **A share read as something about the campaign, when the source's volume sets it.**
   - *Passage.* LH051.md, Answer: "GH Archive's files hold **a small fraction** of the probe pull requests the vendors' GitHub searches counted"; "What this cannot say": it can't say "why the files hold so few of the probes"; Next: a follow-up would tell "whether 0.96 per cent is typical or particular to this campaign".
   - *Evidence.* Per data/hours.csv, the files hold 990 to 2,209 pull request events an hour (median 1,428) and a median of 4,117 events of all kinds. Over Aikido's 27 hours they hold 41,014 pull request events of every kind. Aikido's 61,839 is 1.51 times that, and StepSecurity's 63,000 is 0.73 times the 85,894 in its 56 hours (R-0153_context-output.txt). Even if every pull request event the files hold were a probe opening, they couldn't hold most of Aikido's count. Mean pull request events per hour rose only from 1,336 (baseline) to 1,519 (Aikido's window). So "a small fraction" was close to fixed by how much the files hold at all, before any probe was matched. The method faithfully answers its question, but the answer says little about this campaign and a lot about the source's throughput. A reader (stage 1) takes away "the public record misses this campaign". The evidence says "the public record now holds a few thousand events an hour of everything".
   - *Resolution.* Add to measure 3 and the Answer one labelled sentence giving the files' total pull request events over each window beside the vendor's count (post hoc, a reviewer's calculation, one comparison). Change "why the files hold so few" to say that the files' own hourly volume caps the share, and that what sets that volume is unknown (LH046's question). Reframe Next: the useful follow-up is GH Archive's overall capture against an independent count of all public events, not of one campaign's share.

2. **Few closings read as little cleanup.**
   - *Passage.* LH051.md, In short: "over the following two weeks only 6 closing events and 21 branch deletions". programme.md, Next (LH051 line): "finds the archive holds under 1% of the probes the vendors counted, with few closed".
   - *Evidence.* The files hold about 1 in 100 openings, so a closing count drawn from the same feed is expected to be small whatever happened on GitHub. Measure 5 sets the vendors' "about 90%" and "92%" still open, roughly 8 to 10 per cent no longer open by 12 November, beside 0.61 per cent cumulative in the files that day (record, Measure 5). The record body says correctly that these are "not compared as rates". But "only" in the In short, and "with few closed" in programme.md, invite exactly the reading stage 1 warned about: the probes were barely cleaned up. The study's title asks "how fast do its traces get cleaned up?", and the evidence can't answer that.
   - *Resolution.* Drop "only" from the In short. Add a clause: at this coverage the counts can't say how fast the probes were cleaned up. In programme.md, replace "with few closed" with the counts and the same clause. Say once in the Answer that the title's second question isn't answered by these data.

3. **No held opening after 12 November read as the campaign stopping.**
   - *Passage.* LH051.md, Answer: "No opening is held after 12 November."
   - *Evidence.* StepSecurity, at 07:40 UTC on 12 November, reported new probes "every few minutes" (brief, The event). At roughly 1 in 100, a few probes an hour on GitHub would leave an expected well under one held opening an hour. So the absence is weak evidence that openings stopped. It's an observation, correctly worded, but a reader can take it as an ending.
   - *Resolution.* Follow it with "at this coverage that is weak evidence that new probes stopped."

No other problem matters here. Events aren't read as repositories (the per-cell distinct counts are never added). Association isn't presented as cause. The vendors' figures are attributed throughout. The investigation page still shows the pre-tabulation line ("nothing tabulated yet"). That's stale, not wrong, and is the release step's to update.

**Challenge to the brief.** The brief's expectation ("a few thousand to some tens of thousands" of held openings) and its three-band reading were set without the files' own hourly volume. LH046 already showed reduced volume, so a feasibility bound was available before outcomes were read. That isn't a fault of the record, but the record should say the pre-set reading had little room to come out otherwise.

### Readability

- In short: lead with what the share mostly reflects (how thin the hourly files are), then the campaign figures.
- Answer: the run of parentheses in the onset sentence ("branch creations are unknown in the start hour and in 28 earlier gap hours") is hard to follow. Move it to measure 2.
- Say once, early, what "event" means in plain words (one record in the public feed), since "655 in all" is easily read as 655 repositories.

### Next step

Fix the three passages (and programme.md's line), then the fresh recheck the record already schedules. Write no piece: the record's main lesson is about the source, and that belongs with LH046 and the investigation page's line, not in an article about the campaign. That goes to proposals.md and its gate. No new collection for LH051.
