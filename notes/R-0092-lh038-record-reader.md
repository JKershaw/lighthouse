## Material claim problems

**1. SUSE's "24 October" is one of two SUSE clocks, and the other puts the same advisories after the post.**
- Passages: LH038.md In short, "SUSE's kernel advisories that name it carry release dates from 24 October, three days before Anthropic's post of 27 October" and "The post came 3 to 26 days after the candidate patch dates"; the table row "Earliest followed distribution fix (SUSE Release Date, T5) | 24 Oct | 3"; Finding 4; the P5 and "Distributions after the fix" bullets; Next, the proposed Later evidence line ("SUSE's own advisory pages ... carry release dates of 24 October, three days before the post").
- Evidence: each of the four advisory pages (data/read-2026-11-17T1419Z/suse_suse_su_202624386_1 and the others) does show "Release Date: 2026-10-24T10:42:33Z" or "11:15:59Z". But SUSE's own CVE page, retained by LH036 (studies/LH036/data/read-2026-11-16T1419Z/suse.raw.gz, line 1756 onwards), lists each announcement with a "published" stamp. For these four it gives Thu 29 October 2026, 13:04:53 CET (two of them) and 21:05:43 and 21:05:46 CET, that is, 12:04 and 20:05 UTC on 29 October, two days after the post. Of the 190 announcements on that page with a stamp, none is dated before 27 October. The earliest is 29 October 12:04 UTC (studies/LH038/review/r0092_suse_ids.py, output in r0092_suse_ids_output.txt). The same column gives the Liberty posts of Red Hat's errata as 3 and 6 November, which matches those list pages' own Last-Modified, so it looks like an announcement date. The 2 November advisories show the same lag: Release Date 2 November 08:57Z, CVE-page stamp 3 November 21:05 CET. The brief says "Where several clocks exist they are all kept and none substituted." The record keeps one SUSE clock and builds the range's low end on it.
- Why it matters: a reader, and LH036's surfaces if the Later evidence line goes up, would believe a distribution had published a fix before the post. The evidence supports only this: a SUSE page dates a release on 24 October, while SUSE's announcement listing dates its publication on 29 October. Neither says when fixed packages reached customers, or when SUSE first said so in public. This is a release-date-versus-public-event problem. It leans easy on Anthropic, because it makes a distribution fix appear to come before the post. Under the publication stamp, no followed distribution advisory (SUSE, Red Hat) is public before the post, and Debian's and Ubuntu's dates are unread.
- Resolution: report both SUSE clocks wherever the 24 October date appears (Answer T5, table, Finding 4, the P5 and distributions bullets, In short). Give the range as 12 to 26 days from the kernel's dates, plus "3 days after the Release Date on SUSE's advisory pages, though SUSE's CVE page lists those announcements as published on 29 October, 2 days after the post". Label the CVE-page stamp as found by this review, after outcomes were seen (post hoc), and note that it comes from LH036's read, as reuse. Hold the proposed Later evidence line for LH036 until the clocks are reconciled (see Next step). If it goes up anyway, it must carry both dates. Note too that LH036's "SUSE's ... page gives no dates" is true of product states but not of announcements. Its own retained page dates every announcement, so a Later evidence line built on "a date LH036 couldn't give" would be wrong on that point as well.

**2. The In short gives the patch-to-post days without the policy they are measured against, and turns an author date into "the fix is dated".**
- Passage: In short, "The fix is dated 1 October ... The post came 3 to 26 days after the candidate patch dates (12 to 26 after the kernel's own), and it describes the exploit in prose without publishing code."
- Evidence: the Answer and Finding 3 say the post "falls inside every candidate 45-day window" of P1's "we would generally wait 45 days before publishing full technical details". The In short, the only surface a site reader meets first, names no policy figure, yet it keeps the one detail (no code) that bears on whether the post was "full technical details". Separately, 1 October is the commit's author date. The record says the fix's first public appearance is undated (Limits), so "a patch is available" on 1 October is not shown, and 26 is the upper end only if the author date counts.
- Resolution: add one balanced sentence, for example: "Anthropic's policy page says it generally waits 45 days after a patch is available before publishing full technical details. The post came inside 45 days of every candidate date; neither text defines full technical details, the page allows the wait to be shortened in two cases, and whether either applied isn't stated." Also write "the fix's author date is 1 October (when it was first public isn't dated)".
