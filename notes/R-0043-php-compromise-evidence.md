## What I found and changed

1. **A quotation put in the advisory's mouth (fixed).** The notice said Palo Alto Networks "says in its advisory and threat brief, both updated 29 October," that the flaws "are being actively exploited against PAN-OS management interfaces that are exposed to the internet", with the 0.02 per cent figure. Both the quotation and the figure are in Unit 42's brief only; the advisory has a different sentence (quoted in the dated list) and no figure. Now "says in its threat brief, updated 29 October with its advisory, that ...".
2. **Palo Alto Networks credited with Dream's counts (fixed on five surfaces).** The summary, README, short form, investigation page and programme.md said Palo Alto Networks (or Unit 42) verified "a security firm's report of 25 servers taken over, three in a US government-affiliated network". Unit 42 says only that Dream "has reported a campaign in which attackers chained PHP2Shell with a Linux kernel privilege-escalation vulnerability to gain root access" and that it "independently verified the same attacker's tooling and confirmed the reported results"; it gives no count and does not mention a government network. The count and the government affiliation are Dream's. Each surface now says Palo Alto Networks "confirmed the results reported by" the firm, "which counts 25 servers taken over", three "in what it calls a US government-affiliated environment" (Dream's phrase). The piece's notice already attributed them correctly and is unchanged there.
3. **Half of Dream's AI sentence (fixed in the notice, the dated list and the short form).** All three quoted Dream's "no direct evidence" that the AI tools wrote the exploit and stopped there. Dream's sentence goes on: "the code structure, commenting style, and consistency across ~40 scripts suggest AI-assisted development", and its opening calls the campaign "what appeared to be a Chinese AI-assisted campaign". ## Must fix

1. **The AI absolutes in the summary and on the front page.** The summary still says "Public attack reports do not identify AI in the requests", and the README's first bullet that "neither the honeypot report nor the public traffic accounts establish AI use by attackers". Both are read without the body, carry no window, and now sit beside a public attack report that says Claude Code was installed on the attacker's infrastructure, an offensive tool was configured with DeepSeek and Kimi, and the scripts' style suggests AI-assisted development, while it has no direct evidence the tools wrote the exploit. Neither the summary's new sentence nor the README's new bullet mentions it. The literal claim may survive (Dream identifies tools on infrastructure, not AI in requests), but a reader of the summary alone would take it that no attack report touches AI, and the omission falls in the writer's maker's favour. Suggested: date the old sentence ("By 22 October, public attack reports did not identify AI in the requests") and add to the 29 October sentence, or a following one, what Dream reports about AI tools at its own certainty; the same on the front page. The short form's body ("The AI account documents defensive discovery, not an attacker's tooling") is dated 22 October and its notice now says the rest, so it can stand.
2. **Delft's "code execution", interpreted in our voice.** The dated list says the step "describes what a request carried, not what a system did". The dashboard does not define its steps, and its sensors include a high-interaction honeypot, so whether any of those 10,525 requests ran code on a sensor is not stated on the page as read. Label it as our reading ("which, as we read the page, classes request content; the page does not say whether a sensor ran the code") or drop the clause. The notice's "code reaching decoys and traffic reaching sensors, not intrusions" is safe as it stands.
