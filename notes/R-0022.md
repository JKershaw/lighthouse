# R-0022: review of "recipes-follow-the-pin"

Stage one, written from the full piece, its short form and the README's "Current focus" only; the study record, brief, data and other notes were not opened.

## What a reader would believe, written before reading the record

**Principal finding, said back.** When a Python project holds a library at an older version in a pin or lockfile, the container recipe it keeps usually installs from that file: 129 of the 167 cases fixed rules could decide (77 per cent), and over half however the 68 undecided fall (55 to 84). About half of the 469 moments kept no recipe at all. Exact pins were followed more often than lockfiles, which a build read in only half of decided cases.

**Observed.** Each project's files at its last commit before a library release, April 2025 to August 2026, classed by what each recipe's install command reads, under rules fixed beforehand from installer documentation; a program classed 614 of 731 builds, agents 117; a second reviewer agreed on 57 of 60.

**Explained.** How a pin fails to bind a build: `pip install .` never opens a lockfile; others install another requirements file or the package from PyPI. Only 2 of the 38 non-following cases were shown able to take the new release.

**Caused.** The install command decides whether a pin holds, so the earlier timings of pins mostly stand for what those projects' own recipes would build. The pin-versus-lockfile gap is described, not tested.

**Limits noticed.** No image seen built or run; 68 undecided; development recipes counted like release ones, and one dissenting recipe made a moment "not following" (84 per cent by main recipe alone); not a sample of all software; summaries say "recipes" where the body counts moments; status draft.

## Stage two: against the record

Read: studies/LH015/ (record 0.2, brief, amendments, sources, data/, scripts/ headers), investigations/software-updates.md, programme.md's Next, studies/LH013/brief.md. Checks are in studies/LH015/review/r0022_checks.py and r0022_undetermined.py with their outputs. replay.sh was not run here (its cleanup step is one this session may not run); the record says it reproduces every output.

**What the round establishes.** Every figure the pieces give matches the retained tables: the chart's four rows, the 38's routes (15, 14, 7, 5, 1, 1), S1's 2, 12 and 24, R3's 12, the 45 of 90, the 84 per cent main-recipe view, and bbot's 47.8 days with the release of 21 May and the advisory of 15 June (r0022_checks.txt). The rules were fixed and hashed before any read, the blind check held, and the three corrections after it moved no label. Firm: at 469 project-moments in 332 repositories about half keep a recipe; where the rules decide, 129 of 167 moments (77 per cent; 70 by repository) have some build that installs the library's version from a timed file; 68 of 247 with a recipe are undetermined, so no label stands; for lockfile-only moments the decided share is 21 of 37 with 41 of 153 undetermined. The 68 undetermined moments lie in 32 repositories, 26 of them in four (aider 8, grimoirelab 8, galaxy 6, agentstack 4), and 13 in four repositories are undetermined by the context rule alone (r0022_undetermined.txt). "From the pinned file" is mostly not lock-reading: of 281 class 1 builds, 104 read a lock, 73 install `-r` a timed requirements file and 98 install the project whose manifest pins the library or reads a timed file; 89 of the 129 from-pinned moments never read a timed lock. Could the counts be right and the explanation wrong? As far as container recipes go, no: the classes read instructions, and the inference that the timed moves describe what those recipes would build follows, granted a rebuild. Where the pieces reach past recipes, to downloads and the lockfile census, the evidence thins (problem 4).

**Material claim problems.**

1. *Unit.* articles/recipes-follow-the-pin.md, summary: "129 of 167 recipes installed from the pinned file and 38 did not"; articles/short/recipes-follow-the-pin.md, summary, the same; README.md, Current focus: "129 of 167 recipes installed from the pinned file". Evidence: data/pair_classes.csv counts moments, 167 in 112 repositories; data/recipe_classes.csv gives those moments 456 decided snapshot builds in 411 recipes, of which 281 builds (62 per cent) and 236 recipes (57 per cent) are class 1, two monorepos holding 122 of the 456; data/measures.csv, repository view 70.2 per cent (61.5 to 78.4). 2. *The lockfile split missing from the short form and README.* articles/short/recipes-follow-the-pin.md: "Where the rules could decide, 129 of 167 installed from the pinned file ... and a count of lockfiles should read the recipes beside them", after an opening example that is a lockfile case; README.md, Current focus, asks about "a pin ... or ... a lockfile" and answers with the pooled 129 of 167. Evidence: data/measures.csv, lockfile 21 of 37 (56.8 per cent, 37.8 to 75.0; bounds 26.9 to 79.5); data/described.csv, a timed lockfile read by some build in 45 of 90; pair_classes.csv, 41 of 153 lockfile moments undetermined. A reader of either summary believes lockfiles are followed at about three quarters; the evidence says about half, mostly undecided. Resolution: one clause in each: "for projects holding the version only in a lockfile, 21 of 37 decided, with most left undecided".

3. *Fresh builds in the short form.* articles/short/recipes-follow-the-pin.md: "only 2 were shown to let a fresh build take the new release, and a lockfile left unread, as in bbot's, accounted for 7". Evidence: data/s1_fresh_build.csv, 2 admit, 12 exclude, 24 not determined, bbot (P071) among the 24; the record calls S1 an estimate under an assumption. A reader believes bypassing the pin rarely exposes a build; for 24 of 38, the example included, the recipe does not say. Resolution: "2 could have, 12 could not, and for 24, bbot among them, the recipe does not say".

4. *Container build read as build.* articles/recipes-follow-the-pin.md, "What this does to what we said before": "a count of the lockfiles in public repositories should read the recipes beside the lockfiles: among the 90 decided cases where a lockfile held the older version, a build read that lockfile in only half"; carried into investigations/software-updates.md ("Where the evidence stands", "What is next"). Evidence: studies/LH015/brief.md, "Sources and reads": CI workflows were read "only for the context, file, target and arguments they give a recipe"; no install outside a recipe was classed. The frozen-list reading is about downloads, which every install makes, and a project's CI commonly runs `uv sync` or `pip install -r` on the lock whatever its Dockerfile does. A reader believes half of lockfile-holding projects build without their lock; the evidence covers container recipes only. Resolution: "a container build read that lockfile in only half", and for the census: "read the install commands beside the lockfiles, in recipes and CI workflows".

5. *Minor.* "Either way it is more than half" (full piece, "Where we could tell") holds at the point estimate; data/sensitivities.csv gives the undetermined-as-not bound 54.9 per cent with interval 44.2 to 65.4. Say "at the count".

**Readability.** (a) Say why exact pins are followed more: a pin in the manifest is read by any install of the project, a lockfile only by lock-aware commands, so the gap is mostly in where the pin lives, not in project care. (b) Give lock-unused its own denominator, 7 of the 90 decided moments holding a lockfile, not 7 of 167. (c) Say the 68 undetermined moments are 32 repositories, 26 of them four, and 13 undetermined by the folder rule alone, so the reader can weigh the 55 to 84 band.

**Next step.** The record proposes a context-rule fix, a study of which recipes are published to registries, and that the census count lock-unused; the programme's selected next is LH013, fixed and waiting for ClickPy to hold 12 October, so from about 13 October. The registry study would say which builds make run images, not what makes downloads, on the few projects that publish, so it cannot separate the frozen-list explanation from old Pythons, bounds or resolver walks; the context fix touches 13 moments in four repositories; the census still needs an authenticated reader no task has named. LH013 tests whether the baseline holds, not why, and this round changes nothing in its brief. Recommendation: adjust (problems 1 to 4), then wait for LH013. If a session before 13 October takes a slow-road task, the bounded step worth more than the registry study is to class, under LH015's installer table, the install steps of the same 469 moments' CI workflows and documented install commands: the repositories are already cloned once and the workflows read, the cost is about LH015's, the download log records a CI flag that LH011 and LH012 already read, and it would show whether CI reads the lock that the recipe leaves unread, which is the question the frozen-list reading actually turns on. Fold the context rule and the unmatched-wildcard case into that brief.
