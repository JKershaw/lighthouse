## What a reader would believe

A PHP security emergency produced several different kinds of public evidence. Amazon reports an attempted exploit against a decoy on 17 October, before the 21 October public disclosure and fixes. This is a retrospective account, not a Lighthouse sensor observation, and neither success on a real website nor the attacker's source of knowledge is established. AI helped a researcher discover a flaw; people and organisations coordinated fixes and protection. The attack reports do not identify AI use by attackers.

Some providers say they deployed firewall protection; this is different from updating PHP at a website. Selected official and derivative registry images already declared fixed versions when Lighthouse first checked them, and a selected Drupal image contains matching base-layer digests. Those facts show publication boundaries crossed, not the speed or proportion of deployment. The sources do not establish global attack volume, successful intrusions or how many sites are fixed. The Amazon article was readable through one instrument but could not be archived as article bytes by the direct collector. The account promises further dated readings rather than presenting these first observations as a completed census.
