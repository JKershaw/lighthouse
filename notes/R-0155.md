**The finding, said back.** "They looked for research on whether big or small organisations patch or switch on security features more, and found almost nothing: two papers, not enough to say what the research shows. ### What the round establishes

The reader's account in stage 1 matches the record on everything the record establishes. Six fixed searches and fifteen fetches gave two inclusions, which is below the brief's minimum, so the answer is inconclusive under the brief's first condition. Every claim by size comes from one vendor preprint on one scan day (C07, 16 February 2026). C10 gives a difference by type of response team, with p = 0.00076, but no counts per type are stated. The record makes no causal claim. The C08 exclusion follows the brief's definition of an available fix or feature. The stop, the ceiling and the unread candidates are reported as the brief requires.

Where the reader goes too far is the one line a reader would actually carry away: that bigger organisations start sooner but cover less. The record supports neither half as a statement about size.

### Material claim problems

**1. Two thresholds read as size effects (a share read as a rate, a count read as a tendency).**
- Passage. In LH060.md's In short: "slightly more organisations starting with it among those with bigger web footprints, and, read from a scatter with no test, fewer large networks with it on every address". The Answer says the same: "more organisations starting deployment with more SSL evidence and ... more IPv4 addresses (C07-b); and fewer organisations with full coverage as IPv4 count rises (C07-c)".
- Evidence. register.md (C07 row) gives the outcome as "offers a post-quantum key exchange on at least one SSH server; share of the organisation's IPs that do". It covers 1,523,998 services across 27,012 entities, which is about 56 per entity on average (derived).
  - "At least one" becomes more likely as an organisation has more servers, even if every server is equally likely to offer the feature. "All addresses" becomes less likely in the same way.
  - So C07-b and C07-c could both be accurate readings of the paper and still say nothing about how size bears on deployment. They would follow from server count alone.
  - The 45.2 per cent of hosts against 39.9 per cent of entities fits this too (derived), though it doesn't prove it.
  - The record's own Findings say that fewer points at the top of the scatter "can mean fewer large entities overall, not a lower share among them". The In short drops that warning.
  - A further explanation is unexamined. A large network's addresses may carry other people's servers, through hosting or attribution. Then "every address" would measure what the network hosts, not what the operator chose to deploy. This is set against the authors' "complexity penalty", which the record already marks as asserted.
- Resolution. Make one change in the In short and the Answer. Describe C07-b as "more often offering it on at least one server" and C07-c as fewer reaching every address. Add one sentence: both thresholds rise or fall with how many servers an organisation has, even at an equal rate per server, so neither is a rate and neither says larger operators deploy more or less. Add the same point to Limits, beside the existing caveat about the scatter's density.

**2. "Starting" implies a time course the data can't show.**
- Passage. In the In short, "starting with it". In the Answer, "starting deployment". In the claims table, "larger more (starting deployment)".
- Evidence. The window is one scan day (register.md, C07). Both the derived file and the Findings record that the authors think many entities are "unintentionally PQC-ready" through an SSH upgrade with the feature on by default. The outcome is presence on one day, and it may track software currency rather than any choice to deploy.
- Resolution. Replace "starting" with "offering" throughout. Where the In short first names the feature, add that it may arrive with an ordinary SSH upgrade. "Initiate" can stay inside the quotation as the authors' word.

No other material problem. C10's caveats are stated: the difference runs through intermediaries, "national" is not a sector, and counts per type are missing. The Answer's handling of the structure field already says a network is not an operator, as P-0050 also does. The method the brief fixed in advance asks the right question for a desk study. I'd challenge only its rule that type counts as size: in the record, type is the only measure on which the two documents agree, and the Answer keeps type and size apart.

### Readability (optional)

1. The In short packs four results into one sentence. Split it after the vendor's three readings.
2. "Bigger web footprints" means SSL evidence counts, and "large networks" means IPv4 counts. Say so once, so a reader doesn't take them as one measure of size.
3. The Answer's bold sentence would read more plainly as "All three size readings come from one company's scans of one day".

### Next step

Recommendation: make the two wording changes above, have the recheck read them once, then release the result as a line on the investigation page with no article, and let the study rest.
- The investigation page's line still says "nothing read yet". It should change when the result is released, not before, because the record is unreviewed.
- Don't amend the brief to read C05 or C06's report PDFs, C01 elsewhere, or the two untitled results. That would be reading more because the count fell short, an outcome-led widening labelled post hoc. C05 is vendor material on the vendor's own customers and couldn't lift the vendor limit anyway.
- P-0050 remains the one live follow-up, and its gate should take point 1 with it. A per-network table invites the same any-or-all artefact, and a hosting network's addresses are not one operator's choices. Any brief should fix in advance a patched share per network within size bands, not "any" or "all" thresholds, and should first establish whether the list is complete. Its safety narrowing (bands only, no network named) should stand.
- Neither a continued search nor P-0050 outranks the PHP follow-ups. A desk read that is already inconclusive earns no further GETs.
