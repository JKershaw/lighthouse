## What I found

**Reproduced (derived from re-reads, observation).** All 26 re-read files matched hours.csv and minutes.csv on every field compared, including sha256, 15 to 32 minutes after the study read them, and about 47 minutes after the reader reviewer read two of them (2026-10-08-12 and 2026-11-01-12). Every re-read file held PushEvent, CreateEvent and DeleteEvent all together or not at all; every PushEvent carried exactly `before`, `head`, `push_id`, `ref`, `repository_id` (2,165,324 in the 46 files) and none a commit list; every CreateEvent (218,055) had `ref_type` "branch"; no event fell outside its hour; no line failed to parse.

**Recomputed from hours.csv (derived).** Files without PushEvent in the panel: 26 of 176 (14.8 per cent) from 1 September to 14 October and 64 of 96 (66.7 per cent) from 15 October to 7 November; by hour of day 8, 5, 5, 8 of 44 and 17, 15, 16, 16 of 24; by month 19 of 120, 49 of 124, 22 of 28. No file of the 452 splits the three types. CreateEvents in the 452 first reads: 1,808,380, all `ref_type` "branch" (1,821,094 if the six re-reads are counted again). Reference hours: pull request events 11,989 at 1 January 12:00, 4,659 at 1 May, 442 at 1 June, 425 at 1 July, 95 at 15 August; the four control types 21,503 at 1 January and 189 at 15 August; PushEvents 166,990 of 170,586 events at 15 August. Minimum minutes with events 57. Runs, medians in Findings 6 and 7 and the whole-day counts as the record gives them. The fall in pull request events is large but not steady: 7,714 at 15 January, 10,918 at 1 February, 3,232 at 1 April, 4,659 at 1 May.

One wording point, not an error: the "January to August reference median for those four types (6,397)" in Finding 6 is the sum of the four types' separate medians, as the brief's per-type baseline implies; the median of the summed counts is 7,472, which gives ratios of 0.42 and 0.35 rather than 0.49 and 0.41. Either way the baseline spans a hundredfold fall, so the ratios carry little.

**Unread hours (observation; check made after outcomes were seen).** All 20 present and non-empty, events in all 60 minutes. Predictions: 6 "none", all without pushes; 12 "pushes", all with pushes in all 60 minutes; 2 "either", one without (6 November 03:00) and one partial (6 November 09:00: pushes in minutes 0 to 18, then none, the shape the record describes). Pushes, creations and deletions were together in all 20. 21 October 15:00, during GitHub's incident of 14:52 to 19:22 on its event streaming platform, held pushes in every minute. 3 September 21:00, without pushes, held 27,505 events, 20,990 of them the four control types. On these hours the sample's pattern holds: neighbouring panel files predicted every unread hour they could call.

**What I found wrong or unlabelled (all corrected; see What I changed).**

1. The Answer's first sentence put the 452 files in the window 1 September to 7 November; 20 of them are January to August references and 8 November files.
2. Finding 1 drew "not rewritten later" from the Last-Modified header inside a finding labelled observation.
3. Finding 5 said each hour on 14 October started again "at full rate" at minute 0. It did not: PushEvents in minute 0 were 1,334 at 12:00, 1,837 at 14:00, 777 at 15:00, 969 at 16:00, 833 at 17:00, 735 at 18:00 and 587 at 19:00 (26 October 10:00: 745, against 1,708 at 06:00). The post hoc budget reading rested on the same phrase. Also unmentioned there: all 37 partial push hours, and my 6 November 09:00, lose their pushes before minute 59, and the four that start mid-hour stop again within the hour.
4. Limits said the gaps "do not line up" with GitHub's status incidents. For 22 October the panel cannot say: the run without pushes from 22 October 18:00 follows a 12:00 file with pushes, and the incident that delayed "repository push events (12m)" ran 13:05 to 15:20. The 23 October incident, on Dependabot's processing of "events related to repository pushes", falls inside that run and was not listed (the retained incidents JSON, read as text). The others do not line up: 7 October and 4 November fell inside runs that had begun earlier, 19 and 21 October while the files held pushes.
5. "What this means": "From the files read, that did not hold for most of 2026", said of articles/what-we-can-see.md, rests on reading a fall in the files' counts as events GitHub had and the files lack. That is a strong inference but an inference, which the record's own Limits ("Files, not GitHub") say the counts alone cannot make; and "most of 2026" rests on two hour-12 files a month while early September held more pull request events per hour than January (30,009 at 5 September 00:00, in a file without pushes, against 11,989 at 1 January 12:00). The I28 line's "the hourly files were all present" lacked its sample, and the LH043 line's "if anything understated" was an unlabelled interpretation.
