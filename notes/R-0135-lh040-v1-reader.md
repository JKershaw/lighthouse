# R-0135: reader-and-inference review of LH040 version 1.0, stage one

## What a reader would believe, written before reading the record

**Principal finding, said back.** On 21 October 2026 a LiteLLM release, 1.107.0, that the maintainers and security firms say was poisoned was on PyPI for under two hours, and PyPI's logs (as ClickPy holds them) show about 49,000 downloads of it that day, 1.4 per cent of LiteLLM's downloads. For the time it was the newest version it was picked up about as fast as a normal new LiteLLM release, by a test set in advance. After it was removed, about 1,500 more downloads trickled in over the next month, none by pip.

**Observed.** Download counts per version and day in one copy of PyPI's logs, the installer names on the later fetches, and that a second read two weeks later gave the same figures (the "In short" says so; the proposed line doesn't).

**Explained.** Nothing much is explained. The reader takes it that a bad release spreads like any new release because tools pull the newest version automatically, but that is the reader's inference; the text doesn't say why. Who or what made the post-removal fetches (mirrors, scanners, browsers, researchers) is left open.

**Caused what.** No causal claim is made. The text says outright that downloads aren't installs or infections. A reader might still take "within the ordinary range" as "the attack reached as many people as a normal release would".

**Limits a reader would notice.** A fetch is not an install. The record leans on one source, ClickPy. The window depends on whose end time you use, and under the maintainers' end the release sits near the bottom of the range: the proposed line leaves that out. The record calls itself unreviewed, but the proposed line says "evidence-reviewed". Two later days have no data. The view of what depends on the release is missing.

## Stage two: against the record

Read on 24 November 2026: studies/LH040/ (record 1.0 with its evidence-review edits, brief with the 9 November amendment, sources, tables, data, scripts, closure checkpoint), investigations/software-updates.md, programme.md's Next, and studies/LH056/brief.md. My checks are kept as studies/LH040/review/r0135_index_checks.py, with output in r0135_index_checks_output.txt. No network was used.

**What the round establishes.** The figures in the proposed line match the record and tables/m3_index.csv: 49,043 fetches on day 0, a 1.42 per cent share, 1,502 fetches on 28 of 30 days, and none by pip. The pre-set reading "within the range" holds under all three window ends. The measurements are sound as counts. What can mislead a reader is the reading drawn from them. The rule calls "ordinary" anything from 0.59 to 1.39 times the comparison median. Under the maintainers' end, 1.107.0 sits at 0.68: below all nine comparison releases uploaded before 13:00 UTC (0.249 to 0.344) and below 16 of the 17 overall. So the evidence supports "not faster than an ordinary release, and probably at the low end". It doesn't support "spread just like one". It also can't tell ordinary uptake from uptake held down by up to about 40 per cent, for example by time of day, by caches slow to see a new release, or by something treating the release differently. The record says the index is a description and makes no claim about cause, which is right. My stage-one reader came away believing "it spread like a normal release". Fetches on day 0 after 13:23 don't change this: at day 1's rate they would be about 146, or 0.3 per cent of C0. LH056's installer-only robustness check can't be applied to LH040, because Q4 holds installer rows only for the case and comparison versions.

**Material claim problems.**
1. *Review status.* The proposed line says "evidence-reviewed". studies/LH040/LH040.md says otherwise in several places: the header, `version: 1.0 (record; unreviewed)`; the In short, "this record is unreviewed"; the Answer, which opens "Unreviewed."; and the Limits, "Unreviewed: no evidence review or reader review has read this record". The record's own Corrections log the evidence review's edits. This breaks the consistency rule. Resolution: update the header, In short, Answer and Limits to "evidence-reviewed, reader review pending".
2. *"sits within the range" (the proposed line).* This leaves out the qualifier the record's In short keeps ("low in that range unless the window ended early"). The evidence is in m3_index.csv and my output: 17th of 18, at 0.68 of the median, under the maintainers' end, and the band admits 0.59 to 1.39. Resolution: "...that sits within the range of 17 ordinary LiteLLM releases of the same weeks, by a rule fixed before any count was read, though under the maintainers' end time it is second lowest of the 18 (0.68 of their median), and the rule counts anything from 0.59 to 1.39 times the median as ordinary." The record's Answer should also state the band's width. Its low position should be described, not explained.
3. *"none by pip" (the proposed line).* Read on its own, this suggests no installer fetched it after the pull. The record's Measure 2 gives 954 fetches with no installer name, 489 Browser, 33 uv, 16 requests and 9 Nexus. Resolution: "mostly with no installer name or a browser's; 33 by uv, none by pip".

**Readability.**
- In the line, write "fetched 49,043 times that day, including any fetches after it was pulled", since a reader takes all 49,043 as falling within the two hours.
- Put "1.4 per cent of the day's fetches, in under two hours of it" so that the share isn't read as slow uptake.
- In the line, "for the hours it was the newest release" reads more easily as "per hour as the newest release".

**Next step.** Fix problems 1 to 3, then have a fresh agent recheck the changed sentences. After that, keep the record and the investigation-page line, with no piece, as programme.md's Next already plans; any piece waits for LH053 and LH056. No further public evidence can separate the explanations for the low index. That would need hourly logs, which the gate's no-BigQuery condition rules out, so another LH040 read wouldn't add much. LH056 does better in two ways: it has a floor of 100 fetches, and an installer-only check whose Q3 does fetch all versions (scripts/collect.py), so the check can be computed there. But it shares the same wide "within range" rule, over 13 releases. I recommend that LH056's record and any later side-by-side description report each case's rank, its ratio to the median and the band's min/median to max/median width beside the pre-set word. That is a description, not a change to the protocol, so it needs no post hoc label. Agreement across the three cases should not be read as showing that nothing in the path tells a poisoned release apart.

**Combined disclosure:** clears. The material is aggregate fetch counts and reported, high-level descriptions of an incident that is already public, with no added joins, pointers or recipes.
