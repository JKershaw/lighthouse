# Plan for revising the commissioning essay

**In short.** The essay the keeper supplied when commissioning the robotics investigation can become a published Lighthouse essay, and the revision is earned: its argument, that an obedient machine can harm the people it is used on, is interpretation that doesn't depend on any deployment being proved, and the baseline study now gives checked evidence for some of its examples and a clear account of what is unknown for the others. About a third of its factual sentences rest on reporting we haven't read, and several are written as if established. The plan below keeps the argument, drops or attributes every claim no readable source supports, replaces the "proposed research direction" ending with what the investigation actually found, and waits for tomorrow's final conference read so the essay doesn't describe the conference's outcome from guesswork. Drafting starts on 28 November; checks and reviews by 11 December.

The essay as received ([original-essay.md](original-essay.md), 12 November 2026); the brief, Amendment 1 and the reviewed record version 0.1 of [LH050](../../studies/LH050/LH050.md) (cleared by evidence review R-0073, reader review R-0074 and recheck R-0075 on 16 November); the integration proposal's sections 4 and 6 ([integration-proposal.md](integration-proposal.md)); the investigation page; the retained baseline bodies of PA-S24 to PA-S27, searched on 26 November 2026 at about 15:20 UTC for Eric Trump, AMD, casualties, precision, Gaza, Israel and LeBlanc. No source outside the repository was read for this plan; the post-conference checkpoints of 21 and 26 November were read only through their queue notes.

## Is a revision earned?

Yes, as an essay, under AGENTS.md's essay rule: interpretation, labelled as such, with every figure or claim cited, and ending with what would test it. Three reasons.

1. The essay's core is a way of seeing (accidental, autonomous and institutional harm; "safe for whom"; the machine that obeys) and an untested hypothesis (the economics of power). Neither needs a deployment to be established, and the essay already says the economics is a hypothesis.
2. The baseline record gives checked support for the essay's airport section and an honest account of the Foundation case: what is reported, in whose words, and what couldn't be read. That lets the revised essay be specific without overclaiming.
3. The pilot allows two released pieces. A reader account of the baseline (row LH050-reader-draft) and this essay are those two; they divide cleanly: the account reports what the sources show, the essay argues what it means and links to the account.

What the revision must not do: carry the burden of proving deployments (the proposal's own words), repeat any reading as a finding, or put a claim about a named person's conduct in Lighthouse's voice without a readable source.

## Changes to the text

1. **Header and opening.** Give it an essay's header (site/README.md) and an opening that says it is interpretation, written to commission the investigation in November 2026 and revised once its first study was checked. Keep the airport thought experiment, which is labelled as one; it is the essay's best hook. Remove "this week" and the "proposed research direction for Lighthouse" framing throughout.
2. **The Foundation story.** Rewrite around what the record shows: a humanoid its maker is reported to want to arm, a two-robot logistics demonstration in Ukraine described by eWeek as "far from battlefield-ready" (C029), reported research contracts, and a proposed Gaza use that couldn't be read. Keep the essay's own list of distinctions (a discussion is not an order; armed is not autonomous); they are already right.
3. **The argument sections** ("The problem with the way we talk about AI safety", "The economics of power") stay nearly as they are; light edits for house style (no dashes in prose, plainer sentences). Label the three-way taxonomy as the essay's proposal and the economics as a hypothesis the pilot cannot test; name the evidence a test would need (the brief's list: supervision per system, operating time, total ownership costs, coverage, operations enabled or forgone).
4. **Gaza and the asymmetry of protection.** Keep the argument that protecting operators is not protecting the people subjected to a weapon; it doesn't depend on the unread report. Reword "an occupying force", which is a contested characterisation, so the essay's point (a system that lowers the cost of sustaining operations) is made without taking a position the evidence doesn't need; attribute any legal characterisation. Keep the questions a civilian would ask; they become the essay's frame for what the record could not answer.
5. **From the battlefield to the airport.** Keep, tightened to the supported Changi facts and GIBSON's takeover-for-movement, with San Francisco only if verified (row 9).
6. **The feedback loop.** Keep the structural argument; drop the paragraph on a named person's possible conflict of interest unless both the questions and the denials are read together (row 6).
7. **"Where this fits" and "A modest beginning".** Replace the suggested questions with two short paragraphs linking the investigation page, the record and the reader account if released: what public sources we could read do and don't say about who controls force, with the record's main limit (the two rows that could have tested it were unreadable). Lighthouse's own figures come only from the released account or a released record; if neither is released when the essay is, it links the investigation page and cites no figure of Lighthouse's own.
8. **Ending.** Keep "Who is accountable for what this machine is allowed to do to me?". Add "What would test this": the weekly panel to 24 December (LH050), the policy comparison, and the evidence an economics-of-power test would need, saying none is scheduled.
9. **Colophon.** Supplied by the keeper, generated in another session (author and model as known; the original's model is not recorded), revised by the drafting agent, checked and reviewed as below, released by the run that releases it.

## Method and bounds for the drafting run

- Draft on or after 28 November, after LH050-policy-final has run (or been marked missed), and by 4 December, as the brief's window says.
- Outside reads: at most eight one-off reads under scripts/lh_fetch.py with `access='oneoff'`, `LIGHTHOUSE_PROVIDER=anthropic`, limited to the essay's own cited URLs (FT, WIRED, Reuters, the ICRC FAQ if the retained copy is insufficient) and one San Francisco Board of Supervisors record. These are fact checks for an essay, not LH050 collection: they don't count against the study's baseline or panel bounds and enter no matrix cell, and the essay says so. Keep no copy where `keep_decision` says no; a paywall, challenge or refusal is an observation and ends that route. No search excerpt is cited as a source.
- Keep the revision in essays/ with `status: draft` and the original untouched; record each kept, cut or attributed claim in a short table beside the draft (the rows above, updated).
- Brief every subagent to be cautious and to keep the brief's line: no legality determinations about a specific attack, no identification of anyone a source anonymised, nothing that would help optimise targeting.

## Checks before release

As AGENTS.md sets for an essay: a fresh agent checks every figure and citation against the records and sources cited; then the reader-and-inference review; then one fresh recheck of whatever the resolution writes. Release only if nothing must be fixed, and only within the pilot's two-piece limit and resource envelope. Measured attributable usage for LH050 is a few dollars per run so far (costs.csv, list-rate estimates), well under the $80 reassessment point; the commissioning session's and some runs' costs are unknown, not zero.
