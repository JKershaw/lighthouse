# When AI acquires physical authority

## A proposal for integrating armed robotics into Lighthouse

12 November 2026. Prepared for John Kershaw by OpenAI Codex. Commissioning proposal, not a released Lighthouse finding. Exact model identifier and attributable monetary cost unavailable.

**The decision to track armed AI-powered robots has already been made.** This proposal addresses how to do it. It follows a read-only survey of Lighthouse and a bounded review of public research and monitoring sources. No repository, publication, schedule or automation has been changed.

### Recommendation

Create a dedicated investigation, provisionally titled **When AI acquires physical authority**, asking:

> What can public evidence establish about the capabilities, deployment and human control of armed AI-powered robots, and the consequences for people subjected to them?

Begin with a six-week pilot: one bounded baseline study, a small dated watch, and a reviewed account for readers. Foundation's proposed Gaza application is the first case, with two contrasting cases used to check that the method distinguishes armed robotics, autonomous targeting and ordinary patrol automation. A short policy baseline around the November weapons conference supplies context, not a separate permanent monitoring programme.

Lighthouse's contribution should be a traceable history of changes in capability, institutional authority and evidence. Existing organisations already catalogue weapons and policies. The opportunity is to connect those records over time, preserve their uncertainty and show what the changes mean from a civilian perspective.

### Watch functions and decisions, not just robot shapes

An armed robot can use AI for navigation while a person chooses every target and authorises every shot. An autonomous weapon may use no machine learning at all. An unarmed patrol robot may exercise institutional authority without being a weapon. The ICRC's distinctions make a single label such as "autonomous robot" inadequate.

The core population should include AI-enabled armed robotic systems, whether humanoid, wheeled, quadrupedal or airborne. Non-AI autonomous weapons provide historical context. Unarmed policing systems and military decision-support software enter only as named comparisons or documented connections to the core cases. This keeps the subject broad enough to answer John's question without expanding into all military AI or all surveillance.

The initial cases are purposive and already partly known. They cannot establish prevalence, growth rates or which countries lead.

## 3. What to record

Use the **claim or event about a specified system configuration in a specified context** as the basic unit. A company is too broad: different versions, customers and modes can have different capabilities.

For each case maintain a short readable account, backed by a simple structured event ledger:

| Dimension | Record separately |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Platform, version or configuration, developer, procurer/operator and jurisdiction; unknowns explicit. |
| Capability | Navigation, perception, tracking, target recommendation, target selection, engagement and abort functions. Mark each human-controlled, automated, mixed or unknown, with the source's exact scope. |
| AI involvement | Which function reportedly uses AI, whose claim it is, and what supports it. Do not infer AI from the robot's appearance. |
| Human control | Who sets the mission and permitted targets, who authorises force, what intervention is reported, and which restrictions are documented. Separate stated policy from tested practice. |
| Maturity | Proposal, demonstration, trial, purchase, delivery, operational use, suspension or withdrawal. These are separate events, not an inevitable sequence. |
| Evidence | Source, originating source family, passage, publication/revision date, event date, first observation, access route and contradiction history. |
| Civilian consequences | Reported injuries or deaths, coercion, privacy impacts, restrictions on movement, complaints, investigations and remedies. Keep harm, platform attribution and AI-function attribution separate. |
| Economics | Advertised price, awarded funding, delivered quantity, support costs and supervision requirements when public. Label currency, date, scope and inclusion of maintenance or services. |

Use evidence descriptions such as supplier claim, official account, third-party report and reproducible public artifact. Avoid a numerical trust score that hides different kinds of uncertainty. Ten articles repeating one interview are one source family.

Preserve retractions, failed trials, cancelled purchases and unchanged readings. Otherwise the record will select only apparent progress. A system's later capability does not retrospectively establish its earlier configuration.

**Example:** the FT report motivating the attachment concerns preliminary discussions about supplying robots for Gaza tunnel operations. An attempted opening here reached a subscription page. It belongs initially as an attributed lead, not evidence of an order, deployed armed robots or autonomous targeting. The landing session should obtain an authorised readable copy or retain the access limit.

## 4. The first six weeks

The dates below assume a start on 13 November 2026. If started later, shift the pilot dates while retaining the conference's actual dates. Suggested resource limits are planning choices, not measured cost estimates.

### First: establish the evidence baseline

One study asks: **For three deliberately contrasting cases, what can public records establish about armament, AI functions, human decisions and deployment status?**

Fix the brief before systematic coding. Disclose the prior knowledge in this proposal and the attachment; these are not unseen outcomes. Retain the case-selection rationale and list documents that were unavailable.

1. **Foundation/Phantom and the proposed Gaza application.** Reconstruct the chain of claims through readable reporting, company statements, published partner statements and discoverable official award records. Distinguish funding announced, awarded and inherited through an acquisition. Do not promise that contracts or quantities can be independently established.
2. **An armed non-humanoid comparison.** A candidate is the US Marine Corps' 2023 "robotic goat" test. A search result from the Marine Corps describes remote operation; the article itself returned 403 here. Admit it only once a readable first-party record is retained, or document exclusion and select a replacement under a predeclared rule. It tests classification, not a claim of AI targeting.
3. **Civilian patrol comparison at Changi Airport.** The Singapore Police Force's 15 June 2023 account describes autonomous patrol, cameras, warnings and communication with police. It does not establish an armed deployment. Use it to show how the ledger handles authority without silently labelling every patrol robot a weapon.

Cap the baseline at three cases and 30 substantive source documents, with one bounded follow-up search for a missing key fact. Deliver a case table, source/access register, dated timeline and explicit account of what remains unknowable. No worldwide inventory.

### Alongside it: preserve a policy baseline

The ICRC page identifies the CCW Seventh Review Conference on **16–20 November 2026** as an important decision point. Capture accessible official pre-conference and post-conference documents. Compare proposals, agreed wording and future commitments; do not treat a call for negotiation as an adopted treaty.

Use three planned reads: before the conference if possible, after it closes, and a final document check by 27 November. If records remain inaccessible or unpublished, close that comparison as incomplete. No daily live-blogging is needed.

### Then: keep a small watch

After the baseline, fix no more than 12 public source endpoints for the core Foundation case and relevant policy documents. Read weekly, with at most one additional read in a week when the normal horizon sweep finds a specific material development. Run through **24 December 2026**, then stop unless explicitly renewed.

An endpoint can be an accessible announcement page or public record search with fixed terms. Source discovery is separate from repeated panel reads. Respect access rules; record a missing or blocked source without trying progressively more evasive routes.

A material development is a documented change in capability, authorisation, procurement, delivery, deployment, suspension, harm evidence or accountability. Another rendering of the same promotional video is not a separate advance.

The existing horizon sweep can cover wider leads through its policy and wildcard briefs, explicitly including non-Western developments and local-language reporting where accessible. Keep candidate selection and language limits visible. Do not add an eighth permanent scout by default.

### Publish what the baseline earns

The first reader account could ask **What is an armed robot allowed to decide?** Its central visual should be a sourced matrix of the three cases: what moves autonomously, who selects targets, who authorises force, and what deployment evidence exists. A timeline can place announcements and documented decisions on separate rows. Unknown cells remain visible.

Revise the supplied essay as the thread's interpretation and motivation, keeping the decision to observe settled and replacing its prospective research suggestions with links to the actual investigation. Fact-check its specific reporting and legal statements before review. The essay should not carry the burden of proving deployments.

Follow Lighthouse's existing evidence, reader and recheck requirements before public release. The dated watch log remains visibly unreviewed working material until its changes pass through that process.

### Evaluate the pilot

At the six-week checkpoint compare continuing, narrowing the panel, repairing a measurement, or placing it at rest. This evaluates the method and cadence, not whether the topic deserves attention.

Continue if the pilot produces a useful, reviewable account of at least one important distinction or change, a clear map of evidence gaps, and affordable collection and review. A well-supported finding that public evidence cannot settle deployment or autonomy can itself be useful. If the watch adds no information, rest it with clear reopening triggers rather than manufacturing regular articles.

Suggested initial envelope: one baseline study, at most two public pieces, no more than two researcher-days of active work across the pilot, and a planning ceiling of $100 in attributable agent usage where measurable. This is deliberately a proposal, despite the current programme having no general spend ceiling. Record actual costs and unknown costs; revise the envelope before exceeding it, rather than imply subscriptions have per-run bills we can observe.

## 5. Civilian outcomes and the economics of power

Civilian consequences should be a field in every core case, not a later essay appended to a technology catalogue. Missing evidence must say **not established by the sources read**, not "no civilian harm". Record how far the account can connect an alleged event to an operator, platform, configuration and AI function. A civilian-harm report in an area where robots operate does not by itself make that connection.

Use specialist published assessments and local reporting where available. No unsolicited witness contact, victim identification, graphic-media collection programme or battlefield geolocation work is proposed. Human expertise, particularly language and humanitarian expertise, may become a defined collaboration need if the evidence demands it.

The economics-of-power argument in the attachment is a hypothesis worth keeping. The pilot can record what data would test it: human supervision per system, sustained operating time, total ownership costs, changes in coverage, and operations enabled or forgone. It cannot estimate the cost of coercion from advertised purchase prices or apply Harbour's software-agent economics directly to physical forces.

Competing explanations include expensive prototypes remaining marginal, autonomy staying limited to navigation, public resistance constraining deployment, and changed operational practices reducing some harms. Track these possibilities alongside expansion. Better targeting accuracy, even if established, does not alone establish lower total civilian harm.

## 7. Limits of this survey and handoff

This is a source-grounded integration proposal, not a completed deployment investigation. The public site could not be opened through the web reader, so the architectural survey relies on repository sources rather than a visual audit. The full FT article was inaccessible, Foundation's defence-oriented website timed out, the Marine Corps article returned 403, and some UN pages were available only as search results. No procurement database, company production claim, weapon configuration or casualty attribution was independently audited. The San Francisco reversal in the supplied essay was not reverified here and is not needed for the pilot design.

The external sources cited above were accessed or attempted on 12 November 2026. Readable source material included AWW's about and selection pages, SIPRI's research and procurement publication pages, UNIDIR's portal methodology, the ICRC FAQ, General Comment 37 including paragraph 95, the Singapore Police announcement, Airwars' methodology and the two UK assurance publication pages. SIPRI's complete downloadable report and the full JSP directive were not reviewed. Search results are discovery leads wherever the underlying page could not be read.

The next session should start with the current repository state, record the commissioning decision, settle the proposed capacity and resource envelope, and prepare the baseline brief. It should retain this proposal and the supplied essay as commissioning context, clearly separate from evidence. Dates, source access and available study numbers must be checked at landing.

The intended result is a small, durable thread that can tell a reader what changed, who claims it, what authority was delegated, what is known about the people affected, and where the public evidence stops.
