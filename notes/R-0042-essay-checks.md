## 2. Reader and inference

**What a careful reader comes away believing** (written from the essay alone). On 21 October PHP and Node.js both shipped security fixes. The authors could only watch PHP from outside, through a company that scans the internet for servers announcing their PHP version, and Node.js only from inside, through counts of downloads from its website. They argue this is not just a limitation but a clue: old PHP websites sit untouched for years and change only when someone acts, while much Node.js and Python use is rebuilt constantly by automated systems, which pick up a fix quickly unless a lockfile pins the old version. A week on, both fixes had reached a little over half to about three quarters in their counts, but those numbers measure different things and their similarity means nothing. Each view has a blind spot: downloads never see a forgotten server, and the scanner misses servers that hide their version or are patched without a new number. More than half of PHP servers announcing a version run branches that get no fixes at all, a layer that looks small from the download side. The authors say all this is their interpretation, made after seeing the data, and list what the day-30 figures could show to prove it wrong.

That matches what the essay intends and what the records support, with the qualifications it states. A reader is unlikely to take a ranking from it.

**Could the figures be right and the reading wrong, and does the essay say so?** Yes, and mostly it does: "it could be wrong", the coincidence of the two week-one ranges, "no number here measures another", and the July line as a case that could break the first test. One alternative was missing: that the inside and outside split reflects who chose to publish a count (Censys built its tally around this emergency, and nothing comparable exists for Node.js) as much as how the software lives. I added one sentence saying so after "the side its software turns towards the world". A second alternative is left as a judgement: the 54 per cent unfixed share from outside and the 10 per cent from inside could differ because the scanner over-counts old servers that announce versions and under-counts patched or silent ones, which the essay does say in "What each view cannot see", so it is covered.

**Readings labelled as made after the evidence was seen?** Yes, in the opening, in "Our reading is", "We read that as", "We think", "we expect", the "noticed after the counts were in" of the working week, and the first line of "What would test this". The essay does not give a number of comparisons, which AGENTS.md asks of a post hoc reading; for an essay whose readings are qualitative this is a judgement, not a must-fix.

**Interpretation presented as finding, ranking, cause, or a download or service treated as a machine?**
- "Python showed both" presented the lockfile mechanism as shown; two-days-for-most says "nothing in the log separates those readings". Fixed: "Python's September figures fit both, though they cannot show which mechanism was at work."
- "a change in the instrument, not the internet" stated LH033's interpretation as fact. Fixed (above).
- "Censys can do this largely because ..." was an unsupported causal claim about Censys's method. Fixed (above).
- "misses two common kinds of care": how common is not measured. "common" removed.
- No sentence ranks the ecosystems; "a coincidence, not an agreement" and "not one race run at different speeds" explicitly refuse to. Downloads are called fetches throughout; services are defined as an address and a port. The machine language is in the illustrations ("Picture a PHP website ...", "A forgotten Node.js server") and in "Neither instrument sees the machine itself", which is the right way round.
- "only 57 to 72 per cent a month on": "only" leans slightly, since the top of the range rose 11 points; kept as voice, noted.
- "Node.js has servers that stand for years" rests on no record; it is uncontroversial and the essay says it knows of no count of them. Kept.

**Could the tests fail, and do they name the studies?** The first could fail and names a counterexample. The second, as drafted, stated no failing outcome; it now says "If they have caught up by day 30, this reading fails." The third and fourth honestly say no study exists ("A study could ..."). The essay links both studies' briefs, but neither brief fixes the comparisons the tests use (fourth-week movement against the first week; older lines against newer at day 30 as a test of the reading). AGENTS.md asks the round that puts a post hoc reading on a public surface to name the brief, amendment or issue that will test it, or say none can. I added: "Their briefs fix the day-30 figures but not the comparisons below; unless those are set down before the day-30 reads, the figures will check these readings, not test them as predictions fixed in advance." **Recommended, not required for release:** before the reads of 21 and 22 November, add dated amendments to studies/LH033/brief.md and studies/LH034/brief.md fixing the first two tests (for example: share of the day-0-to-30 rise that falls in days 21 to 30, per branch and line; whether the oldest fixed line or branch is still lowest on day 30), then replace that sentence with links to the amendments.

**Is the story clear and well told?** Yes. It opens with the event, says it is an essay at once, and builds one idea (each instrument sees the side its software turns to the world) through two pictures, standing and rebuilt, then the blind spots, then the old layer. Terms are defined where first needed (branch, service, header, lockfile). "The working week" is a digression, but it is labelled as one and is the kind of detail a curious reader enjoys. The turn to AI in "Side by side" is the largest leap; it is labelled ("We think", "we expect") and tied to the watch's purpose, so it is acceptable. The notes are now accurate about where each figure comes from.
