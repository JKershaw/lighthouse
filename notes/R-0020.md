# R-0020: rarer-further-down, reader review

Stage one, written after reading only the full piece, its short form and the README's "Current focus", and nothing else in the repository.

## What a reader would believe, written before reading the record

**The finding, said back.** Among Python's fifty most downloaded libraries, a new version usually became half of the downloads within two days (26 of 37 that released). Further down it is rarer: about half of libraries ranked 51 to 500, a quarter of those ranked 501 to 5,000. New versions there start from a smaller first-day share (typically 58, 48 and 23 in a hundred) and mostly keep it for a month.

**Observed.** Daily downloads by version from a public copy of PyPI's log, for 80 libraries drawn at random from each band, 52 and 35 of which released between April and August 2026, measured as the top fifty were, by rules fixed beforehand. Libraries mostly fell wholly one way or the other. Mirrors fetched under one per cent and changed no result.

**Explained.** Only that the drop is mostly in the size of a new version's share, not its speed, and that mirrors are not behind it. Nothing is said to cause anything: "rank is not a reason", and dependents, pinning, Python versions and release frequency were not measured. A reader learns that lower rank goes with smaller early uptake, not why.

**Limits a reader would notice.** Small samples with wide intervals, so the first band may be over half. A download is a fetch, not a person or machine, so a small share need not mean a slow spread. Pillow and sentry-sdk were chosen afterwards as illustrations. The short form's summary and the README say 52 and 35 libraries were "drawn"; the full piece says 80 were drawn and 52 and 35 released.
