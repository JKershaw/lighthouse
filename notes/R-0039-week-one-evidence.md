## What I found and changed

1. **Title, a plain error of denominator (fixed).** "more than half of the PHP services we could see stood where no fix reaches": Censys saw 110.9 million PHP services on 28 October and the 24.4 million on unfixed branches are 22 per cent of them; more than half holds only for services showing a version. "No fix reaches" also contradicts the body, which says distributions may still patch some. Now: "more than half of the PHP services showing a version ran branches PHP no longer fixes". The short form's summary had "branches no fix reaches"; now "branches PHP no longer fixes", as its body already said.
2. **"Some organisations took sites offline" (fixed).** No record supports it: LH027's one selected outage notice returned HTTP 403 and its record is inconclusive; the other leads are unopened search results. Replaced with what LH024 read and the released PHP account already says: "British and European Union security agencies advised isolating exposed systems that could not be patched promptly."
3. **"A scheduled security release" (fixed).** Nothing in LH034's brief or record says the Node.js release was scheduled or pre-announced; "scheduled" removed. "Ten flaws" is supported (brief: ten CVEs, from the advisory read 27 October).
4. **"On the second day" (fixed, piece and short form).** With 21 October as day 0, a reader counting the release day as the first day would read 22 October; the figures are for day 2, 23 October. Now "two days after release" (and "six days after" for 8.2 and 8.3). I added to the Node.js sentence that line 22 slipped just under half again over the following weekend (48.1 and 49.3 per cent), which the records report and "passed half" alone hid.
5. **"8.5 ran above both" (fixed).** 8.5 was above every Node.js line only on days 5 to 7 (below on days 0 to 4, e.g. 70.5 against 79.7 on day 2). Now "was above both by then", in the sentence about a week on.
6. **"12 to 17 per cent" (fixed).** The unfixed Node.js lines' daily maximum, 14 to 28 October, is 16.46 per cent; now "12 to 16".
7. **"The largest were 7.4, 8.1, 7.2, 5.6 and 5.2" placed on 28 October (fixed).** That order is the baseline snapshot of 25 October (record); in the read of 29 October 5.6 (2.05 million) is above 7.2 (2.02). Now "7.4 and 8.1, with 7.2, 5.6 and 5.2 at about two million each", true in both snapshots.
8. **"The Python fixes kept creeping up for a month" (fixed).** starlette 1.1.0 fell from 59.5 per cent on day 7 to 57.3 on day 30. Now "three of the four Python fixes were higher a month on than a week on".
9. **"A service that hides its version is not in Censys's count at all; ... that was most of the PHP services it found" (fixed).** Self-contradictory: Censys counts them, as unknown. Now "not in Censys's patched shares at all".
10. **Censys's lag caveat (fixed).** Its notes say "by up to about a week for some websites"; the piece dropped the last three words. Restored.
11. **Post hoc labelling of the July posting-day alignment (fixed).** AGENTS.md asks a post hoc reading on a public surface to carry the number of comparisons and to name what will test it or say none can. The piece now says "a single comparison we chose after seeing the numbers, which no later count can test"; LH034's Findings now say no later read can test it on unread data and the brief's rule stays the measure.
12. **Figure, double rounding (fixed, script re-run).** The tooltip for PHP 8.3 day 2 said 42.1 per cent; the value is 3,071,109 / 7,285,788 = 42.152 per cent (record: 42.2). The script took shares already rounded to four places and rounded again. It now divides the unrounded counts held in the tables (patched/targets; at_or_newer/line_downloads); two points moved by 0.1 px and that one label changed. Re-rendered and re-read in both themes.
13. **LH033 Method contradicted its Limits (fixed).** Method said Censys calls a service patched when "seen on a fixed release, or seen earlier on an affected one and later on a fixed one"; Censys's notes (discovery.md) give only the second, and Limits lists the first case as not checked. Method now follows the notes and points to Limits.
14. **LH033 stated an interpretation as fact (fixed).** "That is a change in the instrument's detection" is now labelled interpretation, citing Censys's note that totals may shift as detection improves, matching the piece's "looks like".

## Judgements for the driver and the reader review (not changed)

- **"Most counts" (title) and "nearly every count we could read" (summary).** On day 7 all seven per-line and per-branch shares were at or above half (lowest 52.4). The hedge is true but unexplained: the only count below half that the records hold is W3Techs (2 to 8 per cent on 25 and 26 October, days 4 and 5, a monthly crawl), and the piece never mentions it. Either name W3Techs and why it lags in "What this cannot tell us", which explains "most", or say "every count", with its window. Recommended before release; not a figure error.
- **Prose sets the PHP service share beside the download shares ("8.5 was above both by then") while the figure caption says the two are drawn apart because they count different things.** The briefs permit such descriptions with units named, and the caveat follows at once; the reader review should judge whether the sentence undoes the caption.
- **Line 26's day-7 share** includes v26.13.0, a non-security release of 28 October, at 18.3 of its 64.7 per cent. The piece says newer releases count; giving the size would help a reader see why line 26 jumps about.
- **Ubuntu's "still calls itself PHP 8.3.6".** LH018 supports the package version `8.3.6-0ubuntu0.24.04.13`. Distribution builds commonly report the suffix in their version header as well; whether Censys reads the suffix was not checked. The point (the upstream number stays 8.3.6, so a floor) stands; the wording could say "keeps the version number 8.3.6".
- **Consistency rule.** AGENTS.md's "Where things stand", the investigation page and the front page do not yet carry these findings; that is release work for the driver.
- **Colophon** still says "Not yet reviewed" and version 0.1 (draft), correctly for now.
