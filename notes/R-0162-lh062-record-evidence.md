## Findings

### Must-fix

**M1. B1 is a study of two platforms, not one, and its kept figures are one named platform's subset (Never done; safety).**
The record says (Findings, B1): "A study of one platform's mini-apps; only its whole-population and sample figures are kept". Method says: "no per-provider or per-platform figure is kept beyond B1's single platform at the study's own level". derived/arxiv2307.09317v1.json says "The study is of one platform's mini-apps; figures are the study's whole population". The reread shows otherwise. The authors "primarily analyze 110,993 WeChat mini-apps, and 10,000 Baidu mini-apps (two of the most prominent super-app platforms)", and report leakage for both (36,425 WeChat; 112 Baidu). The three kept claims (V-B1-1 to V-B1-3) come from the temporal comparison, which covers only the WeChat dataset (DATASET1). So they aren't "study-level figures for the whole population". They're figures for a group defined by naming a platform, which the brief's Never done rules out ("only study-level figures for the whole population, or for groups the study defines without naming providers or platforms, are kept"). The paper also frames the 99.6 per cent as the platform's enforcement being "largely ineffective against existing vulnerable mini-apps". Keeping that figure under the platform's name is close to the "which systems are weak" statement the keeper's decision of 24 November 2026 excludes. The brief's safety clause asks for the check to be repeated when a document contains per-provider weakness, and the record doesn't say it was.
*Fix:* correct the description of B1 in the record, sources.md and the derived file (derived through derive.py) to two platforms. Then either (a) keep B1 as included, since it meets the three conditions, record **no figures** from it, and say in one sentence that its follow-up figures cover one named platform's apps within a two-platform study and are withheld under Never done; or (b) get a keeper line before keeping them. I recommend (a). Under (a), remove V-B1-1 to V-B1-3 from claims.csv, rerun tally.py, and update the counts everywhere they appear (12 claims, venue 7; prompt, venue: unprompted 4, after notification 3; no "mixed"). Remove "B1 a mixed one" and "A07 and B1 check validity" from the Answer, and delete the B1 sentences in the Answer's "Still valid, unprompted" and "After notification" bullets and the V-B1-3 note. Record the repeated safety check, and its outcome, in Method's safety paragraph. Under (a), the writer's flagged questions about B1's "mixed" label and its inclusion become moot. If (b) is chosen instead, "mixed" must be defined in the record as a label outside the brief's categories, which it already half does.

**M2. A07's funders are stated, one of them an AI developer (model and interest disclosure, gate condition 6).**
The record says (header, Model and interest): "Authors' funders are not stated in the text read for A02, A07 or B1". The A07 Findings paragraph says "Funder not stated", and claims.csv says "funder not stated" in interest_funder for V-A07-1 to V-A07-5. A07's Acknowledgments state: "This work was supported in part by the National Science Foundation under Grant Number #2540803, as well as a Sloan Research Fellowship and a gift from Google, Inc.", with one author supported by the EU's Horizon Europe programme (SafeHorizon). Google develops AI models, and A07 mentions Google Cloud among providers that disable leaked keys automatically. The reread found no acknowledgment or funding statement in A02 or B1, so "not stated" stands for those two.
*Fix:* in the header, the A07 paragraph and claims.csv's five A07 rows (and the derived file's "Funder: not stated", through derive.py), say that the authors state support from the US National Science Foundation, a Sloan Research Fellowship, a gift from Google and, for one author, the EU's Horizon Europe programme. Add to Model and interest: "A07's authors acknowledge a gift from Google, which develops AI models."

**M3. A02 never checked whether secrets worked (an error of kind, not of timing).**
The record says (Answer, "Still valid, unprompted"): "A02 checked validity only at discovery". The derived file says "Validity over time: not measured (validity assessed at discovery only)". In A02, "valid" means passing the authors' filters, not checking with a provider. The authors write: "After the filtering in Phase 3, we considered the remaining candidates to be 'valid secrets' … We note that the secrets classified in Phase 3 as 'valid' are not always 'sensitive'" (an RSA key in a unit test, for example). They define a secret as "a cryptographic key or API credential whose privacy must be maintained for security".
*Fix:* Answer: "A02 didn't check whether secrets worked: its 'valid secrets' are candidates that passed the authors' filters, which they note aren't always sensitive". Make the same change to the A02 Findings paragraph ("Validity not checked against providers by this study"), claims.csv's V-A02-1 note, and the derived file.

**M4. A07's after-notification unit: "distinct" isn't the authors' word, and the two A07 labels split one unit in the tally.**
claims.csv labels V-A07-3's unit "credential (distinct credentials exposed)", and the record's table repeats it. The authors write "exposures reducing 50% from 1,405 (Day 0) to 706 (Day 14)". Their Figure 7 caption says "total number of exposed credentials found on web each day", and the paper elsewhere separates "exposures" from "distinct credentials". Whether 1,405 counts distinct credentials or credential-on-site exposures isn't stated in the text reread. The two labels also make tally.py count 12 unit and outcome groups where A07 has one unit (the record half-concedes this in Limits).
*Fix:* give V-A07-3 the same unit as V-A07-4 and V-A07-5, with the note "the authors call these 'exposures' and 'exposed credentials found on web each day'; whether distinct credentials or credentials counted per site is not stated". Rerun tally.py and quote its new group count (11, or 9 after M1(a)). Drop the Limits sentence about the two labels.

**M5. The Answer sets A02 against A07 after saying it doesn't, and the unit question can now be settled.**
The Answer says "no figure from one study is set against another's" and "whether the two are the same kind of count is not established from the retained files … (a judgement for review)". Its "Read together" paragraph then says: "in the two venue studies that followed exposure without notification, most exposed secrets or credentials were still in public view at the end of A02's 16 days and after A07's first month". That sets two studies' figures side by side on the very unit question the record leaves open. The rereads settle it: A02 counts filter-passed candidate strings, some of them not sensitive, and never checked them with providers (M3), while A07 counts credentials its authors verified with provider APIs. They are different counts, so keeping them apart is right under the brief.
*Fix:* replace the unit sentence in the Answer with: "A02 counts secrets that passed its filters, not checked with providers and not all sensitive, while A07 counts credentials its authors verified with providers, so they are different counts and stay apart". Delete the clause from "and in the two venue studies…" to "…A07's first month" in "Read together". Change Next's first bullet so it no longer asks the review to look at whether A02's and A07's units can be compared.

**M6. The In short: what the searches found, an ambiguous share, and absolutes without their window.**
- "Six fixed searches found three research papers and two security companies' blog posts". The searches returned A07 and B1. A02 entered as a known document, not returned under its own title (Method says so). *Fix:* "Six fixed searches and two papers we already knew gave us three research papers and two security companies' blog posts…"
- "half the exposed keys came down within two weeks while about three in four still worked". Read without the body, "three in four" sounds like a share of the exposed keys. It is 865 of the 1,175 that worked at day 0. *Fix:* "…while about three in four of the keys that had worked at the start still did".
- "No study compared notified owners with owners left untold" and "no two studies counted the same thing". These absolutes need their window, and the second is stronger than the Answer, which left the unit question open. *Fix:* "None of them compared…" and "no two of them counted the same thing in the same way", which M5 now supports.
- If M1(a) is taken, the In short needs no B1 change, since it states no B1 figure.

### Suggestions

**S1. W4's clock.** The post says it found secrets "by monitoring GitHub's public event feeds for commits containing secrets", then "tracked each secret for 31 days since the initial exposure date (For secrets exposed on September 28, we tracked them until October 29 …)". So the exposure date is the day the commit appeared in the feed the vendor watched, which by the post's account is also the day it was found. *Wording:* replace "(whether that is commit time or first observation is not stated)" with "(the day the commit appeared in the public event feed the vendor monitored, which the post treats as both exposure and discovery; commit time and first observation are not told apart)". The clock column can stay "initial exposure date".

**S2. W6's class and its outcome label.** Classing W6 measured is defensible: the post is the same vendor presenting its own report's measurement, so post and report are one origin, and the post states the method (repeated checks over five days after its alert). Say so in place of "a judgement for review": "classed measured on the post's description of the vendor's own checks; the report it relays, one origin with the post, wasn't read". W-W6-2's outcome "still exposed (repository public)" goes further than the post. The vendor counts repositories still public after "the secret was gone", not secrets still exposed. *Wording:* "repository still public after the secret was gone (whether the secret stayed reachable is not stated)".

**S3. A07's day-0 counts below 1,748.** The reread found no reconciliation of 1,405 or 1,175 with 1,748 in the paper, beyond the authors' remark that three services blocked their address, which explains only the gap between the two day-0 counts. *Wording:* replace "the derived file records only the authors' explanation of the gap between the two day-0 counts…" with "the paper's text doesn't reconcile them with 1,748 (the evidence review reread it); the authors explain only why the two day-0 counts differ".

**S4. "Told website owners" and "emailed website owners".** A07 notified 2,435 entities, 156 of them third parties, and at least one email reached 1,668 sites (68.5 per cent). *Wording:* in the In short, "after the researchers emailed the sites' owners", and in the Answer, "After the authors emailed website owners and third parties (at least one email reached 68.5 per cent of sites)".

**S5. Microsoft and other AI developers in A07's population.** A07's 14 services include GitHub and Azure (Microsoft) and Alibaba's cloud. Each of those companies develops AI models, and the brief asks for wording neither easier nor harder on AI developers or platforms. *Wording:* "One included document (A07) names one AI developer's model API keys among its 14 services; its population also includes cloud and code-hosting credentials of companies that develop AI models, Microsoft among them. No per-provider figure is kept." The record's silence on A07's per-provider figures, including the one for the named AI developer, is correct and should stay.

**S6. A07's lifetime is bounded.** "Stayed up for about a year on average" (In short) and "an average of 12 months" are the authors' figure, from nine snapshots across up to five years. claims.csv notes the bound, but the record's prose doesn't. *Wording:* add "measured across nine archive snapshots" in the Answer's first bullet.

**S7. "Read together", first clause.** It sets A07 (venue) and W4 (vendor) in one sentence. Each is cited "within their own study", so it isn't a row-for-row comparison, but the brief keeps the streams apart. *Wording:* split it into a venue sentence (A07's day-172 rows) and a separate vendor sentence (W4's taken-down keys).

**S8. Locator rule, searches.md Q3 #7.** The reason "It names a vendor's research that no query returned from the vendor's own site, so no candidate arises" isn't the brief's rule. The brief lets a locator name a stream document, which then becomes a candidate. The sound reason is that the result's title metadata names no document or publisher, and snippets aren't used to screen. *Fix:* correct the reason in searches.md. The outcome doesn't change.

**S9. Housekeeping the writer flagged.** All three are disclosed in Limits, and none changes a figure.
- The derived files' `body_removed` sentence is lh_fetch.write_derived's fixed NOT_KEPT_NOTE, which gives robots or page reservation as the reason. A neutral note in the shared helper would serve every study; that's outside LH062.
- fetch.py's docstring names a nonexistent keep.py.
- fetch.py writes "pending (keep.py)" into body_kept, so fetchlog.csv's body_kept and note columns were completed by hand after reading. Say so in one line in Method or fetchlog's notes.

### Checked and found sound

Vendor labels follow LH061's rule (HTTP Archive open, GitHub's APIs public). Design labels are correct: A07 explicitly says it "is not a vulnerability notification study to understand the effectiveness of different notification controls", and the record uses no effect language.

- The Overlap disclosure for A02, A07 and W4 is accurate. The partial-inconclusive condition on design holds. After M1 and M5, the unit-group condition holds on firmer grounds.
- Writing: British English, and no em or en dashes in the record's prose. The In short opens the record in plain words, and the record's internal codes are appropriate for a record.

## Verdict

**Not cleared.** Six must-fix items: M1 (B1 is two platforms; withhold its one-platform figures under Never done, or get a keeper line; record the repeated safety check), M2 (A07's stated funders, including a gift from Google), M3 (A02 never checked validity with providers), M4 (A07's unit label and the tally regrouping), M5 (remove the A02 and A07 side-by-side and settle the unit question as different counts) and M6 (the In short's "found", "three in four" and windowless absolutes). Suggestions S1 to S9 are wording and housekeeping. After resolution, the brief's sequence applies: the reader-and-inference review, then one fresh agent's recheck of what the resolutions wrote.
