## Five best new leads

1. **WPPConnect, 15 to 16 November (StepSecurity, https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/wppconnect-compromised-malicious-releases-steal-secrets-and-whatsapp-sessions).** A project's own automation published unreviewed commits with valid trusted-publishing credentials; payload runs on import and defeats `--ignore-scripts`; the maintainer account then force-pushed so the tag no longer holds the payload. Why: it is a clean example of residue (a published artifact) outliving the evidence in the repository, and of a provenance signal that does not mean review, which is the CI-trust question in the software-updates investigation. Observations are at risk: the tag and branches were already rewritten, so npm version metadata, Docker digests and the nightly asset are the surviving public record, and deprecation or unpublishing may follow within days. A single, dated read of npm's version list and times for the two packages would be lost if nobody looks soon.
2. **WFX Phantom, final figures and decay (Aikido via a secondary write-up, https://sesamedisk.com/wfx-loot-github-actions-secret-stealer/; StepSecurity 12 Nov).** 4,837 accounts, 61,764 probed repositories, about 8,080 given the payload, at least 1,835 executed, with the count flagged as a floor. Why: it is the largest scripted spread of stolen credentials seen on shared infrastructure this sweep, aimed at AI keys, and the agreed P-0029 watch can compare its own counts against these. Yes, the open pull requests and branches decay as owners clean up; the 92% still-open figure is dated 12 Nov, so a later read shows the clean-up rate, and it is being lost now.
3. **strutil-core's AI-configuration payload and revocation wiper (Socket, 6 Nov, https://socket.dev/blog/shai-hulud-descendant-malicious-ruby-gem).** Writes hooks, MCP entries and `AGENTS.md`/`CLAUDE.md` into repositories, and deletes the home directory when a stolen token is revoked. Why: it is residue that instructs the next AI session, the charter's propagation layer, and it also shows the standard response (revoke) as a trigger. It reports no confirmed spread (89 downloads, 0 on the bad version), so it is a mechanism, not a measured spread. Lower urgency: the facts are fixed, but a check of whether any repository carries such files needs a public-code search, which is a design question.
4. **Optimizely-hosted scripts, 16 November (StepSecurity, single source, https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/optimjack-optimizely-hosted-scripts-crypto-clipper-card-skimmer).** Code placed in a vendor's customer-editable field ran on 178 organisations' pages for about 16 hours. Why: a third-party script path is a supply chain with no package registry, and the shape (a trusted CDN serving attacker code) is the same as the Adform case in July. It does not involve AI and fits the charter only as background to how shared delivery paths are abused. Verification is weak (one source): a second source or Optimizely's own statement should come first; the wallet balances and cleanup times are visible now but will not stay informative.
5. **Wallet-as-dead-drop loaders in npm (Sonatype 4 Nov; StepSecurity 13 Nov).** Two campaigns (10 hijacked packages; ten look-alike packages tied to a fake-interview scheme and the Binance theft) read their server address from Ethereum transactions, which are public and permanent. Why: this is a rare supply-chain instrument whose history cannot be deleted, so the operator's server changes are observable after the packages are gone. The wallet history is not at risk of loss; the registry listings are. Lead only: following wallets or addresses needs care under the rule against visiting attackers' hosts, and a design would read the public ledger entries, never the servers.

## Urgent

WPPConnect (lead 1): the payload tag was rewritten, so the registry record is the main evidence and may change. Window: days. WFX Phantom (lead 2): a second read of open probe pull requests shows the clean-up rate; the first was 12 November, so waiting loses the middle of the curve.
