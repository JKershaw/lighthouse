## Five best leads

### 1. The first reported autonomous ground-robot mission in combat, from a Ukrainian-language digest

- **Source.** https://dou.ua/goto/Jkgd ("Дайджест НРК #56", 2 Nov 2026). It relies on its author's Telegram post.
- **What was reported.** A UGV of the Lava regiment (Khartiya corps) "for the first time" drove 93% of a 20 km combat route by itself, with Apex AI software, and an operator took over for the rest. Armament and task are not stated.
- **Why it matters.** It is the first claim found of autonomy moving from the bench to a combat route, in the theatre with the most ground robots. It also shows what such a claim leaves out: navigation is autonomous, but nothing is said about weapons or who authorises force. That is exactly the split the physical-authority investigation keeps between capability and authority. The digest is a weekly Ukrainian-language series (#56). It could be a standing local-language source for this scout, tracking what the English press misses.
- **Window.** Short for the original: the claim rests on a Telegram post, which can be edited or deleted, and the digest page shows no year. Whether it counts as a "material new claim" for LH050's extra weekly read is for that study to judge. It is a claim about capability, not about authorisation or use of force.

### 2. Microsoft's preliminary review: automated recovery could not recover

- **Source.** NANOG thread "Microsoft post-op" (17 Nov 2026), quoting incidents MO1550821 and EX1550543 behind Microsoft's admin login. Also The Register, 16 Nov (HN, not opened).
- **What was reported.** A latent defect in a fix built for Microsoft 365 gateway servers caused lock contention under load. "Our normally automated recovery processes were not designed to restore service from this broad unresponsive state", and rollback, flighting and feature flags could not revert a months-old change.
- **Why it matters.** It answers the 13 November sweep's fourth lead. Automated defences and recovery turning against their own infrastructure is the failure mode the watch cares about, even with no AI in the story: nothing in the review mentions AI-written code, and the essay in row 13 only speculates. The same outage reached the physical world (lead 3).
- **Window.** The report is behind a tenant login, so Lighthouse's copy is a member's quotation. The final review will follow. Scout 4 should take it.

### 3. A chlorine alarm sent by email, lost in the Microsoft outage: software failure to physical harm

- **Source.** HN thread 50509411 (15 Nov 2026), quoting WDR (www1.wdr.de, disallowed, not fetched).
- **What was reported.** At Phantasialand's hotel, an automatic dosing system overdosed chlorine. The safety system's only alert was an email that never arrived during the outage, and 15 guests were injured.
- **Why it matters.** It is a dated case of a shared-infrastructure failure becoming bodily harm through an automated control loop that had no local alarm. That is the hinge between the charter's infrastructure question and the physical-authority investigation's concern with delegated physical action. The original is German local-language reporting, which this scout exists to catch.
- **Window.** Days: the investigation by local authorities and the operator's account will follow in German regional press. WDR is closed to us by robots.txt, so another regional outlet or an official statement (Rhein-Erft-Kreis, the police) would be the route. That is a P-0022 "better way" note.

### 4. Windows 11 telemetry counts coding agents: an instrument Lighthouse cannot read but should know exists

- **Source.** https://xusheng.dev/posts/windows-coding-agent-telemetry/ (16 Nov 2026), with 527 HN comments.
- **What was reported.** A feature flag `CLICodingAgentUsage` records whether codex.exe, claude.exe, copilot.exe or cursor.exe ran in a reporting window, and sends a bitmap to Microsoft as optional diagnostic data, on by default on the tested install.
- **Why it matters.** The charter asks how much agent activity happens out of sight. This reports that one vendor already measures agent prevalence on endpoints at population scale, from the inside. It belongs in the map of instruments as a closed, private vantage, and it suggests one could ask whether Microsoft publishes any aggregate. Reverse-engineering claims by one author are unverified.
- **Window.** Medium. Microsoft may change or explain the feature after the attention. A reply or documentation change in the coming days would be the first observation of how the vendor frames it.

### 5. Airshow China, 7 to 13 December: NORINCO promises new "mechanical wolves" and ground combat vehicles

- **Source.** https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202610/14/WS6acf219fe4b09a165c78f356.html (CCTV via China Daily, 14 Oct 2026). Background: HUR's flamethrower UGV test (row 10), Japan's JGSDF testing an attack-type THeMIS (row 8), and the 404 Media ledger of ended US security-robot deployments (row 5, withdrawals of unarmed patrol robots, including an airport).
- **What was reported.** The dates are confirmed, and a NORINCO official says unmanned ground combat vehicles and "mechanical wolves" will appear "for the first time", with a much higher share of intelligent, unmanned equipment.
- **Why it matters.** It is the largest non-Western armed-robot showcase inside LH050's pilot. Chinese-language statements there about export, delivery or human control would be new claims, but by the plan's rule another demonstration does not qualify. The 404 Media count (13 of 21 ended) is the only systematic withdrawal evidence found for patrol robots. It bears on the Changi airport case's comparison class, though the robots it covers are unarmed.
- **Window.** Fixed: 7 to 13 December. Statements are made in Chinese first and recycled in English within days, so the first-day Chinese reports are the ones to keep.
