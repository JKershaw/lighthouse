## Five best leads

### 1. GitHub says agents have quadrupled load on a single repository in two months and has rebuilt its Git storage for them

- **What was reported.** GitHub's engineering blog (5 Nov 2026): requests to one repository rose from 44M a day in March 2026 to 181M a day in May, "on an exponential trajectory" since; the new architecture was tested at 5,000 concurrent agents and 7,200 pushes a second; bursts of writes on busy repositories are expected to become sustained within a year.
- **Why it matters.** It is the largest forge, in its own words, saying that agent activity is reshaping the cost of shared infrastructure. It is a primary source rather than a vendor's survey, and it gives one dated series. It sits beside Lighthouse's own finding (LH046) that GH Archive's non-push events thinned sharply during 2026. Whether those two belong together is an untested idea, formed after reading both (post hoc). No source read links them.
- **Fits.** What AI activity costs the shared internet. It also bears on software updates, whose instruments lean on GitHub.
- **Window.** Weeks. The blog post will stay up. What will be lost is the period before the new architecture and any rate or API change that follows it. A brief that dates GitHub's public statements, changelog entries and API behaviour from now on would catch those changes as they happen, not after.

### 2. Agents' residue in the commons: Claude Code permission files and transcripts committed to public repositories

- **What was reported.** Apiiro (5 Nov 2026, primary walled; relayed by runtimewire and by search summaries): 104,185 public copies of `.claude/settings.local.json` in 103,617 repositories, 1,122 holding a secret; 2,539 secrets across 1,994 repositories counting history, `settings.json` and saved agent transcripts. A commenter writing as Apiiro says that in 539 of 1,105 traced repositories a commit co-authored by Claude changed the file, and that the file is git-ignored only globally, so every clone and fork carries it on.
- **Why it matters.** This is the charter's "residue" in its plainest form: an agent's working state written into the public commons, spread by forks and clones, and readable by other agents. It also gives a countable marker, how many public repositories carry agent configuration or transcript files, by creation date, that could grow with agent use. Lighthouse would count aggregates only. It would never read, test or name a secret, a repository or an account, as the keeper's line of 8 November requires.
- **Fits.** What AI activity costs the shared internet. It also touches supply chain (secrets in forks), which scout 3 may also report.
- **Window.** Days. The publicity will make owners delete files and rewrite history, so the count Apiiro saw on 5 November is shrinking now, and the "before" exists only in Apiiro's figures. A first dated count soon, by a public, robots-compliant route, would be the only independent baseline. GitHub code search needs a login and github.com refuses this container (L-0040), so the route itself is a P-0022 question.

### 3. Discovery outruns repair: bounty programmes paused, and a researcher who cannot tell 16,000 infected sites

- **What was reported.** Google paused its open-source VRP from 1 October 2026 after a "significant rise in automated submissions", most not valid, with an update planned for Q1 2027 (eWeek, 6 Oct). Search summaries add curl ending its bounty (January 2026) and HackerOne pausing the Internet Bug Bounty (April 2026). Report URI (6 to 9 Nov) has found 15,579, then 16,068, websites serving malicious JavaScript and says it finds infections "considerably faster than we could ever hope to notify the affected organisations".
- **Why it matters.** The charter asks whether AI activity degrades shared infrastructure. One way it can do so without a single attack is by flooding the channels that turn findings into fixes: triage queues, bounty programmes and notification routes. If cheap discovery, by models among others, swamps those channels, fewer fixes come out and the residue of known-but-unfixed flaws grows. A dated ledger of programmes paused, closed or reopened, with each programme's own stated reason, can be built from public notices. Report URI makes no AI claim, and none should be read into it.
- **Fits.** When a model finds the bug (its other half: what happens after the find).
- **Window.** Moderate. Notices persist, though Google's own blog would not render here. The value of a ledger comes from dating each change as it happens; Google's Q1 2027 update is the next fixed point.

### 4. Residential proxies: the bridge from AI scraping demand to enlisted consumer devices

- **What was reported.** Infoblox (9 June 2026): over 65% of its cloud customers queried residential-proxy domains in 2026, "the most seen proxy service is associated with scraping for AI models", monthly queries rose from nearly 400 billion to over 500 billion between January 2025 and April 2026, with "no reduction" after Google's IPIDEA action. OpenStreetMap (February and March 2026) blocked scrapers spread across 100,000+ addresses and contacted proxy companies. Search summaries add Google's NetNut takedown (2 July 2026, Popa botnet, at least two million devices) and bandwidth-sharing "AI data" networks claiming 0.6 to 8.5 million nodes (promotional, inconsistent).
- **Why it matters.** This is the most concrete mechanism found for AI activity sustaining itself economically through other people's machines. Demand for training data pays for residential exit nodes, which come from bandwidth-sharing apps, free VPNs and infected devices, the same pool that feeds botnets (L-0001, LH042). Lighthouse marks the exposed-device end of this (LH042) and the closed-door end (LH044), but not the market between them.
- **Fits.** What AI activity costs the shared internet.
- **Window.** No urgent window. Vendor reports are periodic. Takedowns are dated events whose before and after can be compared later from public reports.

### 5. The closing web reaches email: Outlook.com rejecting mail since 5 November, unannounced

- **What was reported.** Windows Latest (8 Nov) and Suped (6 Nov): since at least 5 November, mail to Outlook.com and Hotmail has been rejected, intermittently, with 550 5.7.1 "Message lacks SPF/DKIM/DMARC Auth Records (AS9031519)" and 550 5.7.23; Microsoft has confirmed no change; small senders below the 5,000-a-day bulk threshold report it. Suped says the reports do not establish a new policy.
- **Why it matters.** Email is the most distributed of the shared services, and this is a defence against machine-made mail (the charter's spam) narrowing who can take part, the same shape as LH044's walls on the web. No source read ties this change to AI-generated mail; that link is a scenario, not a claim. A public, cheap and robots-free measure exists: DMARC records are DNS. A dated share of a fixed domain panel publishing DMARC, read before and after such a change, would show the defence's reach.
- **Fits.** What AI activity costs the shared internet, as a sibling to the closing web (LH044, P-0013). It is not a fit for software updates.
- **Window.** Days. The first week of an unannounced change is when senders scramble to publish records. A DMARC baseline read after the scramble cannot recover the state before it. Microsoft may also clarify or reverse.
