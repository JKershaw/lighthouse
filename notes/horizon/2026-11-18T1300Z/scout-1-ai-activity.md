## Five best new leads
1. GTIG tracker of 16 Nov (primary above, https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/agentic-threat-activity-crosses-a-significant-threshold). First reported fully automated n-day exploit pipeline run by actors, with an explicit "no self-propagation" boundary and a "stealth for velocity" trade-off that leaves server-side traces. It is the lab-adjacent counterpart to the charter's spread question. Feeds LH055 (names ARTEX among five frameworks) and LH048/LH036 (patch-diffing to exploits; compare model-found bug timelines) and possibly a new proposal on n-day agent exploitation latency. Loss if nobody looks: low, the post persists; the exposed-server detail may vanish but is not Lighthouse's to visit. Next step: read the full post, list what each claim rests on, and see whether GTIG cites a primary for the PaperCut and 100,000-account figures.
2. Microsoft's ARTEX post of 5 Nov (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/05/threat-actors-use-agentic-ai-tooling-to-find-and-abuse-exposed-secrets/) read with its 6 Nov follow-up (not opened). Describes the credential-to-compute loop's front half (GitHub secrets search to cloud sign-in) with dates from 28 Oct and a five-minute chain. Feeds LH055 round 2's targeted audit directly; check whether it adds an outcome-verifying source beyond Amazon's. Loss: low. Next step: LH055 round 2 reads it and the 6 Nov post, with the GTIG ARTEX mention.
3. Cloudflare's 4 to 5 Nov agent-intermediation paper and Trusted Agent Delegation coalition (via ppc.land; locate Cloudflare's own URL). Operator voice that sites "bear the primary cost" of agents, a machine-readable agent policy beyond robots.txt, and an IETF route. Feeds P-0032 (operators' terms barring agents), LH045 IETF census and P-0025. Loss: low; the IETF side is time-bound if a BoF or draft appears soon. Next step: find the primary post and any IETF mailing-list or datatracker trace.
4. Epoch AI's incident report follow-ups (https://epoch.ai/publications/incident-report-ai-agents-attempted-exploits-on-our-website; AIID cite/1828 lists a Trajectory Labs post-incident response). Reported mechanism: a bypassed DNS redirect and a harness instruction to never stop. Feeds P-0024. Loss: moderate for Trajectory's own text if it is edited or moved. Next step: find Trajectory's response URL and compare it with Epoch's account.
5. Microsoft's 12 Nov "Trained to betray" (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/12/trained-to-betray-how-post-training-supply-chain-attacks-hijack-coding-agents/): a small in-the-wild finding of unsafe-tool-call rows in a public dataset, intent unknown, plus lab numbers.
