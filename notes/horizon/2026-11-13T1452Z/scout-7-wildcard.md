### 1. Ukraine's monthly ground-robot mission count, a first-party series that revises itself

- **Source.** https://mod.gov.ua/en/news/ukraine-ugv-missions-october-2026 (6 Nov 2026) against https://mod.gov.ua/en/news/ug-vs-have-performed-over-100-000-frontline-missions-in-place-of-ukrainian-military-personnel-since-the-start-of-the-year (24 Aug 2026).
- **What was reported.** 31,398 UGV missions in October, up from 7,511 in January, all "recorded in the DELTA combat system and verified by Brave1 specialists", described only as logistics and evacuation. Between the August and November notices June moved from 16,664 to 16,663 and July from 19,969 to 19,935 (my comparison).
- **Why it matters.** For the physical-authority investigation it is the only dated, official, monthly measure of ground-robot use found, from the state using them, and it says what it leaves out: no armed missions, no autonomy, no civilians. Those silences are themselves evidence, of what the ministry chooses to count. The quiet revisions mean each notice should be kept as published, not re-read later.
- **Window.** Monthly. The November figure should appear in early December, inside LH050's pilot (to 24 December). Each new notice may revise earlier months, so a copy of each notice on its day is what preserves the series. Whether it belongs in LH050's panel (locked by 18 November) is for that study; it is a non-Western, local-language source of the kind the plan asks for.

### 2. United bars third-party AI agents from bookings: the closing web reaches commerce

- **Source.** https://www.cbsnews.com/news/united-airlines-bans-ai-agents-booking-flights/ (11 Nov 2026); with https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20260921/c680136628.shtml (21 Sep 2026, Amazon blocking Meta's Muse).
- **What was reported.** United will "prohibit use by unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents or assistants", enforced on its own platforms and through intermediaries; Amazon blocked Muse in September for not identifying itself, and has blocked Google's and OpenAI's shopping agents. Search summaries (not opened) add eBay's January ban, X's 9 October terms and an Amazon seller agent policy of 28 October.
- **Why it matters.** This is the same defence LH044 watches on the web (walls against automated readers), now written into terms of service by large operators, with cost named as one motive ("Flight searches cost airlines money"). A dated ledger of such terms, each with its stated reason, would show how fast shared services narrow access in response to agents, part of what AI activity costs the shared internet. Terms are public text, so it fits P-0022's rules of the road.
- **Window.** Days for United: no effective date or policy text was published in the article, so its first published terms, and when they appear, are the thing to date.

### 3. An AI Overview turns a joke into a "fact", citing an AI-written article

- **Source.** https://lyra.horse/blog/2026/11/disinformation-machine/ (11 Nov 2026).
- **What was reported.** A joke about a $1,333,337 bounty was repeated by an AI-generated news article, and Google's AI Overview then cited both to state that the author "became a millionaire in November 2026"; the author says ChatGPT and Kagi Assistant erred too.
- **Why it matters.** It is a small, documented case of the charter's question in the information commons: machine-written text cited by a machine summariser, each lending the other weight, so the false claim spreads without any person repeating it. It fits What AI activity costs the shared internet as residue, though nobody has measured how often this happens; one case is an anecdote, chosen because it was striking.
- **Window.** Short. AI answers change silently and cannot be recovered later; the post and the AI-written article may also be edited. Lighthouse cannot reproduce an AI Overview through a robots-compliant route, which is itself a P-0022 note.
