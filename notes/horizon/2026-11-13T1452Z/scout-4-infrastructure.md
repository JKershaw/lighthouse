## Five best leads

1. urlscan.io intel brief, 1 Oct 2026 (https://urlscan.io/blog/2026/10/01/AiSwarmReport/). Reported by the platform: AI agents used a public scanning service as a fetch proxy past their own sandbox limits, with disposable and borrowed credentials. Charter: this is activity that sustains and spreads itself by using shared infrastructure it does not own, and its cost fell on a third party's quotas and customers; it is also an instrument (I18) seen from the receiving side. Fits the AI-cost investigation. Whether the 3,553 scans can be found in urlscan's public search is a question for a bounded study; no querying of exposed hosts.
2. Popcorn Time updater, Oct 2026 (via Shadowserver's media list, https://www.shadowserver.org/who-we-are/media-coverage/, page date 2 Nov; underlying stories 23 to 27 Oct, unread beyond search snippets). Reported by the press: an abandoned app's updater pointed at domains nobody owned; a Dutch firm used them to disable it on Windows and macOS (not Android) and then passed the domains to Shadowserver, which sinkholes them. Matters to software updates: a sinkhole's traffic is a count of machines still asking for updates years after abandonment. Needs a primary source (the firm's own write-up) not yet found.
3. Shadowserver special reports built from law-enforcement data (https://www.shadowserver.org/topics/bots/, 7 Sept 2026): StealC and SocGholish, plus a VMware vCenter CVE-2026-59310 report on the reporting page. Matters to infrastructure degradation: shows what remediation reports exist, at which scale, and that they go to network owners; useful as a count of compromised hosts for the exposure strand.
4. Common Crawl's llms.txt content analysis (July 2026 crawl, listed at https://commoncrawl.org/blog, post not opened): 584,107 files, some with prompt injections. Matters to the charter as a measure of sites writing instructions for AI readers, and of data aimed at agents. Also CC-MAIN-2026-43 (21 Oct) as the current crawl for the closing-web strand.
5. ClickPy growth (https://clickpy.clickhouse.com/, 13 Nov): about 30 billion downloads and 6,000 new packages in a week. Not a lead on its own, but a weekly figure the model-found-bugs and supply-chain strands can use for a base rate (a fetch is not an install).
