## Five best leads

1. **NCSC UK's 24 Nov blog on agentic offensive frameworks.** https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blogs/familiar-weaknesses-exploited-faster-defending-against-agentic-cyber-attacks. New (the NCSC was a gap before). A national authority says, in its own voice, that since September it has seen a marked rise in AI-assisted attacks, names ARTEX as the framework the activity has become "synonymous" with, and says the cost rests on "cheap, capable open models or illegal API resellers", which is the credential-to-compute question in LH055 from a second, independent source. It reports no counts and no basis for the rise, and says the weaknesses are old ones, so it bears on LH055 (an added reported-by source for the panel, to be read at its next read) and on the investigation page on AI cost and spread; also relevant to LH059's framing of what agents do "on their own".
2. **EPSS rise continues on PHP and the kernel flaw while KEV stays silent.** KEV 2026.11.25 vs EPSS 26 Nov: CVE-2026-115560 0.866, CVE-2026-98432 0.848, nginx2root CVE-2026-123007 0.456. Tracked, not new, but a fresh data point for LH057 and LH036 and for the exploitation-evidence gate in php-advisory-gate: scores of this size are a model output, not observed use. The LiteLLM score fell from 0.828 to 0.202 in three days, a useful reminder that EPSS is not monotone.
3. **KEV additions of 24 to 25 Nov: Drupal Core RCE (CVE-2026-132165), Optimizely, Xen Orchestra, Mitel MiCollab (five).** https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/feeds/known_exploited_vulnerabilities.json. New. Weak individually; the Drupal and Optimizely entries add to the PHP-ecosystem and supply-chain context for the software-updates investigation, and the MONAI Label entry shows the 3-day KEV lag after Amazon's report. Nothing says AI was involved.
4. **AWS agent-credential product posts (24 and 25 Nov).** https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/delegate-user-access-to-ai-agents-with-amazon-cognito-token-exchange/ and https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/secure-agentic-access-to-aws-with-enterprise-managed-authorization/. New, titles only. Why it matters: shows the defender-side tooling for scoping what an agent's credential can do, the control that would test the over-privileged-credential point in lead 1; no outcome data, so only context for LH061's defensive-side desk study.
5. **oss-security thread "Slow Ubuntu security response times" and the OpenSSL "4 Low severity fixes merged publicly" thread.** https://www.openwall.com/lists/oss-security/2026/11/ (titles only, not opened). Not tied to AI; the first bears on how fast one distribution ships fixes, a clock question for the software-updates investigation, and the second is a fix made public before a release date (1 December), an event-definition case for any brief that times "the fix". Weak leads.
