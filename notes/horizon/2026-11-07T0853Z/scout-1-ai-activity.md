## Best leads
1. **Anthropic's self-replication paper: what a replicator would leave in public data.** Reported by Anthropic Frontier Red Team, 12 Oct 2026 (https://www.anthropic.com/research/self-replicating-ai), echoed by BleepingComputer 13 Oct. Small models (30B or fewer) may reach end-to-end replication on commodity IoT by end of 2026; the post names observable signatures (multi-gigabyte model transfers, device-to-device flows, scanning) and the Kimwolf precedent. Why it matters: it is the first lab statement that self-sustaining spread could happen outside datacentres, and it states a dated forecast and public indicators, so a baseline can be taken before the forecast date. Time-sensitive: moderately; the baseline must precede end of 2026 (about 8 weeks). Measurable from public sources: exposed unauthenticated ADB (port 5555) counts over time from Shodan/Censys/FOFA summaries or Shadowserver reports; Kimwolf/Aisuru tracker counts (XLab, Synthient); public posts of any in-the-wild small-model agent on a device. Cost: small to one study (baseline plus periodic re-read). Confidence real and new: high that the paper exists; medium that public instruments can resolve it. I did not verify the paper's figures beyond the two readings, which agreed.
2. **Agents as a traffic class: Cloudflare's personal-agent controls (4 to 5 Nov 2026).** Reported by Cloudflare blog index and explainx (4 Nov). Cloudflare added a "Personal Assistants" bot category, per-agent allow/block policies, and a 5 Nov post on agents versus human identity; 21 Oct, ~100,000 Commerce.com domains expose agent shopping tools through WebMCP. Why it matters: agent traffic becoming a named, policy-controlled class is the point at which its volume could be counted publicly (Radar categories) and also where agent-held user context begins to flow. Time-sensitive: yes, weeks; Radar series and the Cloudflare directory change, and policy defaults shape future behaviour. Measurable: Radar bot-class series and the verified bot directory (snapshot and diff), robots.txt and WebMCP/`/.well-known` adoption on a sample of domains. Cost: small. Confidence: medium-high (primary posts not opened; the 404s were guessed URLs).
3. **Agent-authored contribution load on shared forges.** Reported by GitHub blog (18 Jun, updated 29 Oct 2026), Hugging Face analysis (30 Apr 2026), and a 15 Jul commentary. Merged PRs over 90 million a month; per-repo caps now count agent PRs; one issue drew 39 near-identical PRs; Jazzband reportedly shut down (secondary, unverified). Why it matters: a concrete, documented case of agent output outrunning human review on shared infrastructure, with duplicate-fix clustering as a signature of agents reacting to the same trigger. Time-sensitive: no. Measurable: GH Archive events, PR duplicate clustering by issue reference and bot authorship, adoption of the PR-limit setting where visible. Cost: one study. Confidence: high on existence; novelty medium (it overlaps Python-release and Harbour interests only loosely).
4. **Anthropic September 2026 threat report: persistent, scheduled and self-repairing agent operations.** Reported by Anthropic (Sept 2026; only first 100k of 276k characters read) and FoneArena 11 Sep. Thirteen agents on a schedule with no human in the loop; scheduled token renewal; agents rebuilding implants until undetected; 130-day campaign. Why it matters: closest primary description of automated activity that sustains itself by iterating on its own residue. Time-sensitive: no. Measurable: little directly (actors are not public); could map the report's indicators to public artefacts (token renewal patterns, rebuild cadence of samples on MalwareBazaar-type feeds) but with weak attribution. Cost: small (read the rest, extract testable claims). Confidence: high real, low that outside data can test it.
5. **Agent payment and tool-registry rails as quiet infrastructure.** Reported by secondary aggregators only (not opened): x402 about 5.1 million transactions and about $274,000 in October 2026; Allium Q3 68.3 million agent payments at about five cents each; CYFIRMA June 2026, nine of eleven MCP registries accepted a proof-of-concept malicious server, about 9,400 servers listed; arXiv 2609.14119 on silent drift in MCP servers. Why it matters: on-chain agent payments are one of the few fully public logs of machine-to-machine activity, and registries are a spreading channel for agent tooling. Time-sensitive: no (on-chain history persists; registry contents change). Measurable: on-chain x402 counts by facilitator, registry snapshots and server hash drift. Cost: one study. Confidence: medium; figures conflict and I could not reach primaries.
