Every source reading that was repeated reproduced to the second and the byte: the 200 projects and their download totals, all 948 OSV records, the 68 qualifying advisories and the rule's choice of these four libraries, the eight release upload times, and all 18 moves (snapshot commit, first moving commit, committer time, authorship class). ### The writer's points, and what else was found

1. **Must fix, fixed. The rates' windows.** urllib3's advisory came 3.9 days and cryptography's 5.9 days after their releases, so a window from day 2 to day 7 after the release straddles them, and five of its ten moves are urllib3's in the day after its advisory. The record's table called the row "before every advisory" and the piece read it as the release's spell "easing to about two". Stopping the window at the advisory where it came first gives 1.51, the pace of the weeks between, so the release's spell is two days, not a week. The "7 days before the advisory" row began before the releases for two libraries; it now begins at the release, and is labelled as mixing the release's days. scripts/analyse.py's hazard section was rewritten to take window bounds per pair, data/hazard.csv regenerated (every other table byte-identical), scripts/draw_figure.py's panel and note changed and rates.svg regenerated. The record's Answer, rates table and note, the piece's line 27 and the short form's rates sentence now carry 1.4 (all four libraries, from day 2 to the advisory) in place of 2.2, and "between 1.3 and 1.7" is supported by the rows 1.34, 1.41 and 1.66.

2. **Must fix, fixed. The evening of 15 June.** The record, the piece, the short form and the figure spoke of three advisories that evening. OSV lists ten GitHub advisories against the three libraries between 17:28 and 20:40 UTC, four of them rated high (a second against starlette, fixed in 1.3.1, released 12 June). Every one of the ten named a fix already on PyPI. The record's Findings and Limits now list them and say that a repository reacting to any of them is counted against the chosen one (aindy-runtime moved to 1.3.1 with a message naming four starlette advisories); the piece's opening says "We followed the three"; the short form says "among ten against them that evening"; the figure's annotation says "the three advisories we followed".

3. **Must fix, fixed. geonode's author time.** The record noted that geonode's author and committer times differ by more than a day, and its interpretation paragraph said the three urllib3 Dependabot moves fit a security update opened on the advisory. Read from a fresh clone, the commit was authored by dependabot[bot] at 00:03:28 UTC on Monday 11 May, 14.8 hours before the advisory, and committed to master by a person at 08:57 UTC on 12 May, so the proposal was open before the advisory. devsecops-engine-tools' branch tip was authored 2.2 hours after the advisory; cytomine's squash carries no proposal time. The record's Findings and Limits and the piece's line 47 now say so, and the "fits a security update" reading is narrowed to the two.

4. **Fixed. The count of the study's own reads.** "217 clones and about 1,100 requests to public APIs" counted the 264 clone rows among the requests; read_log.csv holds 877 HTTP requests and 264 clone attempts. The record's header and the piece's colophon now say so.

5. **Kept. "One pinned project moved in the day after" for pyjwt and starlette.** Recomputed: pyjwt 0, starlette 1 (aindy-runtime, 0.33 days), one in all, as the piece says.

6. **Should fix, fixed. "A branch a bot had opened."** Two of the 16 merged branches counted as a bot's are vantage6's release branch and grooveshop-django-api's sync of main into a long-lived branch, each with a github-actions tip; the record's Limits said so and the piece did not. The piece's line 45 now qualifies the 16.

7. **Should fix, fixed. "Dependabot says which it was in its pull requests."** Neither the record nor the piece read a pull request, and the sentence stated as fact what the record lists as unread. It now says the pull requests would say which, and that we did not read them. The other outside facts were checked as far as the reviewer could: Dependabot is GitHub's, the National Vulnerability Database is the US government's, OSV is an open database that aggregates GitHub's advisories among others, and the library descriptions (urllib3 an HTTP client, pyjwt a JSON Web Token library, starlette a web toolkit) are fair in plain words.
