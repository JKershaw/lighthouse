## Findings

### Must fix

1. **A paraphrase presented as experts' quoted words.** Line 59: "experts quoted, unnamed, by TechCentral made a version of that point about humanoid robots, that they "could paradoxically make military action more likely by reducing the perceived risks and consequences"". TechCentral (S24, 22 December 2025) doesn't quote anyone: "Experts, however, caution against the assumption... They argue that such technology could paradoxically make military action more likely by reducing the perceived risks and consequences". The quoted words are TechCentral's summary of unnamed experts' argument, about "such technology". Suggested: "TechCentral reported, citing unnamed experts, that such technology "could paradoxically make military action more likely by reducing the perceived risks and consequences"".

2. **The Gaza proposal spoken of in our own voice.** Line 67: "Whatever the merits of the proposal, which we couldn't read, the argument doesn't depend on it." Line 101: "The two cases that could have tested it, Foundation's proposed use in Gaza and an armed robot of a more ordinary kind, couldn't be read at all". Both take the proposal's existence as given; line 17 rightly says it is "neither established nor ruled out", and the investigation page says "reported proposed use". In line 67 "which we couldn't read" also reads as if the proposal itself were a document we tried. Suggested: line 67 "Whatever the merits of the reported proposal, whose report we couldn't read, the argument doesn't depend on it."; line 101 "Foundation's reported proposed use in Gaza".

3. **A sentence about the conference document that's wrong as written.** Line 106: "An agreement to keep working isn't a treaty, and neither says anything about how any state builds or uses a weapon." The kept final document (S44) does say things about how weapons are used, outside the autonomous-weapons decision: it "condemns any use of incendiary weapons in violation of Protocol III" (paragraph 103), "Calls on all parties to armed conflicts to respect relevant rules of International Humanitarian Law" (104), and reminds parties to take "all feasible precautions, in the use of laser systems" (105) (quote_check.out, last block). "Neither" also has no clear pair. Suggested: "An agreement to keep working isn't a treaty, and nothing in the decision on autonomous weapons sets a rule for how any state builds or uses one."

4. **The conference reading isn't labelled unreviewed where it is made.** Line 106 gives our reading ("We found no mandate to negotiate in it and no deadline for adopting anything, and the words "human control" don't appear") with no label; the unreviewed status appears only 23 lines later, in "What would test this" (line 129). The quotes themselves check out against the document, but the absence reading is the 27 November checkpoint's, which is unreviewed. Suggested: add after the sentence "That's our reading of the text, not yet reviewed." or fold it into "That's the conference's own account of what it decided".

5. **The investigation page doesn't hold what the essay says it does.** Line 101: "[The investigation's page](../investigations/physical-authority.md) holds the current account". The page (read at this commit) has no `current` field and no account of findings: it still says the first study "kept its baseline on 13 November ... it is unreviewed working material, and no deployment or harm finding has been made", and doesn't mention the conference outcome. AGENTS.md's orientation says of this investigation "there is no finding and no current account". Line 128 also cites the page for "Our weekly reading of eight fixed public pages, four trade reports relaying Foundation and four official policy pages"; the page mentions only "a small weekly source panel" (the eight are in studies/LH050/panel.csv and the record's Next). As drafted, the essay's summary is at the reviewed record's certainty while the page it points to says less, which the consistency rule doesn't allow. Suggested: either the driver updates the investigation page before release (record reviewed, unreleased; panel of eight), or the essay says "the first study's record, checked but not yet published, is linked from [the investigation's page]" and line 128 links the record (../studies/LH050/LH050.md) for the panel.

6. **A reading of our own evidence not labelled as made after seeing it.** Line 108: "What all this suggests to us is that the questions this essay cares most about, who decides on force and who answers for it, are the ones least visible in public." AGENTS.md's essay rule asks that each reading of Lighthouse's own evidence made after that evidence was seen be labelled. "Suggests to us" marks it as interpretation but not as post hoc; the first bullet of "What would test this" names the test. Suggested: "What all this suggests to us, read after seeing these sources rather than set out beforehand, is that..."

### Should consider

1. **Line 17, "The commissioning essay began with a news story. In the week before 12 November 2026".** The original (notes/physical-authority/original-essay.md) begins with the airport thought experiment and turns to the story in its fifth paragraph; it says "this week", dated 12 November, which needn't exclude 12 November itself. Suggested: "The commissioning essay turned early to a news story. In the week of 12 November 2026, it said, ..."

2. **Line 55, Sisson's quote ends "challenge."** The Next Web (S27) has "challenge, " said Melanie Sisson", with more of her quote after. Suggested: close the quote before the full stop ("...engineering challenge".), British style for a quoted part-sentence.

3. **Line 106, "the expert group on lethal autonomous weapons "is to continue"".** Paragraph 49's subject is "the work of the open-ended Group of Governmental Experts", not the group. Suggested: "records that the work of the expert group on lethal autonomous weapons "is to continue"".

4. **Line 83, "The ICRC's test for an autonomous weapon is different".** The ICRC states "The key test is not whether the weapon flies autonomously..." in answer to whether drones are autonomous weapons (S11). Applying it to a ground robot is the essay's step. Suggested: "The ICRC, writing about drones, puts the test for an autonomous weapon differently".

5. **Line 83, ""Autonomous patrol" is the police's phrase for finding its way around a terminal" and "how it stops for people in its way".** The first is our reading of SPF's phrase (the GIBSON page supports it, describing navigation by LiDAR, depth cameras and sonar), stated as fact. The second compresses two things HTX and SPF say: it detects obstacles and avoids collisions, and stops when someone tries to mount it. Suggested: "On these pages, "autonomous patrol" describes finding its way around a terminal"; "how it avoids people in its way and stops when someone climbs on".

6. **Line 81, "HTX, the government agency that built it".** SPF's GIBSON page says it was developed by HTX "in partnership with" A*STAR's Institute for Infocomm Research. Suggested: "the government agency that developed it".

7. **Line 23, "nothing we read says a Phantom has been armed, tested with a weapon or fired".** True of events, but both reports describe research contracts "for feasibility testing in ... weapons handling", which a reader may set against "tested with a weapon". Suggested: add "beyond research contracts described as covering weapons handling".

8. **Line 87, "paid for in part, by those reports, by military research contracts".** The reports give the contracts' value and purpose, not that they pay for the robot's development. Suggested: "and holding, by those reports, military research contracts".

9. **Line 85, "Still, they show how the parts of the airport in our opening are arriving".** A strong verb for an interpretation from two unarmed robots described by their operator; SPF's "plans to progressively deploy more patrol robots" is a 2023 plan. Suggested: "they suggest how".

10. **Line 129, "a later issued version of the final document would be seen there".** Only if the conference page lists it; the 27 November checkpoint notes CCW/CONF.VII/11's document route is refused by robots.txt. Suggested: "would be seen there if the page lists it".

11. **Line 104, link text "Armed robots: who decides?".** The draft article's title is "Who decides when a robot may use force? The sources we could read in November 2026 didn't say, and the two cases that might have shown it were closed to us". At the release step, use its title or a short form of it.

12. **Header date.** The byline carries two dates (commissioned 12 November, revised 28 November) and there is no `published` field; site/README.md dates a piece by `published`, else the byline's date. Add `published:` at release so the essay isn't dated 12 November.

13. **Colophon, "Supplied by the keeper".** "Keeper" has no gloss in the essay; the investigation page glosses it as "the person who runs it". Suggested: "Supplied by Lighthouse's keeper, the person who runs it, on 12 November 2026".

14. **Line 25, "its chief executive is reported to have said that it will sometimes need to decide on its own".** The Next Web attributes the first sentence to Pathak ("Pathak said") and the second ("In certain time-critical scenarios...") follows without a fresh attribution. Reading it as his is natural, and line 21 quotes it as the outlet's words, so this is a note for the reader review, not a change.
