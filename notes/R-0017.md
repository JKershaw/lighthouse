## Findings

This is the release gate for the round that restates three findings of LH012 in public without changing a figure: what other libraries' limits could account for is an estimate under the co-fetch assumption; an old Python rules out the newer versions without choosing among the older ones, as boto3's Python 3.9 downloads show; and downloads sharing reported fields are not one machine, owner or population. The reviewer read AGENTS.md, the full diff, both pieces whole, the record at 0.3, the retained analysis tables, the post hoc and driver checks, every changed surface and notice, the LH013 brief against LH012's brief and record, and the reader-review pilot for what the colophons say of it; reran the replay; ran the site's tests and build; recounted every class and figure the reworded sentences rest on from the retained tables (studies/LH012/review/r0017_check.py, r0017_check.txt); and re-read the two pages the LH013 brief cites for its one new field. The diff touches no figure, table, script or SVG, and the replay regenerates every output byte for byte. Every reworded sentence in the piece, the short form and the record says what the tables support, no more and no less, with its assumption beside it. What had to be fixed was one surface the round missed, programme.md's "Later evidence" line under LH011, which still said identified bounds were found "accounting for most in four", and one condition in the LH013 brief whose named check cannot establish it; four wordings were tightened. The verdict is that the piece and its short form can be released at version 1.1 with the record at 0.3, and that the LH013 brief can be snapshotted as it now stands.

### Must fix, fixed

1. **programme.md, the "Later evidence" line under LH011's answer.** It still said LH012 "found identified bounds accounting for most in four", the version 1.0 claim, on a surface the consistency rule covers. Now: "and where downloads from excluded Pythons were most, in three, the old Python does not say which older version was fetched; identified bounds could account for most in four, under an assumption the log cannot check; frozen lists could not be counted."
2. **studies/LH013/brief.md, the eligibility condition.** It requires that every day of W1 and W2 be held for all 37 projects, but named as its check LH012's coverage query, which returns only the first and last day held and cannot show a missing day between them. Now it also names a query listing the days held per project over the two weeks (project and date only, no count), and says that a day not held once 12 October is held is that project's gap day under Missing evidence rather than a reason to wait longer. That last clause is the reviewer's choice; the driving session should confirm it before the snapshot, since AGENTS.md and programme.md say "and every day of its weeks".

### Should fix, fixed

3. **Piece, "Libraries holding one another back".** "boto3's limits fit the versions of those two that were downloaded" reads as all; the guard gives 75.8 per cent of botocore's older downloads and 89.5 per cent of s3transfer's at versions the held boto3 admits. Now "Most of the older downloads of those two were of versions that boto3's limits allow, but the log cannot say how many of them the limits hold."
4. **Piece, Corrections.** "versions older than that Python needs" is not what the check counts; now "older than the last that Python can take".
5. **Record, Limits.** "the older share of boto3 is mostly downloads reporting one Python, installer and system" claimed the installer and system for the week, where they were read for 23 September only; now "one Python, and on the one day broken down one installer and system". A sentence in the 0.3 Corrections entry records the change.
6. **studies/LH013/brief.md, resource ceiling.** "Expected use is about 20 billion, most of it two passes of `pypi.pypi`" assumes one pass a week, but C, C5 and their older-only secondary are three groupings; read as separate passes at LH012's 9.4 billion rows each they would reach about 40 billion, over the 30 billion ceiling. The brief now says the passes are combined or the study stops at the ceiling with the unread part stated.
7. **studies/LH013/brief.md, `country_code`.** It said the field is "the country the package index's content network assigns to the requesting address", citing the parser and the BigQuery page; the parser shows only a field passed through from the log line, and the page does not mention it. The brief now says so, labels the content-network attribution as general knowledge and not a source, and notes the review's re-read. Its header now says the reviewer read it before the snapshot and what the edits touch.

### Notes, kept
