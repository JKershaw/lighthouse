## What I found

### Must-fix

1. **The keeper-rule split doesn't add up for a reader** (body, "191 in all, or 133 leaving aside the reviewer's requests that broke nothing else"). The reader has just been told 113 under the fixed rules and 19 more under the new one, and 113 + 19 = 132. 133 is right only because one of the 59 reviewer requests (the BleepingComputer article, whose robots.txt disallows ClaudeBot) breaks the new rule too, so under reading B only 58 break nothing else: 191 - 58 = 133. Equivalently, the new rule catches 20 requests outside the 113: 19 not counted at all before, plus 1 already counted only for its name. Say which, for example "...191 in all. Leave out the nameless-name class and it's 133: the 113, plus 20 that the new rule catches, one of them a reviewer request already counted for its name." Or drop the 133 from the piece; the record keeps it.
2. **"4 got no answer" contradicts "None of the five was answered"** (body, "These are counts of requests ..."; the figure's footer; the alt text). The five requests to the two sites whose robots.txt couldn't be reached are all among the 172, and none was answered. The audit table marks four as "no (no answer)" and one, LH042's collector's request to dashboard.shadowserver.org at 19:09:20, as "status not recorded". But that collector's own read log has `URLError: <urlopen error [Errno 104] Connection reset by peer>` for the page, and fetch.py (lines 98 to 112) writes that error column only when the page request itself raises. So 5 of the 172 got no answer. Change the body, the footer and the alt text to 5, or say "4 got no answer and 1 has no recorded status" and drop "None of the five was answered". The first matches the primary log. The record's F1 and "What the counts count" say "4 got no answer ... 1 has no recorded status"; that's a record-level suggestion (below), not a block on the piece.
3. **"88 of the 89 were answered 'not found', so no package record came back"** is wrong for the 89th request. The reader review's `https://pypi.org/pypi/litellm/json` at 19:24:59 got a 200, so one package record did come back. All 85 of the collector's got a 404. Say, for example, "88 of the 89 were answered 'not found'; only one package record came back, to a reviewer."

### Suggested

4. **The two flagged glosses.** "Half a minute earlier" is right (finding 4 above). "Most of our collectors ask robots.txt before each request" is defensible and matches the investigation page. The 9 November sweep put every collector it listed behind the shared gate, but LH043's to LH046's collectors (the ones whose requests this audit judged, apart from LH041 and LH042) aren't among them: LH044 and LH045 keep their own robots.txt checks, and LH044's still has its 64 KiB cut as far as the record says. The sentence could be made exact: "Since 9 November the collectors of most of our studies ask one shared tool about robots.txt before each request; a few older ones keep their own checks, and one-off commands and reviewers' reads don't yet go through it." The gloss "which is probably what most sites mean" for page-to-page Crawl-delay is the drafter's reading, with no source. Crawl-delay isn't in RFC 9309, and nothing read says what sites mean. Make it ours ("which we think is the more natural reading") or drop it.
5. **"Most of them in groups addressed to ClaudeBot"** (body, and "mostly its crawler ClaudeBot" in the figure). All 19 are disallowed by a group naming ClaudeBot; 9 by ClaudeBot alone, the rest by ClaudeBot and other Anthropic tokens. Only 5 of the 19 would break a group addressed to Anthropic's user-triggered and agent tokens alone (the record's "agent tokens 118 (177)"). "All of them in groups that name ClaudeBot, Anthropic's training crawler, and 14 of them only because the rule covers that crawler's name" is exact, and it ties into the piece's closing question about crawler versus fetcher tokens.
6. **The figure's "12 other requests that a site's robots.txt asked us not to make"** includes the 5 where no robots.txt could be read, so no file asked anything; RFC 9309's rule for an unreachable file made them disallowed. The body handles this correctly. The legend and alt text could say "12 other requests robots.txt rules ruled out, 5 of them because the file couldn't be reached".
7. **"5 went to two sites whose robots.txt couldn't be read at the time."** For shadowserver that was observed at the time. For web.archive.org the robots.txt read that failed was LH044's at 19:30:29, about 25 minutes after the three requests (19:04:55 to 19:05:48), which themselves got connection resets. The aggregate is disclosed under "How sure we are" (these 3 are among the 56). A word here ("couldn't be reached at the time or soon after") would match it.
8. **RFC wording.** "If it can't be reached at all, the reader should assume it may go nowhere": the RFC says MUST, and "unreachable" includes server errors (5xx), not only no connection. "Must assume it may go nowhere, and that includes a server error" is closer.
9. **"We replaced all five with placeholders on 9 November"** is true of the working tree. The original bodies remain in the repository's public history. A clause ("though earlier versions remain in the repository's history") would keep the sentence exact.
10. **The later week is now named.** Brief amendment 2 (17 November, after the draft) fixes ISO week 47, 16 to 22 November 2026, with the re-audit's earliest date 23 November and its deadline 14 December. The closing paragraph could name the week, since it was fixed before any of its requests was judged.
11. **Title length** is for the reader review, as the drafter says.

### Record-level, not blocking the piece

12. Record F1 ("11 got no answer; 3 have no recorded status") and "What the counts count" ("4 got no answer ... 1 has no recorded status"): by LH042's read log, the shadowserver page request got a connection reset, so 12 and 2 (and 5 and 0 among the 172). normalise_logs.py classes a blank status with an error as "status not recorded". A correction can go into the record's next version, or into the re-audit (row LH047-reread-record).
