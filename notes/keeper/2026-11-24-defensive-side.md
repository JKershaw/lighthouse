# The keeper's decision, 24 November 2026: the defensive side of the question

**In short.** Lighthouse asks whether AI activity could sustain and spread itself in ways that degrade shared infrastructure. It answers that by watching what happens and by asking whether defences work. It doesn't work out, map or write down how such activity could be built. On 24 November the keeper confirmed that the three defensive questions they added on 23 November (containment, small operators' deployment of fixes, recovery exercises) are how Lighthouse pursues this side of the charter's question, and asked that they go ahead.

Decided in conversation with the keeper in the Horizon session of 24 November 2026 (Claude Code, Anthropic cloud; run 2026-11-24T121300Z-anthropic-horizon). Written by that session's driver, in words the keeper approved before it was pushed.

## Why

What Lighthouse writes is public, and it becomes context for other people and other agents. A study of how self-sustaining AI activity could be put together would risk helping the very behaviour Lighthouse exists to watch, even if every part of it were already public somewhere, because selecting and joining things is itself a contribution. The keeper put it this way: Lighthouse must avoid "inadvertently benefiting the very behaviour we want to observe". If unwanted AI activity does happen, what helps everyone is knowing which defences work, how fast fixes reach the systems that need them, and how well organisations recover together.

## What follows

- **The defensive strand belongs to [What AI activity costs the shared internet](../../investigations/ai-cost-and-spread.md).** P-0046 (does containment work in practice?) and P-0048 (what do exercises show about recovery?) are agreed and attached there. P-0047 is already running as LH060 under [Where software updates go](../../investigations/software-updates.md). A gate shouldn't send a defensive question back for lacking an AI element of its own: its link to the charter is this decision.
- **Lighthouse doesn't study feasibility from the attacker's side.** No study, proposal, lead, review note or issue assesses, ranks or assembles how AI activity could spread or sustain itself, at any level of detail. Reports by others of such activity in the wild are still tracked under the existing rules: claims and dates, attributed, with mechanisms described only at the level a reader needs.
- **Defensive work keeps to outcomes.** It reports what was measured and for how long, never a catalogue of what defeats a defence, which systems are weak or how organisations depend on each other.
- **Records cite this note** rather than restating or extending its reasons.

The [research safety assessment](../../schedule/research-safety.md) applies to every step, as for all work.
