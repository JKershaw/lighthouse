# The keeper's decision, 21 November 2026: reading by the owner's intention, and keeping no copy against it

**In short.** Lighthouse's robots.txt rule of 8 November obeyed every token of the provider running it, so a site that refused only AI training crawlers also refused a single targeted read, even where the owner had said in its own file that such reads were welcome. On 21 November the keeper decided that Lighthouse follows the owner's intention as well as the letter: a one-off read follows the group for the running provider's user-triggered fetcher, else `*`; recurring collection keeps the strict rule; and where an owner refuses AI training or archiving crawlers, or all bots, Lighthouse keeps no copy of the page in its public repository, since others' crawlers can take copies from it. Existing copies of that kind are replaced by the facts the records use; git history is left as it is.

Decided in conversation with the keeper in the Watch session of 21 November 2026 (Claude Code, Anthropic cloud; run 2026-11-21T141050Z-anthropic-watch), after the evidence below. Written by that session's driver.

## Why

The keeper made the rule of 8 November strict because Claude had earlier ignored site owners' requests. In conversation on 21 November the keeper asked whether the rule now cost Lighthouse more than it should, and put the aim this way: "It's less the request (though obviously important) and more the intention. If we can't read a page as the owner requests it not be used for training, we're doing ourselves a disservice, but if we aim for only lax, we're potentially going against the intention we want to respect." And, on copies: "as long as we factor in people using our 'copy' of their data against how they want it used." The goal, in the keeper's words, is to be a good citizen of the internet.

## The decision

1. **Reading.** A one-off, targeted read obeys the group that names Lighthouse's token or the running provider's user-triggered fetcher (Claude-User under Anthropic, ChatGPT-User under OpenAI, every provider's when it cannot tell), else `*`. Recurring collection, such as a daily panel, keeps the rule of 8 November: every token of the provider, strictest wins. The tokens and their kinds stay in scripts/robots_tokens.json; the rule is not tied to one provider.
2. **Keeping.** Where robots.txt refuses a URL to `*`, to any provider's training crawler, or to a training or archiving crawler in the file's retention list, or where the page itself carries a noai or TDM reservation, Lighthouse keeps no body of it: the URL, read time, status, SHA-256 of the bytes received, the facts used and short attributed quotes. This holds whichever provider ran the read, since a copy in a public repository is open to everyone's crawlers.
3. **Walls stay walls.** A login, paywall, challenge, rate limit or refusal of the page is final, as before. A robots.txt answering 4xx is still read as no rules.
4. **Existing copies.** The bodies that fall under 2 are replaced by derived files holding the facts the records use, with URL, read time and hash, checked to rebuild every record's tables identically, and then deleted, with a note in each data folder. Git history is not rewritten: the keeper is content to leave it, rewriting would break every clone and the record of changes, and AGENTS.md forbids it on shared branches.
5. **Procedure without ceremony.** The rule text in AGENTS.md, the gate, the LH033 brief's retention amendment and the removal are done in the same session, each reviewed by a fresh agent where it is code or touches a record's evidence; the queue gets rows for them, done, with their references.
