## Findings

### Must-fix

**M1. C08 fails inclusion criterion 1 (my ruling on judgement call 1). With it gone, two documents are included and the brief's first inconclusive condition applies.**

The brief defines an available security fix or feature as "a patch, fixed release or configuration that was published and obtainable at the time measured (a software update, a fixed version, or a security feature such as a protocol or setting the operator could enable)", and the outcome as "whether, how far or how fast operators applied it". C08's outcome is the respondent's answer to which "security model" their organisation uses: perimeter, zero trust or hybrid. The paper says the labels were "harmonized to a consistent taxonomy" by the authors, and it never says what a respondent had to have deployed to answer "zero trust" or "hybrid". That's a self-applied label for an overall architecture, not the application of any particular published fix, release, protocol or setting. The paper's own framing is readiness and perception: "ZTA familiarity", "perceived necessity", satisfaction. The self-reported class doesn't save it, because what is reported isn't the deployment of a definable feature. Counting zero trust as a feature reads the brief's definition more widely than its examples allow. And the record was written knowing that this inclusion is what keeps it at three.

What has to change:
- C08 moves to the excluded table: "fails 1: the security model in use (perimeter, zero trust or hybrid) is a self-applied label for an architecture, not the deployment of a published fix or feature as the brief defines one". It may stay as context with nothing counted, as C09 and C13 are.
- The Answer, under the brief ("Fewer than three documents included: the record lists them and says the six searches found too little to describe the literature"), becomes inconclusive. The claims fall to five from two documents, all five measured, four of them vendor from one origin. **Every size claim (C07-a, b, c) then comes from one vendor paper on one scan day.** The type claims are C07-d and C10-a. The brief's third condition doesn't apply, since C10-a is measured and not vendor.
- The In short, Tallies, "What each document reports" (C08 paragraph), Judgement calls (record each as resolved by this review), Limits and Next change to match.

If the driver or the reader review reads criterion 1 the other way, the record should say that this inclusion is contested and why, and the S2 corrections below apply to C08's row. I don't think the wider reading is the better one.

**M2. C07's IPv4 claim is quoted in part, and the dropped clause runs the other way on the same size measure.**

The record (In short, Answer, claims table, C07 paragraph) gives IPv4 count as "larger less", quoting "adoption density thins as network size increases". The full sentence in the paper's Key Findings is: "While higher SSL and IPv4 counts correlate with PQC support, adoption density thins as network size increases, reflecting the complexity of migrating legacy infrastructure." So the paper says IPv4 count goes with *more* support on whether an organisation has started, as SSL active count does. The paper isn't consistent about this. Section IV.C says adoption is "widespread across organizational sizes when measured by digital size metrics (SSL active and IPv4 counts)" and gives the "slightly higher propensity" to SSL active count only. The "thins" statement in IV.C is narrower: "As the number of IPv4 addresses increases, the density of entities achieving 100% PQC support thins out". That's read from the second column of their Figure 4, a scatter of the percentage of IPs per entity against IPv4 count, with no rate and no test. Fewer points at the top of a scatter can mean fewer large entities overall, not a lower share among them.

What has to change: C07-b covers "SSL active count (section IV.C); SSL active and IPv4 counts (key findings)", with the key-findings quotation. C07-c's outcome becomes "fewer entities reach 100% coverage as IPv4 count rises", quoting IV.C, with the note "read from a scatter's point density, no rate or test stated; the paper's section IV.C and key findings differ on whether IPv4 count goes with starting deployment". In short and Answer follow (proposed text below).
