## What a reader of the opening would believe

Anthropic said on 27 October that its AI wrote a working exploit for a serious Linux kernel bug fixed on 15 October. Three weeks on, the official signals have not treated it as exploited: CISA has not listed it, and NVD finished its analysis without scoring it. The exploit-prediction score jumped two days *before* the post, then roughly doubled in the weeks *after* it. A reader would likely take the second part as the post's effect, and the first as something else (perhaps a leak or other news) moving the score. Most mainstream distributions now have fixes: Debian, Red Hat 8 to 10, Ubuntu's newer releases; SUSE about a third of its products; Ubuntu's oldest still waiting. A reader would not learn from the opening whether fixes were out when the post appeared. The limits a reader would notice: it is unreviewed, interim, and reads continue.

## Must-fix findings

1. **The record says it is unreviewed and that no review has happened.** Limits, first bullet: "**Unreviewed.** This interim record has had no evidence review, reader review or recheck (schedule rows LH036-interim-evidence-review, -reader-review and -recheck)." The same label is in the header ("version: 0.1 (interim, to the read of 16 November 2026, day 32 after E0; unreviewed)"), the In short ("To 16 November, unreviewed:") and the Answer ("Interim, unreviewed, to the read of 16 November 2026."). The Corrections section of the same file records the evidence review (R-0077) and the recheck (R-0078), so the Limits bullet is now false and contradicts the record. *Resolution:* when this review's findings are resolved, say in the header, the In short, the Answer and Limits what the record has had (evidence review R-0077, recheck R-0078, reader-and-inference review R-0079 and its resolution), and keep the self-interest sentence about what reviews were asked to look for.

2. **The opening invites a causal reading of the score around the post, and gives the signals' timing against the post but not the fixes'.** In short: "the exploit-prediction score's only one-day rise by a factor of two or more came on 25 October, two days before the post, and after the post smaller daily rises together about doubled it by 13 November". Both halves are true by dates (tables.json `epss_series`), but set side by side, with no caveat, they lead a reader to take the doubling as the post's effect and the earlier rise as something else's. The record's own rule, carried in the Answer ("A step near an event is a coincidence of dates; nothing here says why the score moved"), is missing from the opening, and the 25 October step falls one day after E1, the CNA container's update, which the opening does not mention. In the same opening, the fix sentence gives only the 16 November state, while the brief's named comparison is the fixes against E2. At the first read, five days after the post, no Red Hat product and none of Ubuntu's seven affected releases carried a fix (Red Hat 0 of 9 rows fixed; Ubuntu four pending, three needed: tables.json per_read 2026-11-01T0830Z), Debian's security suites and 19 of 112 SUSE products did, and no tracker in the panel dates a fix on or before the post (the record's comparison section). A reader of the opening alone cannot tell whether fixes were out when the post appeared, so the opening is easier on the post's timing than the record. Both points are of the kind the brief's disclosure asks reviews to catch, one in each direction. *Resolution:* add to the In short a clause such as "the dates do not say why the score moved", and a sentence such as "At our first read, five days after the post, Debian's security suites and 19 SUSE products had a fix and no Red Hat or Ubuntu release did; no tracker dates a fix on or before the post." If length is a concern, shorten the SUSE and Ubuntu detail, which the Answer carries.

## Suggestions (optional)
