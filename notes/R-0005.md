## Findings

This is the release gate for a new piece, articles/what-a-download-shows.md, its illustration, its short form, and the front page that carries it (README.md above the horizontal rule), together with the study record studies/LH005/ and the four new entries S20 to S23 in sources.md. The reviewer read the dispatch, AGENTS.md and R-0004, then the piece once straight through as a newcomer would, then sentence by sentence against studies/LH005/LH005.md at version 0.1, its brief, its source assessment, and the 37 tables in studies/LH005/data/. Unlike R-0004, this review did fetch: the record's counts were re-queried from ClickPy with eleven small SQL queries (one of which the server refused for a syntax fault of the reviewer's own, and which a local recomputation replaced), pypistats.org's overall series was read again, and the eight documentation pages the record quotes were fetched and every quotation searched for in their text. Every derived figure the piece uses, and most the record uses, was also recomputed with a short Python script from the retained reads, so that the check runs both from the primary source to the record and from the record's own inputs to its outputs. The piece's SVG was regenerated from its script and compared byte for byte, and rendered in headless Chromium in light and dark mode and looked at.

The verdict is that the piece, the short form and the front page can be released after the must-fixes below, none of which changes a conclusion: every count reproduces from ClickPy to the digit, every quotation is on its page, and every link resolves. What must change is three figures in the record that its own tables do not support as worded (the reading times, the lean-lsp-mcp shares, and "smaller than the day-to-day swing"), one figure in the piece and the record that mislabels its denominator ("all older versions" for a class that leaves one older version out), two sentences in the standfirst and on the front page whose verb ("It shows") is firmer than the record's label (interpretation), and one adverb ("barely") that the per-week figures do not bear.

### The interpretation boundary

5. **Must fix. Piece, line 5: "It shows the new version arriving in days, carried by the programs that install software rather than by any project's decision."** The record labels the carrying an interpretation and the piece's own body says "We think that is what most of these downloads were" (line 33) and "on our reading" (line 47). The standfirst says it flat. One clause brings it inside the label.

6. **Must fix. README.md, line 5: "It shows new versions carried by installers settling open version ranges, mostly one called uv, rather than by any project's decision."** The same verb, and "new versions" in the plural generalises to releases the study did not read by installer: the installer and CI analysis is of 1.27.0 alone, and 1.26.0 was read only for its pace.

7. **Must fix. Piece, line 39: "downloads of the new version barely fell at weekends, to 0.88 of the weekday level"; record, "Traffic on the new release barely dips at weekends."** The three-week ratio is right, but it is not steady: week by week 1.27.0's weekend-over-weekday ratio is 1.24, 0.91 and 0.62, and without Sunday 12 April, the peak share and the new version's largest day in the first two weeks, the three-week figure is 0.82. In the third week the new version fell at the weekend by more than a third. 1.26.0's ratio is steady (0.55, 0.48, 0.52), so the contrast the piece draws survives; "barely" does not. The record should carry the per-week figures beside the three-week ones, since a reader of the record cannot see the instability from the ratios alone.

8. **Note. "we think a bad one would travel as fast" (line 47).** The mechanism the piece describes, an installer taking the newest release that fits a range, does not weigh the release's quality, and the sentence is carried as "we think", which is the record's Next entry restated as a scenario. It is within the rules. One qualifier the driver may want: as fast for as long as it stayed on the registry, since a release found bad can be yanked. No change required.

9. **Should fix. Piece, line 11: "Each entry names the library, the version, the day".** The sentence describes the registry's log; the log keeps a timestamp (L2, L8), and it is the copy read that keeps only the day (record, Limits, "Day, not hour"). Four words make it true of both.

10. **Should fix. Piece, line 33: "So a new environment set up on 3 April received 1.27.0 because it existed".** No such environment was observed; this is what an installer resolving an open range does. "would have received" keeps the verb honest, as the same construction does at line 43.

The record's "tools resolving afresh" for uv and pip states as fact what the log cannot show. One sentence in each.

Bazel's 24,660 downloads over twenty days is a small population and the 234 could be a handful of projects; the piece does not lean on it beyond "under 1 per cent". Within the label.

13. **Should fix. Piece, line 49: "it cannot tell a person from a build server or an agent".** Line 35 has just explained that the flag does tell a build server when the installer reports one. The closing line should not take that back.
