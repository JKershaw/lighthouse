## What I found

1. **CRAN's robots.txt is cut mid-line, and the fragment disallows the probe (must-fix, corrected in the checkpoint).** CRAN's robots.txt was the only one truncated at the collector's 64 KiB cap. Its last kept line is `Disallow: /web/pac`, the start of a longer DESCRIPTION rule, and that fragment is the only rule that matches the probe `/web/packages/ggplot2/index.html` (review/robots_check-output.txt). Nothing in the part read disallows the probe. So 31 robots.txt files, 22 of them named services, disallow the probe in the text read, and CRAN's rule is unknown. The deeper fault: RFC 9309 section 2.5 says a parsing limit MUST be at least 500 KiB, so the brief's "robots.txt obeyed, RFC 9309" does not hold for long files, and the error can run either way: here it hid a service, and a cut that drops a later Disallow would send a probe the operator refused.
2. **P04 is a captcha page, at robots.txt and at the probe (must-fix, corrected).** The checkpoint called it "a 202 with an empty body". Both kept responses are 206-byte pages that refresh at once to `/.well-known/sgcaptcha/`, a captcha path (the vendor is my reading of the path, not verified). The robots.txt answer was parsed as an empty robots.txt, so the collector sent a probe where the brief means no probe to follow a challenge. No signature in the brief covers it, so the class stays `open_unconfirmed`; read as a challenge, post hoc, 2 of 26 drawn services read were walled.
3. **Responses did echo our addresses (must-fix, corrected).** summary.json's `egress_ip_echoed` is empty because collect.py looks only for an `x-ip` header. In the read, Wikimedia's `x-client-ip` (five responses) and Docker Hub's `x-ratelimit-ip` (one) echoed six different addresses in 160.79.106.0/24, inside the block the brief cites as Anthropic's; the statement run's Wikimedia responses add a seventh. The proxied route does not leave from one address. P04's captcha URLs echoed 34.172.54.233 and 34.136.29.35 on the plain-HTTP route, one per request; I did not look up their registrant.
4. **The two strata were read largely from different addresses (must-fix as a statement, corrected; a design limit).** The plain-HTTP route carried 44 of the drawn stratum's 60 robots.txt requests and 15 of its 25 probes, against 2 of the named stratum's 164 requests. The brief's comparison that matters, named against drawn, is therefore also, at the baseline, Anthropic's block against other addresses. A wall set for Anthropic's addresses would show in the named stratum and not the drawn, which the brief's selection reading would not separate. The checkpoint's model disclosure said the study's requests leave from Anthropic's block; most do, not all.
5. **The study page the User-Agent names did not exist (must-fix before read 1).** https://lighthouse.harbour.cat/studies/LH044/ answered 404 at 20:21:57 UTC; the site is built from main, which by this checkout's copy did not yet hold the study, so an operator following the link during the read most likely found nothing. Recorded in the checkpoint's correction.
6. **The compatible User-Agent is honest in form, with three qualifications (interpretation).** `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; Lighthouse-research/1.0; +URL)` is the convention self-identifying crawlers use; it carries no browser engine or version token, names Lighthouse, carries the RFC 9309 product token, and gives a URL. It does not pretend to be a browser. But: (a) the URL led nowhere at the baseline (5); (b) the brief chose the `Mozilla` prefix so that walls set for browsers are shown to it, and filters that treat a Mozilla-prefixed client from a cloud address as a fake browser (kernel.org's post describes banning cloud ASNs for exactly that) may wall it for the prefix, so a page-surface wall may partly measure this choice; that belongs in the record's limits; (c) it honours robots.txt for its own token only, and 8 probes went to hosts whose robots.txt disallows an Anthropic token at the probe path, two of them (wiki.gentoo.org, ftp.eq.uc.pt) Claude-User and one (ftp.eq.uc.pt) a `Claude-Code` group (review/ai_tokens_check-output.txt). RFC 9309 asks no more, and Lighthouse is a distinct client; but the requests are made by an Anthropic model's session from Anthropic's addresses, and an operator who refuses Claude-User has said something about that. Not must-fix under the brief; a question for the keeper (Suggestions, 1).
7. **The rate-limit sentence is careful but loose (should-fix).** "One request each from this study cannot exceed a rate limit by itself: these limits were spent, if by anyone, by other traffic from the same address." The proxied route rotates among addresses (3), the 429s echoed none, and a 429 can be a policy rather than a count. The sentence also leaves unsaid whose traffic shares the address: other sessions on Anthropic's infrastructure, which the study cannot see. Proposed wording in Suggestions, 4.
8. **Smaller points.** (a) "Open, by marker: 51 named" is 50 by marker and 1 with no marker set (M10). (b) Amendment 1 says the Debian frame holds "387 `/debian/` URLs on 351 hosts, not '351 distinct URLs'"; the original was right: 387 links, 351 distinct URLs, 351 hosts. Nothing in the draw changes. (c) coded.csv says Wikimedia's edit date was "not re-read"; the kept page says "last edited on 15 October 2026, at 22:40". (d) `robots_outcome` counts as `wall_at_robots` any robots.txt the classifier calls `rate_limited` (also 503 with Retry-After, and 403 or 503 with rate wording), wider than the brief's "a challenge or a 429"; stricter, and only crossref's 429 was affected at the baseline. (e) 42 of the 101 named markers are substrings of their own probe URL (for example `gtk`, `pip`, `open`), so a 2xx challenge page that echoes the requested URL would carry the marker and be called `open`, overriding a challenge signature. It did not happen at the baseline. ## Suggestions

Protocol changes for the driving session to make by dated amendment before read 1 (15 to 17 November), each labelled post hoc since the baseline was seen:

1. **robots.txt parse limit (must-fix 1 in the Verdict).** Read robots.txt up to at least 512 KiB, as RFC 9309 section 2.5 requires; if a file is still cut, drop the incomplete last line and treat the service as `robots_unreachable` for that read rather than apply a partial file. Re-read CRAN at read 1 under the new rule; its baseline stays as read.
2. **Echoed addresses (must-fix 2 in the Verdict).** `egress_ip_echoed` should read `x-client-ip`, `x-ratelimit-ip`, `x-forwarded-for`-style echoes and `ipr:`-style addresses in kept bodies, per route, so the brief's "a different address block echoed" condition can actually fire. Record the route in classes.csv, and report the named and drawn tallies within route as well as by stratum (Found, 4). A single RDAP lookup of the plain-HTTP addresses would say whose they are.
3. **A captcha signature for P04's kind.** Add a signature for a page that refreshes or redirects at once to a captcha path (such as `/.well-known/sgcaptcha/`), so a robots.txt answered that way stops the probe, and say the baseline's P04 would have read as `wall_at_robots`.
4. **Wording for the rate limits** (checkpoint and record): "A 429 to a single request says nothing about that request's own rate. Whatever limit it reflects counts traffic we can't see: the address it left from, which is shared with other sessions on Anthropic's infrastructure and rotates within its block, or a policy that answers 429 without counting."
5. **For the keeper (Found, 6c).** Whether Lighthouse, run by Anthropic models from Anthropic's addresses, should also honour robots.txt groups for Anthropic's agent tokens (Claude-User, and a `Claude-Code` group where a file names one) as well as its own. Doing so from read 1 would stop probes to wiki.gentoo.org and ftp.eq.uc.pt; honouring ClaudeBot and anthropic-ai too would stop six more. Either way, the record should list the eight.
6. **Markers.** Let a challenge signature win unless the marker is found outside any echo of the request URL, or replace markers that occur in their own URL with content-only strings (Found, 8e).
7. **Coding additions.** kernel.org's post also says the crawlers moved to "millions of random residential or mobile IPs" and that it banned whole cloud ASNs whose clients posed as browsers; Read the Docs says it "temporarily blocked all traffic from bots Cloudflare identifies as AI Crawlers". Both bear on what this instrument can see from a cloud address and should be coded. Fill in Wikimedia's edit date from the kept page.
8. **The record's limits** should carry Found, 6b (the `Mozilla` prefix may draw walls set against fake browsers) and the denominators of Checked, 4 (7 of 61 probes answered, plus 6 walls at robots.txt).
9. **Amendment 1's Debian sentence** could gain a dated note that the original "351 distinct URLs" was right (Found, 8b).
10. **Under amendment 3,** P31's robots.txt redirect ends at an article page, which will parse as a robots.txt with no rules and so allow the probe. That follows RFC 9309, but it is worth listing when read 1 is reported, with any similar chain.
