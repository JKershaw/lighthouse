# R-0116: reader-and-inference review of the Microsoft 365 report comparison (version 0.2)

Reviewer: a Claude Code subagent on Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`), 22 November 2026, for the Research run. Object: [notes/microsoft-outage-report/comparison.md](microsoft-outage-report/comparison.md), version 0.2. README.md has no "Current focus" section; only its opening was read in stage 1.

## What a reader would believe, written before reading the record

Principal finding, said back: Microsoft first said the November Microsoft 365 outage was "not related to cyber or AI / agents". Its later post-incident report, read from a consultancy's copy, only says "This change was not caused by AI tooling", without saying how it knows and without mentioning agents. So Lighthouse calls the AI claim "qualified": the report doesn't contradict it, but it backs a narrower version, not the original.

Observed: the report's text (from a third-party PDF, hash-matched across two reads the same day); its account of a July Windows connection-handling change with a latent defect, exposed by maintenance capacity loss, a Teams failover and peak load; the single AI sentence; the absence of the word "agent"; ordinary automation in detection and recovery.

Explained: why "qualified" rather than "confirmed" (the subject switches from "This incident" to "This change"; agents unaddressed; no basis given). The referent of "This change" is flagged as interpretation.

Caused what: by Microsoft's account, the defect plus three conditions caused the outage; AI did not cause the change. Lighthouse does not claim AI caused or did not cause anything.

Limits a reader would notice: it is a copy, not Microsoft's file; everything is Microsoft's own say-so; silence about AI elsewhere settles nothing; one incident. A careful reader might still leave thinking Microsoft "walked back" or hedged its AI claim, despite the note's disclaimer, because "narrower" and "qualified" sound like a retreat.

**Material claim problems**

1. *The referent of "This change" is ranked without its alternatives laid out.* Passage: comparison.md line 36, "Interpretation: the most likely referent is the July change that introduced the defect ... a looser reading (the incident as a whole) can't be excluded", repeated at line 76 ("most likely the July change"). Evidence: the comparison's own observation on the same line says the executive summary uses "the fix" and "the update", "the last meaning the repair", and never uses "change" before the sentence. So the repair update is a third candidate the text offers, and it's the nearer one in the summary. The grounds given for "most likely" are Lighthouse's own earlier reading (readings/2026-11-22.md line 41, an interpretation by the same pipeline, not independent) and, implicitly, 365Fanatic's paraphrase (line 13 of the same reading), which is a third party's interpretation, not Microsoft's. The classification survives under the July and repair readings. Under the incident-as-a-whole reading it moves towards confirm for AI, with agents still unaddressed. A reader comes away thinking the sentence is probably about the root-cause change, but the record doesn't support that ranking. Resolution: name all three candidates (the July change, the repair update, the incident). Give the actual grounds for preferring the July change, if any survive, which are the p. 6 wording "The change that introduced the defect" and 365Fanatic's paraphrase, labelled as a third party's reading. Otherwise drop "most likely" and call the referent unresolved. State in one line that "qualified" holds under every reading except the incident-as-a-whole one, where it is qualified only because agents and basis are absent.

2. *"Narrower" and "qualified" invite a retreat reading, and the comparison omits the evidence that Microsoft hasn't withdrawn the broad line.* Passage: comparison.md line 3, "The 15-page report ... says something narrower ... classes the AI and agents exclusion as **qualified**". Line 85 disclaims a retreat, but only well below the opening. Evidence: after the report-published notice of 21 November 01:04 UTC, both public copies of Microsoft's posts still carry the unchanged final status "not related to cyber or AI / agents" (readings/2026-11-21.md lines 3, 12, 14, 25; readings/2026-11-22.md line 12). So on the evidence, Microsoft's public record now holds both statements: the report adds a narrower one and withdraws nothing. My own stage-1 reading shows the risk. A careful reader of the opening may conclude that Microsoft hedged or walked back its claim, which the evidence does not support. Resolution: in the In short and under B1, add that the broad status text stood unchanged in both copies of Microsoft's posts on 21 and 22 November, after the report's publication notice. Phrase the result as "the report backs only a narrower statement and gives no basis for it; it doesn't withdraw the broad one". Move the line-85 sentence ("doesn't mean ... Microsoft retreated") up into the opening. Any public line should carry both.

Considered and not raised as material. The third-party copy is handled throughout. B2's "confirmed" is explicitly self-report, not independent confirmation (line 89). Silence is never read as evidence. B3's "qualified" over "contradict" is argued on the text. The rules' single-label categories do squeeze a mixed result (partly confirmed for the change's authorship, unaddressed for agents and for AI in the conditions and the response) into one word. Problem 2's wording fix answers that without changing the rules.

**Readability**

1. Line 103: "the preliminary report of 15 November" takes one side of a date the readings disagree on. It is dated 15 November in 365Fanatic's account (readings/2026-11-19.md line 14), "issued on November 16" in Redmond's (2026-11-18.md line 13), and announced in the posts at 16 November 04:46 UTC (2026-11-20.md line 12). Suggest "dated 15 November, announced in Microsoft's posts on 16 November".
2. The opening could say in one plain clause what the report says caused the outage (a months-old Windows change with a latent defect, exposed by three conditions on 12 November). A reader then knows what "This change" might refer to before meeting the classification.
3. The subject switch ("This incident" to "This change") is the clearest single fact behind the classification. The In short doesn't yet name it, and it could.
