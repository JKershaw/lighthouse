## Findings

This is the release gate for LH011 and the piece it earned. The reviewer read AGENTS.md, the brief with its amendment, the record, its sources, the piece, its short form and figure script, and the surface changes in 1bc6e80; reran the replay; recomputed every headline figure from the retained frame and count files with its own code; re-read a few counts and upload times from the live sources; checked the brief's rules against the commit and read-log times; and traced every figure in the piece and short form to a table. Every figure in the record's Answer stands. What had to be fixed was wording: four truncated medians, one "stayed flat" that the day-30 medians do not bear out, a "below zero" whose summary needed naming, a missing limit on what was kept, a wrong writing window in the amendment, a figure alt text that was wrong for three libraries, and a sentence in the programme's checkpoint that described two papers as saying what their abstracts do not. The verdict is that the piece can be released.

The reviewer's calculations are kept in studies/LH011/review/: recompute.py and recompute.txt (the headline figures from data/releases.csv, data/pypi_versions.csv, data/counts/ and data/ci/, without analyse.py), piece_checks.py and piece_checks.txt (the piece's and record's smaller figures from the analysis tables), figure_checks.py and figure_checks.txt (the alt text's ranges), and requery.py and requery.txt (the live re-reads).

### Must fix, fixed

1. **Truncated medians** (record, Findings). aiobotocore 1.9, boto3 7.4, litellm 9.4 and s3transfer 33.9 where project_outcomes.csv holds 1.98, 7.46, 9.50 and 33.97: now 2.0, 7.5, 9.5 and 34.0. The other seven were right. The piece's "between 2 and 34" and the figure reading were already right.
2. **"stayed flat at between 2 and 34 per cent"** (record, Answer; programme.md's LH011 answer). The eleven's day-30 medians run from 2.5 to 37.0, and litellm's rises from 9.5 to 21.0; now "between 2 and 34 per cent on the day after release, moved little over the month, to between 2 and 37 per cent by day 30, litellm's rising most", with the day-30 range added to Findings.
3. **"Every project's median difference was below zero"** (record, Answer and Findings). True as the median of each project's per-release differences, which is the brief's measure; but for boto3, botocore and litellm the median new-release share is above the median replaced share (by 0.9, 1.4 and 2.3 points), so a reader comparing the two columns of ci_projects.csv would see the opposite sign. Both places now name the summary and say so. The piece's "for every one of the 37 libraries the typical difference went the same way" is the median of differences and stands.
4. **What was kept** (record, Limits). data/counts/ folds every version older than a project's first qualifying release into one "~other" row a day (confirmed against the live table: the fold equals the sum of the other 137 numpy versions and 2,005 boto3 versions on the days re-read), and data/ci/ keeps date, version, installer and flag only, no Python version. A new Limits paragraph says so, as the programme's checkpoint already relied on it.
5. **Amendment 1's writing window** (brief). "Between 19:57 and 20:10 UTC" where its commit is at 19:58:37; now 19:57 to 19:58, with a bracketed dated note saying what changed and why, since an amendment is not silently edited.
6. **The figure's alt text** (articles/two-days-for-most.md and the `<desc>` written by scripts/draw_piece_figure.py). "Every line is low on the day of release" is false for pathspec (68 per cent on day 0) and two more above half; "between about 50 and 83 per cent" is 49 to 85 over the month. Now "every line starts lower on the day of release than the day after" and "about 49 and 85". The script was rerun; the SVG's lines and the printed reading (34 of 37) are unchanged.
7. **The checkpoint's two papers** (programme.md). PyPitfall (2507.18075) and LibEvoBench (2606.25402) were said to "read dependency metadata and requirements files and warn that download counts mix builds, mirrors and transitive installs". Their abstracts, read now, say nothing of download counts: PyPitfall reads the declared dependencies of 378,573 PyPI packages for vulnerable versions, and LibEvoBench is a benchmark of whether code-generating models keep track of library versions. The sentence now says that, and that the search summaries had suggested more. The selection does not rest on either.
