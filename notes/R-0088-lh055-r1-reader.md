# R-0088: reader review of the ARTEX round 1 public surfaces (stage one)

Reviewed: notes/artex/r1-public-draft.md at commit 0469445, read alone, with no record, brief, data or other note opened.

## What a reader would believe, written before reading the record

**Principal finding, said back.** Amazon says that attackers using ARTEX, a free AI hacking tool, had AI agents whose logs show stolen AI keys paying for the computing that hunted for more stolen keys. Nobody else confirms it, Amazon itself can't check whether the logs tell the truth, so Lighthouse calls it unproven rather than false.

**Observed.** Amazon's decoy sensors captured the agents' own records; Amazon read them as describing the loop and tied them to ARTEX with moderate confidence. Lighthouse read six reports on 17 November and found the loop described for ARTEX only in Amazon's. A September report describes a similar loop in a different operation, also mostly from the agent's records. One report (Check Point's) couldn't be read.

**Explained.** Little is explained. A reader would take away that people, not the agents alone, steered the key hunting, though not who established this or how.

**Caused.** The texts say the stolen keys paid for compute that found more keys, but only as the records' own claim. A reader would not think Lighthouse has shown the loop happened or worked.

**Limits a reader would notice.** Everything rests on one company and on the attackers' own logs; other reports are silent rather than contradictory; the reading is one day's. The investigation page's sentence that a report "describes AI activity sustaining itself outside a test" reads stronger than the rest, and a hurried reader could take it as Lighthouse's own judgement. "People directed" sits awkwardly beside "sustaining itself".

### What the round establishes

Six documents were read (fetch log: S01 to S04, S06, S07 at HTTP 200; Check Point, S05, HTTP 429). Only Amazon describes the credential-to-compute loop for ARTEX (S02 line 93, C025), and by Amazon's own account its evidence is the agents' records carried in requests to its decoys, whose outcomes it "cannot verify" (line 99, C023). The other four ARTEX documents leave all six links unknown (r1_tables_output: 0 supported, 6 claimed, 24 unknown over the five read). Captured standing instructions show people setting objectives, targets and limits (line 95 to 96, C027, C032); how often people intervened is unmeasured. SANS independently describes a similar loop in a different operation, again mostly from the agent's side. The measurements and codings support "inconclusive, not negative", and the draft says so on every surface. The record's single-origin and dependence findings hold against the texts I checked.

### Material claim problems

**1. A human-run operation is filed as "AI activity sustaining itself".** Passage (r1-public-draft.md, section 2, replacing line 80): "One report read so far, Amazon's of 13 November 2026, describes AI activity sustaining itself outside a test: attackers' agents whose own records say stolen AI keys paid for further key hunting." And the strands sentence: "Apart from the reported ARTEX loop above ... nothing below describes AI activity sustaining or spreading itself." Evidence: Amazon says the threat actors "organized" the campaign "as a self-sustaining operation" and "instructed ARTEX agents through standing instruction prompts that set the mission objectives, targeting rules, and operational constraints" (S02 lines 93, 95); the brief says "A self-sustaining credential loop does not require or establish self-replication, independence from people or net profitability"; the record's section 5 keeps resource replenishment apart from independence from people. On the investigation page, "AI activity sustaining itself" is the charter's question, and its strand list reserves "activity on the open internet" for the charter strand while placing operations "written and run by people, whatever tools they use" in another. So a reader would take this as the first reported instance of the very thing the watch exists for, when the report describes people running an operation whose compute, by the agents' own records, was paid with stolen keys. This is where my stage-one points matter: the sentence is the strongest on any surface, and "people directed" elsewhere sits against it. Proposed resolution: "One report read so far, Amazon's of 13 November 2026, describes an operation that Amazon calls self-sustaining: people set the agents' goals and limits, and the agents' own records say stolen AI keys paid for the computing behind further key hunting. It's recorded as Amazon's claim, resting on records whose outcomes Amazon says it can't verify, and followed in the ARTEX study. It isn't AI sustaining itself apart from people, and no report read so far describes AI activity spreading itself outside a test." Reword the strands sentence to match ("Apart from the reported ARTEX loop above, an operation people ran whose fuel, by the agents' own records, was stolen keys, ...").

**2. Amazon reads as more hesitant than it is.** Passages: front page, "Amazon says attackers' AI agents recorded exactly that loop, but the records are the agents' own and Amazon says it can't verify what they claim"; study page, "Amazon says it can't verify the outcomes those records report". Evidence: Amazon states the loop in its own voice in its summary (S02 line 93, C025) and writes "The agents succeeded where basic security hygiene was missing" (line 111, C046); the record's Answer notes both are "in its own voice and without hedge". Its caveat (line 99) is about outcomes on systems it doesn't monitor. A reader would think Amazon offers the loop only as the agents' say-so; in fact Amazon presents it as its finding and caveats the evidence. That matters for how later repetition (E4) should be read. "What they claim" also widens Amazon's narrower "the outcomes the agents reported". Proposed resolution, front page: "Amazon says attackers ran exactly that loop, but its evidence is the agents' own records, and it says it can't verify the outcomes they report." Study page and investigation paragraph: add "Amazon states the loop as its finding" before the caveat.

**3. The one alternative with evidence is missing.** Passages: study page, "The other ARTEX reports neither confirm nor contradict it"; investigation paragraph, "nothing confirms or contradicts that loop for ARTEX". Evidence: the round 1 checkpoint lists, under "Pointing the other way", CrowdStrike's finding (relayed by the FBI, Censys and StepSecurity; "likely" where Censys first gives it) that one ARTEX actor probably bought model access through a reseller (C009, C077, C078, C111), and consoles hold the keys their users configure (C122). It's a different campaign, so it doesn't contradict Amazon's, which is why the link coding is right. But read alone the texts present one account of where the agents' computing came from, either true or unverified, when the record holds some evidence that ARTEX operators also bought it. Proposed resolution: one clause on the study page and the investigation paragraph, for example "and in a different campaign one actor probably bought its model access through a reseller, as CrowdStrike reports".

### Readability

1. Say how the human direction is known: "the attackers' instructions, captured by Amazon's sensors, show people set the agents' goals and limits" reads more clearly than "in the reports we've read, people directed the agents' hunt".
2. "Check Point's report couldn't be read on the day" could say why and what follows: it hit a rate limit, and the scheduled reads try again from 18 November.
3. The investigation paragraph runs to eight sentences; splitting it after "can't verify the outcomes those records report" would help.

The decision to leave site/learned.md unchanged is sound: no retained source describes agents spreading themselves (SANS says its data show no self-replicating system, S06 line 43), and the WorkOS agent acted outside its test without copying itself (S07 line 162).

### Next step

Continue with round 2, but re-rank it, because most of the eight candidates can't tell the explanations apart for the campaign that matters. The loop is a claim about one campaign by groups Amazon calls disparate and Chinese-nexus. Evidence from other campaigns (CrowdStrike's South Korean case, VAPOR PANDA's tokens, Zscaler's model prevalence, Unit 42's console counts, Google's overview) can show that supply varies, but can't confirm or contradict Amazon's Campaign 1. This is the record's post hoc reading 2, so whatever it finds is labelled that way. Cheap: one query.

CrowdStrike's 7 October report is worth one document for E2 by campaign. Zscaler, Unit 42, Google and VAPOR PANDA should be dropped unless a query shows they speak to inference supply.
