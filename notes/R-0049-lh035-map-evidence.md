## What I found

Checked and right: 48; ten layers each with at least one row; certificates the thinnest at 3, then names and running services at 4 each; 11 of 48; 1.3 per cent of about 74,000 (L06, a preprint, labelled so); "heavily biased towards larger networks" (L05, truncated at "and IXPs" but faithful); Censys's "basic visibility" and its paper's IPv6 sentence; Netlas's 50 a day on an account; DNS Coffee's count and access terms; NVD's backlog; EPSS's "cannot be released"; Epoch's 45 per cent; the telescope's 1/256th; four US cable operators to the end of 2024; the Docker, npm and Hugging Face counting rules in substance; Shodan's exclusion; the limits paragraph (crt.sh, OpenINTEL, IODA and the Internet Archive named as unread, twenty leads unreached, the free-plan choice). The post hoc reading is labelled ("ours, made after we had written the rows, not tested by anything") and says no study tests it. The problems:

1. **The title's "each one watches from a single place" is false read literally, and is a reading stated as a finding.** RIPE Atlas has "over 14,000" probes, RouteViews collects "from over a thousand peers across dozens of collectors", GreyNoise has "thousands of sensors". What the record says is that each sees *from where it stands*; the grouping of blind spots by vantage is its post hoc reading. The summary's "each watcher stands in one place" and the short form's "each sees from one place" repeat it, and the body's "turned out to be the pattern" and "it is the same kind of thing each time" state the generalisation without the "as we read it" the record's Findings give it.
2. **"Anyone can use without paying" (summary) and "48 free instruments" (short form) overstate the inclusion test.** The brief includes an instrument when "some of its data can be read without payment (a free tier counts)". Rows include Netlas (account needed), the UCSD telescope (most data for vetted academics; "Several historical datasets are available for download, either publicly or upon request"), Shadowserver (reports to "vetted subscribers"), MAWI ("only research purposes"), Ark (latest year restricted) and RIPEstat and DNS Coffee (bulk use restricted). The body's "publishes something anyone can use without paying" is fair; the summary is read without the body.
3. **The no-limit count does not reproduce as printed, and "at all" is contradicted.** No script prints it. Counting rows whose "cannot_see" field begins "not stated" gives 16 (12 of I01 to I40, 4 of I41 to I48), where the record says 13 plus 4. Seventeen needs I06 (RIS: "No coverage limit stated", beside a note on deprecated collectors); adding I17 ("no general statement", one report's spoofing caveat) gives 18; dropping the two rows the record itself excepts gives 14 (recount_output.txt). Those two are Hugging Face ("not stated, beyond the double counting of GGUF files on cloning") and Software Heritage ("beyond its errata"), so "state no limit ... at all" is stronger than the record. "Pages" is also wrong: the unit is an instrument, some read through several pages.
4. **The colophon's "Every count is printed by the study's scripts" is untrue** for 11 of 48 and 17 of 48 (build.py prints neither). The colophon also says only CISA's row rests on an extract; the record says Docker's pull definition and Netlas's collection policy rest on 2 November extracts (instruments.json, I43 and I45 how_read), and the piece quotes Docker's rule. I confirmed it is not in the raw bodies of 5 November: the usage page has no pull definition.
5. **The IPv6 sentence is contradicted by a page read.** Censys's documentation page (data/reads-2026-11-06/19-I12-...) says "The host dataset provides comprehensive information on public IPv4 and IPv6 hosts": a page we read describes such a view, even if its own paper denies it. CAIDA's Ark page read also lists "The IPv6 Routed /48 Topology Dataset" (not a row, not read). The piece softens Censys's claim to "names IPv6 hosts", and the short form's "No page we read describes a view across the IPv6 address space" is plainly contradicted. The record's wording ("no included instrument's pages state an IPv6-wide view") has the same weakness, but the piece is stronger.
6. **"Only one ... puts a number on how much it covers" is contradicted by the piece itself.** The telescope's page gives "approximately 1/256th of all IPv4 addresses", which the piece quotes, and the interconnection project gives ">75%" of the US population. Epoch is the only one that estimates its own *completeness*. Epoch's raw page of 6 November also dates its figure "as of November 6, 2026", so it is a live value.
7. **"Why it matters" lists "which names exist" among what the instruments can't show**, against the piece's own names paragraph (DNS Coffee shows which names its imported zone files hold; .com showed 168,656,928) and the record's version 0.2 qualification.
8. **MAWI is now two links, not one.** Its page: "Now, the samplepoint-F contains the traffic from the 2 links, the transit link and the BBIX link" (added 8 August 2023). The piece says "one academic link between a Japanese research network and its upstream provider".
9. **The post hoc reading does not say what it was made from.** The record: "a post hoc reading made from 40 rows by the writer who wrote them, with no test, and it was not revisited for the eight rows of 5 November", and "No brief or issue exists for it yet". AGENTS.md's Evidence section asks for the post hoc label with the number of comparisons and for the round to name what will test it or say none can. The piece says only "after we had written the rows" and "no study of ours does that yet".
10. **"192,000 links ... that no public listening post saw" is wider than the paper, and one-sided.** L06: bgp.tools "saw 192k AS links no RIS or RouteViews point saw, and they saw 401k it missed". PCH (row I07) is also a public collector, and the omitted 401,000 reverses the impression that the private collector simply sees more.
