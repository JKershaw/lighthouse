# Close-out, ARTEX run of 17 November 2026

**In short.** The ARTEX study took its baseline the evening it was commissioned and finished round 1 through both reviews and a recheck. Round 2's targeted search followed and waits for review. Public evidence doesn't yet establish that stolen AI keys paid for further key hunting: the loop rests on one report, Amazon's, built from the attackers' own agent records, whose outcomes Amazon says it can't verify.

- **Learned.** Of six reports read on 17 November, only Amazon's describes the loop for ARTEX, and Amazon states it as its own finding; the attackers' captured instructions show people set the agents' goals and limits. Round 2 (unreviewed) found no Amazon statement on how its decoys came to be used and no later report with telemetry of its own on the loop. It found Microsoft telemetry of agents hunting AI keys in another campaign, and a description of ARTEX holding user-supplied keys in a pool. CrowdStrike's reseller finding says "accessed", not "bought".
- **Current account.** The investigation page no longer says no report describes AI activity sustaining itself. It gives Amazon's attributed claim, filed as an operation run by people, and says nothing we've read confirms it. The reseller sentence there was narrowed to CrowdStrike's words ahead of round 2's review.
- **Uncertain.** Check Point's report, the source most likely to bear on hallucinated successes and operator-supplied keys, hit a rate limit and is unread. Round 2's codings, especially Microsoft's, are unreviewed.
- **Next outward question.** Does any source outside the agents' records (Amazon on its decoys, Check Point, a victim or provider) show acquired keys consumed and further hunting following from them? The dated panel reads from 18 November and round 2's reviews by 26 November carry it. The likeliest end on 4 December is inconclusive, then rest unless someone publishes telemetry of their own.
- **Spend.** About $22.57 at list rates: driver $7.07 (hb drivercost, output tokens estimated from characters), seven Opus 5.5 subagents $15.50 (hb tokens). Incomplete: the commissioning session's Codex usage is unpriced.
