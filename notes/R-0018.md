## What a reader would believe, written before reading the record

**Finding, said back.** Older versions of heavily used Python libraries keep a large part of their downloads, typically 37 per cent. The obvious reason, Pythons too old for the new version, fails for most libraries: in 32 of 37, most older downloads came from Pythons that could have taken a newer one. Where old Pythons dominate (boto3, aiobotocore, numpy), the Python only rules out the new version; in boto3 and aiobotocore something else chose the versions. A new version's share settles within two days, tested on 355 unread releases (28 of 32 libraries).

**Observed.** One week of the download log, 21 to 27 September 2026, for 37 libraries, by version and reported Python, plus the top 500 libraries' declared limits. And boto3's 23 September: 55 of 88 million downloads from Python 3.9, 95 per cent older than the last version 3.9 supports.

**Explained; causes.** Little, as the piece says. Old Pythons account for most older downloads in three libraries; other libraries' limits could in four, on an unchecked assumption; in 18 the rest merely looks like lockfile rebuilds. Established: installers take the last supported version on an old Python; pydantic's pin drives pydantic-core's downloads and settling failures.

**Limits noticed.** A download is a fetch, not a machine; the reported Python is the installer's; downloads are never linked; one week; the boto3 case was found after the counts. Also: the title claims a per-library majority, while the pooled share, with boto3 so large, is never given; five libraries drop from the settling test unexplained; "12 to 18 per cent" is an unexplained range.

## What the round establishes

The counts stand as counts, and the release review recomputed them; I did not repeat that. The title holds per library, 32 of 37 having most older downloads from admitted Pythons alone, and pooled, 64.7 per cent (r0018_check.txt D). Two of my stage-one doubts dissolve: the five libraries missing from the settling test had no release from November to March (brief.md), and the CI half of the frozen-list trace is not noise, the differences being two-sided and mostly over ten points, pure-Python utilities' older downloads more often CI and uv, the boto3 family's less (r0018_check.txt F). The wheel measure adds nothing firm for pandas: its 20.6 points of "wheel-excluded" downloads from admitted Pythons are older manylinux_2_17 wheels, counted excluded only because pandas 3.0.5 raised its glibc floor to 2.24, and an older wheel's floor is a lower bound on the machine's glibc, not its value (r0018_check.txt C).

What the record does not say and its retained files show (post hoc, mine; r0018_staircase.txt H, r0018_walk.txt I to M): the count-by-version profile of older downloads is, for boto3, botocore and aiobotocore on Pythons 3.7 to 3.10 and for grpcio-status on 3.8 to 3.10, a flat staircase descending from each Python's edge. From Python 3.9, boto3 1.42.96 down to 1.42.88 were each fetched between 886,561 and 891,453 times in the week (1.42.87, not yanked, at 486,869); below the edge, 2,021 versions carry 95 per cent of boto3's 3.9 downloads, 95 per cent of that mass lies on such plateaus, and the plateau steps down at about 100 and 600 versions' depth to nothing by 1,000. On the admitted Python 3.10 the plateau starts at the newest version and runs through R at about 175,000 a version. grpcio-status's staircase skips exactly its two yanked versions, 1.78.1 and 1.82.0 (5 and 2,063 downloads against 155,000 and 875,000 for their neighbours; data/versions.csv). Every other project's profile is jagged, with spikes at known pin targets (numpy 1.26.4, requests 2.31.0, pandas 2.2.3). A dependency resolver walking the version list from the newest admitted version down until a constraint is satisfied, skipping yanked versions, leaves exactly this; environments installing pinned versions do not. pip has backtracked so since 20.3 and has used PEP 658 metadata files instead of downloading each candidate since 22.3 (its changelog, #11111), so the plateaus are what pips older than 22.3 leave, unless the log also counts `.metadata` requests, which linehaul's parser neither settles nor rules out: it takes the package type from the log line. That the plateaus sit on 3.7 to 3.10, faintly on 3.11 and not on 3.12 or 3.13 fits the pips that Debian 11, RHEL 9, Amazon Linux 2023 and Ubuntu 22.04 ship with those Pythons, general knowledge not checked here. The walk runs every day, a sixth lower at weekends. On this reading, walk-shaped fetches are 79 per cent of boto3's older downloads, 84 of aiobotocore's, 11 to 63 of grpcio-status's, 12 to 17 of botocore's, up to 46 of fsspec's, and 17 to 21 per cent of the 37 projects' older downloads pooled; the median project has none (r0018_walkshare.txt N). The brief's three explanations all take a download to be an installation of a chosen version; the installer's own fetches of versions it inspects and rejects were outside the frame, and they are the largest single thing in the pooled older downloads. Whether the walk is one process or many the log still cannot say; it can say that about 127,000 resolves a day went at least one version below boto3's 3.9 edge and 73,000 went 500 deep (r0018_walk.txt J). So yes: the measurements can be correct while the explanation is wrong in kind for boto3, aiobotocore and grpcio-status, whose older versions were not chosen by anything but tried by the installer.

## Material claim problems

1. **The boto3 downloads are read as versions chosen, when their profile is that of versions inspected.** Passages: articles/old-versions-new-pythons.md, line 46, "Those downloads were of versions older still, chosen by something other than the Python: a pin, a lockfile, a limit we did not read, or something else the log does not show"; line 100, "The log cannot say whether they come from one population of machines fetching again and again or from many that report the same details"; line 8, "55 million of those downloads came from Python 3.9"; articles/short/old-versions-new-pythons.md, line 8, "The old Python ruled out the new version; it did not choose the old ones"; studies/LH012/LH012.md, line 16, "so something other than the Python chose them (interpretation)". A reader believes 55 million old boto3 versions a day are installed on Python 3.9 machines, held there by pins or lists. Evidence: the staircase and its yanked-version skips above (r0018_staircase.txt, r0018_walk.txt I to M, r0018_walkshare.txt N). Under the walk those versions were fetched and discarded by installers resolving a conflicting requirement; most of boto3's 3.9 downloads are not installations; P's "accounts for most" for boto3 and aiobotocore counts walk fetches (numpy's does not); and line 100's "one population or many" is moot, since the count is of versions inspected per resolve, not of machines. Resolution: keep every figure. In "What an old Python rules out" and "What a download is not", add that a download can be a version an installer inspected and rejected, that boto3's, aiobotocore's and grpcio-status's older downloads have that shape and the others' do not, and that pip fetched each candidate whole before 22.3; recast line 100's lesson as: a library's count can be dominated by installers' resolution walks, which no field in the log marks. In the record, a post hoc description with the profile scripts, a Limit, and the same qualification on P's class for the two. In the short form, "it did not choose the old ones" becomes "the installer inspected them, on the full piece's reading". Label the reading post hoc; its tests are under Next step.

2. **"Limits could account for most in four" reads as a cause distinct from lists; in three of the four the hold is by older versions of the other library.** Passages: articles/old-versions-new-pythons.md, lines 52 to 56, "the limits could account for most of the older downloads of four libraries: ... fsspec, held by s3fs ...; botocore and s3transfer, held by versions of boto3 from before May 2026"; articles/short/old-versions-new-pythons.md, line 14, "Other libraries' limits could account for most in four". Evidence: studies/LH012/data/analysis/bounds.csv, largest_held_by_current_versions: pydantic-core 53.2 per cent, fsspec 5.3, botocore 2.1, s3transfer 0.0, the rest of each hold coming from older dependent versions (r0018_check.txt E), which the record itself says "moves the question to why those dependents are held" (LH012.md, line 43) and the piece does not say. For botocore and s3transfer the boto3 fetches that would hold them are largely the walk of problem 1, which brings no botocore, as the 7.51 ratio already showed. Resolution: one sentence after the list, "Only pydantic's current versions hold by themselves; s3fs's and boto3's holds are carried by their older versions, so for the other three the question moves to why those are fetched"; in the short form, "in four, though only pydantic's current pin holds by itself".

3. **"Settles within two days" counts two libraries whose share grows by two thirds after day 2** (modest). Passages: line 77, "28 of the 32 libraries settled, 88 per cent"; line 91, "That a new version's share settles within two days and holds for the month is now a tested result"; short form, line 16. Evidence: studies/LH012/data/analysis/firstday_releases.csv via r0018_check.txt B: boto3's median release goes from 14.1 per cent on day 2 to 23.7 on day 30 (ratio 0.60; median Δ +9.6 points of a ten-point band; 55 of 99 releases within it), aiobotocore's from 2.4 to 4.0 (0.58). The band is absolute, so a small share cannot fail it. The brief fixed the ratio of the day-2 to the day-30 share as a secondary descriptor ("The first-day observation, measured") and the record does not report it. Resolution: the record reports the ratio; the piece adds "within ten points; for boto3 and aiobotocore the share is small and still growing".

None of the three changes a figure.
