## On the questions the driver asked

**(a) The time-bound reading of the 7.1.5 rule.** The framing that this reading "moves the headline 7.1 figure from 40.2% to 43.2%" (it is in the commit message of 8818067 too) isn't right, and the record itself doesn't make it. At day 24 the read is after WordPress published 7.1.5, so the brief's rule as written ("If the 7.1.5 label resolves to a release WordPress publishes, it counts as fixed") counts it, whether "while it does not" is read as time-bound or not; treatment (iii), 7.1.5 fixed at every read, gives the same 43.16% to 43.26%. What moves day 24 from 40.2% to 43.2% is that E3 is now known. The time-bound reading affects only the ten reads before 18 November 17:09 UTC, by at most 0.08 points on the upper bound, and Findings 3 says so and labels it post hoc. So 43.2% is the as-written figure, the In short and Answer are right to lead with it, and 40.2% is checkpoint 2's figure under an unknown E3, now a post hoc treatment, given in the very next sentence of Answer 1. Labelling is honest and consistent. Two small things would stop the commit message's framing travelling: say in Answer 1 in a clause that the day-24 figure is the same under either reading of "while it does not" (only E3's date decides it), and put "(both post hoc)" so the label attaches to the 40.2% as well as the 7.1.4-alone figure.

**(b) The one-off read after the stop.** Within the brief, in my judgement: E3 is defined by "the stable-check and version-check APIs and the release feed", and E0 itself is the feed's pubDate, so using the feed's pubDate for E3 is the brief's own clock, not Lighthouse's interval clock. The read used access='oneoff', was allowed by wordpress.org's robots.txt and keep_decision, made two GETs, and is disclosed in the header, Answer 4, Method and Limits. Two gaps in disclosure (smaller points 1 and 2).

**(c) Post hoc count.** Each post hoc item is labelled with its own count (6 treatments; 2 same-day comparisons; the time-bound reading; the counters' interpretation in Findings 6). No total is given (smaller point 3).

**(d) Absolutes and figures in the In short and Answer.** All checked against the window; two are wrong or unsupported (must-fix 1 and 2). "Rose at every daily read" holds for the reads of 9 to 21 November and the In short starts its series at day 12, which carries the window.

**(e) Units and causation.** Packagist is described as fetches by developers and build systems, W3Techs as shares of websites it detects; measure 2 says a difference is a description and that updating WordPress doesn't update PHP. No causal claim. The In short's "Developer downloads" is a little narrower than the brief's "a fetch by a developer or a build system" (smaller point 5).

**(f) Missing evidence.** api.wordpress.org's refused endpoints are missing, not negative, throughout; 20 November's Packagist day is called missing. But 18 and 19 November, which the record rightly calls lower bounds, are then counted as days below half and as part of "never to 19 November" (must-fix 2).

**(g) Writing.** In short present after the title; British English; no em or en dashes anywhere in the file.

## Must-fix

1. **Answer 2 misstates the day-14 comparison.** "At day 14, WordPress 7.1's fixed share (23.70% to 23.72%) was above all four PHP branches' shares at PHP day 12 (10.7% to 23.8%)": PHP 8.5 was 23.8% at day 12 (23.80% to 23.95% recomputed), above WordPress. WordPress was above 8.2, 8.3 and 8.4 and below 8.5 at PHP day 12. The same sentence's "and below PHP 8.5 at day 19 (38.9%)" leaves out that it was also below 8.4 at day 19 (27.2%). Checkpoint 2 has the same error, worse ("above every PHP branch's share at day 12, and above 8.2, 8.3 and 8.4 at day 19 but below 8.5", checkpoints/2026-11-21.md line 73; 8.4 at day 19 was 27.2%, above 23.7%). Fix Answer 2 and add the correction of checkpoint 2's sentence to the record's Corrections paragraph on differences from the checkpoints. The conclusion ("inside PHP's range, not clearly faster") is unchanged and if anything strengthened.
2. **Lower-bound Composer days are counted as below half.** Answer 3 and Findings 5 say 7.0 "never took half in either package to 19 November" (table: "none to 19 Nov") and that 6.9 was below half on "21 of the 23 days from 28 October to 19 November for roots, 19 of 23 for johnpbloch". 18 and 19 November are lower bounds (the record says so): the releases of 18 November count as fixed and their downloads weren't read, so whether those days were below half is unknown. For example roots 7.0 on 19 November, 169 of 1,472 counted, would reach half if 7.0.9 had 1,134 or more downloads that day, which nothing read rules out. Say "to 17 November" (the last day the counts cover every published release) with 18 and 19 November given as lower bounds, and give the 6.9 counts as 19 of the 21 days from 28 October to 17 November (roots) and 17 of 21 (johnpbloch).
