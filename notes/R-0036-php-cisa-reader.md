## What a reader would believe, written before reading the record

A careful reader would say: two serious PHP flaws were fixed on 21 October, but Amazon says a decoy server saw an attempt to exploit them on 17 October, so attackers may have known first. Since then the US government agency CISA has officially classed both flaws as exploited, which most readers would take as confirmation that real attacks happened, beyond decoys and firewall matches, and as backing for Amazon's and Cloudflare's reports. The notice's caveats (unstated criteria, no count, no named victim, sources unknown) would temper that, but "known exploited" with a 29 October deadline reads as an authority confirming the danger. Some readers would also infer that the third flaw, being unlisted, has not been exploited; the piece does not claim that.

Observed by Lighthouse itself: fixed image digests, five derivative images declaring fixed versions, one Drupal layer match, page revisions, CISA's feed lacking then holding the entries, and a partly readable institution panel. Reported by others, not observed: Amazon's attempts, Cloudflare's traffic and protection times, OpenAI's account of AI-assisted discovery. Causal chain a reader takes away: AI helped a researcher find a flaw, coordination produced fixes, the fixes flowed into published images. No causal link is drawn from attempts to compromise, or from images to fixed sites.

Limits a reader would notice: no attack totals, no deployment or pull counts, no evidence attackers used AI, CISA's evidence unknown, Amazon's text not retained as bytes, an incomplete search log, and a later flaw the fixes do not close.

## Material claim problems

**M1. The listing reads as independent confirmation of real attacks, but may rest on the same reported attempts.** Passages: articles/php-emergency-first-reading.md, attack section, "so it stands beside these two reports as a judgement, not as a total"; the 27 October notice, "An entry is CISA's classification of a flaw as known to be exploited, by criteria the feed itself does not state". The front page and the summary carry the same framing. In stage one I came away believing an agency had confirmed exploitation beyond decoys and edge matches. Evidence: in r0036_check.txt, the entries' notes cite only php.net, CISA's own 21 October alert, two BOD 26-04 pages and NVD. They name no source for exploitation, and the feed nowhere contains "honeypot", "in the wild" or "criteria". The LH031 brief ("What is already known") records SOCRadar's report that CISA said on 21 October it had not observed exploitation. LH028 holds only attempts at a decoy and traffic at an edge, with no compromise. So the record fits equally well with a listing that restates Amazon's and Cloudflare's public reports, which would add no second line of evidence, and with no attack known to have succeeded. One further point comes from my own background knowledge, not from any retained source, so it needs checking: CISA's published KEV criteria, as I knew them before June 2026, count attempted exploitation, honeypot hits included. Only the dated list's "cannot say whether it was Amazon's, Cloudflare's or something else" addresses this, and it sits far below the notice. Resolution: in the notice and the attack section, replace "stands beside" with something like: "The feed cites no source for exploitation, so the listing may rest on the same public reports of attempts; it does not show that any attempt succeeded." Add "or proof that any succeeded" after "not a count of attacks" in the summary and on the front page. Describe what an entry requires only after a retained read of CISA's criteria page.

**M2. The unlisted third flaw invites a "not exploited" reading.** Passages: the full piece's notice, "CVE-2026-115560, below, is not listed"; the short form, "The further flaw PHP described on 23 October, in the next notice, is not listed." Placed straight after "known exploited", the sentence reads as a contrast. Evidence: LH031.md, Limits, "its absence of an entry is not a negative finding"; the LH031 brief, "an absence is not evidence that no exploitation occurred". In addition, the fixed versions for that flaw are not yet released (LH033 brief). The study states this assumption, and the pieces drop it. Resolution: add "; its absence is not a finding that it has not been exploited" in both notices.
