## Rulings on the writer's judgement calls

- **A03 (one GET returned the authors' project page, not the paper).** The reread confirms the page holds the title, authors, abstract and a "Paper" link, and nothing else of the paper. The brief reads each document "by one gated one-off GET of its full text" and says "the abstract page is not fetched separately"; its only ground for excluding a document as unavailable is "full text unavailable (a wall, refusal or error)". A 200 page that is not the document is none of these, and the full text is linked from it. So the exclusion has no ground in the brief, the GET was not the read the brief defines, and a full-text GET is allowed within the twenty before 15 December 2026, counting the first GET as used (conservatively). A03 is a peer-reviewed USENIX Security 2018 paper on revoking abused code-signing certificates, squarely a Part A measure on its title and abstract. A candidate is still readable, so the record is interim (M1). I did not make that read: it is the study's read, not a review's, and the brief allows one reading subagent.
- **Meli and others as containment.** Upheld. The measure is a repository owner removing a leaked secret from the head of the branch, which is taking content down by an operator, and the brief listed Meli as a known Part A document to be "checked at source" on exactly this point. The paper's own second finding (A02-2, removed secrets stayed in history) is what keeps the reading honest, and the record reports it. The record already says it isn't revocation.
- **arXiv 2611.30483 (A08) at the containment edge.** Upheld as an inclusion, by the brief's own direction ("recorded for its recoverability outcome only"), which a fixed protocol makes binding. On the definition alone, undoing damage from a backup or snapshot is recovery rather than an action that stops harmful activity under way, so the record should say in its Answer that A08 is a laboratory recovery measure included because the brief named it (folded into M2's new text).
- **Quantum Dawn VII as a cyber recovery exercise.** Upheld, narrowly, with a should-fix (S6). The report frames the exercise within "significant operational outages such as a cyber incident", sits in SIFMA's cybersecurity exercise series, and its day three covers "data resilience status and recoverability". But it never calls the scenario a cyber incident: it was a "data disruption event" at a fictional cloud-hosted critical third party, and "the cause of the outage was due to an issue with the third party and not cloud related". The record should give that wording so a reader sees the edge.
- **SIMEX 24's exclusion.** Upheld. The reread page never mentions cyber; the scenario is "a major infrastructure failure that would require a total shut down and restart of the sector", and the page gives no result. Condition 1 fails on the document read. The difference from Quantum Dawn VII is that VII's own text names cyber incidents and data recovery and SIMEX 24's names neither. The brief's assurance-only clause applies only to a document that meets all three conditions.
- **A05-5's setting.** Change to laboratory (S1). The brief's laboratory label covers "a controlled test or simulation"; 42 of 45 is the authors' system estimate of takedowns that were never carried out, a simulation on field data.
- **B11-3's class.** Upheld as asserted. The report's method draws on "players, evaluators and controllers", but for "minimal interaction between players" it shows no count, observation method or objective, which the brief's measured class needs. See N8 on B11-5 and B11-6.
- **A08's population mismatch.** A real error, not a summary choice (M2): the 0-to-15 per cent range is from agent trials on nine engines, not "scripted and agent mistakes" on "four relational and other engines".

The Answer repeats the range.

**M3. A05-3 says the opposite of the paper on lookups to sinkholed domains.** The record: "At the second, lookups to sinkholed domains dropped but related domains left untouched kept receiving lookups". The paper, on the March 2012 takedown: "We see a spike in lookup traffic directed towards the seed domains and domains that resolve to Microsoft's sinkhole, indicating increased sinkholing action at the time of takedown", and "We see a drop in lookups to Dr that corresponds to the Microsoft sunk domains" (Dr being domains its reputation system flagged); that further malicious domains were missed is the authors' reading of their improvement ratios (Table 2), not a stated count of lookups. The direction, mixed, can stand. The derived file's paraphrase has the same slip.
