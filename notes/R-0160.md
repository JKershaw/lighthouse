# R-0160: reader-and-inference review of LH061 record 0.2

## What a reader would believe, written before reading the record

Read from lines 1 to 23 only (the header, In short and Answer), on 26 November 2026.

**The finding, said back.** Most of what researchers have published about stopping internet attacks while they're under way, like taking down botnets and domains or revoking leaked keys and certificates, comes from real measurement. But the results point in every direction and can't be added up. Exercises that practise recovery across organisations publish no measured results at all, only what participants said and what organisers claimed.

**Observed.** Seven containment papers were read, and 22 of their 24 claims were measured, mostly in the field. Several findings are reported one at a time: one takedown stopped lookups on the day, domains stayed down about two years, most leaked secrets stayed up for 16 days, certificate revocation was slow and patchy, exposed credentials halved after notification and then crept back. Two exercise reports were read, with 12 claims and no measured outcome among them. Three of the five named exercise series couldn't be read.

**Explained.** Little is explained. The study counts what kind of evidence sits behind each claim. It doesn't ask why the outcomes differ.

**Caused what.** A reader might take the paper findings, such as notification halving exposures, as causal. The record presents them as each author's own result, in the author's terms, and doesn't endorse those results as causes.

**Limits a reader would notice.** The measures are too different to compare. Nothing here says whether containment works in general or works now. One included paper is really about recovery. The exercise result covers two reports and may lean towards those that are openly published. The version is unreleased and still waiting on further review.

### Material claim problems

**1. The measured share is built into the sample, and the gap between Part A and Part B reflects the kinds of document each part took in.**
- Passage: LH061.md line 3, "We asked how much of what's said about containing and recovering from internet incidents has actually been measured. For containment ... 22 of their 24 outcome claims were measured". The same sentence is in sources.md's In short.
- Evidence: brief.md, inclusion condition 2 for Part A, admits only documents that measure, model or report an outcome, and Part A's venues are research conferences, journals, arXiv and agency reports. Vendor pages and blogs, where the brief's writer expected most credential-revocation claims to live (brief.md, "The writer's expectation"), count only as locators. sources.md, "Context, not counted", sets aside a takedown vendor's and a secret-scanning vendor's figures on that rule. Part B's candidates are organisers' own after-action reports (brief.md, Venues, Part B), a genre made of polls and conclusions.
- Why it matters: "what's said" mostly sits outside Part A's sample. So 22 of 24 describes peer-reviewed outcome studies, which measure by design. It doesn't show that containment claims in general are measured. The contrast between the parts also follows from source type at least as much as from containment versus recovery. A reader (stage one above) takes both as findings about the fields. The second half of the question, "what does it only assert", could hardly get a non-trivial answer in Part A under this design.
- Resolution: in the In short and Answer, and in sources.md's In short, say the share belongs to the outcome studies the rule admits, which report outcomes by construction. Say Part A and Part B drew on different kinds of document, so the contrast between them is partly a matter of source type. Add a line to Limits saying Part A couldn't count assertions, because vendor and guidance sources were locators only. No figure changes.

**2. The In short counts a recovery test among the containment documents.**
- Passage: LH061.md line 3, "For containment (taking down domains and botnets, sinkholing, removing or revoking leaked keys and certificates), seven documents were read and included".
- Evidence: the Answer (line 20) and claims.csv for A08-1 and A08-2 say A08 measures undoing damage in a laboratory, "recovery rather than stopping harm under way", included because the brief named it. A02 measures owners' removal of their own secrets, "a judgement for review" (sources.md).
- Why it matters: a reader of the In short believes there are seven field containment studies. Six are containment, one only arguably, and the one measured recovery outcome in the whole study sits in Part A, beside "recovery ... neither reports a measured outcome".
- Resolution: in the In short, write "seven documents, one of them a laboratory test of recovery that the brief named for this part". Optionally say in the Answer that A02's measure is owners removing their own leaks.

**3. The vendor label isn't applied consistently.**
- Passage: LH061.md line 20, "8 rest on security companies' own data (... 3 on a company's internal passive DNS database)".
- Evidence: A05-1, A05-3 and A05-4 are labelled vendor because they run on a company's passive DNS database (claims.csv). A01's five measured claims rest on "commercial passive DNS (Farsight)" (derived/A01-alowaisheq-ndss2019.json, data_public) and are labelled vendor no. The difference seems to be A05's author affiliation, which the brief's definition ("a vendor's own customers or product telemetry") doesn't mention.
- Resolution: apply one rule and state it. Either relabel A01 (13 of 22 measured claims vendor) or define the label as resting on a company's own data with an author tied to that company. Neither choice triggers the brief's "all vendor" condition, so the Answer's conclusion stands either way.

Considered and not material: (a) association read as cause. A07's "halved 14 days after notification" and A05's "stopped on the day" are attributed in the authors' terms, the unprompted baseline (A07-1) is reported, and the brief rules out causal claims in Lighthouse's voice. (b) "Measured" read as "shown to work". The In short's "run in every direction" and the Answer's last sentence block that reading. (c) Commissioning against method. The keeper asked whether containment works in practice (P-0046). The brief answers a narrower question about evidence types, and the record says plainly that it can't say whether anything works. That is a design choice, stated, not a contradiction. (d) Part B's two reports are uneven. Quantum Dawn VII is included though its report "doesn't call it a cyber incident", while SIMEX 24 is excluded for that reason (sources.md, B07 and B04), and the evidence review upheld both narrowly. Losing B07 would leave Part B with one report and an unchanged answer.

## Resolution, by the driving session

Written on 26 November 2026 by a Claude Code subagent on Claude Opus 5.5 for Research run 2026-11-26T201000Z-anthropic-research, at the driving session's request. The record is now version 0.3; the reviewer's text above is unchanged. No network reads were made.

**Problem 1, the measured share built into the sample.** Accepted. The In short, the Answer (both parts) and sources.md's In short now say that Part A admitted only documents from its listed venues that measure, model or report an outcome, excluded guidance reporting none, and kept vendors' pages, blogs and news as leads only, so 22 of 24 describes the outcome studies admitted and not containment claims in general; and that Part A and Part B drew on different kinds of document, so the contrast between them is partly one of source type. A Limits line says Part A couldn't count assertions in the way the question asks. No figure changed.

**Problem 2, A08 counted as containment.** Accepted. The In short now says one of the seven documents is a laboratory test of recovery that the brief named for Part A, and both the In short and the Answer say it is the study's only measured recovery outcome; the Part B answer says so beside "neither reports a measured outcome", noting that A08 is not an exercise. The optional point on A02 is taken: the Answer describes it as repository owners removing their own leaked secrets, a judgement.

**Problem 3, the vendor label.** Accepted, by relabelling A01 rather than by the affiliation rule. The brief's definition says nothing of authors' employers, so a rule turning on affiliation would add a condition the brief doesn't have. The rule now stated in Method: a claim is vendor when the observations it counts come in whole or in part from data a company collects and holds for itself or sells (customer or product telemetry, a sample repository, a commercial or internal passive DNS database); authors' employment is recorded as an interest, not used for the label; openly published data and public documents found by way of company data (A03-3's revocation lists) don't count. The rule was stated after all claims were read and is labelled post hoc in Method. A01-1 to A01-5 are relabelled in claims.csv; the tally was rerun (`python3 -I studies/LH061/scripts/tally.py studies/LH061`) and scripts/tally-output.txt updated: Part A vendor 14 of 24 claims (was 9), 13 of the 22 measured (was 8); every class, setting and data count is unchanged. The brief's all-vendor condition is still not met. One judgement for the recheck: A01-2's outcome (release for purchase) was observed by the authors' own registrar queries, and it is labelled vendor because its population of taken-down domains and their dates rest in part on the commercial passive DNS, per the derived file; the paper wasn't reread to confirm which data each A01 claim uses.

**Readability.** 1, taken: Locked Shields is now described as having no public report located (its series page links none, fetchlog.csv row L5), in the In short, the Answer and sources.md's In short. 2, taken: the Answer's Part A findings are a list, one per document. 3, taken in part: the review-sequence sentence left the In short and the header's Version field carries it, but AGENTS.md asks each In short to say what happens next, so one plain line stays ("This version isn't released: one fresh check of what its last two revisions wrote comes first").
