## Caveats for copy and implementation

- N4 and N6 are historical readings with current qualifications, not live conclusions revived from their titles. Their figures stand for their samples; their proposed general explanations weakened.
- N7's current version is 2.0, carrying the uncertainty established in 1.1. Version 1.0's suggestion that the result leaned against the notes was corrected: failure to distinguish typical libraries was not evidence of no effect (`the-lead-was-a-few-libraries.md:L6,L86`). Do not label this node “notes made no difference”.
- N8 uses version 1.2. Do not say “never” without the observation window, treat litellm's six hits as gradual takeovers, or say shares stood still (`two-days-for-most.md:L6–L7,L17,L81`). The later lower-rank boundary remains visible beside this branch rather than buried as provenance.
- Keep event dates, original read dates, publication/revision dates and this pilot's assertion date separate. The early articles are retrospective reads of March–August records, not a live watch in those months.
- The investigation's explicit direction-change bullets support the inquiry arrows. Article colophons and record links support provenance; neither is evidence of intellectual influence by an unnamed person or a model.
- Do not infer AI self-spread, deployment uptake, vulnerability exposure, successful attacks or benefit from this map. The released synthesis explicitly separates ordinary software from AI activity (`F:articles/where-software-updates-go.md:L84–L90`), and every selected public record stops before a run (`L82`).
- This proposal covers the core Python inquiry. The investigation also places PHP, Node.js and newer side studies beside it. Do not imply that absence from this first diagram makes them absent from Lighthouse's accumulated understanding.
