The last relation does not connect adjacent cards at all. The topic trail similarly draws a reading route whose first two data relations run from the lower card to the upper card. Its third is disclosure protocol `uses` watch protocol. There are no arrowheads, which reduces but does not remove the ambiguity: “Read top to bottom” and a connecting line encourage the adjacent-card reading. The plain-text connection section states the correct graph directions. Keep the reading order if useful, but label every connector with the actual two endpoints and direction, or distinguish reading-route lines from graph relations. Do not imply that correcting the visual order would establish causation. **Resolved in the final source reread:** `loadLineage` now groups links by their actual subject, panels name the predicate and target, and jump links point to the object node. Export the full row or sufficient adjoining sentence(s), with the same frozen hashes and line spans. Do not present retained review quotations as a fresh pilot reading of METR.

5. **Repair the missing evaluation route and document the amended sample rule.** In the inspected build, `/notes/knowledge-lineage/` is linked as “Pilot record and evaluation” but has no generated index. ## Structural coverage and static reader checks

`python3 scripts/lineage/extract.py --check` passed. The export contains 87 numbered input rows, the fixed 17 ordinals 1, 6, …, 81, 19 row-to-source edges, 12 panel-membership edges, six trail edges and two candidates. All 17 sampled rows have at least one resolved source reference, with no substitution or failed reference-ID match. All recorded input hashes independently matched bytes obtained with `git show` at the frozen commit. This establishes deterministic extraction and positional coverage, not substantive support. The external records are correctly labelled panel membership only; they do not overstate successful source access.

The inspected generated HTML contains the reading text, cards, evidence navigation and native `details`/`summary` controls without requiring JavaScript to construct them. Base scripts are present, so this is a no-JavaScript content-availability check, not a claim that no scripts exist. The page has one main heading, labelled trail sections/figures and descriptive card links. Native links and summaries are keyboard-operable by design; visible focus rules exist for both. In the initial inspected stylesheet the 520px breakpoint stacks choices and clocks, long URLs can wrap, and there is no lineage animation or transition requiring reduced-motion treatment. Both colour schemes inherit contrasting ink, muted text and accent variables; relationship status also appears in words and line style.

These are positive static findings. They do not establish absence of overflow at 390px, actual focus traversal and scroll behaviour, browser treatment of anchors into closed details, text zoom, contrast in rendered states, or usability with assistive technology. Repeated labels such as “Exact retained passage” rely on nearby context rather than unique destination names; adding the card title to accessible link text would improve link-list navigation. Printing hides choices; the final template reread shows the plain-text trail evidence now opens by default, improving print availability. Other closed details and the final print view still need a check before calling it a complete evidence handout. Separate render proofs can supplement this note, but an unfamiliar human journey and the normal release review remain future requirements. No comprehension gain or research benefit is established here.
