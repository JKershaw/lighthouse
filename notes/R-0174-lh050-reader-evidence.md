## Must fix

1. **The Financial Times access line is out of date.** Piece, "The two we couldn't read": "were closed, when we tried on 13 November, to the automated readers we run on, by the sites' own rules for robots (their robots.txt files), which we follow." Colophon: "Not readable: the Financial Times and Wired (refused by robots.txt)". Short form: "One was refused to our automated readers by the site's rules". Source: on 13 November the refusing group was ClaudeBot, `Disallow: /`, under the strict rule then in force (data/2026-11-13T1454Z/robots/S01_ft.json, S02_wired.json). On 28 November 15:14:35Z a one-off read under the keeper's rule of 21 November found the FT allowed under `*` and the server answered HTTP 403, a wall, not retried; Wired was refused again, by its Claude-User group (notes/physical-authority/essay-checks/2026-11-28/fetchlog.md, committed before HEAD in 6cb4ab0). So the robots.txt refusal is now the whole story only for Wired. Suggested: "On 13 November both sites' robots.txt files refused the automated agents whose rules we then followed. A single read of the Financial Times page on 28 November, under a narrower rule for one-off reads, was refused by the site itself (HTTP 403); Wired's rules still refused us." Colophon: "Not readable: Wired (refused by robots.txt on 13 and 28 November) and the Financial Times (refused by robots.txt on 13 November; HTTP 403 on a one-off read on 28 November)". Short form: "One was closed to us, by the site's robots rules and then by the site itself".

2. **The Gaza lead's provenance is not said.** Piece, "Three kinds of robot": "a search summary attributes that report to the Financial Times, which we couldn't read"; and "The two we couldn't read": "The Financial Times report on the proposed Gaza use". Source: the search summary was seen by the commissioning survey (an OpenAI Codex session, 12 November 2026) and is not retained (sources.md, PA-S01, and its preamble: "no raw source bodies or replayable fetch logs came with that survey"); the brief records it as "A search summary attributed preliminary Gaza discussions to the Financial Times" (brief.md line 23). Lighthouse's own searches of 13 November found nothing naming Gaza (searches/foundation_followup.json, selection note; claim C020). The piece never implies the FT was read, which is right, but the present tense "attributes" and "The Financial Times report on the proposed Gaza use" present the report's subject as known, from a source a reader can't check and that the piece doesn't say was someone else's and unkept. Suggested: "...whose proposed use in Gaza we tried to follow: a search summary seen when this work was commissioned on 12 November, which we didn't keep, credited a report of preliminary Gaza discussions to the Financial Times, and we couldn't read that report." In the later section: "The Financial Times report said to describe the Gaza discussions".

3. **The opening's present tense claims currency the sources don't give.** Piece, first paragraph: "In the departure hall of Changi Airport's Terminal 4, a police robot called GIBSON patrols on its own". Source: SPF's page of 21 July 2025 (S28: "On trial at the Terminal 4 Departure Hall since July 2025") and HTX of 31 July 2025 (S29). The piece itself says, rightly, "nothing we read reports either robot's status after those dates" (the record's Answer says the same). Suggested: "In the departure hall of Changi Airport's Terminal 4, a police robot called GIBSON began a trial in July 2025, patrolling on its own..." or "...was patrolling on its own in July 2025, the police said". Same for "Officers can call it" (could) if the paragraph is recast.

4. **The 26 November reread claim outruns what was retained and reviewed.** Piece, "Three kinds of robot": "and reread the four news reports on Phantom on 19 and 26 November; their text hadn't changed." Source: three reports check out against retained copies (reread_compare.out), and Assembly's 19 November copy matches by stored hash; but Assembly's 26 November comparison was made from a copy not kept (checkpoints/2026-11-26-read.md, Accounting: "The comparison below used this run's local copy, which git ignores and isn't retained"; its derived file records no article-text hash). Both weekly checkpoints are also unreviewed, and the colophon labels only the Geneva reads as unreviewed. Suggested: "...and reread the four news reports on Phantom on 19 and 26 November; as far as we could check, their text hadn't changed (one site's rules meant we kept no copy of its page on 26 November, so that comparison can't be repeated)." In the colophon's Methods, name the weekly rereads of 19 and 26 November as unreviewed working reads, with this review's check of three of them.

## Should consider

1. **The AI hedge.** "Nor, as we read them, does any source say which of these robots' functions, if any, use AI, as distinct from automated navigation." It holds narrowly: no source names a function as using AI, and every AI-function cell is not established. But eWeek calls them "autonomous humanoid robots" (S25) and The Next Web, under an "Artificial Intelligence" label, ends "The age of AI robots in war is near" (S27). And "as distinct from automated navigation" sits awkwardly beside The Next Web's reported "fully autonomous decisions", which is not navigation. A reader who meets those words in the sources may think the piece missed them. Suggested: "Some reports call the robots autonomous, and one writes of 'AI robots' in war, but none, as we read them, says which of the robots' functions, if any, use AI."
2. **"The automated readers we run on".** The 13 November refusal was decided by the ClaudeBot group, Anthropic's crawler token, which the strict rule then obeyed. Lighthouse doesn't run on ClaudeBot. Must fix 1's wording covers this.
3. **Whose sentence the time-critical autonomy is.** TNW: "Pathak said some weaponised uses of the Phantom robots will retain human confirmation in the decision loop. In certain time-critical scenarios, the robots will need to make fully autonomous decisions." The second sentence isn't explicitly attributed. The record and baseline treat it as the CEO's, as reported, which is a fair reading. Consider "reported ... and went on, apparently still giving his view, that..." or keep "as reported" in the short form too.
4. **TechCentral's credit.** "TechCentral wrote in December 2025, in its own words". The article ends with a Business AM credit, and the record says it "may be a syndicated copy" (LH050.md, Limits). Consider "TechCentral, in an article credited to Business AM, wrote in its own words...".
5. **Who developed GIBSON.** "HTX, the Singapore government agency that developed it": both SPF and HTX say it was developed by HTX with A*STAR's Institute for Infocomm Research. Consider "that developed it with A*STAR researchers".
6. **Changi is two robots, not configurations.** "The third is the pair of police patrol robots at Changi" may read as two units; SPF's 2023 release speaks of "patrol robots", number not given. And "The Phantom and Changi cases split into several versions, since a robot can be configured differently for different jobs" fits Phantom, not Changi's two different robot types. Consider "two kinds of police patrol robot" and "The Phantom case splits into three versions, and Changi into its two robots, which gives six rows."
7. **Wired's description.** "a Wired article that a search listing described as about the company's weapons plans": the register's note ("Foundation weapons ambitions; search result only in the survey") is the commissioning survey's description, not a quoted listing. Consider "a Wired article that the earlier survey's search turned up on the company's weapons ambitions".
8. **The Ukraine paragraph.** It is labelled as chosen after seeing it, accurate (S30, 31,398 "logistics and evacuation missions"; no word on arms or control in S30 or S32), and does no harm. But it doesn't bear on the question the piece asks, it brings in the piece's largest number, and the record notes the ministry has revised earlier monthly figures (June 16,664 and July 19,969 in the August notice against 16,663 and 19,935 in October's; LH050.md, Context series). Consider cutting it; if kept, add "a count the ministry has revised before".
9. **The title.** At about 30 words over two sentences it is long for a title read without the body. It does state the tension, with its window. Consider "Who decides when a robot may use force? The sources we could read didn't say" and leave the closed cases to the summary, which already carries them.
10. **"A camera on a mast".** SPF says "extendable mast with 360-degree field of view". Consider "an extendable mast with a 360-degree view".
11. **Consistency at release.** The investigation page still says the baseline "is unreviewed working material" (record 0.1 was reviewed on 16 November), says nothing about the conference outcome, and has no current account or `pieces` entry. AGENTS.md's orientation says "there is no finding and no current account". The piece is no more certain than the record and checkpoints, but before release the investigation page needs a line that carries this account at the same certainty, with the conference outcome given "as the posted final document records it" (checkpoint of 27 November, For Research, item 4). The short form is 255 words to its link, a touch over the house's 250; fine.
